
\section*{4. Passage to the quotient by a finite flat pre-equivalence
  relation (proof of a special case)}

\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{4. Passage to the quotient by a finite flat pre-equivalence relation}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-V-4.1}
\noindent\textbf{Theorem 4.1.}---
\textit{Let}

\begin{equation*}
  \resizebox{0.40\textwidth}{!}{%
    \SGAThreeNativeInput{figures/exp5/p261_theorem41_groupoid.png}}
  \phantomsection\label{fig:sga3-exp5-theorem41-groupoid}
\end{equation*}

\textit{be a \((\mathbf{Sch}/S)\)-groupoid such that
\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-V-4.1-a}\(d_1\) is finite locally free and that,
\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-V-4.1-b}for every \(x\in X_0\), the set
\(d_0d_1^{-1}(x)\) is contained in an affine open subset of
\(X_0\). Then:}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V, observes
that these hypotheses are symmetric in \(d_0\) and \(d_1\): with \(\sigma\) the
involutive automorphism of \(X_1\) of \S1, \(d_0=d_1\sigma\) is finite locally
free as well, and \(d_1^{-1}(x)=\sigma(d_0^{-1}(x))\), whence
\(d_0d_1^{-1}(x)=d_1d_0^{-1}(x)\). It adds that the second hypothesis cannot be
omitted: H.~Hironaka has given an action of \(\mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z\) on a proper
algebraic variety over \(\mathbf C\) whose quotient is an algebraic space that
is not a scheme (H.~Hironaka, \emph{An example of a non-Kählerian
complex-analytic deformation of Kählerian complex structures}, Ann.\ of
Math.\ \textbf{75} (1962), 190--208; the re-edition also refers to D.~Mumford,
\emph{Geometric invariant theory}, Springer, 1965, Chapter~4, \S3).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)]\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-V-4.1-i}%
  \textit{there exists a cokernel \((Y,p)\) of \((d_0,d_1)\) in
  \((\mathbf{Sch}/S)\). Moreover, such a pair \((Y,p)\) is a
  cokernel of \((d_0,d_1)\) in the category of all ringed spaces;}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{thm:sga3-V-4.1:item:ii}(ii)]\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-V-4.1-ii}%
  \textit{\(p\) is integral, and \(Y\) is affine if
  \(X_0\) is affine;}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V, adds
that \(p\) is open. Indeed, \(d_1\) is finite, locally free and surjective (it
has the section \(s\)), hence flat and of finite presentation, hence open
(EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 2.4.6); so for every open subset \(U'\) of \(X_0\), the
set \(d_1(d_0^{-1}(U'))\) of points equivalent to points of \(U'\) is open, and
\(p(U')\), which is its image, is open in \(Y\), since \(Y\) has the quotient
topology by~(i).}
\item[(iii)]\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-V-4.1-iii}%
  \textit{the morphism
  \[
    X_1\longrightarrow X_0\times_YX_0
  \]
  with components \(d_0\) and \(d_1\) is surjective;}
\item[(iv)]\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-V-4.1-iv}%
  \textit{if \((d_0,d_1)\) is an equivalence pair, then
  \(X_1\to X_0\times_YX_0\) is an isomorphism}
  \textit{and \(p:X_0\to Y\) is finite locally free.}\footnote{Translator's note: as the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V,
notes, in this case \(X_1\to X_0\times_SX_0\) is an immersion, being the
composite of the isomorphism \(X_1\to X_0\times_YX_0\) and of the immersion
\(X_0\times_YX_0\to X_0\times_SX_0\) (EGA~I, 5.3.10). For quotients by
equivalence relations with \(d_0\), \(d_1\) finite but not necessarily flat, as
schemes or as algebraic spaces, it refers to S.~Anantharaman, \emph{Schémas en
groupes, espaces homogènes et espaces algébriques sur une base de
dimension~1}, Mém.\ Soc.\ Math.\ France \textbf{33} (1973), 5--79, \S1.1;
D.~Ferrand, \emph{Conducteur, descente et pincement}, Bull.\ Soc.\ Math.\
France \textbf{131} (2003), 553--585; and J.~Kollár, \emph{Quotients by finite
equivalence relations}, arXiv:0812.3608.}
\end{enumerate}

It follows immediately from \hyperref[thm:sga3-V-4.1-i]{(i)} that the underlying topological space
of \(Y\) is the quotient of the underlying topological space of
\(X_0\) by the equivalence relation defined by the
\((\mathbf{Sch}/S)\)-groupoid \(X_\ast\).

We shall first prove \hyperref[thm:sga3-V-4.1]{the theorem} when \(X_0\) is affine and \(d_1\) is
locally free of constant rank \(n\). We shall then see how to reduce
the general situation to this special case.
