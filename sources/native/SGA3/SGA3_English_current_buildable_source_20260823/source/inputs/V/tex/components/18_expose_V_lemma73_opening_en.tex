\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-V-7.3}%
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 7.3.}---%
\emph{Retain the notation and hypotheses of
Theorem~\hyperref[thm:sga3-V-7.1]{7.1}. Every point \(z\in X_0\)
that is closed relative to \(S\) has a saturated open neighborhood
\(U_z\) such that the groupoid induced by \(X_\ast\) on \(U_z\) has
a quasi-section.}

The assertion is local on \(S\), so we may suppose that \(S\) is
affine and apply \hyperref[lem:sga3-V-7.2]{the preceding lemma} to the diagram
\SGAThreeDiagram[0.30\textwidth]
  {figures/exp5/p276_lemma73_base_square.png}
  {The diagram to which \hyperref[lem:sga3-V-7.2]{Lemma~7.2} is applied.}
  {fig:sga3-V-7.3-base-square}
  {Polo--Gille re-edition, Expos\'e V}
in \((\mathbf{Sch}/S)\). Thus let \(F\) be a closed subscheme of
\(X_0\) such that
\[
  d_0\bigl(d_0^{-1}(F)\cap d_1^{-1}(z)\bigr)
\]
is finite and nonempty, and such that the restriction of \(d_1\) to
\(d_0^{-1}(F)\) is flat at the points of \(d_1^{-1}(z)\).

Let \(F_1\) and \(F_2\) be the inverse images of \(F\) under \(d_0\)
and \(d_0d'_0=d_0d'_1\), respectively, and let
\(\widetilde d_0,\widetilde d_1,\ldots\) denote the morphisms induced
by \(d_0,d_1,\ldots\). We then have a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=4.4em, row sep=4.2em]
F_2
  \arrow[r, shift left=0.9ex, "{\widetilde d'_1}"]
  \arrow[r, shift right=0.9ex, swap, "{\widetilde d'_0}"]
  \arrow[d, swap, "{\widetilde d'_2}"]
&
F_1
  \arrow[r, "{\widetilde d_0}"]
  \arrow[d, "{\widetilde d_1}"]
&
F
\\
X_1
  \arrow[r, shift left=0.9ex, "d_1"]
  \arrow[r, shift right=0.9ex, swap, "d_0"]
&
X_0
&
\end{tikzcd}
\]
in which the two left-hand squares are Cartesian and the first row is
exact (cf. the diagram
\hyperref[sga3:v:lemma:1-2:equation:0-1-2]{\((0,1,2)\)} in \hyperref[sec:sga3-V-1]{\S1}).

We first show that only finitely many points of \(F_1\) lie over
\(z\). Indeed, let \(s\) be the image of \(z\) in \(S\); since
\(\widetilde d_0(\widetilde d_1^{-1}(z))\) is finite, the points of this set
are closed in the fiber of \(X_0\) over \(s\); hence there are only finitely
many points \(u\) of \(X_0\times_SX_0\) whose second projection is \(z\) and
whose first projection belongs to \(\widetilde d_0(\widetilde d_1^{-1}(z))\);
finally, since \(X_1\to X_0\times_SX_0\) is quasi-finite, each such point
\(u\) comes from only finitely many points of \(X_1\), whence the assertion.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V, details
this step and the role of properness in it. The set
\(\widetilde d_0(\widetilde d_1^{-1}(z))\) is finite by the choice of \(F\); it is
closed in the fibre \(F_s\) of \(F\) over \(s\), because
\(\widetilde d_1^{-1}(z)\) is closed in the fibre of \(F_1\) over \(s\) (\(z\) being
closed in its fibre) and \(\widetilde d_0\), deduced by base change from
\(d_0=d_1\sigma\), is proper. A finite closed subset of \(F_s\), which is of
finite type over \(\kappa(s)\), consists of closed points, and these are
closed in the fibre of \(X_0\), \(F\) being closed in \(X_0\). If \(y\) is one of
them, \(\kappa(y)\) and \(\kappa(z)\) are finite over \(\kappa(s)\), so the points
of \(X_0\times_SX_0\) above \((y,z)\), which correspond to the prime ideals of
\(\kappa(y)\otimes_{\kappa(s)}\kappa(z)\), are finite in number. Compare 8.1,
where \(d_1\) is not assumed proper.}
