The purpose of this expos\'e is to prove the theorems stated in TDTE~III.\footnote{Translator's note: TDTE~III is A.~Grothendieck, \emph{Technique de
descente et théorèmes d'existence en géométrie algébrique.~III. Préschémas
quotients}, Séminaire Bourbaki, exposé~212 (1961). The Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Exposé~V, specifies the theorems meant: 5.1 and 5.3 of
TDTE~III, which correspond to \hyperref[thm:sga3-V-4.1]{Theorem~4.1} below; 6.1 and 6.2, which correspond to
\hyperref[thm:sga3-V-7.1]{Theorems~7.1} and~\hyperref[thm:sga3-V-8.1]{8.1}; and 7.2, which is proved in Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, 3.2
and~3.3.}
If \(X\) and \(T\) are two objects of a category~\(\mathscr C\), we write
\(X(T)\) instead of \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr C}(T,X)\). Similarly, if
\(\varphi:Y\to X\) and \(f:U\to T\) are two arrows of \(\mathscr C\), then
\(\varphi(T)\) denotes the map \(g\mapsto\varphi\circ g\) from \(Y(T)\) to
\(X(T)\), and \(Y(f)\) the map \(g\mapsto g\circ f\) from \(Y(T)\) to
\(Y(U)\). Finally, if \(P\) is a scheme, we denote by \(\underline P\) the underlying
set of~\(P\).

As an exception, in the present expos\'e we do not follow the convention on
quotient notation stated in \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:scholium:4:6:15}{IV~4.6.15} (\emph{loc. cit.}, top of p.~227),\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has ``top of page~68''. The
convention in question is at the top of p.~227 of the 1970 volume, the page
given by the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V.} because we wish to give here a construction of quotients that
also applies to ``pre-equivalence relations''
that are not equivalence relations.

\section*{1. \(\mathscr C\)-groupoids}
\phantomsection\label{sec:sga3-V-1}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{1. \texorpdfstring{\(\mathscr C\)}{C}-groupoids}

\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-V-1-a}%
Let \(\mathscr C\) be a category in which products and
fiber products exist. Recall first that a diagram

\SGAThreeDiagram[0.39\textwidth]
  {figures/exp5/p252_exact_pair_cokernel.png}
  {A parallel pair followed by its proposed cokernel.}
  {fig:sga3-exp5-p252-exact-pair-cokernel}
  {Polo--Gille re-edition, Expos\'e V}

\noindent in \(\mathscr C\) is said to be \emph{exact} if
\(p d_0=p d_1\) and if, for every \(T\in\mathscr C\), the map \(T(p)\) is a
bijection from \(T(Y)\) onto the subset of \(T(X_0)\) consisting of the
arrows \(f:X_0\to T\) such that \(f d_0=f d_1\). One also says that
\((Y,p)\) is the \emph{cokernel} of \((d_0,d_1)\), and writes
\phantomsection\label{eq:sga3-V-1-cokernel}%
\begin{equation*}
  (Y,p)=\operatorname{Coker}(d_0,d_1).
\end{equation*}

\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-V-1-b}%
For example, let \(\mathscr C\) be the category of ringed spaces. In this
case a cokernel \((Y,p)\)
always exists, and it may be described as follows. The underlying
space of \(Y\) is obtained from \(X_0\) by identifying the points \(d_0(x)\)
and \(d_1(x)\), and by giving \(Y\) the quotient topology. The canonical map
\(\pi:X_0\to Y\), together with \(d_0,d_1\), then induces a pair of arrows
of sheaves of rings on \(Y\):

\SGAThreeDiagram[0.66\textwidth]
  {figures/exp5/p252_ringed_space_sheaf_pair.png}
  {The pair of induced arrows between direct-image sheaves.}
  {fig:sga3-exp5-p252-ringed-space-sheaf-pair}
  {Polo--Gille re-edition, Expos\'e V}

Here \(\mathcal O_i\) is the structure sheaf of \(X_i\). As the sheaf of
rings on \(Y\), one chooses the subsheaf of \(\pi_*(\mathcal O_0)\) whose
sections \(s\) satisfy \(\delta_0(s)=\delta_1(s)\). The arrow \(p\) is
defined in the evident way.

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V; our
formulation). The re-edition inserts here two lemmas on these cokernels,
which its versions of \S\S5--9 use. Let \(d_0,d_1\colon X_1\rightrightarrows
X_0\) be morphisms of ringed spaces, with cokernel \((Y,p)\) as just
described, and call an open subset \(U\) of \(X_0\) \emph{saturated} if
\(d_0^{-1}(U)=d_1^{-1}(U)\). If \(x\in U\) and \(x=d_i(z)\), then \(z\) lies
in \(d_i^{-1}(U)=d_{1-i}^{-1}(U)\), so \(d_{1-i}(z)\in U\); hence a chain of
identifications that starts in \(U\) stays in \(U\), one has
\(U=p^{-1}(p(U))\), and \(p(U)\) is open in \(Y\). (a)~For a saturated open
\(U\), put \(U_1=d_0^{-1}(U)=d_1^{-1}(U)\); then \(p(U)\), with the
restriction of \(p\), is the cokernel in the category of ringed spaces of
the pair \(U_1\rightrightarrows U\) induced by \(d_0,d_1\). Indeed, by what
precedes the two quotient sets and quotient topologies agree; and for an
open \(V\subset p(U)\) one has \(p^{-1}(V)\subset U\) and
\(d_0^{-1}(p^{-1}(V))\subset U_1\), so that both structure sheaves have, over
\(V\), the same sections, namely the \(s\in\Gamma(p^{-1}(V),\mathcal O_0)\)
with \(\delta_0(s)=\delta_1(s)\). (b)~If \(X_0,X_1\) are schemes and
\(d_0,d_1\) morphisms of schemes, and if \(Y\) is a scheme and \(p\) a
morphism of schemes, then \((Y,p)\) is also the cokernel of \((d_0,d_1)\) in
the category of schemes: this is the argument at the end of 4~c) below,
which uses only that \(p\) is surjective and that the homomorphisms
\(\mathcal O_{p(x)}\to\mathcal O_x\) are local. (c)~Consequently, if every
point of \(X_0\) has a saturated open neighbourhood \(U\) such that the
cokernel in ringed spaces of the induced pair is a scheme and the quotient
map a morphism of schemes, then by~(a) the open sets \(p(U)\), which cover
\(Y\), are schemes and \(p\) is a morphism of schemes above each of them;
so \(Y\) is a scheme, \(p\) is a morphism of schemes, and \((Y,p)\) is the
cokernel in the category of schemes by~(b). Assertion~(a) fails for
cokernels in the category of schemes; the re-edition gives the following
example. Let \(\mathbf G_m\) act on
\(\mathbf A^2=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf C[x_1,x_2]\) by homotheties,
\(d_1\) being the action and \(d_0\) the second projection of
\(\mathbf G_m\times\mathbf A^2\). On the saturated open set
\(\mathbf A^2-\{0\}\) the cokernel, in ringed spaces as in schemes, is the
projective line. The cokernel \(Y\) of the whole pair in ringed spaces is
the projective line together with the image \(y_0\) of the origin; every
saturated open set containing the origin is all of \(\mathbf A^2\), so
\(Y\) is the only open neighbourhood of \(y_0\), and
\(\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)\), the ring of invariant polynomials, is
\(\mathbf C\). In the category of \(\mathbf C\)-schemes, however, the
cokernel is \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf C\): if
\(f\colon\mathbf A^2\to T\) satisfies \(fd_0=fd_1\), the inverse image of an
affine open neighbourhood \(\operatorname{Spec}A\) of the image of the
origin is a saturated open set containing the origin, hence is
\(\mathbf A^2\), and \(A\to\mathbf C[x_1,x_2]\) takes its values in the
invariant polynomials, that is, in \(\mathbf C\).
