
For example, let \(S\) be an arbitrary scheme and let
\(X_2\xrightarrow{d'_j}X_1\xrightarrow{d_i}X_0\) be a
\((\mathbf{Sch}/S)\)-groupoid on which we make the following hypotheses:
\(X_0\) is of finite presentation and quasi-projective over \(S\); \(d_1\) is of
finite presentation, proper, and flat; the morphism
\(X_1\to X_0\times_SX_0\) with components \(d_0\) and \(d_1\) is quasi-finite.
\emph{Then every point \(x\) of \(X_0\) has an open neighborhood \(W\) which is
saturated and such that the groupoid induced by \(X_\ast\) on \(W\)
possesses a quasi-section.}

Indeed, the question is local on \(S\), so that we may suppose \(S\)
affine. There then exist a ring \(A\) of finite type over \(\mathbb Z\), a
morphism \(S\to T=\operatorname{Spec}A\), and a \((\mathbf{Sch}/T)\)-groupoid
\(Z_\ast\) such that \(X_\ast\) is identified with \(Z_\ast\times_TS\)
(EGA~IV, 8); moreover, we may suppose that \(Z_\ast\) satisfies the
conditions of Theorem~\hyperref[thm:sga3-V-7.1]{7.1} (EGA~IV, 8). Consequently \(Z_\ast\)
``locally'' possesses quasi-sections, and the remarks of \hyperref[par:sga3-V-9-a]{a)} apply.

\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-V-9.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 9.1.}---%
\textit{Consider morphisms of schemes}
\[
  X_0\xrightarrow{\,p\,}Y\xrightarrow{\,q\,}S
\]
\textit{such that \(qp\) is of finite type (respectively, of finite
presentation) and \(p\) is faithfully flat and of finite
presentation. Then \(q\) is of finite type (respectively, of finite
presentation).}%
\begingroup
\renewcommand{\thefootnote}{*}%
\footnote{\label{note:sga3-V-9.1-generalization}See
EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 17.7.5 for a more general result.}
\endgroup
\addtocounter{footnote}{-1}%

\noindent Since \(p\) is surjective and \(qp\) is quasi-compact,
\(q\) is quasi-compact. We may therefore suppose that \(S\), \(Y\),
and \(X_0\) are affine, with rings \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\),
respectively. We have
\[
  B=\varinjlim_i B_i,
\]
where the \(B_i\) range over the finitely generated
\(A\)-subalgebras of \(B\). Since \(C\) is of finite presentation
over \(B\), there exist an index \(i_0\), a \(B_{i_0}\)-algebra
\(C_{i_0}\) of finite presentation, and an isomorphism
\[
  C\simeq C_{i_0}\otimes_{B_{i_0}}B.
\]
For \(i\geq i_0\), put
\[
  C_i=C_{i_0}\otimes_{B_{i_0}}B_i;
\]
then
\[
  C\simeq C_i\otimes_{B_i}B.
\]

\SGAThreeDiagram[0.22\textwidth]
  {figures/exp5/exp5_localp033_algebra_limit_diagram.png}
  {The compatible \(A\)-algebras used in the limit argument.}
  {fig:sga3-V-9.1-algebra-limit}
  {LNM 151, pp.~285--286}

Since \(C\) is faithfully flat over \(B\), EGA~IV, 11, gives an index
\(i_1\geq i_0\)\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V, gives the
precise references EGA~IV\textsubscript{3}, 11.2.6 and 8.10.5~(vi), here and below.} such that
\(C_{i_1}\) is faithfully flat over \(B_{i_1}\). Consequently,
\(C_i\) is faithfully flat over \(B_i\) for every \(i\geq i_1\).
For \(i\geq i_1\), the canonical map \(C_i\to C\) is then injective,
because it is obtained from \(B_i\to B\) by faithfully flat base
extension.


If \(C\) is of finite type over \(A\), it follows that the map
\(C_i\to C\), and hence also the map \(B_i\to B\), is bijective for \(i\)
large enough.

Now suppose that \(C\) is of finite presentation over \(A\). By what
precedes, \(B\) is of finite type over \(A\), and hence has the form
\[
  B=\overline B/I,
\]
where \(\overline B\) is a polynomial algebra over \(A\) in finitely
many indeterminates and \(I\) is an ideal of \(\overline B\). The
ideal \(I\) is the union of its finitely generated subideals
\(I_\alpha\); consequently,
\[
  B=\varinjlim_\alpha B_\alpha,
  \qquad B_\alpha=\overline B/I_\alpha.
\]
Proceeding as above, there exist an index \(\alpha_0\), a
\(B_{\alpha_0}\)-algebra \(C_{\alpha_0}\) of finite presentation,
and an isomorphism
\[
  C\simeq C_{\alpha_0}\otimes_{B_{\alpha_0}}B.
\]
For \(\alpha\geq\alpha_0\), put
\[
  C_\alpha=C_{\alpha_0}\otimes_{B_{\alpha_0}}B_\alpha,
\]
so that
\[
  C\simeq C_\alpha\otimes_{B_\alpha}B
  \qquad(\alpha\geq\alpha_0).
\]
Again by EGA~IV, 11,
\(C_\alpha\) is faithfully flat over \(B_\alpha\) for all sufficiently
large \(\alpha\). In that case, the kernels of the maps
\(C_\alpha\to C\) and \(C_\alpha\to C_\beta\), respectively, identify
with
\[
  C_\alpha\otimes_{B_\alpha}(I/I_\alpha)
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  C_\alpha\otimes_{B_\alpha}(I_\beta/I_\alpha)
  \qquad(\beta\geq\alpha).
\]

Since \(C_\alpha\) and \(C\) are of finite presentation over \(A\)
and \(C_\alpha\to C\) is surjective,
\[
  C_\alpha\otimes_{B_\alpha}(I/I_\alpha)
\]
is a finitely generated ideal\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V, refers
here to EGA~IV\textsubscript{1}, 1.4.4.}, and it is the union of the
ideals
\[
  C_\alpha\otimes_{B_\alpha}(I_\beta/I_\alpha).
\]
Thus, for all sufficiently large \(\beta\),
\[
  C_\alpha\otimes_{B_\alpha}(I_\beta/I_\alpha)
  =
  C_\alpha\otimes_{B_\alpha}(I/I_\alpha).
\]
Since \(C_\alpha\) is faithfully flat over \(B_\alpha\), this also
gives \(I_\beta=I\). Therefore \(B\) is of finite presentation over
\(A\).

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V; our
formulation). The re-edition ends the exposé with a \S10, ``Complément :
quotients par un schéma en groupes'', written following indications of
M.~Raynaud, which applies the preceding theorems to the action of a group
scheme. Its 10.1 recalls \SGARefLink{sga3:xvi:theorem:2:1}{Exposé~XVI, 2.1}, \SGARefLink{sga3:xvi:theorem:2:2}{2.2} and \SGARefLink{sga3:xvi:corollary:2:3}{2.3}: the
representability theorem for the quotient of \(X\) by the equivalence
relation defined by a morphism \(X\to Y\) with \(X\times_YX\to X\) flat (after
A.~Grothendieck and J.~P.~Murre, \emph{Representation of unramified
functors. Applications}, Séminaire Bourbaki, exposé~294, 1965), and the
quotients \(G/H\) and \(G/\operatorname{Ker}(u)\) by flat subgroups. The rest runs
as follows, in our formulation.

Let \(S\) be a noetherian scheme, \(X\) an \(S\)-scheme of finite type, and
\(G\) a flat \(S\)-group scheme of finite type acting on \(X\) on the left; put
\(G_X=G\times_SX\), and consider the \((\mathbf{Sch}/S)\)-groupoid of 2~a), with
\(d_0(g,x)=gx\) and \(d_1(g,x)=x\), whose cokernel, when it exists, is written
\(G\backslash X\). Let \(F\subset G_X\) be the stabilizer of the diagonal, that is,
the inverse image of the diagonal of \(X\times_SX\) under
\(d=(d_0,d_1)\colon G_X\to X\times_SX\); it is a subgroup scheme of the
\(X\)-group scheme \(G_X\), of finite type over \(X\) since \(G_X\) is noetherian,
and closed if \(X\) is separated over \(S\); and \(G\) acts freely on \(X\), that
is, \(d\) is a monomorphism, if and only if \(F\) is the unit group. After
extension of the residue field, the fibre of \(d\) above a point of
\(X\times_SX\) with second projection \(x\) is empty or a torsor under \(F_x\);
so \(d\) is quasi-finite if and only if \(F\) is quasi-finite over \(X\), that
is, if and only if the stabilizers of the geometric points of \(X\) are
finite.

(1)~\emph{If \(F\) is quasi-finite over \(X\)}, Theorem~8.1 applies (\(d_1\) is flat
and of finite type): there is a dense saturated (that is, \(G\)-stable)
open subset \(U\subset X\) such that \(V=G\backslash U\) exists in \((\mathbf{Sch}/S)\) and
is also the quotient in ringed spaces, \(p\colon U\to V\) is surjective, open
and of finite presentation, \(V\) is of finite presentation over \(S\), and
\(G\times_SU\to U\times_VU\), \((g,x)\mapsto(gx,x)\), is surjective. If moreover
\(G\) acts freely, this morphism is an isomorphism and \(p\) is faithfully
flat (8.1~(iv)): \(U\) is a \(G\)-torsor over \(V\), locally trivial for the fppf
topology. (For a reductive \(G\) acting freely and linearizably on \(X\), the
re-edition notes that \(G\backslash X\) is representable and \(X\to G\backslash X\) is a
torsor, by results of Raynaud and Seshadri; see J.-L.~Colliot-Thélène and
J.-J.~Sansuc, \emph{Fibrés quadratiques et composantes connexes réelles},
Math.\ Ann.\ \textbf{244} (1979), 105--134, Proposition~6.11.)

(2)~For an \(S\)-scheme \(T\), the map \((g,x)\mapsto(gx,x)\) identifies
\(G_X(T)/F(T)\), where \(F(T)\) acts by right multiplication on the first
coordinate, with the set \(\Gamma(T)\) of the pairs \((y,x)\) with
\(y\in G(T)x\): indeed \((g,x)\) and \((g',x)\) have the same image if and only
if \((g^{-1}g',x)\in F(T)\). Passing to the associated fppf sheaves, the
quotient sheaf \(G_X/F\) is identified with the image sheaf
\(\widetilde\Gamma\subset X\times_SX\) of \(d\). \emph{The sheaf \(\widetilde\Gamma\) is
representable if and only if \(F\) is flat over \(X\).} Indeed, the map
\(((g,x),(g',x))\mapsto((g,x),(g^{-1}g',x))\) identifies
\(G_X\times_{X\times_SX}G_X\) with \(G_X\times_XF\), the first projection becoming
the base change of \(F\to X\) along the faithfully flat morphism \(d_1\); so
the first projection \(G_X\times_{X\times_SX}G_X\to G_X\) is flat if and only if
\(F\) is flat over \(X\). If it is flat, \SGARefLink{sga3:xvi:theorem:2:1}{Exposé~XVI, 2.1}, applied to \(d\),
factors \(d\) as a faithfully flat morphism of finite presentation \(G_X\to Y\)
followed by a monomorphism \(Y\to X\times_SX\), and \(Y\) represents
\(G_X/F\simeq\widetilde\Gamma\). Conversely, suppose that \(\widetilde\Gamma\) is
represented by a scheme \(Y\). The functor \(\widetilde\Gamma\) commutes with
filtered inductive limits of rings (fppf coverings, sections of \(G\) and
equalities of points of \(X\) descend to a stage of the limit), so \(Y\) is
locally of finite presentation over \(S\), hence over \(X\) (by the second
projection); and above each point \(x\) of \(X\), the morphism \(G_X\to Y\) is
the quotient morphism of \(G_{\kappa(x)}\) by \(F_x\), which is faithfully flat
(Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, 3.2). By the fibrewise criterion of flatness
(EGA~IV\textsubscript{3}, 11.3.10), \(G_X\to Y\) is flat, hence faithfully flat and
of finite presentation; so the first projection above is flat by
XVI~2.1, and \(F\) is flat over \(X\). (The re-edition derives this converse
from Exposé~IV, 6.3.3, which presupposes the flatness of the equivalence
relation \(G_X\times_XF\) on \(G_X\); the fibrewise criterion supplies it.) This
result is part~(2) of Theorem~3 of M.~Raynaud, \emph{Sur le passage au
quotient par un groupoïde plat}, C.~R.\ Acad.\ Sci.\ Paris Sér.~A
\textbf{265} (1967), 384--387, a note which also sketches another proof of
XVI~2.1.

(3)~\emph{If \(F\) is flat over \(X\)}, the two morphisms \(G_X/F\to X\) induced by
\(d_0\) and \(d_1\) are faithfully flat and of finite presentation, since
\(d_0\) and \(d_1\) are and \(G_X\to G_X/F\) is faithfully flat and of finite
presentation (EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 2.2.13~(iii), and EGA~IV\textsubscript{3}, 11.3.16);
and \(G_X/F\to X\times_SX\) is a monomorphism. Theorem~8.1, applied to this
equivalence pair, gives a dense saturated open subset \(U\subset X\) such that
the fppf quotient \(G\backslash U\) is represented by an \(S\)-scheme of finite type
and \(U\to G\backslash U\) is faithfully flat and of finite presentation. If \(X\) is
reduced, the largest open subset of \(X\) over which \(F\) is flat is dense, by
generic flatness (EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 6.9), and saturated, since the pull-backs
of \(F\) along \(d_0\) and along \(d_1\) are isomorphic over \(G_X\) (by
conjugation, \((h,x)\mapsto(ghg^{-1},gx)\) above \((g,x)\)) and flatness descends
along the faithfully flat \(d_0\) and \(d_1\); so the conclusion holds for every
reduced \(X\).

In (1) and (3), as in the Addition at the end of \S8, there is a largest
open set with the stated properties; in case (3), the re-edition adds that
a point \(x\) of codimension~1 of \(X\) lies in it if and only if
\((G_X/F)\times_X\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_{X,x}\to
\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_{X,x}\times_S\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_{X,x}\) is a
closed immersion (see the references at the end of \S8).
