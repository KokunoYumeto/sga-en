
Let \(Y'\) be an open subset of \(Y\), and put
\[
  U'=q^{-1}(Y'),\qquad X'_0=p^{-1}(Y'),
\]
and so forth. It is clear that \(U'\) is an open subset of \(U\) saturated for
the equivalence relation defined by the groupoid \(U_\ast\). It therefore
follows from Theorem~\hyperref[thm:sga3-V-4.1]{4.1} that \(Y'\) is the cokernel of the groupoid
induced by \(U_\ast\) on \(U'\). Likewise, \(X'_0\) is an open subset of \(X_0\)
saturated for the equivalence relation defined by \(X_\ast\), and
\(U'=X'_0\cap U\). Consequently, \(U'\) is a quasi-section of the groupoid
induced by \(X_\ast\) on \(X'_0\). What precedes then shows that \(Y'\) is also
the cokernel of this last groupoid.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V, details
these steps. That \(Y'\) is the cokernel of the groupoid induced by \(U_\ast\)
on \(U'\), in schemes and in ringed spaces, also follows from the Addition at
the end of \S1, (a) and~(b). The open set \(U'\) satisfies condition~(1) for
the groupoid induced on \(X'_0\), because \(X'_0\) is saturated, so that
\(d_0^{-1}(U')\to X'_0\) is deduced from \(v\) by the base change
\(X'_0\to X_0\); and condition~(2), because a set of pairwise equivalent points
of \(U'\) is finite and lies in an affine open subset \(W\) of \(U\), hence in the
quasi-affine scheme \(W\cap U'\), in which a finite set has an affine open
neighbourhood (EGA~II, 4.5.4 and 5.1.2).}

In particular, for every \(S\)-scheme \(T\), the sequence
\begin{equation}
  T(Y')
  \xrightarrow{\,T(p|_{X'_0})\,}
  T(X'_0)
  \underset{T(d_0|_{X'_1})}
    {\overset{T(d_1|_{X'_1})}{\rightrightarrows}}
  T(X'_1)
  \label{eq:sga3-V-6.1-T-exact}
\end{equation}
is exact. If \(T\) is the affine line \(\mathbf G_{a,S}\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:4-3}{Expos\'e I, 4.3}), this sequence identifies with
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \Gamma(Y',\mathcal O_Y)
  &\longrightarrow
  \Gamma(p^{-1}(Y'),\mathcal O_{X_0})
  \underset{\delta_0}{\overset{\delta_1}{\rightrightarrows}}
  \Gamma(d_0^{-1}p^{-1}(Y'),\mathcal O_{X_1}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sga3-V-6.1-sections-exact}
\end{equation}
which is therefore exact for every open subset \(Y'\).

\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-V-6-d}%
\noindent\textbf{d) Proof of \hyperref[lem:sga3-V-6.1-iv]{(iv)}.}---%
If \((d_0,d_1)\) is an equivalence pair, then so is
\((u_0,u_1)\). It follows from
Theorem~\hyperref[thm:sga3-V-4.1]{4.1} that
\[
  u_0\boxtimes u_1:U_1\longrightarrow U\times_Y U
\]
is an isomorphism, and hence so is \(v_0\boxtimes v_1\); see the
Cartesian squares in part~\hyperref[par:sga3-V-6-b]{(b)}. Since
\(v\times v\) is faithfully flat and quasi-compact,
\(d_0\boxtimes d_1\) is an isomorphism (SGA~1, VIII, 5.4).

Moreover, \(q\) is faithfully flat, hence so is \(r\), since \(u\) is
faithfully flat. Since \(r\) and \(v\) are faithfully flat, so is \(p\).

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V; our
formulation). The re-edition states Lemma~6.1 in a stronger form.
(1)~The hypothesis that \(d_0\) is faithfully flat and quasi-compact is used
only in d), and only through the flatness of \(d_0\): assertions (i)--(iii)
hold for every \((\mathbf{Sch}/S)\)-groupoid with a quasi-section, and the
conclusion of~(iv) holds as soon as \(d_0\) is flat. Indeed, \(u\) is then
flat, so \(r=qu\) is flat, \(q\) being flat by \hyperref[thm:sga3-V-4.1]{4.1}; since \(r=pv\) with \(v\)
faithfully flat, \(p\) is flat (EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 2.2.13~(iii)), and it is
surjective by~(ii). (2)~If \(d_0\) is universally closed, so is \(p\): \(u\) is
universally closed, being deduced from \(d_0\) by base change, and \(q\) is
integral, so \(r=qu\) is universally closed; after any base change, \(r'=p'v'\)
with \(r'\) closed and \(v'\) surjective, so \(p'(F)=r'(v'^{-1}(F))\) is closed for
every closed set \(F\). (3)~If \(S\) is locally noetherian and \(X_0\) is only
locally of finite type over \(S\), the argument of a) shows that \(Y\) is
locally of finite type over \(S\), hence locally noetherian; then \(p\) is
locally of finite type (EGA~I, 6.6.6), so that \(p\) and \(Y\to S\) are locally
of finite presentation. If \(X_0\) is of finite type over \(S\), \(p\) and
\(Y\to S\) are moreover of finite presentation (EGA~IV\textsubscript{1}, 1.6.2~(v)).
