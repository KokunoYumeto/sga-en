
denotes the inverse image of \(U_\ast\) under the base change \(u:V\to U\),
that is, also the inverse image of \(X_\ast\) under the base change
\(V\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{inclusion}\ }X_1\xrightarrow{\ d_0\ }X_0\)).

Likewise, since \(v\) is an effective and universal epimorphism,
\(\operatorname{Coker}(d_0,d_1)\) coincides with the cokernel of the inverse
image of \(X_\ast\) under the base change
\(v:V\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{inclusion}\ }X_1\xrightarrow{\ d_1\ }X_0\). But this
inverse image is isomorphic to \(V_\ast\) by \hyperref[par:sga3-V-3-c]{\S3.c)}.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V, gives
the reasons: \(u\) has a section, deduced from that of \(d_0\) by base
change, and a morphism with a section is a universal effective epimorphism
(\SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:1:12}{Exposé~IV, 1.12}); \(v\) is finite, locally free and surjective by
condition~(1), hence faithfully flat and quasi-compact, hence a universal
effective epimorphism (Exposé~IV, 6.3.1 (iii) and (v)).} We have therefore
proved the first assertion of \hyperref[lem:sga3-V-6.1]{the following lemma}:

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-V-6.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 6.1.}---\emph{Suppose that the
\((\mathbf{Sch}/S)\)-groupoid \(X_\ast\) possesses a quasi-section and that
\(d_0:X_1\to X_0\) is faithfully flat and quasi-compact. Then:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{lem:sga3-V-6.1:item:i}(i)]\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-V-6.1-i}%
\emph{There exists a cokernel \((Y,p)\) of
  \((d_0,d_1)\) in \((\mathbf{Sch}/S)\). Moreover, every such
  \((Y,p)\) is a cokernel of \((d_0,d_1)\) in the category of all
  ringed spaces.}
\item[(ii)]\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-V-6.1-ii}%
\emph{The morphism \(p\) is surjective; it is open if \(d_0\) is; if \(S\)
  is locally Noetherian and \(X_0\) is of finite type over \(S\), then \(Y\) is
  of finite type over \(S\).}
\item[(iii)]\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-V-6.1-iii}%
\emph{The morphism
  \(X_1\to X_0\times_Y X_0\) with components \(d_0\) and \(d_1\) is
  surjective.}
\item[(iv)]\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-V-6.1-iv}%
\emph{If \((d_0,d_1)\) is an equivalence pair, then
  \(X_1\to X_0\times_Y X_0\) is an isomorphism and \(p\) is faithfully
  flat.}
\end{enumerate}

Before proving the second assertion of \hyperref[lem:sga3-V-6.1-i]{part~(i)}, we shall prove
\hyperref[lem:sga3-V-6.1-ii]{parts~(ii)} and \hyperref[lem:sga3-V-6.1-iii]{(iii)}.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-V-6-a}%
\noindent\textbf{a) Proof of \hyperref[lem:sga3-V-6.1-ii]{(ii)}.}---%
We have just seen that \((Y,p)\) identifies both with
\(\operatorname{Coker}(v_0,v_1)\) and with
\(\operatorname{Coker}(u_0,u_1)\). Let \(q\) and \(r\) be the
canonical epimorphisms from \(U\) and \(V\), respectively, to \(Y\):
\SGAThreeDiagram[0.39\textwidth]
  {figures/exp5/p271_lemma61_cokernel_triangle.png}
  {The canonical epimorphisms to \(Y\).}
  {fig:sga3-V-6.1-cokernel-triangle}
  {Polo--Gille re-edition, Expos\'e V}
Since \(u\) and \(v\) are surjective, \(p\) is surjective if and only if
\(r\) is, hence if and only if \(q\) is. But \(q\) is surjective by
Theorem~\hyperref[thm:sga3-V-4.1]{4.1}; hence \(p\) is surjective.

Likewise, if \(U'\) is any open subset of \(U\), the saturation of \(U'\) for
the equivalence relation defined by the groupoid \(U_\ast\) is
\(u_1(u_0^{-1}(U'))\); it is therefore open in \(U\), which shows that the
morphism \(q\) is open. If \(d_0\) is open, then \(u\) is also open, and so is
\(r\); since \(v\) is surjective, it follows that \(p\) is open.

Now suppose that \(S\) is locally Noetherian and that \(X_0\) is of finite
type over \(S\). We show that \(Y\) is of finite type over \(S\). Indeed, since
\(U\) is quasi-compact over \(S\) and \(q\) is surjective, \(Y\) is
quasi-compact over \(S\). Moreover, let
\[
  S'=\operatorname{Spec}R
\]
be an affine open subset of \(S\), let
\[
  Y'=\operatorname{Spec}B
\]
be an affine open subset of \(Y\) mapping into \(S'\), and let
\[
  U'=\operatorname{Spec}A
\]
be the inverse image of \(Y'\) in \(U\). We must show that \(B\) is a finitely
generated \(R\)-algebra. By \hyperref[sec:sga3-V-4]{\S\S4} and \hyperref[sec:sga3-V-5]{5}, \(B\) is contained in \(A\), which is
a finitely generated \(R\)-algebra; the assertion therefore follows
because \(R\) is Noetherian and \(A\) is integral over \(B\).
