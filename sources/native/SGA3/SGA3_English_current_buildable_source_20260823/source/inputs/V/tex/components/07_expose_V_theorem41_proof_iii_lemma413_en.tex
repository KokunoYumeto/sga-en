
\hyperref[proof:sga3-V-4.1-b]{Part~b)} can then be restated by saying that the
map
\[
  \underline{d_0}\boxtimes\underline{d_1}:
  \underline{X_1}\longrightarrow
  \underline{X_0}\times_{\underline Y}\underline{X_0}
\]
with components \(\underline{d_0}\) and \(\underline{d_1}\) is
surjective. This map factors as
\[
  \underline{X_1}
  \xrightarrow{\ \underline{d_0}\boxtimes\underline{d_1}\ }
  \underline{X_0}\times_{\underline Y}\underline{X_0}
  \xrightarrow{\ q\ }
  \underline{X_0\times_YX_0},
\]
where \(q\) is the canonical map. The image of
\(\underline{d_0}\boxtimes\underline{d_1}\) therefore contains every
point \(v\in\underline{X_0\times_YX_0}\) such that
\[
  \{v\}=q^{-1}(q(v)).
\]
This condition is satisfied in particular when \(v\) is
\emph{rational over \(Y\)}, that is, when the residue field
\(\kappa(v)\) is identified with the residue field \(\kappa(w)\) of
the image \(w\) of \(v\) in \(Y\).%

If \(v\in\underline{X_0\times_YX_0}\) is not rational over \(Y\),
again let \(w\)
be its image in \(Y\); there then exist a local ring
\(C\) with radical \(\mathfrak m\) and a local, flat homomorphism
\[
  f:\mathcal O_{Y,w}\longrightarrow C
\]
such that \(C/\mathfrak m\) is isomorphic to \(\kappa(v)\) as a
\(\kappa(w)\)-algebra. Put \(Y'=\operatorname{Spec}C\), and let
\(\pi:Y'\to Y\) be the morphism induced by \(f\). The canonical
projection
\[
  (X_0\times_YX_0)\times_YY'\longrightarrow X_0\times_YX_0
\]
sends some point \(v'\), rational over \(Y'\), to \(v\). Since
\[
  (X_0\times_YX_0)\times_YY'
  \simeq
  (X_0\times_YY')\times_{Y'}(X_0\times_YY'),
\]
and since the arguments made above remain valid after the base change
\(\pi:Y'\to Y\), the point \(v'\) is the image of an element
\[
  u'\in\underline{X_1\times_YY'}
\]
under the base change of \(d_0\boxtimes d_1\). If \(u\) is the image
of \(u'\) in \(X_1\), then
\[
  v=(d_0\boxtimes d_1)(u),
\]
as required.

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V; our
formulation). The re-edition states as a lemma (its 4.1.2) the fact used
in e), which appeared in the 1965 edition of SGA~3 as Lemma
VI\textsubscript{B}, 4.5.1 and is proved in EGA~\(0_{\mathrm{III}}\), 10.3.1 (where \(B\) is
moreover noetherian if \(A\) is): \emph{if \((A,\mathfrak m)\) is a local ring
with residue field \(k\) and \(K\) is an extension of \(k\), there is a local
ring \(B\), flat over \(A\), with a local homomorphism \(A\to B\) such that
\(B/\mathfrak mB\) is \(k\)-isomorphic to \(K\); if \([K:k]\) is finite, \(B\) can be
chosen finite and free over \(A\).} Here is a proof. If \(K=k(t)\) with \(t\)
transcendental over \(k\), take for \(B\) the localization of \(A[T]\) at the
prime ideal \(\mathfrak mA[T]\), the kernel of \(A[T]\to k[T]\): it is flat over
\(A\), with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak mB\) and residue field \(k(T)\). If
\(K=k(t)\) with \(t\) algebraic over \(k\), of minimal polynomial \(f\), take
\(B=A[T]/(F)\), where \(F\) is a monic polynomial lifting \(f\): it is free of
rank \(\deg f\) over \(A\), hence integral over \(A\), so that its maximal
ideals contain \(\mathfrak mB\), and \(B/\mathfrak mB=k[T]/(f)\simeq K\) is a field;
thus \(B\) is local with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak mB\). In general, let
\((t_i)_{i\in I}\) be a family of generators of \(K\) over \(k\), indexed by a
well-ordered set, and construct by transfinite induction local rings
\(A_i\), flat over \(A\), with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak mA_i\) and residue field
\(k(t_j)_{j\leq i}\), and local flat homomorphisms \(A_j\to A_i\) for \(j<i\):
for each \(i\), apply the monogenic case to the inductive limit of the
\(A_j\), \(j<i\) (to \(A\) if \(i\) is the least element), which is a local ring,
flat over \(A\) and over each \(A_j\), with maximal ideal generated by
\(\mathfrak m\) and residue field the union of the residue fields of the
\(A_j\). The inductive limit of all the \(A_i\) is the required \(B\). When
\([K:k]\) is finite, finitely many algebraic steps suffice, and \(B\) is finite
and free over \(A\).

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V; our
formulation). In f), the 1970 text uses the following fact, which the
re-edition states and proves as a lemma (its 4.1.3, after M.~Demazure and
P.~Gabriel, \emph{Groupes algébriques}, Masson and North-Holland, 1970,
I, \S5, 1.5; see also the proof of EGA~IV\textsubscript{3}, 8.11.5): \emph{a finite
monomorphism of schemes \(f\colon T\to Z\) is a closed immersion.} Indeed, the
question is local on \(Z\) and \(f\) is affine, so one may suppose
\(Z=\operatorname{Spec}R\) and \(T=\operatorname{Spec}A\), with \(A\) a finite
\(R\)-algebra. For a prime ideal \(\mathfrak q\) of \(R\), the base change of \(f\)
to \(\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(\mathfrak q)\) is again a monomorphism, so the
multiplication \(D\otimes_{\kappa(\mathfrak q)}D\to D\) of
\(D=A\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak q)\) is bijective (EGA~I, 5.3.8); as \(D\) has
finite dimension over \(\kappa(\mathfrak q)\), this gives
\((\dim D)^2=\dim D\), so that \(\kappa(\mathfrak q)\to D\) is surjective. Hence
\(A_{\mathfrak q}=R_{\mathfrak q}\cdot1+\mathfrak qA_{\mathfrak q}\), and
\(R_{\mathfrak q}\to A_{\mathfrak q}\) is surjective by Nakayama's lemma,
\(A_{\mathfrak q}\) being a finite \(R_{\mathfrak q}\)-module. The cokernel of
\(R\to A\) is thus zero at every prime ideal, hence zero, and \(f\) is a
closed immersion. In f), the morphism \(X_1\to X_0\times_YX_0\) is finite,
since \(A_1\) is finite over \(A_0\) through \(\delta_1\), hence over
\(A_0\otimes_BA_0\); being a monomorphism, it is a closed immersion, which
gives the surjectivity of \(A_0\otimes_BA_0\to A_1\) used there.
