
\medskip
\phantomsection\label{proof:sga3-V-4.1-b}%
\noindent\textbf{b)} Consider two prime ideals \(x\) and \(y\) of
\(A_0\). We shall show that the equality
\[
  x\cap B=y\cap B
\]
implies the existence of a prime ideal \(z\) of \(A_1\) such that
\[
  x=d_0(z),\qquad y=d_1(z).
\]

Indeed, if this assertion were false, \(x\) would be distinct from
\(\delta_0^{-1}(t)\) for every prime ideal \(t\) of \(A_1\) such that
\(\delta_1^{-1}(t)=y\). For every such \(t\), one would have
\[
  \delta_0^{-1}(t)\cap B
  =\delta_1^{-1}(t)\cap B
  =y\cap B=x\cap B.
\]
It would follow from Cohen--Seidenberg that \(x\) is contained in none of
the ideals \(\delta_0^{-1}(t)\); there would therefore be an element
\(a\in x\) belonging to none of the ideals \(\delta_0^{-1}(t)\) (for there
are at most \(n\) prime ideals \(t\) of \(A_1\) such that
\(\delta_1^{-1}(t)=y\)). Consequently, \(\delta_0(a)\) would belong to none
of these ideals \(t\), so that the norm \(N_{\delta_1}(\delta_0(a))\) would
not belong to \(y\) (this norm is computed by regarding \(A_1\) as an
\(A_0\)-algebra through \(\delta_1\); with the notation of
\hyperref[proof:sga3-V-4.1-a]{part~a)}, \(N_{\delta_1}(\delta_0(a))=\sigma_n\)). But this norm would
belong to \(B\cap x=B\cap y\), a contradiction.

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V; our
formulation). The re-edition details this argument as follows. By a),
\(A_0\) is integral over \(B\); since \(\delta_0^{-1}(t)\cap B=x\cap B\), an
inclusion \(x\subset\delta_0^{-1}(t)\) would be an equality (incomparability;
Bourbaki, \emph{Algèbre commutative}, V, \S2, Corollary~1 to
Proposition~1), and \(x\neq\delta_0^{-1}(t)\) by assumption. The prime ideals
\(t\) with \(\delta_1^{-1}(t)=y\) correspond to the prime ideals of
\(A_1\otimes_{A_0}\kappa(y)\), an algebra of dimension \(n\) over the field
\(\kappa(y)\), so there are at most \(n\) of them; and an ideal contained in
none of finitely many prime ideals is not contained in their union (prime
avoidance; \emph{loc.\ cit.}, II, \S1, no.~1, Proposition~2). The norm
\(\sigma_n\) lies in \(B\) by a), and in \(x\) because the relation proved in
a) gives \((-1)^{n-1}\sigma_n=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^i\sigma_ia^{n-i}\), with
\(\sigma_0=1\), a multiple of \(a\). Finally, the passage from
``\(\delta_0(a)\) lies in no prime ideal \(t\) above \(y\)'' to
``\(N_{\delta_1}(\delta_0(a))\notin y\)'' is the following criterion, which the
re-edition takes from M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel, \emph{Groupes
algébriques}, Masson and North-Holland, 1970, III, \S2, Lemma~4.3:
\emph{if \(A\to A'\) makes \(A'\) a projective \(A\)-module of rank \(n\), if
\(\mathfrak p\) is a prime ideal of \(A\) and \(a'\in A'\), then the norm
\(N(a')\) lies outside \(\mathfrak p\) if and only if \(a'\) lies in no prime
ideal of \(A'\) above \(\mathfrak p\).} Indeed, the norm commutes with
localization at \(\mathfrak p\); over the local ring \(A_{\mathfrak p}\), the
algebra \(A'_{\mathfrak p}\) is a free module of rank \(n\), \(N(a')\) is the
determinant of multiplication by \(a'\), and this determinant is a unit if
and only if multiplication by \(a'\) is bijective, that is, if and only if
\(a'\) is a unit of \(A'_{\mathfrak p}\); and since \(A'_{\mathfrak p}\) is finite
over \(A_{\mathfrak p}\), its maximal ideals are its prime ideals above
\(\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}\), which correspond to the prime ideals of \(A'\)
above \(\mathfrak p\).
