
The morphism \(\widetilde d_1\) is therefore quasi-finite and flat at
the points of \(F_1\) lying over \(z\). Let \(\Phi\) be the closed subset of
\(F_1\) consisting of the points at which \(\widetilde d_1\) is not both flat
and quasi-finite (SGA~1, IV, 6.10 and EGA~III, 4.4.10). Since \(d_1\) is
proper, \(\widetilde d_1(\Phi)\) is closed and does not contain \(z\);
consequently, if we put
\[
  W=\widetilde d_1(F_1)-\widetilde d_1(\Phi),
\]
\(\widetilde d_1\) is flat and quasi-finite, that is, finite and locally
free (EGA~III, 4.4.2), above \(W\). Since the restriction of \(\widetilde d_1\)
to \(\widetilde d_1^{-1}(W)\) is open, \(\widetilde d_1(F_1)\) is a
neighborhood of \(z\),\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has ``open neighbourhood''. The
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V, writes ``neighbourhood'' and details
the argument: above \(X_0-\widetilde d_1(\Phi)\), the morphism \(\widetilde d_1\) is
proper, flat and quasi-finite, and of finite presentation, the schemes
being locally noetherian; hence it is finite and locally free (EGA~III,
4.4.2) and open (EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 2.4.6) there, so that \(W\), the image of
\(\widetilde d_1^{-1}(X_0-\widetilde d_1(\Phi))\), is an open neighbourhood of \(z\),
contained in the closed set \(\widetilde d_1(F_1)\).} and \(W\) is the largest open subset of \(X_0\)
contained in \(\widetilde d_1(F_1)\) over which \(\widetilde d_1\) is both flat
and quasi-finite.

We shall see in Lemma~\hyperref[lem:sga3-V-7.4]{7.4} that the inverse
images of \(\Phi\) under \(\widetilde d'_1\) and
\(\widetilde d'_0\) both identify with the set of points of \(F_2\)
at which \(\widetilde d'_2\) is not both flat and quasi-finite.
It follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
  d_0^{-1}(W)
  &=
  \widetilde d'_2(F_2)
  -
  \widetilde d'_2\bigl((\widetilde d'_0)^{-1}(\Phi)\bigr),\\
  d_1^{-1}(W)
  &=
  \widetilde d'_2(F_2)
  -
  \widetilde d'_2\bigl((\widetilde d'_1)^{-1}(\Phi)\bigr)
\end{aligned}
\]
coincide. Thus \(W\) is saturated.

Set
\[
  W_1=\widetilde d_1^{-1}(W).
\]
The equality \(d_0^{-1}(W)=d_1^{-1}(W)\) implies
\[
  (\widetilde d'_2)^{-1}\bigl(d_0^{-1}(W)\bigr)
  =
  (\widetilde d'_2)^{-1}\bigl(d_1^{-1}(W)\bigr),
\]
that is,
\[
  (\widetilde d'_0)^{-1}(W_1)
  =
  (\widetilde d'_1)^{-1}(W_1).
\]
Since \(\widetilde d_0\) is faithfully flat and quasi-compact and the
square

  {The Cartesian square used to descend \(W_1\).}
  {fig:sga3-V-7.3-descent-square}
  {LNM 151, p.~280}
is Cartesian,\footnote{Translator's note: \(\widetilde d_0\) is deduced by base change from
\(d_0=d_1\sigma\), which, like \(d_1\), is surjective, proper and flat; the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V, says so here.} it follows that
\[
  W_1=\widetilde d_0^{-1}(U)
\]
for an open subset \(U\) of \(F\) (SGA~1, VIII, 4.4). This open
subset \(U\) is a quasi-section of the groupoid with base \(W\)
induced by \(X_\ast\). We may therefore take \(U_z=W\).
