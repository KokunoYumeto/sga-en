
\phantomsection\label{proof:sga3-V-5-b-general}%
By \hyperref[par:sga3-V-5-a]{part~a)}, we may suppose that \(d_1\) is locally free of rank \(n\).
Let \((Y,p)\) be a cokernel of \((d_0,d_1)\) in the category of all
ringed spaces. The argument at the end of \hyperref[proof:sga3-V-4.1-c]{4(c)} shows that, in order to
prove \hyperref[thm:sga3-V-4.1-i]{Theorem~4.1(i)}, it is enough to show that \(Y\) is a scheme and
that \(p:X_0\to Y\) is a morphism of schemes. The question is therefore
local on \(Y\).
Let \(y\in Y\) and choose \(x\in X_0\) with \(p(x)=y\). If \(x\) has a
saturated affine open neighborhood \(U\), then \hyperref[sec:sga3-V-4]{Section~4} shows that
\(p(U)\) is an affine open subset of \(Y\), and
\(p|_U:U\to p(U)\) is a morphism of schemes. Thus it remains to prove
that every \(x\in X_0\) has a saturated affine open neighborhood. We
proceed as follows; the argument is taken from SGA~1, VIII,
Corollary~7.6.

\SGAThreeDiagram[0.90\textwidth]
  {figures/exp5/p268_section5_affine_saturated_chain.png}
  {The nested open subsets used to construct a saturated affine
  neighborhood of \(x\).}
  {fig:sga3-exp5-p268-affine-saturated-chain}
  {LNM 151, p.~270}

We take for \(V\) an affine open subset of \(X_0\) containing
\(d_1(d_0^{-1}(x))\).\footnote{Translator's note: such a \(V\) exists by the hypothesis of
\hyperref[thm:sga3-V-4.1]{Theorem~4.1} on \(d_0d_1^{-1}(x)\), since \(d_1d_0^{-1}(x)=d_0d_1^{-1}(x)\) (see the
note to the statement of 4.1); the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V,
says so here.}
Put \(F=X_0\setminus V\). Since \(d_1\) is integral,
\(d_1(d_0^{-1}(F))\) is closed, and
\[
  V'=X_0\setminus d_1(d_0^{-1}(F))
\]
is the largest saturated open subset contained in \(V\). The set
\(V'\) is a neighborhood of the finite set
\(d_1(d_0^{-1}(x))\). Hence there is a section \(f\) of the structure
sheaf of \(V\), vanishing on \(V\setminus V'\), such that
\(d_1(d_0^{-1}(x))\) is contained in the principal open subset
\(V_f\subseteq V\). We shall prove that the largest saturated open
subset \((V_f)'\) contained in \(V_f\) is affine; it will then be the
required neighborhood.

Put \(Z(f)=V'\setminus V_f\). Then \(d_0^{-1}(Z(f))\) is the set of
points of
\[
  d_0^{-1}(V')=d_1^{-1}(V')
\]
at which \(d_0^\ast(f)\) vanishes. On the other hand, since \(d_1\) induces
a morphism, locally free of rank \(n\), from this common inverse image
onto \(V'\), the set \(d_1(d_0^{-1}(Z(f)))\) is the set of points at which
the norm \(N\) of \(d_0^\ast(f)\) relative to \(d_1\) vanishes.\footnote{Translator's note: this is the norm criterion proved in the Addition
after 4~b), to which the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V, refers
here.} It follows that
\[
  (V_f)'=V'\setminus d_1(d_0^{-1}(Z(f)))
\]
is the set of points of \(V_f\) at which \(N\) does not vanish.
Consequently, \((V_f)'\) is affine.

This proves \hyperref[thm:sga3-V-4.1-i]{Theorem~4.1(i)}; the other assertions are clear.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V, adds to
4.1~(iv), after M.~Raynaud, \emph{Passage au quotient par une relation
d'équivalence plate}, in \emph{Proceedings of a Conference on Local Fields}
(Driebergen, 1966), Springer, 1967, 78--85, Theorem~1~(iii): when
\((d_0,d_1)\) is an equivalence pair, \((Y,p)\) is also the cokernel of
\((d_0,d_1)\) in the category of sheaves for the fppf topology, and for every
base change \(Y'\to Y\), \(Y'\) is the cokernel of the groupoid deduced from
\(X_\ast\) by the base change \(X_0\times_YY'\to X_0\); in particular
\(Y\times_SS'\) is the cokernel of \(X_\ast\times_SS'\) for every \(S'\to S\).
Indeed, \(p\) is then finite, locally free and surjective, hence faithfully
flat and of finite presentation, and \(X_1\simeq X_0\times_YX_0\); so the
equivalence relation \(X_1\) is effective for this family of morphisms, and
the assertions follow from \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:corollary:6:3:3}{Exposé~IV, 6.3.3} and \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:3:4:3:1}{3.4.3.1}.}

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~V; our
formulation). The re-edition adds a Remark~5.1: \emph{under the hypotheses of
\hyperref[thm:sga3-V-4.1]{Theorem~4.1}, if \(S\) is locally noetherian and \(X_0\) is quasi-projective
over \(S\), then \(Y\) is quasi-projective over \(S\).} Here is an argument.
Let \(\mathscr L\) be an invertible \(\mathcal O_{X_0}\)-module ample relative
to \(S\). By the argument of 6.1~a) below, \(Y\) is of finite type over \(S\).
For \(y=p(x)\in Y\), the class \(p^{-1}(y)=d_1(d_0^{-1}(x))\) is finite and lies
in an affine open set, and an invertible module over a semi-local ring is
free; so \(\mathscr L\) is trivial on an open neighbourhood \(U\) of \(p^{-1}(y)\), and hence
on the saturated open neighbourhood \(X_0-d_1d_0^{-1}(X_0-U)\) of \(p^{-1}(y)\)
(\(d_1\) is finite, hence closed). For a section \(a\) of \(\mathcal O_{X_0}\) over
a saturated open set \(W\), put \(N(a)=N_{d_1}(d_0^\ast a)\); the argument of
4~a), which only uses the Cartesian squares (0) and (2), shows that
\(N(a)\) is a section of \(\mathcal O_Y\) over \(p(W)\), and by the norm criterion
of the Addition after 4~b), \(N(a)\) vanishes at \(p(x)\) if and only if \(a\)
vanishes at some point of \(p^{-1}(p(x))\). Applying \(N\), which is
multiplicative, to the transition functions of \(\mathscr L\) relative to a
covering by saturated open sets on which \(\mathscr L\) is trivial, one obtains
an invertible \(\mathcal O_Y\)-module \(\mathscr M\) with
\(p^\ast\mathscr M\simeq N_{d_1}(d_0^\ast\mathscr L)\) (EGA~II, 6.5), and, for every
section \(t\) of \(\mathscr L^{\otimes k}\) over \(X_0\), a section \(N(t)\) of
\(\mathscr M^{\otimes k}\) over \(Y\) such that \(Y_{N(t)}\) is the set of the \(y\)
with \(p^{-1}(y)\subset(X_0)_t\). To show that \(\mathscr M\) is ample relative
to \(S\), one may suppose \(S\) affine. Let \(y\in Y\) and let \(V\) be an open
neighbourhood of \(y\); as \(p^{-1}(y)\) is finite, there is a section \(t\) of
some \(\mathscr L^{\otimes k}\) with \(p^{-1}(y)\subset(X_0)_t\subset p^{-1}(V)\)
(EGA~II, 4.5.4), and then \(y\in Y_{N(t)}\subset V\). Thus the \(Y_{N(t)}\) form
a basis of the topology of \(Y\), \(\mathscr M\) is ample (EGA~II, 4.5.2), and
\(Y\), being of finite type over \(S\), is quasi-projective over \(S\) (EGA~II,
5.3.1). (The re-edition writes \(Y_{N(t)}=p((X_0)_t)\); in general only the
inclusion \(Y_{N(t)}\subset p((X_0)_t)\) holds, which is enough.)
