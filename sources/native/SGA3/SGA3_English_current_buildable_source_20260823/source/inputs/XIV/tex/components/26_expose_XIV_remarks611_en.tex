
\paragraph{\textbf{Remarks 6.11.}}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xiv:6.11}
\textup{(a)} Rosenlicht gave examples of twisted forms of
\(\mathbb G_a\) over a nonperfect field having only finitely many
rational points; \emph{a fortiori}, they are not unirational.
Chevalley, on the other hand, gave an example of a torus over a
field of characteristic zero that is not rational. By contrast,
Chevalley's theory of semisimple groups shows that, over an
algebraically closed field, every smooth connected affine
algebraic group is rational. In any case, the question of
unirationality arises only for affine algebraic groups: by
Chevalley's structure theorem, a unirational algebraic group is
necessarily affine.

\textup{(b)} With the notation of \SGARefLink{sga3:xiv:6.6}{6.6}, it is tempting to seek a
criterion for the unirationality of \(G\) in terms of \(G/H\),
which is unipotent when \(G\) is affine. Clearly \(G/H\) must be
unirational; is that condition also sufficient? An example of
Rosenlicht shows that a smooth connected unipotent algebraic
group \(U\) may be rational without being \(k\)-unipotent.

\textup{(c)} Over a finite field \(k\), it would be interesting
to study questions of ``density'' such as the following one,
raised by Rosenlicht: if \(G\) is a smooth connected algebraic
group over \(k\), is \(G\) generated by its Cartan
subgroups?\footnote{This question was subsequently answered in
the affirmative by Steinberg.}

For Rosenlicht's question one may reduce to the affine case by
dividing by the center. In the semisimple case, an affirmative
answer would follow from a strengthening of Chevalley's
existence result for regular points mentioned in \SGARefLink{sga3:xiv:3.20}{3.20}: one would
need a regular element of \(\mathfrak g\) outside
\(\mathfrak h=\operatorname{Lie}(H)\), where \(H\ne G\) is a
prescribed smooth algebraic subgroup of an adjoint semisimple
group \(G\) over the finite field \(k\).

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Exposé~XIV; our formulation). The question
has an affirmative answer for connected reductive groups, in particular for semisimple ones: \emph{if \(k\) is
finite, a connected reductive \(k\)-group \(G\) is generated by its maximal tori defined over~\(k\)}, which for
reductive \(G\) are its Cartan subgroups defined over~\(k\). Here is a proof. Let \(G^\sharp\) be the subgroup
generated by these tori; it is a smooth connected \(k\)-subgroup
\textup{(VI\textsubscript{B}~\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-7.1}{7.1})}. Put \(q=\operatorname{Card}(k)\), let
\(\gamma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\bar k/k)\) be \(x\mapsto x^q\), and let \(F\) be the Frobenius endomorphism of
\(G_{\bar k}\); it acts on \(G(\bar k)\) as \(\gamma\), and a smooth subgroup of \(G_{\bar k}\) is defined over
\(k\) if and only if it is \(F\)-stable. By Lang's theorem (loc.\ cit.\ in \SGARefLink{sga3:xiv:1.1}{1.1}), for
every connected \(k\)-group \(H\) the map \(y\mapsto y^{-1}F(y)\) of \(H(\bar k)\) to itself is surjective. If
\(B_0\) is a Borel subgroup of \(G_{\bar k}\), then \(F(B_0)=gB_0g^{-1}\) for some \(g\), and \(B=yB_0y^{-1}\),
where \(y^{-1}F(y)=g^{-1}\), is \(F\)-stable. Thus \(G\) has a Borel subgroup \(B\) defined over \(k\), and \(B\)
has a maximal torus \(T\) defined over \(k\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:xiv:1.1}{1.1})}, which is a maximal torus
of \(G\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:xiv:4.2}{4.2})}. Let \(R\) be the root system of \(T_{\bar k}\) in
\(G_{\bar k}\), \(U_a\) \((a\in R)\) the root subgroups, \(R^+\) and \(\Delta\) the positive and the simple roots
defined by \(B\), and \(W=N(T)/T\). Transport of structure by \(\gamma\) makes \(\gamma\) act on the character
group of \(T_{\bar k}\), preserving \(R\), and also \(R^+\) and \(\Delta\) since \(B\) is defined over \(k\); one
has \(F(U_a)=U_{\gamma a}\), and if \(n\in N(T)(\bar k)\) represents \(w\in W\), then \(F(n)\) represents
\(\sigma(w)=\gamma w\gamma^{-1}\).

\textup{(i)} Let \(L\) be a connected \(k\)-subgroup of \(G\) containing \(T\), let \(n\in N_L(T)(\bar k)\)
represent \(w\in W_L=N_L(T)/T\), and choose \(y\in L(\bar k)\) with \(y^{-1}F(y)=n\). Then \(T_w=yTy^{-1}\) is a
maximal torus of \(L\) defined over \(k\). Let \(H\subset L\) be a smooth connected \(k\)-subgroup containing
\(T\) and \(T_w\). Then \(T_w=hTh^{-1}\) for some \(h\in H(\bar k)\); the element \(n_1=h^{-1}y\) normalizes
\(T\), and \(h^{-1}F(h)=n_1nF(n_1)^{-1}\) lies in \(N_H(T)\). So some element \(xw\sigma(x)^{-1}\)
\((x\in W_L)\) has a representative in \(N_H(T)\).

\textup{(ii)} By XXII~5.4.2 and~5.4.3, \(G^\sharp_{\bar k}=H_{R'}\), where \(R'\) is the set of the \(a\in R\)
with \(U_a\subset G^\sharp\); this set is \(\gamma\)-stable. If \(R'\) contains \(\pm\Delta\), then for
\(a\in\Delta\) the subgroup \(G^\sharp\) contains \(T\), \(U_a\) and \(U_{-a}\), hence a representative of the
reflection \(s_a\) \textup{(XX~3.1)}; thus \(R'\) is stable under \(W\), hence equal to \(R\), every root being
conjugate to a simple root, and \(G^\sharp=G\) \textup{(XXII~5.4.5)}. Let \(O\) be a \(\gamma\)-orbit in
\(\Delta\), \(R_O=R\cap\mathbf ZO\), and \(L\) the \(k\)-subgroup of \(G\) with \(L_{\bar k}=H_{R_O}\)
\textup{(XXII~5.4.7)}, which is \(F\)-stable since \(R_O\) is \(\gamma\)-stable: \(L\) contains \(T\) and the
\(U_{\pm a}\), \(a\in O\), its maximal tori are maximal tori of \(G\), and \(W_L\) is generated by the \(s_a\),
\(a\in O\). Since the subgroup \(L^\sharp\) generated by the maximal \(k\)-tori of \(L\) lies in \(G^\sharp\), it
is enough to prove that \(L^\sharp=L\) for every such \(O\).

\textup{(iii)} The connected components of the Dynkin diagram of \(O\), say \(m\) of them, are permuted
cyclically by \(\gamma\), and \(\gamma^m\) acts transitively on the nodes of each; so \(R_O\) is of type
\(A_1^m\) or \(A_2^m\). In the first case \(O=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}\) with \(a_{i+1}=\gamma a_i\), and the
\(\gamma\)-stable subsets of \(R_O\) are \(\varnothing\), \(\pm O\) and \(R_O\). In the second the components are
\(\{b_i,b'_i\}\), with \(b_{i+1}=\gamma b_i\) and \(b'_{i+1}=\gamma b'_i\) for \(i<m\), \(\gamma b_m=b'_1\) and
\(\gamma b'_m=b_1\); put \(c_i=b_i+b'_i\) and \(C=\{c_1,\ldots,c_m\}\). The roots of a smooth connected subgroup
containing \(T\) form a closed set, since the bracket of the root spaces of two roots of one component whose sum
is a root is the root space of the sum (as in \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\) or \(\mathfrak{pgl}_3\)); the
\(\gamma\)-stable closed subsets of \(R_O\) are \(\varnothing\), \(\pm C\), \(\pm(O\cup C)\), \(C\cup-C\) and
\(R_O\). Hence, if \(L^\sharp\neq L\), either \(L^\sharp_{\bar k}\) lies in \(B_{\bar k}\) or in the Borel
subgroup opposite to it relative to \(T_{\bar k}\), and then \(N_{L^\sharp}(T)=T\)
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:xiv:4.4}{4.4})}, or \(L^\sharp_{\bar k}=H_{C\cup-C}\), and then the image of
\(N_{L^\sharp}(T)\) in \(W_L\) preserves \(C\cup-C\), so lies in the subgroup generated by the \(s_{c_i}\). Take
\(w=s_{a_1}\), resp.\ \(w=s_{b_1}\), in (i), with \(H=L^\sharp\). In the first case \(W_L=(\mathbf Z/2)^m\),
\(\sigma\) permutes the coordinates cyclically, and \(x+\sigma(x)\) has an even number of nonzero coordinates, so
no \(xw\sigma(x)^{-1}\) is \(1\). In the second case \(W_L\) is a product of \(m\) copies of \(S_3\);
identified by means of \(\gamma\), \(\sigma\) shifts the factors, followed on the first factor by conjugation by
\(s_{c_1}\), and the condition \(xw\sigma(x)^{-1}\in\langle s_{c_1},\ldots,s_{c_m}\rangle\) forces
\(x_1s_{b_1}s_{c_1}x_1^{-1}\in\{1,s_{c_1}\}\), which is impossible, \(s_{b_1}s_{c_1}\) having order~\(3\). This
contradicts (i), as \(T_w\subset L^\sharp\); hence \(L^\sharp=L\).
