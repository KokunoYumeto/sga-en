\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:4-8}\textbf{Proposition 4.8.}
\textit{Let \(S\), \(\mathscr I\), \(\mathscr J\) and \(X\) be as in
\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-5}{4.5}, \(\mathscr I\) being nilpotent.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, drops
the hypothesis that \(\mathscr I\) be nilpotent, which is not needed. In our
words: it serves in the proof only to identify the underlying spaces of \(T\),
\(T_0\) and of \(X\), \(X_0\); in general one uses the direct image
\(f_*(\mathscr J\mathscr O_T)\), which is annihilated by \(\mathscr I\) (as
\(\mathscr I\mathscr J=0\)) and by \(\mathscr I_Y\), hence is the direct image of
the \(\mathscr O_{T_0}\)-module \(\mathscr J\mathscr O_T\) by \(f_0\) followed by
\(X_0\to X\), and the adjunction for \(f_0\) gives \(c(X,Y,f)\) as below.} Let
\(Y\) be a subscheme of \(X\).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:4-8:item:i}(i)] \textit{Let \(T\) be an \(S\)-scheme and \(f:T\to X\) an
  \(S\)-morphism such that \(f_{\mathscr J}:T_{\mathscr J}\to X_{\mathscr J}\)
  factors through \(Y_{\mathscr J}\). One can then define an} obstruction
  \[
   c(X,Y,f)\in
   \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{T_0}}
   \bigl(f_0^*(\mathscr N_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathscr O_Y}\mathscr O_{Y_0}),
   \mathscr J\mathscr O_T\bigr)
  \]
  \textit{whose vanishing is necessary and sufficient for the existence of a
  factorization of \(f\) through \(Y\).}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:4-8:item:ii}(ii)] \textit{Suppose in particular \(Y\) flat over \(S\), and let
  \(Y'\) be a subscheme of \(X\), flat over \(S\), such that
  \(Y'_{\mathscr J}=Y_{\mathscr J}\). Consider the canonical morphism}
  \[
   u:\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{Y_0}}
   \bigl(\mathscr N_{Y_{\mathscr J}/X_{\mathscr J}}
   \otimes_{\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}}\mathscr O_{Y_0},
   \mathscr J\mathscr O_Y\bigr)
   \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
   \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{Y_0}}
   \bigl(\mathscr N_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathscr O_Y}\mathscr O_{Y_0},
   \mathscr J\mathscr O_Y\bigr).
  \]
  \textit{If \(i':Y'\to X\) denotes the canonical immersion, one has}
  \[
   c(X,Y,i')=u(d(Y,Y')),
  \]
  \textit{where \(d(Y,Y')\) is the deviation of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-7}{4.7}.}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:4-8:item:iii}(iii)] \textit{Consider the canonical morphism \textup{(SGA~1,
  II, formula 4.3)}}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, refers also to
  EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 16.4.21, and recalls that \(D\) sends the class of a
  local section \(x\) of \(\mathscr I_Y\) to \(dx\otimes1\).}
  \[
   \mathscr N_{Y/X}\xrightarrow{\ D\ }\Omega^1_{X/S}\otimes_{\mathscr O_X}\mathscr O_Y.
  \]
  \textit{It induces, for every \(f_0:T_0\to X_0\) factoring through \(Y_0\), a
  morphism}
  \[
   v_{f_0}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{T_0}}
   \bigl(f_0^*(\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0}),\mathscr J\mathscr O_T\bigr)
   \longrightarrow
   \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{T_0}}
   \bigl(f_0^*(\mathscr N_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathscr O_Y}\mathscr O_{Y_0}),
   \mathscr J\mathscr O_T\bigr)
  \]
  \textit{\textup{(}the first member is none other than
  \(\operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(T,L_X)\), cf. 0.2\textup{)}. Let
  \(a\in\operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(T,L_X)\); consider the composite morphism}
  \[
   g:T\xrightarrow{\ a\times f\ }L_X\times_{X^+}X\longrightarrow X,
  \]
  \textit{where \(f:T\to X\) is a morphism of the kind studied in (i). Then}
  \[
   c(X,Y,g)-c(X,Y,f)=v_{f_0}(a).
  \]
\end{enumerate}

We shall prove part (i) of the proposition, leaving it to the reader to
(not) verify assertions (ii) and (iii); this verification proceeds by
reduction to the affine case and then by comparison of the explicit
definitions.

Let us therefore prove (i). Since \(T\), \(T_{\mathscr J}\) and \(T_0\) on the
one hand, and \(X\), \(X_{\mathscr J}\) and \(X_0\) on the other, have the same
underlying topological space, the continuous maps underlying \(f\),
\(f_{\mathscr J}\) and \(f_0\) are the same. Denote by
\(f(\mathscr O_T)=f_0(\mathscr O_T)\) the (set-theoretic) direct image of the
sheaf of rings \(\mathscr O_T\) under this continuous map. The morphism
\(f:T\to X\) defines a morphism of sheaves of rings
\(\mathscr O_X\to f(\mathscr O_T)\).

As usual, one may restrict to the case where \(Y\) is closed, hence defined by
a sheaf of ideals \(\mathscr I_Y\). For \(f\) to factor through \(Y\), it is
necessary and sufficient that the composite map
\(\mathscr I_Y\to\mathscr O_X\to f(\mathscr O_T)\) be zero. Since
\(f_{\mathscr J}\) factors through \(Y_{\mathscr J}\), the composite map
\(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\to\mathscr O_{X_{\mathscr J}}\to
f(\mathscr O_{T_{\mathscr J}})\) is zero. Consider the commutative diagram, whose
first row is exact,
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
 0\arrow[r]&f(\mathscr J\mathscr O_T)\arrow[r]&f(\mathscr O_T)\arrow[r]
   &f(\mathscr O_{T_{\mathscr J}})\\
 &&\mathscr O_X\arrow[u]\arrow[r]&\mathscr O_{X_{\mathscr J}}\arrow[u]\\
 &&\mathscr I_Y\arrow[u]\arrow[r]\arrow[uul,dashed,"h"]
   &\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\arrow[u]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
it is completed by a morphism \(h:\mathscr I_Y\to f(\mathscr J\mathscr O_T)\).
But, since \(\mathscr J^2=0\) and \(\mathscr I\mathscr J=0\), one has a
factorization
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
 \mathscr I_Y\arrow[r,"h"]\arrow[d,"\mathrm{surj.}"']
   &f(\mathscr J\mathscr O_T)=f_0(\mathscr J\mathscr O_T)\\
 \mathscr I_Y/\mathscr I_Y^2\otimes_{\mathscr O_Y}\mathscr O_{Y_0}
   \arrow[ur,"h'"']&
\end{tikzcd}
\]
By the definition of the direct image, \(h'\) defines a morphism
\[
 f_0^*(\mathscr N_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathscr O_Y}\mathscr O_{Y_0})
 \longrightarrow\mathscr J\mathscr O_T,
\]
which is the required obstruction \(c(X,Y,f)\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III,
details this step. In our words: \(h\) is \(\mathscr O_X\)-linear, and
\(f(\mathscr J\mathscr O_T)\) is annihilated by \(\mathscr I_Y\), whose image in
\(f(\mathscr O_T)\) lies in \(f(\mathscr J\mathscr O_T)\) while
\(\mathscr J^2=0\), and by \(\mathscr I\), as \(\mathscr I\mathscr J=0\); so
\(h\) kills \(\mathscr I_Y^2\) and \(\mathscr I\mathscr I_Y\), which gives
\(h'\).}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:remark:4-9}\textbf{Remark 4.9.}
This obstruction is computed locally on \(T\). If one supposes \(T\) affine,
say \(T=\operatorname{Spec}C\), and likewise \(X=\operatorname{Spec}B\),
\(Y=\operatorname{Spec}B/I_Y\), \(S=\operatorname{Spec}A\),
\(S_{\mathscr J}=\operatorname{Spec}A/J\), \(S_0=\operatorname{Spec}A/I\), it is
computed by the following commutative diagram:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
 I_Y\arrow[r]\arrow[d]&B\arrow[r,"f"]&C\\
 I_Y/I_Y^2\arrow[r]&I_Y/I_Y^2\otimes_BC_0\arrow[r,"c"]&JC\arrow[u,hook]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The obstruction \(c\) is thus defined as the unique morphism of
\(C_0\)-modules
\[
 I_Y/I_Y^2\otimes_BC_0\longrightarrow JC
\]
such that, with the evident notation, \(c(x\otimes1)=f(x)\) for every
\(x\in I_Y\).

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III; our
formulation). The re-edition carries out the verifications of 4.8 (ii) and
(iii) left to the reader. (ii) When \(f\) is the immersion \(i'\), the
composite \(\mathscr I_Y\to\mathscr O_X\to\mathscr O_{Y'}\) takes its values in
\(\mathscr J\mathscr O_{Y'}\simeq\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
\mathscr O_{Y_0}\simeq\mathscr J\mathscr O_Y\) and factors through
\(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\), and then through
\(\mathscr N_{Y_{\mathscr J}/X_{\mathscr J}}\otimes\mathscr O_{Y_0}\): it is the
deviation of \(Y\) and \(Y'\) as described in the Addition after 4.4 (there
written \(d(Y,Y')\)), and \(c(X,Y,i')\) is its image under \(u\); so (ii) holds
when the deviation of 4.7 is taken in that explicit form. (iii) In the affine
situation of 4.9, \(a\) corresponds to an \(A\)-derivation \(\delta\colon B\to
JC\), and \(g\) corresponds to \(f+\delta\) (Addition after 0.2, part (c)); for
\(x\in I_Y\), the difference \(c(X,Y,g)(x\otimes1)-c(X,Y,f)(x\otimes1)\) is
\(\delta(x)\), the value of \(a\) on \(dx\otimes1=D(\bar x)\), that is,
\(v_{f_0}(a)(x\otimes1)\).

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-10}\textbf{Remark 4.10.}
If \(Y_0\) is locally complete intersection in \(X_0\) (which implies that
\(Y\) is so in \(X\)), one has
\(\mathscr N_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathscr O_Y}\mathscr O_{Y_0}=\mathscr N_{Y_0/X_0}\).
More precisely, one has in any case a surjective morphism of
\(\mathscr O_{Y_0}\)-Modules
\[
 \mathscr N_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathscr O_Y}\mathscr O_{Y_0}
 \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{surj.}\ }\mathscr N_{Y_0/X_0},
\]
which is also injective if \(Y\) is locally complete intersection in \(X\) (as
follows at once from the fact that \(Y\) and \(\mathscr N_{Y/X}\) are flat over
\(S\)). In the ``affine'' case of the preceding remark, this translates into a
morphism
\[
 I_Y/I_Y^2\otimes_BB_0\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{surj.}\ }I_{Y_0}/I_{Y_0}^2,
\]
hence a morphism
\[
 I_Y/I_Y^2\otimes_BC_0\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{surj.}\ }
 I_{Y_0}/I_{Y_0}^2\otimes_{B_0}C_0,
\]
which is bijective if \(Y\) is locally complete intersection in \(X\).

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III; our
formulation). The re-edition moves this remark before 4.8 and shows that the
hypothesis of complete intersection is not needed: if \(S'\) is a closed
subscheme of \(S\), \(X'=X\times_SS'\) and \(Y'=Y\times_SS'\), the morphism
\(\mathscr N_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathscr O_Y}\mathscr O_{Y'}\to\mathscr N_{Y'/X'}\)
is surjective, and bijective as soon as \(Y\) is flat over \(S\). Indeed,
locally \(Y\) is closed with ideal \(\mathscr I_Y\), and \(\mathscr I_{Y'}\) is
the image of \(\mathscr I_Y\otimes_{\mathscr O_S}\mathscr O_{S'}\) in
\(\mathscr O_{X'}\); if \(\mathscr O_Y\) is flat over \(S\), the sequence
\(0\to\mathscr I_Y\otimes\mathscr O_{S'}\to\mathscr O_X\otimes\mathscr O_{S'}
\to\mathscr O_Y\otimes\mathscr O_{S'}\to0\) is exact, so
\(\mathscr I_{Y'}=\mathscr I_Y\otimes_{\mathscr O_S}\mathscr O_{S'}\), and
\(\mathscr I_{Y'}^2\) is the image of \(\mathscr I_Y^2\otimes\mathscr O_{S'}\);
hence \(\mathscr I_{Y'}/\mathscr I_{Y'}^2=(\mathscr I_Y/\mathscr I_Y^2)
\otimes_{\mathscr O_S}\mathscr O_{S'}=\mathscr N_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathscr O_Y}
\mathscr O_{Y'}\). Accordingly, the re-edition drops from 4.12 and 4.13 the
hypothesis that \(Y'_0\) be locally complete intersection in \(X'_0\), which
serves there only through this isomorphism, for \(Y'\) flat over \(S\).
