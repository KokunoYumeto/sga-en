\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:0-11}\textbf{Proposition 0.11.}
\textit{Let \(E\) be an \(S\)-scheme, and put
\(X=\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_S(E)\). The kernel of the morphism of
\(S\)-group functors}
\[
 p_X:X\longrightarrow X^+
\]
\textit{is canonically identified with the commutative \(S\)-group functor
\(L'_X\) defined by}
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,L'_X)
 =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{E_0\times_{S_0}Y_0}}
 \left(
   \Omega^1_{E_0/S_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{Y_0},
   \mathscr J\mathscr O_{E\times_S Y}
 \right),
\]
\textit{where \(Y\) denotes a variable \(S\)-scheme.}

Indeed, for a variable \(S\)-scheme \(Y\), we have
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,X)=\operatorname{Aut}_Y(E\times_S Y)
\]
and
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,X^+)
  &=\operatorname{Hom}_{S_{\mathscr J}}(Y_{\mathscr J},X_{\mathscr J})\\
  &=\operatorname{Aut}_{Y_{\mathscr J}}(E\times_S Y_{\mathscr J})\\
  &=\operatorname{Aut}_{Y_{\mathscr J}}
    \bigl((E\times_S Y)\times_Y Y_{\mathscr J}\bigr).
\end{align*}
Applying \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:corollary:0-7}{0.7} to the \(Y\)-scheme \(E\times_S Y\), one obtains an
isomorphism of \(S\)-groups
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,L'_X)
 \simeq
 \operatorname{Ker}\!\left(
   \operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,X)\longrightarrow
   \operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,X^+)
 \right),
\]
an isomorphism which one easily checks to be functorial in the
\(S\)-scheme \(Y\); this completes the proof.

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:0-12}\textbf{Remark 0.12.}
There is a natural action \(f\) of \(X\) on \(L'_X\), defined as follows: for
every \(Y\to S\), one has
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,X)=\operatorname{Aut}_Y(F)
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,L'_X)
 =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{F_0}}
 \bigl(\Omega^1_{F_0/Y_0},\mathscr J\mathscr O_F\bigr),
\]
where \(F\) is the \(Y\)-scheme \(E\times_SY\);\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, notes
that this agrees with the formula of 0.11, since
\(\Omega^1_{F_0/Y_0}\simeq\Omega^1_{E_0/S_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
\mathscr O_{Y_0}\), the formation of differentials commuting with base change
(EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 16.4.5).} the first group acts on the second by
transport of structure, and this action is indeed functorial in \(Y\). One
then has the formula
\[
 x\,i(m)\,x^{-1}=i(f(x)m)
\]
for every \(Y\to S\) and all \(x\in\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,X)\),
\(m\in\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,L'_X)\); it suffices indeed to apply
\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:corollary:0-7}{0.7} to the \(Y\)-scheme \(F\).

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:paragraph:0-13}\textbf{Reminder 0.13.}
The direct image of a quasi-coherent module under a morphism of finite
presentation is quasi-coherent. Under the same hypotheses, formation of
the direct image commutes with flat base change. In the situation

\SGAThreeNativeDiagram[0.50\textwidth]
  {native_diagrams/exp3/diagram_026_exp3_p020_reminder013_flat_base_change_square.tex}
  {The flat base-change square in Reminder \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:paragraph:0-13}{0.13}.}
  {fig:exp3-reminder-013-flat-base-change}
  {LNM 151, pp.~97--98}

if \(f\) (and hence \(f'\)) is of finite presentation and \(g\) (and
hence \(g'\)) is flat, then for every quasi-coherent
\(\mathscr O_T\)-module \(\mathscr F\),
\[
 f_*(\mathscr F)\otimes_{\mathscr O_S}\mathscr O_{S'}
 =f'_*\bigl(\mathscr F\otimes_{\mathscr O_S}\mathscr O_{S'}\bigr),
\]
or, more elegantly,
\[
 g^*\bigl(f_*\mathscr F\bigr)=f'_*\bigl(g'^*\mathscr F\bigr).
\]
These two facts are valid more generally for a quasi-compact and
quasi-separated morphism (cf. EGA~III, 1.4.15 in the quasi-compact separated
case, and EGA~IV\textsubscript{1}, 1.7.4 and 1.7.21).\footnote{Translator's
note: the 1970 text refers to ``EGA~III 6.9.10'' (a statement on the fibres of
the cohomology of a complex) in place of EGA~IV\textsubscript{1}, 1.7.4 and
1.7.21, which extend EGA~I, 9.2.2 and EGA~III, 1.4.15 to quasi-compact
quasi-separated morphisms. These references are those of the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, which also cites EGA~I, 9.2.1 for the
quasi-coherence and the erratum EGA~III\textsubscript{2},
Err\textsubscript{III}~25, by which EGA~III, 1.4.15 holds for quasi-compact
(rather than finite type) separated morphisms.}

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:remark:0-14}\textbf{Remark 0.14.}
In the notation of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:0-11}{0.11}, suppose \(E\) of finite presentation over \(S\)
and \(Y\) flat over \(S\). One then has successively
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,L'_X)
 &=\Gamma\bigl(E\times_SY,\mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_E}
   (\Omega^1_{E/S},\mathscr J\mathscr O_E)\otimes_{\mathscr O_S}\mathscr O_Y\bigr)\\
 &=\Gamma\bigl(Y,g_*\bigl(\mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_E}
   (\Omega^1_{E/S},\mathscr J\mathscr O_E)\otimes_{\mathscr O_S}\mathscr O_Y\bigr)\bigr)\\
 &=\Gamma\bigl(Y,f_*\bigl(\mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_E}
   (\Omega^1_{E/S},\mathscr J\mathscr O_E)\bigr)\otimes_{\mathscr O_S}\mathscr O_Y\bigr)\\
 &=\mathbf W\bigl(f_*\bigl(\mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_E}
   (\Omega^1_{E/S},\mathscr J\mathscr O_E)\bigr)\bigr)(Y),
\end{align*}
where \(f\) and \(g\) are the structural morphisms
\[
 g:E\times_SY\longrightarrow Y,\qquad f:E\longrightarrow S.
\]
We have therefore shown that
\[
 L'_X=\mathbf W\bigl(f_*\bigl(\mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_E}
 (\Omega^1_{E/S},\mathscr J\mathscr O_E)\bigr)\bigr)
\]
on the category of \(S\)-schemes \emph{flat} over \(S\). Note moreover that
the module of which one takes the \(\mathbf W\) is
\emph{quasi-coherent}.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, justifies these steps. In our
words: \(\mathscr J\mathscr O_{E\times_SY}\) being annihilated by \(\mathscr I\),
\(L'_X(Y)\) is also
\(\operatorname{Hom}(\Omega^1_{E/S}\otimes_{\mathscr O_S}\mathscr O_Y,
\mathscr J\mathscr O_{E\times_SY})\); since \(Y\) is flat over \(S\),
\(\mathscr J\mathscr O_{E\times_SY}=(\mathscr J\mathscr O_E)
\otimes_{\mathscr O_S}\mathscr O_Y\), and since \(\Omega^1_{E/S}\) is of finite
presentation (EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 16.4.22), EGA~0\textsubscript{I}, 6.7.6
gives the first equality. The third is the flat base change of 0.13, \(f\)
being of finite presentation, and 0.13 (or EGA~I, 9.1.1 and 9.2.1) also gives
the quasi-coherence.}

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III; our
formulation). Keep \(E\) of finite presentation over \(S\), let
\(\pi_0\colon E_0\to S_0\) be the structural morphism, and put
\[
 L_0=\mathbf W\bigl(\pi_{0*}\mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_{E_0}}
 (\Omega^1_{E_0/S_0},\mathscr J\mathscr O_E)\bigr),
\]
an \(S_0\)-functor; \(\mathscr J\mathscr O_E\) is an
\(\mathscr O_{E_0}\)-module, being annihilated by \(\mathscr I\). If \(Y\) is
flat over \(S\), then
\(\mathscr J\mathscr O_{E\times_SY}=(\mathscr J\mathscr O_E)
\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{Y_0}\), and the computation above, made
over \(S_0\) for \(E_0\) and the flat \(S_0\)-scheme \(Y_0\), gives
\(\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,L'_X)=L_0(Y_0)\); in the notation of II, 1,
therefore, \(L'_X=\prod_{S_0/S}L_0\) on the category of \(S\)-schemes flat
over \(S\). If moreover \(E\) is flat over \(S\), then
\(\mathscr J\mathscr O_E=\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{E_0}\),
and for every \(S_0\)-scheme \(T\) flat over \(S_0\) the same computation
gives
\[
 L_0(T)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{E_0\times_{S_0}T}}
 \bigl(\Omega^1_{E_0\times_{S_0}T/T},
 \mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{E_0\times_{S_0}T}\bigr)
\]
(for an arbitrary \(S_0\)-scheme \(T\) the two sides can differ, these modules
of homomorphisms not commuting with base change in general); on it the
group \(\operatorname{Aut}_T(E_0\times_{S_0}T)=X_0(T)\), where
\(X_0=\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{S_0}(E_0)\), acts by transport of
structure, functorially in \(T\). For \(Y\) flat over \(S\), the action of
\(X(Y)=\operatorname{Aut}_Y(E\times_SY)\) on \(L'_X(Y)=L_0(Y_0)\) defined in
0.12 then factors through \(X(Y)\to X_0(Y_0)\): an automorphism of
\(F=E\times_SY\) acts on \(\Omega^1_{F_0/Y_0}\) and on
\(\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{F_0}\) through its
restriction to \(F_0\).

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:0-15}\textbf{Proposition 0.15.}
\textit{(SGA~1, Expos\'e~III, 6.8)}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, observes that
this global result of SGA~1 rests on the local lifting theorem SGA~1, III, 4.1,
proved there for a locally noetherian base, and refers to
EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 18.1.1, for that local result over an arbitrary
base.}
\textit{For every \(S_{\mathscr J}\)-scheme \(X_{\mathscr J}\) smooth over
\(S_{\mathscr J}\) and affine, there exists an \(S\)-scheme \(X\) smooth over
\(S\) reducing to \(X_{\mathscr J}\), and such an \(X\) is unique up to
(non-unique) isomorphism.}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:0-16}\textbf{Proposition 0.16.}
\textit{(SGA~1, Expos\'e~III, 5.5)}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, notes that
with the definition of smooth morphisms of EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 17.3.1
(and 17.1.1), this is immediate: a formally smooth morphism has, by
definition, the lifting property for affine schemes over \(S\) and their
closed subschemes defined by nilpotent ideals.}
\textit{Let \(X\) be an \(S\)-scheme smooth over \(S\). For every affine
\(S\)-scheme \(Y\), the canonical map}
\[
 p_X(Y):\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,X)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,X^+)
 =\operatorname{Hom}_{S_{\mathscr J}}(Y_{\mathscr J},X_{\mathscr J})
\]
\textit{is surjective.}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:0-17}\textbf{Corollary 0.17.}
\textit{Let \(E\) be an \(S\)-scheme affine over \(S\) and smooth over \(S\);
put \(X=\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_S(E)\). For every affine \(S\)-scheme
\(Y\), the canonical map}
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,p_X):\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,X)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,X^+)
\]
\textit{is surjective.}

Indeed, \(Y\times_S E\) is affine over the affine scheme \(Y\), hence
is affine. Applying \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:0-16}{0.16}, we see that every \(S_{\mathscr J}\)-morphism
\[
 Y_{\mathscr J}\times_{S_{\mathscr J}}E_{\mathscr J}
 \longrightarrow E_{\mathscr J}
\]
extends to an \(S\)-morphism
\[
 Y\times_S E\longrightarrow E.
\]
In other words, every
\(S_{\mathscr J}\)-morphism
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\to\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{S_{\mathscr J}}(E)\)
extends to an \(S\)-morphism \(Y\to\underline{\operatorname{End}}_S(E)\). But
\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:corollary:0-7}{0.7} shows that the same holds when \(\underline{\operatorname{End}}_S(E)\) is
replaced by \(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_S(E)\), which is the asserted
property.
