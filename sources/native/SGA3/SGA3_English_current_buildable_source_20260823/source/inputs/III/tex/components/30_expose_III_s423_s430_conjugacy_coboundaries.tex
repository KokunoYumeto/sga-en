\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-23}\textbf{4.23. ---}
Still under the hypotheses of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:theorem:4-21}{4.21}, we shall now study how the set of
\(Y\) lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) behaves under conjugation by sections of
\(X\). If \(x\) is a section of \(X\) over \(S\) inducing the unit section
of \(X_{\mathscr J}\), the inner automorphism \(\operatorname{Int}(x)\)
defined by \(x\) sends flat subgroups of \(X\) lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\)
to flat subgroups of \(X\) lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\). Now, under the
conditions of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:theorem:4-21:item:ii}{4.21(ii)}, the set of these subgroups is principal homogeneous
under \(Z^1(Y_0,N_0)\); we shall see that there is then a subgroup of
\(Z^1(Y_0,N_0)\)\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, says ``a subgroup
\(\Gamma\) of \(B^1(Y_0,N_0)\)'', which is what the proof gives
(\(\Gamma=\partial d^0C^0(Y_0,L_0^X)\subset\partial C^0(Y_0,N_0)=B^1(Y_0,N_0)\),
by 4.28); the printed statement is also correct, as \(B^1\subset Z^1\).} such that two subgroups of \(X\), flat over \(S\) and
lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\), are conjugate (by elements \(x\in X(S)\)
inducing the unit of \(X(S_{\mathscr J})\)) if and only if their
``difference'' in \(Z^1(Y_0,N_0)\) is an element of this subgroup. In the
best cases we shall show that the latter is none other than
\(B^1(Y_0,N_0)\), hence that the set of flat subgroups of \(X\) lifting
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\), modulo conjugation by the elements \(x\in X(S)\)
inducing the unit of \(X(S_{\mathscr J})\), is either empty or principal
homogeneous under \(H^1(Y_0,N_0)\) (Proposition \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-36}{4.36}).

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-24}\textbf{4.24. ---}
Let \(Y\) be a flat subgroup of \(X\), reducing to \(Y_{\mathscr J}\).
Recall that in \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:remark:0-5}{0.5} we introduced
the functor \(L_0^X\) (respectively \(L_0^Y\)) defined, for a variable
\(S_0\)-scheme \(T\), by the identity
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(T,L_0^X)
 =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_T}
 \left(
  \omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_T,
  \mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_T
 \right),
\]
and similarly with \(Y\) in place of \(X\), as well as the functor
\[
 L'_X=\prod_{S_0/S}L_0^X.
\]
We then have:

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:lemma:4-25}\textbf{Lemma 4.25.}
\textit{There is a canonical exact sequence of \(Y_0\)-modules}
\[
 \tag{+}\label{sga3:iii:lemma:4-25:equation:plus}
 \mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}
 \xrightarrow{d}
 \omega^1_{X_0/S_0}
 \longrightarrow
 \omega^1_{Y_0/S_0}
 \longrightarrow 0
\]
\textit{having the following properties:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:lemma:4-25:item:i}(i)] \textit{By inverse image on \(Y\), \(d\) gives the morphism
  \(D\) of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-8:item:iii}{4.8 (iii)}.}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:lemma:4-25:item:ii}(ii)] \textit{If \(X_0\) and \(Y_0\) are smooth over \(S_0\), then
  \(d\) is injective. Since the two modules of differentials are then
  locally free of finite type, the same is true of
  \(\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}\), and the sequence is locally split.}
\end{enumerate}

By SGA~1, II, formula (4.3), there is a canonical exact sequence of
\(\mathscr O_{Y_0}\)-modules
\[
 \mathscr N_{Y_0/X_0}
 \xrightarrow{D}
 \Omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{X_0}}\mathscr O_{Y_0}
 \longrightarrow
 \Omega^1_{Y_0/S_0}
 \longrightarrow 0.
\]
Since this sequence is invariant under the translations of \(Y_0\), it
comes from a sequence \((+)\) on \(S_0\) by inverse image under the
projection of \(Y_0\) onto \(S_0\). Letting now the inner automorphisms of
\(Y_0\) act, one sees that \((+)\) is an exact sequence of
\(Y_0\)-modules. Since \((+)\) becomes exact on tensoring with
\(\mathscr O_{Y_0}\), it is already exact (\(Y_0\) is faithfully flat over
\(S_0\)). For the same reason, \(d\) is injective if and only if \(D\) is.
The rest follows from SGA~1, II, 4.10.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, writes
this proof with the equivalence \(\mathscr E\mapsto\pi_0^*\mathscr E\),
\(\mathscr F\mapsto\varepsilon_0^*\mathscr F\) between quasi-coherent
\(\mathscr O_{S_0}\)-modules and \(Y_0\)-equivariant \(\mathscr O_{Y_0}\)-modules,
and its variant for \(Y_0\)-\(\mathscr O_{S_0}\)-modules (Exposé~I, Addition at
the end, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:6-8}{(g)}): the conormal sequence consists of modules and morphisms
equivariant under \(Y_0\times_{S_0}Y_0\), so it is the pull-back by
\(\pi_0\colon Y_0\to S_0\) of its pull-back \((+)\) by the unit section
\(\varepsilon_0\); and when \(X_0\) and \(Y_0\) are smooth over \(S_0\), \(D\) is
injective and the sequence is locally split (SGA~1, II, 4.10;
EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 17.2.3 (i) and 17.2.5), hence \(d\) is injective. It
cites EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 16.4.21, for the conormal sequence.}

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:lemma:4-27}\textbf{Lemma 4.27.}
\textit{Under the conditions of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:theorem:4-21}{4.21} for \(S\), \(\mathscr I\),
\(\mathscr J\) and \(X\), let \(Y\) be a subgroup of \(X\), flat over
\(S\) and lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\). Let \(x\in C^0(Y_0,L_0^X)\) be a
section of \(X\) over \(S\) inducing the unit section of
\(X_{\mathscr J}\). Then \(d(Y,\operatorname{Int}(x)Y)\in C^1(Y_0,N_0)\) is
given by the formula}
\[
 d\bigl(\operatorname{Int}(x)Y,Y\bigr)=\partial d^0x=d^1\partial x.
\]

Indeed, put \(Y'=\operatorname{Int}(x)Y\). By the formula
\[
 xyx^{-1}=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}y
 =(x-\operatorname{Ad}(y)x)y=-(\partial x)(y)\cdot y,
\]
one sees that \(Y'\) can be described as the image of \(Y\) under the
composite morphism (cf. \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-8:item:iii}{4.8 (iii)})
\[
 Y\xrightarrow{(-\partial x,i_Y)}L'_X\mathbin{\times_S}X
 \longrightarrow X.
\]
If \(i_Y\) denotes the canonical immersion of \(Y\) in \(X\), one has, by
\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-8:item:iii}{4.8 (iii)} and the preceding result,
\[
 c\bigl(X,Y',\operatorname{Int}(x)\circ i_Y\bigr)-c(X,Y',i_Y)
 =-d^1\partial x.
\]
But \(\operatorname{Int}(x)\circ i_Y\) factors through \(Y'\) by
definition, and the first term is zero; by \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-8:item:ii}{4.8 (ii)},
\(c(X,Y',i_Y)=d(Y',Y)\). This entails \(d(Y',Y)=d^1\partial x\).

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III; our
formulation). The re-edition states 4.27 as part (ii) of a lemma whose part
(i) is the general form of the argument: if \(i'\colon Y\to X\) is a
morphism of \(S\)-schemes lifting \(i_0\) (hence an immersion),
\(Y'=i'(Y)\), and \(d(i,i')\in C^1(Y_0,L_0^X)\) is the element such that
\(i'=d(i,i')\cdot i\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:1-2-4}{1.2.4}), then \(d(Y,Y')=d^1(d(i,i'))\). Indeed, by
4.8 (iii) and \(d^1=v_{i_{Y_0}}\),
\(c(X,Y',i')-c(X,Y',i)=d^1(d(i,i'))\); the first term is zero since \(i'\)
factors through \(Y'\), and \(c(X,Y',i)=d(Y',Y)=-d(Y,Y')\) by 4.8 (ii). For
\(i'=\operatorname{Int}(x)\circ i\) one has \(d(i,i')=-\partial x\), and one
recovers 4.27.

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:4-28}\textbf{Corollary 4.28.}
\textit{Two subgroups \(Y\) and \(Y'\) of \(X\),\footnote{Translator's note:
the 1970 text has ``of \(G\)'', a misprint (also in the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Exposé~III).} flat over \(S\) and
lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\), are conjugate by a section of \(X\) over
\(S\) inducing the unit section of \(X_{\mathscr J}\) if and only if}
\[
 d(Y,Y')\in
 \partial d^0 C^0(Y_0,L_0^X).
\]

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:4-29}\textbf{Corollary 4.29.}
\textit{If \(d^0\) is surjective, then \(Y\) and \(Y'\) as above are
conjugate by a section of \(X\) over \(S\) inducing the unit section of
\(X_{\mathscr J}\) if and only if \(d(Y,Y')\in B^1(Y_0,N_0)\).}
