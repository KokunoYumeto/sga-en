We can now state:

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:0-2}\textbf{Proposition 0.2.}
\textit{For every \(S\)-scheme \(X\), one can define a left action of
the \(X^+\)-abelian group \(L_X\) on the \(X^+\)-object \(X\), with
the following properties:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:0-2:item:i}(i)] \textit{this action makes \(X\) formally principal
homogeneous under \(L_X\) over \(X^+\); that is, the morphism}
\[
 L_X\times_{X^+}X\longrightarrow X\times_{X^+}X
\]
\textit{is an isomorphism;}

\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:0-2:item:ii}(ii)] \textit{the action is functorial in the \(S\)-scheme
\(X\): for every \(S\)-morphism \(f:X\to W\), the following diagram
is commutative;}

\SGAThreeNativeDiagram[0.55\textwidth]
  {native_diagrams/exp3/diagram_018_exp3_p013_proposition02_functoriality_square.tex}
  {Functoriality of the action in \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:0-2:item:ii}{Proposition 0.2(ii)}.}
  {fig:exp3-proposition-02-functoriality}
  {LNM 151, p.~88}

\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:0-2:item:iii}(iii)] \textit{this action ``commutes with fiber products'': for all
\(S\)-schemes \(X\) and \(X'\), the following diagram is commutative:}
\end{enumerate}
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small,row sep=large]
  (L_{X'}\times_{X'^+}X')\times_S(L_X\times_{X^+}X)
    \arrow[r]
    \arrow[d,"\simeq"']
  &
  X'\times_SX
  \\
  (L_{X'}\times_SL_X)\times_{(X'^+\times_SX^+)}(X'\times_SX)
    \arrow[r,"\simeq"]
  &
  L_{X'\times_SX}\times_{(X'\times_SX)^+}(X'\times_SX)
    \arrow[u]
\end{tikzcd}
\]

In SGA~1, III, 5.1,\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has ``SGA III
5.1''.} the following is proved: for every \(S\)-scheme \(Y\) and every
\(S_{\mathscr J}\)-morphism
\(g_{\mathscr J}:Y_{\mathscr J}\to X_{\mathscr J}\), the set of
\(S\)-morphisms \(g:Y\to X\) reducing to \(g_{\mathscr J}\) is empty or
principal homogeneous under the commutative group
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}}
 \bigl(h^*(\Omega^1_{X/S}),\mathscr J\mathscr O_Y\bigr),
\]
where \(h\) is the composite morphism
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\xrightarrow{g_{\mathscr J}}X_{\mathscr J}\to X\).

Translated into our language, this says exactly that, for every
\(X^+\)-scheme \(Y\), the set \(\operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(Y,X)\) is empty or
principal homogeneous under the group above. Let us first show that this
group is none other than
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(Y,L_X)
 =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{Y_0}}
 \bigl(g_0^*(\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0}),\mathscr J\mathscr O_Y\bigr).
\]
For this it suffices to remark that \(\mathscr J\cdot\mathscr O_Y\) is
annihilated by \(\mathscr I\mathscr O_Y\), so that one may replace
\(h^*(\Omega^1_{X/S})\) by the sheaf it induces on \(Y_0\), which one sees
without difficulty to be isomorphic to \(g_0^*(\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0})\).

This done, to complete the proof one would have to check that the
construction of SGA~1, III, 5.1 is functorial in the \(X^+\)-scheme \(Y\),
and then that the action so defined has the functorial properties required
in (ii) and (iii). We shall not do so; the sceptical reader may consult EGA~IV
on this subject, where he will perhaps find some elements of a proof.

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III; our
formulation). The re-edition carries out these verifications, after
recalling the statement and proof of SGA~1, III, 5.1; see also
EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 16.5.14--16.5.18, and M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel,
\emph{Groupes algébriques}, tome~I, Masson, Paris, and North-Holland,
Amsterdam, 1970, chap.~I, \S4, nos.~1--2. Here is an argument. Fix an
\(S\)-scheme \(X\).

(a) \emph{\(L_X\) is a functor.} Let \(Y\) be an \(X^+\)-scheme, given by
\(g_{\mathscr J}\colon Y_{\mathscr J}\to X\), and let \(f\colon Z\to Y\) be an
\(S\)-morphism, \(Z\) being an \(X^+\)-scheme through
\(g_{\mathscr J}\circ f_{\mathscr J}\). Applying \(f^*\) to the inclusion
\(\mathscr J\mathscr O_Y\subset\mathscr O_Y\) gives a morphism
\(f^*(\mathscr J\mathscr O_Y)\to\mathscr O_Z\) with image
\(\mathscr J\mathscr O_Z\), because
\(\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_S}\mathscr O_Z\) maps onto
\(f^*(\mathscr J\mathscr O_Y)\) and onto \(\mathscr J\mathscr O_Z\). Both
modules being annihilated by \(\mathscr I\), this is a morphism of
\(\mathscr O_{Z_0}\)-modules
\(f_0^*(\mathscr J\mathscr O_Y)\to\mathscr J\mathscr O_Z\); composing
\(f_0^*(m)\) with it defines a homomorphism
\(L_X(f)\colon L_X(Y)\to L_X(Z)\), and
\(L_X(f\circ f')=L_X(f')\circ L_X(f)\).

(b) \emph{Local form.} Let \(U=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\) be an affine open
subset of \(X\) lying over an affine open subset
\(\operatorname{Spec}(\Lambda)\) of \(S\), let \(I,J\subset\Lambda\) be the
ideals given by \(\mathscr I\) and \(\mathscr J\), and let
\(V=\operatorname{Spec}(B)\) be an affine open subset of \(Y\) with
\(g_{\mathscr J}(V)\subset U\) (the schemes \(Y\) and \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) have
the same underlying space). On \(U_0\), \(\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\) is given by
\(\Omega^1_{A/\Lambda}\otimes_AA/IA\), and \(JB\) is annihilated by \(IB\);
so the restriction of \(m\in L_X(Y)\) to \(V\) is the same thing as a
\(\Lambda\)-derivation \(D_m\colon A\to JB\), where \(JB\) is an \(A\)-module
through \(A\to B/JB\to B/IB\). If \(W=\operatorname{Spec}(C)\) is an affine
open subset of \(f^{-1}(V)\) and \(\theta\colon B\to C\) is given by \(f\),
then \(L_X(f)(m)\) corresponds on \(W\) to \(\theta\circ D_m\).

(c) \emph{The action.} Let \(g\colon Y\to X\) be an \(S\)-morphism lifting
\(g_{\mathscr J}\), given on \(V\) by \(\varphi\colon A\to B\). Since \(JB\)
is annihilated by \(IB\), the \(A\)-module structure of \(JB\) through
\(\varphi\) is the one used in (b), so \(D_m\) is a \(\varphi\)-derivation;
since \((JB)^2=0\), because \(\mathscr J^2=0\), the map \(\varphi+D_m\) is
again a homomorphism of \(\Lambda\)-algebras, congruent to \(\varphi\) modulo
\(JB\). These homomorphisms are compatible with restriction to smaller
affine open subsets, hence define an \(S\)-morphism \(m\cdot g\colon Y\to X\)
lifting \(g_{\mathscr J}\), with the same underlying continuous map as
\(g\). Conversely, if \(\varphi'\colon A\to B\) also lifts
\(A\to B/JB\), then \(\varphi'-\varphi\) is a \(\varphi\)-derivation, the
product of two elements of \(JB\) being zero. As \(D_m+D_{m'}=D_{m+m'}\),
the set \(\operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(Y,X)\) is thus empty or principal
homogeneous under \(L_X(Y)\): this is SGA~1, III, 5.1 in the form given
above. If \(f\colon Z\to Y\) is as in (a), the morphisms
\((m\cdot g)\circ f\) and \(L_X(f)(m)\cdot(g\circ f)\) have the same
underlying map and are both given on \(W\) by
\(\theta\circ\varphi+\theta\circ D_m\); so they are equal, the bijections
\((m,g)\mapsto(m\cdot g,g)\) are natural in the \(X^+\)-scheme \(Y\), and
(i) follows. The re-edition also observes that, in the proof of SGA~1, III,
5.1, a homomorphism of \(\mathscr O_Y\)-algebras
\(\mathscr O_Y\oplus M\to\mathscr O_Y\) inducing the identity on
\(\mathscr O_Y\), where \(M\) is an ideal of square zero, corresponds to an
\(\mathscr O_Y\)-linear map \(\psi\colon M\to\mathscr O_Y\) only when
\(\psi(M)^2=0\), since \(\psi(x)\psi(y)\) must vanish; here \(\psi\) takes
its values in \(\mathscr J\mathscr O_Y\), whose square is zero, and the
conclusion is unaffected.

(d) \emph{Property (ii).} Let \(f\colon X\to W\) be an \(S\)-morphism. For an
\(X^+\)-scheme \(Y\), \(f^+\) sends \(g_{\mathscr J}\) to
\(f\circ g_{\mathscr J}\), and \(L_f\) sends \(m\) to \(m\circ g_0^*(u)\),
where \(u\colon f_0^*(\Omega^1_{W_0/S_0})\to\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\) is the
canonical morphism; in particular \(L_f\) depends only on \(f_0\). In (b),
take \(U\) so small that \(f(U)\) lies in an affine open subset
\(\operatorname{Spec}(R)\) of \(W\) over \(\operatorname{Spec}(\Lambda)\), and
let \(\psi\colon R\to A\) be given by \(f\). The derivation corresponding to
\(L_f(m)\) is \(D_m\circ\psi\), since \(u\) sends \(dr\) to \(d\psi(r)\),
and \(f\circ(m\cdot g)\) is given on \(V\) by
\((\varphi+D_m)\circ\psi=\varphi\circ\psi+D_m\circ\psi\), which is the
homomorphism of \(L_f(m)\cdot(f\circ g)\). This is (ii).

(e) \emph{Property (iii).} Let \(X'\) be a second \(S\)-scheme and
\(X''=X\times_SX'\), with projections \(p\) and \(p'\). One has
\(X''^+\simeq X^+\times_SX'^+\), both functors having the \(Y\)-points
\(\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y_{\mathscr J},X)\times
\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y_{\mathscr J},X')\), and the decomposition
\(\Omega^1_{X''_0/S_0}\simeq p_0^*(\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0})\oplus
p_0'^*(\Omega^1_{X'_0/S_0})\) (EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 16.4.23) gives
\(L_{X''}(Y)\simeq L_X(Y)\times L_{X'}(Y)\) for every \(X''^+\)-scheme
\(Y\): this is the commutative square preceding 0.2, and the vertical
isomorphism on the left in (iii) is a formal property of fibre products. On
affine open subsets, with \(\varphi\colon A\to B\) and
\(\varphi'\colon A'\to B\) given by \(g\) and \(g'\), the derivation of
\(A\otimes_\Lambda A'\) corresponding to \((m,m')\) is
\(a\otimes a'\mapsto D_m(a)\varphi'(a')+\varphi(a)D_{m'}(a')\), and
\((\varphi+D_m)\otimes(\varphi'+D_{m'})\) is \(\varphi\otimes\varphi'\)
plus this derivation, the term \(D_m(a)D_{m'}(a')\) lying in
\((JB)^2=0\). Hence \((m,m')\cdot(g,g')=(m\cdot g,m'\cdot g')\), which is
(iii); alternatively, (iii) follows from (ii) applied to \(p\) and \(p'\),
since \(L_p(m,m')=m\) and \(L_{p'}(m,m')=m'\).
