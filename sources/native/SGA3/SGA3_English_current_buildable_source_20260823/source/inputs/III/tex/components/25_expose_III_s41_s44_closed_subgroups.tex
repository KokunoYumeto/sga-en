

\section*{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:section:4}4. Infinitesimal extensions of closed subgroups}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{4. Infinitesimal extensions of closed subgroups}

We first state a result valid in an arbitrary abelian category.

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:lemma:4-1}\textbf{Lemma 4.1.}
\textit{Let
\[
 0\longrightarrow A'\xrightarrow{i}A\xrightarrow{p}A''\longrightarrow0
\]
be an exact sequence, let \(\phi:A'\to Q\) be a morphism, and let
\(\pi:A''\to P\) be an epimorphism with kernel \(C\). Let \(E\) be the
set, up to isomorphism, of quadruples \((B,f,g,h)\) such that the sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow Q\xrightarrow{f}B\xrightarrow{g}P\longrightarrow0
\]
is exact and the following diagram commutes:}

\begin{center}
  \SGAThreeNativeInput[0.78\textwidth]
    {native_diagrams/exp3/diagram_031_exp3_p042_lemma41_commutative.tex}
\end{center}

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \textit{The set \(E\) is nonempty if and only if the image in
  \(\operatorname{Ext}^1(C,Q)\) of the element \(A\) of
  \(\operatorname{Ext}^1(A'',A')\) is zero.}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:lemma:4-1:item:ii}(ii)] \textit{Under these conditions, \(E\) is a principal homogeneous
  set under the abelian group \(\operatorname{Hom}(C,Q)\).}
\end{enumerate}

Introduce the amalgamated sum
\[
 B'=A\mathop{\sqcup}\limits_{A'}Q.
\]
There is then a commutative diagram with exact rows:

\begin{center}
  \SGAThreeNativeInput[0.78\textwidth]
    {native_diagrams/exp3/diagram_032_exp3_p043_lemma41_pushout.tex}
\end{center}

and it is clear that the category of solutions of the problem posed is
canonically isomorphic to the category of solutions of the corresponding
problem for the sequence\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, justifies the exactness of
the lower row of the preceding diagram: \(j\) is a monomorphism, as a pushout
of the monomorphism \(i\) in an abelian category, and its cokernel is
\(B'/Q=A/A'=A''\). It refers for this to P.~Freyd, \emph{Abelian
Categories}, Harper and Row, 1964, Theorem 2.5.4.}
\[
 0\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow B'\longrightarrow A''\longrightarrow0
\]
and the morphisms \(\operatorname{id}_Q\) and \(\pi:A''\to P\). One is
therefore reduced to proving the lemma in the case where \(A'\to Q\) is an
isomorphism. In this case, the set \(E\) is clearly in bijection with the set
of subobjects \(N\) of \(A\) such that \(A\to A''\) induces an isomorphism of
\(N\) with the kernel \(C\) of \(A''\to P\), that is, with the set of
morphisms \(C\to A\) lifting the canonical morphism \(C\to A''\); this gives
the desired result.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, spells out this last step. In our
words: two liftings \(C\to A\) of \(C\to A''\) differ by a morphism
\(C\to A'=Q\), and adding such a morphism to a lifting gives a lifting, so
the liftings form an empty or principal homogeneous set under
\(\operatorname{Hom}(C,Q)\); a lifting exists if and only if the extension
of \(C\) by \(Q\) obtained from \(A\) by pullback along \(C\to A''\) splits,
that is, if and only if the image of the class of \(A\) in
\(\operatorname{Ext}^1(C,Q)\) is zero.}

One deduces immediately from (ii):

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:4-2}\textbf{Proposition 4.2.}
\textit{Let \((X,\mathscr O_X)\) be a ringed space, \(\mathscr J\) an ideal of
square zero of \(\mathscr O_X\), and \(\mathscr F\) an \(\mathscr O_X\)-module.
Put \(\mathscr F_0=\mathscr F/\mathscr J\mathscr F\), and let \(\mathscr G_0\) be
a quotient module of \(\mathscr F_0\). Suppose given a morphism of
\(\mathscr O_X/\mathscr J\)-modules}
\[
 f:\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_X/\mathscr J}\mathscr G_0\longrightarrow\mathscr Q.
\]
\textit{Let \(\mathcal E\) be the sheaf on \(X\) defined as follows: for every
open subset \(U\) of \(X\), \(\mathcal E(U)\) is the set of quotient modules
\(\mathscr G\) of \(\mathscr F|_U\) such that
\(\mathscr G/(\mathscr J|_U)\mathscr G=\mathscr G_0|_U\) and for which there is
an isomorphism \textup{(}clearly unique\textup{)}
\((\mathscr J|_U)\mathscr G\simeq\mathscr Q|_U\) making the diagram}
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
 (\mathscr J|_U)\otimes(\mathscr G_0|_U)
   \arrow[r,"f|_U"]
   \arrow[d,"\mathrm{can.}"']
 &
 \mathscr Q|_U
 \\
 (\mathscr J|_U)\mathscr G
   \arrow[ur,"\simeq"']
 &
\end{tikzcd}
\]
\textit{commute. Then \(\mathcal E\) is a sheaf formally principal homogeneous
under the sheaf in commutative groups}
\[
 \mathscr A=\mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_X/\mathscr J}(\mathscr H_0,\mathscr Q),
\]
\textit{where one has put \(\mathscr G_0=\mathscr F_0/\mathscr H_0\).}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III,
restates 4.2 for an \(S\)-scheme \(X\) and an ideal \(\mathscr J\) coming from
\(S\), as used in 4.3, and supplies a proof. In our words: if \(\mathcal E(U)\)
contains some \(\mathscr G\), then \(f|_U\), composed with the epimorphism
\(\mathscr J\otimes\mathscr F_0\to\mathscr J\otimes\mathscr G_0\), factors
through the epimorphism \(\mathscr J\otimes\mathscr F_0\to\mathscr J\mathscr F\),
via \(\mathscr J\mathscr F\to\mathscr J\mathscr G\simeq\mathscr Q\), giving
an epimorphism \(\varphi\colon\mathscr J\mathscr F|_U\to\mathscr Q|_U\) that
does not depend on \(\mathscr G\); and the elements of \(\mathcal E(U)\) are
then exactly the solutions of 4.1 for
\(0\to\mathscr J\mathscr F\to\mathscr F\to\mathscr F_0\to0\), \(\varphi\) and
\(\mathscr F_0\to\mathscr G_0\) (in such a solution \(B\), the map from
\(\mathscr F\) is onto, since its image contains that of \(\mathscr Q\) and maps
onto \(\mathscr G_0\)), so that 4.1 (ii) applies on each \(U\).}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:4-3}\textbf{Proposition 4.3.} \textit{(TDTE IV 5.1) Let \(S\) be a
scheme, let \(S_{\mathscr J}\) be the closed subscheme defined by a
quasi-coherent square-zero ideal \(\mathscr J\), let \(X\) be an
\(S\)-scheme, and let \(\mathscr F\) be a quasi-coherent
\(\mathscr O_X\)-module. Put
\[
 X_{\mathscr J}=X\times_S S_{\mathscr J},\qquad
 \mathscr F_{\mathscr J}
 =\mathscr F\otimes_{\mathscr O_S}\mathscr O_{S_{\mathscr J}}.
\]
Let
\(\mathscr G_{\mathscr J}=\mathscr F_{\mathscr J}/\mathscr H_{\mathscr J}\)
be a quasi-coherent quotient module of \(\mathscr F_{\mathscr J}\), flat
over \(S_{\mathscr J}\). For each open subset \(U\) of \(X\), let
\(\mathcal E(U)\) be the set of quotient modules
\(\mathscr G\) of the \(\mathscr O_U\)-module \(\mathscr F|_U\),
flat over \(S\), such that
\(\mathscr G\otimes_{\mathscr O_S}\mathscr O_{S_{\mathscr J}}\simeq
\mathscr G_{\mathscr J}|_U\) (they are necessarily quasi-coherent, as
extensions of two quasi-coherent modules; cf. EGA~III, 1.4.17). Then the \(\mathcal E(U)\) form a sheaf of
sets \(\mathcal E\) on \(X\), formally principal homogeneous under the
sheaf of commutative groups
\[
 \mathscr A=
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{X_{\mathscr J}}}
  \left(
   \mathscr H_{\mathscr J},
   \mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_{\mathscr J}}}
   \mathscr G_{\mathscr J}
  \right).
\]}

\noindent\textit{Proof.}
Following the fundamental criterion of flatness (see for example SGA~1,
IV, 5.1),\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, refers also to N.~Bourbaki,
\emph{Algèbre commutative}, chap.~III, \S5, Theorem~1.} \(\mathscr G\) is
flat over \(S\) if and only if \(\mathscr G_{\mathscr J}\) is flat over
\(S_{\mathscr J}\) and the canonical morphism
\(\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_{\mathscr J}}}\mathscr G_{\mathscr J}
\to\mathscr J\mathscr G\) is an isomorphism. It therefore suffices to apply
the preceding proposition, taking for \(f\) the identity morphism of
\(\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_{\mathscr J}}}\mathscr G_{\mathscr J}
=\mathscr J\mathscr O_X\otimes_{\mathscr O_{X_{\mathscr J}}}
\mathscr G_{\mathscr J}\).

Taking now \(\mathscr F=\mathscr O_X\) and reasoning as usual, one
deduces:\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, makes this reasoning explicit as a
Corollary 4.3.1. In our words: with \(\mathscr F=\mathscr O_X\) and
\(\mathscr G_{\mathscr J}=\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\), 4.3 says that the closed
subschemes \(Y\) of the open sets \(U\) of \(X\), flat over \(S\), with
\(Y\times_SS_{\mathscr J}=Y_{\mathscr J}\cap U\), form a sheaf formally
principal homogeneous (a pseudo-torsor) under
\(\mathscr A=i_*\mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_{X_{\mathscr J}}}
(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}},\mathscr J\otimes\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}})\),
\(i\colon X_{\mathscr J}\to X\); if it has sections locally, it is a torsor,
whose class in \(H^1(X,\mathscr A)=H^1(X_{\mathscr J},
i^{-1}\mathscr A)\) vanishes exactly when it has a global section (see
EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 16.5.15).}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:4-4}\textbf{Corollary 4.4.}
\textit{Let \(S,S_{\mathscr J},\mathscr J,X,X_{\mathscr J}\) be as above. Let
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\) be a closed subscheme of \(X_{\mathscr J}\), flat over
\(S_{\mathscr J}\). Let \(E\) be the set of closed subschemes \(Y\) of \(X\),
flat over \(S\), such that \(Y\times_S S_{\mathscr J}=Y_{\mathscr J}\).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \textit{The set \(E\) is empty or principal homogeneous under}
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{X_{\mathscr J}}}
 \left(
  \mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}},
  \mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_{\mathscr J}}}\mathscr J
 \right),
\]
\textit{where \(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\) is the Ideal defining
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\) in \(X_{\mathscr J}\).}

\item[(ii)] \textit{The set \(E\) is nonempty if and only if both of the
following conditions hold:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \textit{The problem has a solution locally on \(X\).}
\item[(b)] \textit{A certain obstruction vanishes; it lies in}
\[
 H^1\!\left(
  X_{\mathscr J},
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{X_{\mathscr J}}}
  \left(
   \mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}},
   \mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_{\mathscr J}}}
   \mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}
  \right)
 \right).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III; our
formulation). The re-edition adds here a complement, which it uses to prove
4.8 (ii): an explicit description of the action in 4.4 (i). Let \(Y\) and
\(Y'\) be two elements of \(E\), with ideals \(\mathscr I_Y\) and
\(\mathscr I_{Y'}\) in \(\mathscr O_X\). Since \(\mathscr O_Y\) and
\(\mathscr O_{Y'}\) are flat over \(S\), the ideals
\(\mathscr J\mathscr O_Y\) and \(\mathscr J\mathscr O_{Y'}\) are both
\(\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_{\mathscr J}}}\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\),
and the surjections \(\mathscr I_Y\to\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\),
\(\mathscr I_{Y'}\to\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\) induced by
\(\mathscr O_X\to\mathscr O_{X_{\mathscr J}}\) have kernels
\(\mathscr I_Y\cap\mathscr J\mathscr O_X\) and
\(\mathscr I_{Y'}\cap\mathscr J\mathscr O_X\), which are both the kernel
\(\mathscr K\) of \(\mathscr J\mathscr O_X\to\mathscr J\otimes
\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\). For a local section \(z\) of
\(\mathscr I_{Y'}\), the image of \(z\) in \(\mathscr O_Y\) lies in
\(\mathscr J\mathscr O_Y\), since \(Y\) and \(Y'\) have the same reduction;
it depends only on the image \(\bar z\) of \(z\) in
\(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\), because two choices differ by a section of
\(\mathscr K\subset\mathscr I_Y\). This defines an
\(\mathscr O_{X_{\mathscr J}}\)-linear map
\(d(Y',Y)\colon\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\to\mathscr J\otimes
\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\), with \(d(Y,Y')=-d(Y',Y)\) and
\(d(Y'',Y)=d(Y'',Y')+d(Y',Y)\) (lift a section of
\(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\) to \(\mathscr I_Y\), \(\mathscr I_{Y'}\),
\(\mathscr I_{Y''}\) and compare the differences in
\(\mathscr J\mathscr O_X\)). Moreover \(Y'\) is determined by \(Y\) and
\(d(Y',Y)\): the ideal \(\mathscr I_{Y'}\) consists of the local sections of
\(\mathscr O_X\) whose image in \(\mathscr O_{X_{\mathscr J}}\) is a section
\(u\) of \(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\) and whose image in \(\mathscr O_Y\) is
\(d(Y',Y)(u)\).
