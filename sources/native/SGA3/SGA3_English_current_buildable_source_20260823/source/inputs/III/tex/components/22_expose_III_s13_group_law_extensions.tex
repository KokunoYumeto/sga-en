

\subsection*{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:subsection:1-3}1.3. Extensions of group laws.}
Consider the following situation: one has a morphism in
\(\widehat{\mathscr C}\),
\[
 p:X\longrightarrow Y,
\]
and a commutative \(\widehat{\mathscr C}\)-group \(M\) acting on
\(X\), such that \(X\) is formally principal homogeneous over \(Y\)
under \(M_Y\).

If \(g:Y\to Z\) is an arbitrary morphism in
\(\widehat{\mathscr C}\), then \(g\circ p:X\to Z\) is invariant
under \(M\): for every \(S\in\operatorname{Ob}\mathscr C\), the map
\((g\circ p)(S)\) is invariant under the action of \(M(S)\) on
\(X(S)\). We shall assume that the following condition
holds for \(n=1,2,3,4\):

\medskip
\noindent\((+)_{n}\): \textit{Every morphism from \(X^n\) to \(M\)
that is invariant under the action of \(M^n\) on \(X^n\) factors
uniquely through \(p^n:X^n\to Y^n\), where the superscript \(n\)
denotes a Cartesian power.}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:lemma:1-3-1}\textbf{Lemma 1.3.1.}
\textit{If \(h\) is a morphism from \(Y\) to \(M\), the automorphism of \(X\)
defined set-theoretically by \(x\mapsto h(p(x))x\) commutes with \(p\) and
with the operations of \(M\) on \(X\). This construction gives a bijective
correspondence between morphisms from \(Y\) to \(M\) and automorphisms of
\(X\) commuting with \(p\) and with the operations of \(M\).}

The first part is clear.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, writes it out: for
\(x\in X(S)\) and \(m\in M(S)\), one has \(p(h(p(x))x)=p(x)\), and
\(m\cdot h(p(x))x=h(p(mx))\cdot mx\) because \(p(mx)=p(x)\) and \(M\) is
commutative. It also replaces ``commutes with \(p\)'' by ``preserves the
fibres of \(p\)'', which says the same, \(p\circ u=p\).} Conversely, an
automorphism \(u\) of \(X\) commuting with \(p\) can be written
set-theoretically as \(x\mapsto g(x)x\), where \(g\) is a certain morphism
from \(X\) to \(M\). If \(u\) commutes with the operations of \(M\), this
morphism is invariant under \(M\),\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, verifies this:
\(m\cdot g(x)x=m\cdot u(x)=u(mx)=g(mx)\cdot mx\), so \(g(mx)=g(x)\), since
\(M\) is commutative and acts freely on the fibres of \(p\).} and one
concludes by condition \((+)_{1}\).

Suppose now, in addition, that a group law on \(Y\) and an action of
\(Y\) on \(M\) are given, that is, a morphism of
\(\widehat{\mathscr C}\)-groups
\[
 f:Y\longrightarrow
 \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\widehat{\mathscr C}\text{-gr.}}(M).
\]

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:definition:1-3-2}\textbf{Definition 1.3.2.}
\textit{A composition law on \(X\),}
\[
 P:X\times X\longrightarrow X,
\]
\textit{is called \emph{admissible} if it satisfies the following two
conditions:}

\begin{enumerate}
 \item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:definition:1-3-2:item:i}(i)] \textit{\(P\) lifts the group law of \(Y\); that is, the
 following diagram is commutative.}

 \SGAThreeNativeDiagram[0.28\textwidth]
  {native_diagrams/exp3/diagram_029_exp3_p029_admissible_law_square.tex}
  {The compatibility of an admissible composition law with the group
   law on \(Y\).}
  {fig:exp3-p029-admissible-law-square}
  {LNM 151, pp.~108--109}

 \item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:definition:1-3-2:item:ii}(ii)] \textit{For every \(S\in\operatorname{Ob}\mathscr C\)
 and all \(x,y\in X(S)\), \(m,n\in M(S)\), one has}
 \[
  (++)\qquad
  P(m\cdot x,n\cdot y)
   =m\cdot f(p(x))(n)\cdot P(x,y).
 \]
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:1-3-3}\textbf{Proposition 1.3.3.}
\textit{For a group law \(\ast\) on \(X\) to be admissible, it is
necessary and sufficient that the following four conditions hold:}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(i)] \textit{\(p:X\to Y\) is a group morphism.}

 \item[(ii)] \textit{The morphism \(i:M\to X\), defined by
 \(i(m)=m\cdot e_X\), is a group isomorphism from \(M\) onto
 \(\ker(p)\); in other words, set-theoretically,}
 \[
  (m\cdot e_X)\ast(n\cdot e_X)=(mn)\cdot e_X.
 \]

 \item[(iii)] \textit{One has
 \(m\cdot x=(m\cdot e_X)\ast x\) for every
 \(m\in M(S)\) and \(x\in X(S)\).}

 \item[(iv)] \textit{The inner automorphisms of \(X\) act on
 \(\ker(p)\) according to the set-theoretic formula}
 \[
  x\ast i(m)\ast x^{-1}=i\bigl(f(p(x))m\bigr).
 \]
\end{enumerate}
The proof is immediate.

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:lemma:1-3-4}\textbf{Lemma 1.3.4.}
\textit{Let \(u\) be an automorphism of \(X\) of the type described in
\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:1-3-1}{1.3.1}. If \(P\) and \(P'\) are two composition laws on \(X\) deduced
from one another by means of \(u\) \textup{(}by \(P(ux,uy)=uP'(x,y)\)\textup{)},
then \(P\) is an admissible composition law \textup{(}respectively, an
admissible group law\textup{)} if and only if \(P'\) is one.}

Trivial.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, makes this explicit, for use in step e)
of the proof of 1.3.6. In our words: let \(u(x)=h(p(x))x\) with
\(h\colon Y\to M\), and put \(v=p(x)\), \(w=p(y)\). If \(P\) is admissible,
then \(P(ux,uy)=h(v)\,f(v)(h(w))\,P(x,y)\) by (++), and
\(p(P(x,y))=vw\); applying \(u^{-1}\colon z\mapsto h(p(z))^{-1}z\) gives
\[
 P'(x,y)=h(vw)^{-1}h(v)\,f(v)(h(w))\,P(x,y)=(\partial h)(v,w)\,P(x,y),
\]
from which conditions (i) and (ii) of 1.3.2 for \(P'\) follow at once,
\(M\) being commutative; and \(u\) is an isomorphism from \((X,P')\) onto
\((X,P)\), so \(P'\) is a group law if \(P\) is. Since \(u_h^{-1}=u_{h^{-1}}\)
and \(u_h\circ u_k=u_{hk}\) for the automorphisms \(u_h\colon x\mapsto
h(p(x))x\), the relation of 1.3.5 is an equivalence relation.}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:definition:1-3-5}\textbf{Definition 1.3.5.}
\textit{Two admissible composition laws obtained from one another by
the procedure of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:1-3-4}{1.3.4} are called \emph{equivalent}.}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:1-3-6}\textbf{Proposition 1.3.6.}
\textit{Suppose that an admissible composition law on \(X\) exists.
Then:}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:1-3-6:item:i}(i)] \textit{There exists a canonically determined class
 \(c\in H^3(Y,M)\), whose vanishing is necessary and sufficient for
 the existence of an admissible associative composition law on \(X\).}

 \item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:1-3-6:item:ii}(ii)] \textit{If \(c=0\), the set of admissible associative
 composition laws on \(X\) (respectively, the set of their equivalence
 classes) is principal homogeneous under \(Z^2(Y,M)\) (respectively,
 \(H^2(Y,M)\)).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text, and the Gille--Polo
 re-edition (2011), have \(Z^2(G,M)\) and \(H^2(G,M)\); there is no \(G\) in
 this statement.}}
\end{enumerate}

The proof proceeds in several steps.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(a)}
Let \(P\) be an admissible composition law on \(X\). Since \(P\)
lifts the composition law of \(Y\), which is associative, there is a unique
morphism \(a:X^3\to M\) such that
\[
 P(x,P(y,z))=a(x,y,z)\cdot P(P(x,y),z).
\]
Applying condition (ii), one sees at once that \(a\) is
invariant under the action of \(M^3\) on \(X^3\),\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III,
verifies this. In our words: for \(m,m',m''\in M(S)\), put \(u=p(x)\) and
\(v=p(y)\); applying (++) twice, \(P(mx,P(m'y,m''z))\) and
\(P(P(mx,m'y),m''z)\) are obtained from \(P(x,P(y,z))\) and
\(P(P(x,y),z)\) by the action of the same element
\(m\,f(u)(m')\,f(uv)(m'')\) of \(M(S)\); as \(M\) is commutative and acts
freely, \(a(mx,m'y,m''z)=a(x,y,z)\).} whence, applying hypothesis
\((+)_{3}\), the following result:

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{(1) There exists a unique morphism
\(DP:Y^3\to M\) such that}
\[
 P(x,P(y,z))=DP(p(x),p(y),p(z))\cdot P(P(x,y),z),
\]
\textit{and \(P\) is associative if and only if \(DP=0\).}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(b)}
If one computes \(P(P(P(x,y),z),t)\) step by step by means of the preceding
formula, one finds, putting
\[
 p(x)=u,\qquad p(y)=v,\qquad p(z)=w,\qquad p(t)=h,
\]
the relation\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III,
draws this computation as a pentagon. In our words: with
\(A=P(P(P(x,y),z),t)\), \(B=P(P(x,P(y,z)),t)\), \(C=P(x,P(P(y,z),t))\),
\(D=P(x,P(y,P(z,t)))\) and \(E=P(P(x,y),P(z,t))\), formula (1) and
condition (++) give \(B=DP(u,v,w)\,A\), \(C=DP(u,vw,h)\,B\),
\(D=f(u)(DP(v,w,h))\,C\), \(E=DP(uv,w,h)\,A\) and \(D=DP(u,v,wh)\,E\);
comparing the two resulting expressions of \(D\) in terms of \(A\), \(M\)
acting freely, gives this relation.}
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &DP(u,v,w)\cdot DP(u,vw,h)\cdot f(u)(DP(v,w,h))\\
 &\qquad\cdot DP(u,v,wh)^{-1}\cdot DP(uv,w,h)^{-1}=e_M,
\end{aligned}
\]
One therefore has \(\partial DP(u,v,w,h)=e_M\). Since, moreover, the
left-hand side of the preceding formula can be written, by means of \(P\) and
\(a\), as the expression at \((x,y,z,t)\) of a certain morphism
\(X^4\to M\), it follows from the uniqueness assertion in \((+)_{4}\) that
\(\partial DP\) and \(e_M\), which factor the same morphism, are
equal; hence:

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{(2) \(DP\) is a cocycle; that is,
\(DP\in Z^3(Y,M)\).}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(c)}
If \(P\) and \(P'\) are two admissible composition laws on \(X\),
there is a unique morphism
\[
 b:X^2\longrightarrow M
\]
such that \(P'(x,y)=b(x,y)P(x,y)\). One sees as usual that \(b\) is
invariant under \(M^2\), whence:

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{(3) For every pair \((P,P')\) of admissible
composition laws there exists a unique morphism
\(d(P,P'):Y^2\to M\) such that}
\[
 P'(x,y)=d(P,P')(p(x),p(y))\cdot P(x,y),
\]
\textit{and the set of admissible composition laws is thereby made
principal homogeneous under}
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(Y^2,M)=C^2(Y,M).
\]

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(d)}
Under the preceding conditions one has
\[
 (4)\qquad DP'-DP=\partial^2d(P,P').
\]

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(e)}
The laws \(P\) and \(P'\) are equivalent if and only if there is a
morphism
\[
 h\in C^1(Y,M)=\operatorname{Hom}(Y,M)
\]
such that \(d(P,P')=\partial^1h\); this follows easily from the definition of
equivalence.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(f)}
It remains only to conclude. We seek an associative \(P'\), that is,
one for which \(DP'=e_M\). Now \(DP\) is a cocycle whose class in
\(H^3(Y,M)\) does not depend on the chosen admissible composition law
\(P\), by (3) and (4). This class is the required obstruction \(c\).
An appropriate \(P'\) can be chosen if and only if \(c=0\). Indeed,
after choosing an arbitrary \(P\), one must solve, by (1),
\[
 0=DP'=DP+\partial^2d(P,P'),
\]
which is possible, by (3) and (4), if and only if \(c=0\). Again by
(3) and (4), the set of associative laws \(P'\) is principal
homogeneous under \(Z^2(Y,M)\). By (e), the set of associative laws
\(P'\) modulo equivalence is principal homogeneous under
\(H^2(Y,M)\).
