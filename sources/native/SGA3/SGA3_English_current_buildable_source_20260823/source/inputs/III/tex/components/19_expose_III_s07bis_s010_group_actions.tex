

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III; our
formulation). The re-edition adds here a statement, 0.7~bis, which makes
explicit a group structure on \(L_X\) used implicitly in 0.8. For an
\(S\)-scheme \(Y\), let \(D(Y)\) be the spectrum of the
\(\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\)-algebra
\(\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\oplus\mathscr J\mathscr O_Y\), in which the
\(\mathscr O_{Y_0}\)-module \(\mathscr J\mathscr O_Y\) is an ideal of square
zero; \(D(Y)\) is an \(S_{\mathscr J}\)-scheme, it contains \(Y_{\mathscr J}\)
as the closed subscheme defined by that ideal, and the projection
\(r\colon D(Y)\to Y_{\mathscr J}\) is a retraction. By SGA~1, III, 5.1
(applied to \(Y_{\mathscr J}\subset D(Y)\)), the \(S\)-morphisms
\(D(Y)\to X\) restricting to a given \(h\colon Y_{\mathscr J}\to X\) form a
set which is principal homogeneous under
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{Y_0}}(h_0^*(\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0}),
\mathscr J\mathscr O_Y)\), and which contains \(h\circ r\). Hence
\(m\mapsto m\cdot(h\circ r)\) gives a bijection
\(L_X(Y)\xrightarrow{\sim}X_{\mathscr J}(D(Y))\), compatible with the
projections to \(X^+(Y)=X_{\mathscr J}(Y_{\mathscr J})\) and natural in
\(Y\) and in \(X\) (the local computations (b) and (d) of the Addition
after 0.2 apply without change). Suppose now that \(X_{\mathscr J}\) is an
\(S_{\mathscr J}\)-group, or only an \(S_{\mathscr J}\)-monoid, with law
\(\mu\) and unit section \(e\). Then \(X_{\mathscr J}(D(Y))\) is a group
(a monoid), functorially in \(Y\), and the maps
\(X_{\mathscr J}(Y_{\mathscr J})\rightleftarrows X_{\mathscr J}(D(Y))\)
given by \(r\) and by restriction are homomorphisms: \(L_X\) becomes an
\(S\)-group (an \(S\)-monoid), and \(p\colon L_X\to X^+\) is a split
surjection with section \(s\) given by \(r\). Its kernel consists of the
points over the unit; with \(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}=e_0^*(\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0})\),
it is the functor \(L'_X\) of 0.4. On \(L'_X\) the law \(\mu\) is the
addition of homomorphisms: if \(a,b\) in the kernel correspond to \(m,m'\),
then \((a,b)\colon D(Y)\to X_{\mathscr J}\times X_{\mathscr J}\) corresponds to
\((m,m')\) along \((e,e)\), where the differentials of
\(X_{\mathscr J}\times X_{\mathscr J}\) are the direct sum of those of the
factors, and \(\mu(a,b)\) corresponds to \(m\circ\alpha+m'\circ\beta\), with
\(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) the endomorphisms of \(e_0^*(\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0})\)
induced by \(\mu\circ(\operatorname{id},e)\) and \(\mu\circ(e,\operatorname{id})\);
both are the identity, so \(\mu(a,b)\) corresponds to \(m+m'\). In the group
case, therefore, \(L_X\) is the semidirect product of \(X^+\) by \(L'_X\),
\(X^+\) acting on \(L'_X\) by conjugation through \(s\).

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:paragraph:0-8}Now let \(X\) be an \(S\)-scheme such that \(X_{\mathscr J}\) is an
\(S_{\mathscr J}\)-group. Suppose that there is an \(S\)-morphism
\[
 P:X\times_S X\longrightarrow X
\]
whose base change
\[
 P_{\mathscr J}:
 X_{\mathscr J}\times_{S_{\mathscr J}}X_{\mathscr J}
 \longrightarrow X_{\mathscr J}
\]
is the group law of \(X_{\mathscr J}\). (An important special case of the
preceding situation will be the case where \(X\) is an \(S\)-group and \(P\)
is taken to be its group law.)

One deduces a morphism
\[
 L_P:L_X\times_S L_X\longrightarrow L_X
\]
which, in fact, does not depend on \(P\), since it can be computed from the
group law \(P_{\mathscr J}\) of \(X_{\mathscr J}\), as we shall now see. The
morphism
\[
 P_0^*(\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0})\longrightarrow
 \Omega^1_{X_0\times_{S_0}X_0/S_0}
\]
translates, in \(T_{X_0/S_0}\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:ii:subsection:4-1}{II~4.1}), into the group law of the latter,
which one writes down at once using the decomposition of \(T_{X_0/S_0}\) as a
semidirect product of \(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(X_0/S_0)\) by \(X_0\).
Using (ii) and (iii) of 0.2, one obtains, after all
calculations:\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, carries out this calculation with the
group structure of \(L_X\) of the preceding Addition. In our words: by (ii)
and (iii) of 0.2 applied to \(P\), one has \(P(mx,m'x')=L_P(m,m')P(x,x')\),
where \(m\) and \(m'\) are viewed in \(L_X(S')\) over \(g=p_X(x)\) and
\(g'=p_X(x')\); since \(L_P\) depends only on \(P_{\mathscr J}\), it is the
group law of \(L_X=L'_X\rtimes X^+\), and there the product of \(m\) over
\(g\) and \(m'\) over \(g'\) is \(m\cdot\operatorname{Ad}(g)(m')\) over
\(gg'\), the conjugation action of \(X^+\) on \(L'_X\) being induced by the
adjoint action of \(X_0\) on \(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\).}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:0-8}\textbf{Proposition 0.8.}
\textit{Let \(P:X\times_S X\to X\) be an \(S\)-morphism such that
\(P_{\mathscr J}\) equips \(X_{\mathscr J}\) with an
\(S_{\mathscr J}\)-group structure. Denote by \((m,x)\mapsto mx\) the
morphism}
\[
 L'_X\times_S X\longrightarrow X
\]
\textit{defining the action of \(L'_X\) \textup{(}defined in 0.4\textup{)} on
\(X\). Denote by}
\[
 \operatorname{Ad}:X^+\longrightarrow
 \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(L'_X)
\]
\textit{the action of \(X^+\) on \(L'_X\) deduced from the adjoint action of
\(X_0\) on \(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\). For every \(S'\to S\) and all
\(x,x'\in X(S')\), \(m,m'\in L'_X(S')\), one has}
\[
 P(mx,m'x')=\bigl(m\cdot\operatorname{Ad}p_X(x)(m')\bigr)\,P(x,x').
\]

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:0-9}\textbf{Corollary 0.9.}
\textit{Let \(X\) be an \(S\)-group. Then \(X^+\) is naturally equipped with an
\(S\)-group structure, \(p_X\) is a morphism of \(S\)-groups, and the
\(S\)-morphism}
\[
 i:L'_X\longrightarrow X
\]
\textit{defined set-theoretically by \(m\mapsto me\) \textup{(}where \(e\) is
the unit section of \(X\)\textup{)}\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text
has ``of \(G\)''.} is an isomorphism of \(S\)-groups from \(L'_X\) onto the
kernel of \(p_X:X\to X^+\).}

The first two assertions have already been proved. Since \(X\) is
formally principal homogeneous over \(X^+\) under
\(L_X=L'_X\times_S X^+\), the morphism in the statement is indeed an
isomorphism (of \(S\)-functors) from \(L'_X\) onto the kernel of \(p_X\). That
it respects the group structures follows from the explicit formula of 0.8.

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:0-10}\textbf{Corollary 0.10.}
\textit{With the preceding notation, for every \(S'\to S\) and all
\(x\in X(S')\) and \(m\in L'_X(S')\), one has}
\[
 x\,i(m)\,x^{-1}=i(\operatorname{Ad}p_X(x)m).
\]
This follows from the formula given above and from
\(i(m)x=mx\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, adds this formula to 0.9. It is the case
\(x=e\), \(m'=1\) of 0.8, since \(P(me,x')=i(m)x'\) and \(1x'=x'\).}

Thus, when \(X\) is an \(S\)-group, we have explicitly determined the
kernel of \(X\to X^+\) and the action of the inner automorphisms of
\(X\) on this kernel. We shall now see that the same can be done for
certain, not necessarily representable, \(S\)-group functors. One case
will be useful to us, namely the functors
\(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}\) of \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:1:7}{Expos\'e~I, 1.7}. We state it at
once in the next proposition.
