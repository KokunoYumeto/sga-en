\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:4-31}\textbf{Corollary 4.31.}
\textit{Let \(Y\) be as in \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:4-27}{4.27}. The set of conjugates of \(Y\) by
sections of \(X\) over \(S\) inducing the unit section of
\(X_{\mathscr J}\) is isomorphic to}
\[
 E=\partial C^0(Y_0,L_0^X/L_0^Y)
 =C^0(Y_0,L_0^X/L_0^Y)\big/H^0(Y_0,L_0^X/L_0^Y).
\]

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:4-32}\textbf{Corollary 4.32.}
\textit{Suppose in addition that \(S_0\) is affine and that
\(\omega^1_{Y_0/S_0}\) is finite locally free.}\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970
text has ``\(\operatorname{Lie}(Y_0/S_0)\) locally free''; the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, corrects this to ``\(\omega^1_{Y_0/S_0}\) finite
locally free'', which the proof uses (through 0.6) and which does not follow
from the former: for \(\mu_p\) over \(\mathbf Z_{(p)}\), the Lie algebra is zero
while \(\omega^1\) is \(\mathbf Z_{(p)}/p\mathbf Z_{(p)}\).} \textit{If}
\[
 \mathscr F_0=
 \bigl(\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)/
       \operatorname{Lie}(Y_0/S_0)\bigr)
 \otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr J,
\]
\textit{then
\(E=\Gamma(S_0,\mathscr F_0)/H^0(Y_0,\mathscr F_0)\).}

Indeed,\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, details this proof. Since
\(\omega^1_{Y_0/S_0}\) and \(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\) are finite locally free (\(X\)
being smooth), 0.6 gives \(L_0^Y=\mathbf W(\operatorname{Lie}(Y_0/S_0)
\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr J)\) and \(L_0^X=\mathbf W(\operatorname{Lie}
(X_0/S_0)\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr J)\); the surjection
\(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\to\omega^1_{Y_0/S_0}\) of 4.25 between finite locally free
modules gives, by duality, a locally split exact sequence \(0\to\operatorname{Lie}
(Y_0/S_0)\otimes\mathscr J\to\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)\otimes\mathscr J\to
\mathscr F_0\to0\), whence the exact sequence above, of
\(Y_0\)-\(\mathscr O_{S_0}\)-modules; and the map \(C^0(Y_0,L_0^X)\to
C^0(Y_0,\mathbf W(\mathscr F_0))\) is \(\Gamma(S_0,\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)
\otimes\mathscr J)\to\Gamma(S_0,\mathscr F_0)\).} one has for every \(T\) over \(S_0\) an exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(T,L_0^Y)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(T,L_0^X)
 \longrightarrow\mathbf W(\mathscr F_0)(T).
\]
By the reasoning that served to prove \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:corollary:4-31}{4.31}, we can compute \(E\) as
the image of \(C^0(Y_0,L_0^X)\) in \(C^1(Y_0,\mathbf W(\mathscr F_0))\). But
since \(S_0\) is affine, \(C^0(Y_0,L_0^X)\to C^0(Y_0,\mathbf W(\mathscr F_0))\)
is surjective, and one indeed finds the announced result.

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:4-33}\textbf{Corollary 4.33.}
\textit{Let \(S,\mathscr I,\mathscr J\) and \(X\) be as in \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:theorem:4-21}{4.21}. Suppose
\(S\) affine. Let \(Y\) be a diagonalizable subgroup of \(X\), with
\(\omega^1_{Y_0/S_0}\) finite locally free.}\footnote{Translator's note: the
1970 text does not have the hypothesis on \(\omega^1_{Y_0/S_0}\); the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, adds it, as the proof uses it
through 4.32 (for a diagonalizable \(Y\) it can fail, e.g. for \(\mu_p\) when
\(p\) is neither zero nor invertible on \(S_0\)), and it supposes \(S_0\)
affine instead of \(S\), which is equivalent here, \(\mathscr I\) being
nilpotent (EGA~I, 5.1.9).} \textit{The set of
subgroups of \(X\) conjugate to \(Y\) by a section of \(X\) over
\(S\) inducing the unit section of \(X_{\mathscr J}\) is isomorphic
to}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, adds that \(E\) is thus
\(C^0(Y_0,L_0^X)/H^0(Y_0,L_0^X)\), as used in 4.34: indeed
\(C^0(Y_0,L_0^X)=\Gamma(S_0,\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)\otimes\mathscr J)\) and
\(H^0(Y_0,L_0^X)=\Gamma(S_0,\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)^{\operatorname{ad}(Y_0)}
\otimes\mathscr J)\), \(S_0\) being affine and \(Y\) diagonalizable.}
\[
 E=\Gamma\!\left(
 S_0,
 \frac{\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)}
      {\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)^{\operatorname{ad}(Y_0)}}
 \otimes_{\Gamma(S_0,\mathscr O_{S_0})}\Gamma(S_0,\mathscr J)
 \right).
\]

Indeed, by I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:proposition:4-7-3}{4.7.3} (cf. \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:corollary:2-13}{2.13}), write
\[
 \operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)
 =\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)^{\operatorname{ad}(Y_0)}
  \oplus\mathscr R.
\]
Since \(Y_0\) is commutative,
\[
 \operatorname{Lie}(Y_0/S_0)
 \subset\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)^{\operatorname{ad}(Y_0)},
\]
and hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathscr F_0={}&
 \left(
  \frac{\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)^{\operatorname{ad}(Y_0)}}
       {\operatorname{Lie}(Y_0/S_0)}
 \right)\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr J
 \ \oplus\ \mathscr R\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr J,\\
 \mathscr F_0^{\operatorname{ad}(Y_0)}={}&
 \left(
  \frac{\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)^{\operatorname{ad}(Y_0)}}
       {\operatorname{Lie}(Y_0/S_0)}
 \right)\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr J.
 \end{aligned}
\]
By \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:corollary:4-32}{4.32}, therefore,
\(E\simeq
\Gamma(S_0,\mathscr R\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr J)\).
Returning to the definition of \(\mathscr R\) completes the proof.

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:4-34}\textbf{Corollary 4.34.}
\textit{Let \(S,\mathscr I,\mathscr J\) and \(X\) be as in \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:theorem:4-21}{4.21}. Suppose
\(S\) affine. Let \(Y\) be a diagonalizable subgroup of \(X\), with
\(\omega^1_{Y_0/S_0}\) finite locally free.}\footnote{Translator's note: as in
4.33, the hypothesis on \(\omega^1_{Y_0/S_0}\) is added by the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, which supposes \(S_0\) affine.} \textit{If
\(x\in X(S)\) induces the unit section of \(X_{\mathscr J}\) and
normalizes \(Y\), then it centralizes \(Y\).}

This follows immediately by comparing the preceding corollary with
\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:corollary:2-14}{2.14}. Indeed, \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:corollary:4-33}{4.33} shows that the elements of \(C^0(Y_0,L_0^X)\)
which globally preserve \(Y\) are the elements of
\(H^0(Y_0,L_0^X)\), and \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:corollary:2-14}{2.14} shows that these are precisely the
elements which act trivially on the canonical immersion
\(Y\longrightarrow X\).

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-35}\textbf{4.35. ---}
Let us return to the general situation of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:theorem:4-21}{4.21} and suppose
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\) smooth over \(S_{\mathscr J}\). Then (SGA~1, II, 4.10)
every subscheme \(Y\) of \(X\) lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) and flat will be
smooth over \(S\). Moreover, by \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:4-25:item:ii}{4.25 (ii)}, one has an isomorphism of
\(Y_0\)-modules
\[
 \mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}^{\vee}
 =\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)/\operatorname{Lie}(Y_0/S_0).
\]

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III; our
formulation). The re-edition states here a fact implicit in 4.38, (5).
Suppose moreover \(S_0\) and \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) affine. As
\(\mathbf n^\vee_{Y_0/X_0}\) is locally free, the exact sequence \(0\to
\operatorname{Lie}(Y_0/S_0)\to\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)\to\mathbf n^\vee_{Y_0/X_0}
\to0\) stays exact after tensoring with \(\mathscr J\) and pulling back to the
affine schemes \(Y_0^n\); since \(L_0^Y\), \(L_0^X\) and \(N_0\) are the functors
\(\mathbf W\) of these three modules tensored with \(\mathscr J\) (0.6 and 4.22),
one gets an exact sequence of complexes \(0\to C^*(Y_0,L_0^Y)\to C^*(Y_0,L_0^X)
\xrightarrow{d^*}C^*(Y_0,N_0)\to0\), hence a connecting map
\(\partial^1\colon H^1(Y_0,N_0)\to H^2(Y_0,L_0^Y)\). Let \(Y\) and \(Y'\) be
subgroups of \(X\) lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\), flat, hence smooth, over \(S\). By
0.15 there is an isomorphism of \(S\)-schemes \(f\colon Y\to Y'\) inducing the
identity on \(Y_{\mathscr J}\); let \(\mu_1(x,y)=f^{-1}(f(x)f(y))\) be the group
law of \(Y'\) transported to \(Y\), and \(\mu_Y\) the law of \(Y\). Then
\(\partial^1\) sends the class of \(d(Y,Y')\in Z^1(Y_0,N_0)\) to the class of
\(\delta(\mu_Y,\mu_1)\in Z^2(Y_0,L_0^Y)\) (notation of the Addition after
3.5). Indeed, by 1.2.4, \(f(y)=a(y_0)y\) with \(a\in C^1(Y_0,L_0^X)\), and
\(d^1(a)=d(Y,Y')\) by the Addition after 4.27, applied to the immersion
\(Y\xrightarrow{f}Y'\to X\); so \(\partial^1\) sends the class of \(d(Y,Y')\)
to that of \(\partial a\). On the other hand, writing
\(\mu_1(x,y)=b(x_0,y_0)xy\) with \(b=\delta(\mu_Y,\mu_1)\) and applying \(f\),
one finds \(a(x_0y_0)\,b(x_0,y_0)\,xy=f(x)f(y)=a(x_0)\operatorname{Ad}(x_0)
\bigl(a(y_0)\bigr)xy\), whence \(b(x_0,y_0)=\operatorname{Ad}(x_0)\bigl(a(y_0)
\bigr)-a(x_0y_0)+a(x_0)=(\partial a)(x_0,y_0)\).
