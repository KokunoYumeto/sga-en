

Let us now carry out a number of transformations. Since
\(\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_{\mathscr J}}}\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\) is
annihilated by \(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\),\footnote{Translator's note: the
1970 text gives as the reason that \(\mathscr J\) is of square zero. The
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, gives the reason above: a
homomorphism \(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\to\mathscr J\otimes
\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\) vanishes on \(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}^2\)
because its target is annihilated by \(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\).} one
may also write
\[
 \mathscr A_{\mathscr J}
 =\mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_{X_{\mathscr J}}}
  \bigl(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}},
  \mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_{\mathscr J}}}\mathscr J\bigr)
 =\mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_{X_{\mathscr J}}}
  \bigl(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}/\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}^2,
  \mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_{\mathscr J}}}\mathscr J\bigr).
\]
Since \(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}/\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}^2\) is annihilated
by \(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\), one may regard it as a sheaf on
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\), which one denotes \(\mathscr N_{Y_{\mathscr J}/X_{\mathscr J}}\),
the \emph{conormal sheaf} to \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) in \(X_{\mathscr J}\). Putting
\[
 \mathscr A'=\mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}}
 \bigl(\mathscr N_{Y_{\mathscr J}/X_{\mathscr J}},
 \mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_{\mathscr J}}}\mathscr J\bigr),
\]
one will have \(H^i(X_{\mathscr J},\mathscr A_{\mathscr J})
=H^i(Y_{\mathscr J},\mathscr A')\), \(i\geq0\).

Introducing now two ideals \(\mathscr I\) and \(\mathscr J\), reasoning as in
0.2, and suppressing in the usual way the hypothesis ``\(Y\) closed'', one
obtains\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, explains these two steps. In our words: an
open subset \(U_{\mathscr J}\) of \(X_{\mathscr J}\) in which \(Y_{\mathscr J}\)
is closed comes from the open subset \(U\) of \(X\) with the same underlying
space, and a lifting \(Y\) of \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) has the same underlying space
as \(Y_{\mathscr J}\), hence is closed in \(U\); so one may replace \(X\) by
\(U\). And when \(\mathscr I\supset\mathscr J\), \(\mathscr I\mathscr J=0\),
the module \(\mathscr J\) is annihilated by \(\mathscr I\), so that
\(\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_{\mathscr J}}}\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}
=\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{Y_0}\) and
\(\mathscr A'=\mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_{Y_0}}(\mathscr N_{Y_{\mathscr J}/
X_{\mathscr J}}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}}\mathscr O_{Y_0},
\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{Y_0})\), which is the
\(\mathscr A_0\) below.}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:4-5}\textbf{Proposition 4.5.}
\textit{Let \(S\) be a scheme, and let \(S_0\) and
\(S_{\mathscr J}\) be the closed subschemes defined by quasi-coherent
ideals \(\mathscr I\) and \(\mathscr J\), where
\(\mathscr I\supset\mathscr J\) and
\(\mathscr I\mathscr J=0\). Let \(X\) be an \(S\)-scheme and let
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\) be a subscheme of \(X_{\mathscr J}\), flat over
\(S_{\mathscr J}\). Define the \(\mathscr O_{Y_0}\)-module
\[
 \mathscr A_0=
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{Y_0}}
 \left(
  \mathscr N_{Y_{\mathscr J}/X_{\mathscr J}}
   \otimes_{\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}}\mathscr O_{Y_0},
  \mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{Y_0}
 \right).
\]
Then:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:4-5:item:i}(i)] \textit{A subscheme \(Y\) of \(X\), reducing to
  \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) and flat over \(S\), exists if and only if the
  following two conditions hold:}
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:4-5:item:a}(a)] \textit{Such a \(Y\) exists locally on \(X\).}
  \item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:4-5:item:b}(b)] \textit{A certain obstruction in
    \(R^1\Gamma(Y_0,\mathscr A_0)\) vanishes.}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, explains
    this notation: \(R^1\Gamma(Y_0,\mathscr A_0)\) is the cohomology group \(H^1\)
    of the sheaf \(\mathscr A_0\) on \(Y_0\), written so to distinguish it from the
    Hochschild cohomology groups \(H^i(Y_0,M_0)\) of an \(S_0\)-group \(Y_0\), used
    from 4.16 on.}
  \end{enumerate}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:4-5:item:ii}(ii)] \textit{Under these conditions, the set of subschemes \(Y\)
  satisfying the stated requirements is principal homogeneous under the
  abelian group \(\Gamma(Y_0,\mathscr A_0)\).}
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:remark:4-6}\textbf{Remark 4.6.}
If \(X\) is flat over \(S\) and \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) is locally
\emph{complete intersection} in \(X_{\mathscr J}\), then condition \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-5:item:a}{(a)} is
always satisfied, and every \(Y\) flat over \(S\) lifting
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\) is then locally complete intersection in \(X\). If,
moreover, \(Y_0\) is \emph{affine}, condition \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-5:item:b}{(b)} is also satisfied.


\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III; our
formulation). The re-edition makes ``locally complete intersection'' precise
and proves the assertions of 4.6. It takes the definition of SGA~6, VII, 1.4
(rather than that of EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 16.9.2): a subscheme \(Y\) of
\(X\), closed in an open subset \(U\), is locally complete intersection in
\(X\) if every point of \(Y\) has an affine open neighbourhood \(V\) in \(U\) on
which \(\mathscr I_Y\) is the image of a \emph{regular} morphism
\(f\colon\mathscr O_V^d\to\mathscr O_V\), that is, one whose Koszul complex
\(K_\bullet(f)=(\bigwedge^\bullet\mathscr O_V^d,\partial)\) is a resolution of
\(\mathscr O_{Y\cap V}\); then \(\mathscr N_{Y/X}\) is locally free of finite
rank (SGA~6, VII, 1.2).

(a) Let \(A\) be a ring, \(J\) an ideal of square zero, \(\bar A=A/J\), \(B\) a
flat \(A\)-algebra, and \(f\colon E\to B\) a \(B\)-linear map from a free
\(B\)-module of finite rank, whose reduction \(\bar f\colon\bar E\to\bar B\)
modulo \(J\) is regular with \(\bar C=\bar B/\bar f(\bar E)\) flat over \(\bar A\).
Then \(f\) is regular and \(C=B/f(E)\) is flat over \(A\). Indeed, \(K_\bullet(f)\)
consists of flat \(A\)-modules and \(K_\bullet(f)\otimes_A\bar A=K_\bullet(\bar f)\),
so there is an exact sequence of complexes \(0\to J\otimes_{\bar A}K_\bullet(\bar f)
\to K_\bullet(f)\to K_\bullet(\bar f)\to0\); since \(K_\bullet(\bar f)\) is a
resolution of \(\bar C\) by flat \(\bar A\)-modules and \(\bar C\) is flat, the first
complex has homology \(J\otimes_{\bar A}\bar C\) in degree \(0\) only. The homology
sequence shows that \(K_\bullet(f)\) is acyclic in positive degrees and that
\(0\to J\otimes_{\bar A}\bar C\to C\to\bar C\to0\) is exact, so \(C\) is flat
over \(A\) by the local flatness criterion.

(b) If \(J\) is a nilpotent ideal of a ring \(A\) and \(N\subset M\) are
\(A\)-modules with \(M/N\) flat, elements of \(N\) whose images generate the
image of \(N\) in \(M/JM\) generate \(N\): that image is \(N/JN\), by flatness
of \(M/N\), so the submodule \(N'\) they generate satisfies \(N=N'+JN\), hence
\(N=N'+J^kN=N'\) for large \(k\).

(c) Let \(X\) be flat over \(S\) and \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) locally complete
intersection in \(X_{\mathscr J}\). Locally, lift the components of a regular
\(\bar f\) defining \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) to sections of \(\mathscr O_X\); by (a)
(with \(\mathscr J\) for \(J\)) they define a regular \(f\) whose cokernel is
flat over \(S\), that is, a local solution: condition (a) of 4.5 holds. If \(Y\)
is any lifting flat over \(S\), then \(\mathscr I_Y\) is locally generated, by
(b), by liftings of generators of \(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\), hence is the
image of such an \(f\), which is regular by (a): \(Y\) is locally complete
intersection in \(X\). Finally \(\mathscr N_{Y_{\mathscr J}/X_{\mathscr J}}\) is
locally free of finite rank, so \(\mathscr A_0\) is quasi-coherent and
\(R^1\Gamma(Y_0,\mathscr A_0)=0\) when \(Y_0\) is affine: condition (b) holds.

(d) A regular sequence generating \(\mathscr I_{Y_{\mathscr J}}\) need not lift to a
regular sequence. Let \(k\) be a field, \(\bar A=k[x,y]\), and
\(A=\bar A\oplus k\varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon^2=x\varepsilon=y\varepsilon=0\),
so that \(J=k\varepsilon\) and \(A/J=\bar A\); let \(B=A[z,t]\), flat over \(A\).
In \(\bar B=k[x,y,z,t]\), \(xz-yt\) is irreducible, so \((xz-yt,xz-1)\) is a
regular sequence; it generates \((xz-1,yt-1)\), and
\(\bar B/(xz-1,yt-1)=k[x,y,x^{-1},y^{-1}]\) is flat over \(\bar A\). But every
lifting \(xz-yt+\lambda\varepsilon\) (\(\lambda\in k[z,t]\)) of \(xz-yt\)
annihilates \(\varepsilon\), so no lifting of this sequence is a regular sequence
in \(B\); by (a), the Koszul complex of a lifting is nevertheless acyclic in
positive degrees. This is why regularity is defined through Koszul complexes.

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-7}\textbf{Remark 4.7.}
It follows from \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-5:item:ii}{4.5 (ii)} that for every pair \((Y,Y')\) of
subschemes\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has ``closed
subschemes''; the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, corrects this to
``subschemes'', 4.5 having been stated without the hypothesis that the
subschemes be closed.} of \(X\), flat over \(S\) and reducing to
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\), one has a \emph{deviation}
\[
 d(Y,Y')\in H^0(Y_0,\mathscr A_0)
 =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{Y_0}}
 \bigl(\mathscr N_{Y_{\mathscr J}/X_{\mathscr J}}
  \otimes_{\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}}\mathscr O_{Y_0},
  \mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{Y_0}\bigr).
\]
