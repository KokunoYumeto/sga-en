\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:4-36}\textbf{Proposition 4.36.}
\textit{Under the hypotheses of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:theorem:4-21}{4.21}, suppose in addition that
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\) is smooth over \(S_{\mathscr J}\) and that \(S_0\)
is affine. The set of sub-\(S\)-groups \(Y\) of \(X\), flat (or smooth)
over \(S\), reducing to \(Y_{\mathscr J}\), modulo conjugation by
sections of \(X\) over \(S\) inducing the unit section of
\(X_{\mathscr J}\), is either empty or a principal homogeneous set under
the group}
\[
 H^1\!\left(
 Y_0,
 \bigl[\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)/\operatorname{Lie}(Y_0/S_0)\bigr]
 \otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr J
 \right).
\]

It is enough to verify that \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:corollary:4-29}{Corollary 4.29} applies, that is, that
\[
 d^0:
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
   (\omega^1_{X_0/S_0},\mathscr J)
 \longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
   (\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0},\mathscr J)
\]
is surjective. This follows from the fact that the sequence \((+)\) of
\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:4-25:item:ii}{4.25(ii)} is split, since \(S_0\) is affine.

Let us finally state a corollary common to \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:theorem:4-21}{4.21} and \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-36}{4.36}. In fact, this
will be the only form in which we shall subsequently use the general
results of this section.

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:4-37}\textbf{Corollary 4.37.}
\textit{Let \(S\) be a scheme and let \(S_0\) be the closed subscheme
defined by a nilpotent ideal \(\mathscr I\). Let \(X\) be an \(S\)-group
smooth over \(S\), and let \(Y_0\) be a sub-\(S_0\)-group of \(X_0\),
flat over \(S_0\).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:4-37:item:i}(i)] \textit{If \(S_0\) is affine, \(Y_0\) is smooth over \(S_0\),
and if}
\[
 H^1\!\left(
 Y_0,
 \bigl[\operatorname{Lie}(X_0/S_0)/\operatorname{Lie}(Y_0/S_0)\bigr]
 \otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
 \mathscr I^{n+1}/\mathscr I^{n+2}
 \right)=0
\]
\textit{for every \(n\geq 0\), then any two sub-\(S\)-groups of \(X\),
flat (or smooth) over \(S\) and reducing to \(Y_0\), are conjugate by a
section of \(X\) over \(S\) inducing the unit section of \(X_0\).}

\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:4-37:item:ii}(ii)] \textit{If \(Y_0\) is affine and of finite presentation,
and if}
\[
 H^2\!\left(
 Y_0,
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
 \bigl(\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0},
 \mathscr I^{n+1}/\mathscr I^{n+2}\bigr)
 \right)=0
\]
\textit{for every \(n\geq 0\), then there exists a sub-\(S\)-group of
\(X\), flat over \(S\), reducing to \(Y_0\).}
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-38}\textbf{4.38.}
It remains for us to relate the three constructions that we have made in
this Expos\'e. To avoid inessential complications, we shall place
ourselves in the following situation: \(S_0\) is the spectrum of a field
\(k\), \(S\) is the spectrum of the dual numbers \(D(k)\), \(G\) is an
\(S\)-group \emph{smooth} over \(S\), and \(K\) a sub-\(S\)-group, affine and
\emph{smooth} over \(S\). Put \(\mathfrak g_0=\operatorname{Lie}G_0\) and
\(\mathfrak k_0=\operatorname{Lie}K_0\). One has an exact sequence of
\(k\)-vector spaces\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, specifies: with the
adjoint action of \(K_0\).}
\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathfrak k_0
 \xrightarrow{\ i\ }\mathfrak g_0
 \xrightarrow{\ d\ }\mathbf n^{\vee}_{K_0/G_0}
 \longrightarrow 0,
\]
giving rise to an exact cohomology sequence (where we write \(H^i(\ )\) for
\(H^i(K_0,\ )\)):
\[
\begin{aligned}
0&\longrightarrow H^0(\mathfrak k_0)
 \xrightarrow{\ i^0\ }H^0(\mathfrak g_0)
 \xrightarrow{\ d^0\ }H^0(\mathfrak g_0/\mathfrak k_0)\\
&\xrightarrow{\ \partial^0\ }H^1(\mathfrak k_0)
 \xrightarrow{\ i^1\ }H^1(\mathfrak g_0)
 \xrightarrow{\ d^1\ }H^1(\mathbf n^{\vee}_{K_0/G_0})
 \xrightarrow{\ \partial^1\ }H^2(\mathfrak k_0).
\end{aligned}
\]

Now these various groups all have a geometric meaning.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[a)]
\(H^0(\mathfrak k_0)
=\operatorname{Lie}\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}(K)_0\)\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, writes
\(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}(K_0)\),
\(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G_0}(K_0)\) and
\(\underline{\operatorname{Norm}}_{G_0}(K_0)\) for
\(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}(K)_0\),
\(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_G(K)_0\) and
\(\underline{\operatorname{Norm}}_G(K)_0\), the formation of centralizers and
normalizers commuting with base change (Exposé~I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:scholium:2-3-3-1}{2.3.3.1}).}
(Exp.~II, \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:theorem:5-2-3}{5.2.3}).

\item[b)]
\(H^0(\mathfrak g_0)
=\operatorname{Lie}\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_G(K)_0\)
(idem).

\item[c)]
\(H^0(\mathfrak g_0/\mathfrak k_0)
=\operatorname{Lie}\underline{\operatorname{Norm}}_G(K)_0/\mathfrak k_0\)
(idem).

\item[d)]
\(H^1(\mathfrak k_0)
=\operatorname{Lie}\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{S\text{-gr.}}(K)_0
/\operatorname{Im}(\mathfrak k_0)\),
where \(\operatorname{Im}(\mathfrak k_0)\) denotes the image of
\(\mathfrak k_0\) under \(\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{Int})_0\),
deduced from
\(\operatorname{Int}:K_0\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{S\text{-gr.}}(K)\);
this follows at once from \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:theorem:2-1}{2.1} and II, 4.2.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III,
details: by 2.1 (ii), applied with \(Y=X=K\) and \(f_0=\operatorname{id}_{K_0}\),
\(Z^1(K_0,\mathfrak k_0)\) is the group of infinitesimal automorphisms of the
\(S_0\)-group \(K_0\), and \(H^1(K_0,\mathfrak k_0)\) its quotient by the inner
ones, the image of \(\mathfrak k_0\); and
\(Z^1(K_0,\mathfrak k_0)=\operatorname{Lie}\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{S_0\text{-gr.}}
(K_0)\) by II, 4.2.2.}

\item[e)]
\(H^1(\mathfrak g_0)\) = group of deviations between homomorphisms
\(K\to G\) extending the canonical immersion \(i_0:K_0\to G_0\), modulo
the deviations obtained through the action of the inner automorphisms of
\(G\) defined by elements of \(G(S)\) giving the unit of \(G(S_0)\) (that
is, elements of \(\mathfrak g_0\)) (cf. \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:theorem:2-1}{2.1} and \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:1-2-2}{1.2.2}).

\item[f)]
\(H^1(\mathbf n^{\vee}_{K_0/G_0})\) = group of deviations between subgroups
\(K'\) of \(G\), flat over \(S\), extending \(K_0\), modulo the deviations
obtained through the action of the inner automorphisms of \(G\)
constructed as above (cf. the proof of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:theorem:4-21}{4.21}).

\item[g)]
\(H^2(\mathfrak k_0)\) = group of deviations between group structures on
\(K\) extending that of \(K_0\), modulo the \(S\)-automorphisms of \(K\)
inducing the identity on \(K_0\) (cf. \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:theorem:3-5}{3.5}).
\end{enumerate}

We now propose to show how the six morphisms of the preceding exact
sequence can be made explicit in the geometric interpretation that we
have just given.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[1)]
\(i^0\) and \(d^0\) are none other than the morphisms obtained by passing
to the Lie algebra (and then to the quotient, for \(d^0\)) from the
canonical monomorphisms
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{Centr}}(K)
 \longrightarrow
 \underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_G(K)
 \longrightarrow
 \underline{\operatorname{Norm}}_G(K).
\]
This indeed follows immediately from the definition of the
identifications a), b) and c).

\item[2)]
One constructs \(\partial^0\) as follows. Let
\(\bar x\in\operatorname{Lie}\underline{\operatorname{Norm}}_G(K)_0
/\mathfrak k_0\). Lift it to an
\(x\in\operatorname{Lie}\underline{\operatorname{Norm}}_G(K)_0
\subset\underline{\operatorname{Norm}}_G(K)\). Then
\(\operatorname{Int}(x)\) defines an automorphism of \(K\) inducing the
identity on \(K_0\), hence an element of
\(\operatorname{Lie}\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{S\text{-gr.}}(K)_0\).
Denote by \(\overline{\operatorname{Int}(x)}\) the image of this element in
\(\operatorname{Lie}\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{S\text{-gr.}}(K)_0
/\operatorname{Im}(\mathfrak k_0)\). Then one has
\[
 \partial^0(\bar x)
 =-\overline{\operatorname{Int}(x)}
 =\overline{\operatorname{Int}(x^{-1})}.
\]
Indeed, let us compute the element of
\(\operatorname{Lie}\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{S\text{-gr.}}(K)_0\)
defined by \(\operatorname{Int}(x)\). It corresponds by definition to an
element \(a\) of \(Z^1(K_0,\mathfrak k_0)\) such that
\[
 xyx^{-1}=a(y_0)y,
 \qquad y\in K(S'),\quad S'\longrightarrow S.
\]
But this can also be written
\(a(y_0)=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}=x-\operatorname{Ad}(y)x=-\partial(x)(y_0)\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, completes the
argument: \(\partial x\in C^1(K_0,\mathfrak g_0)\) takes its values in
\(\mathfrak k_0\) and is a cocycle; its class in \(H^1(K_0,\mathfrak k_0)\)
depends only on \(\bar x\) and is \(\partial^0(\bar x)\), by the definition
of the connecting homomorphism; with \(a=-\partial(x)\) this gives the
formula.}

\item[3)]
Let \(\bar u\) be an element of \(H^1(\mathfrak k_0)\), the canonical
image of an element
\(u\in\operatorname{Lie}\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{S\text{-gr.}}(K)_0
\subset\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{S\text{-gr.}}(K)\).
Then, if \(i:K\to G\) is the canonical immersion, one has
\[
 i^1(\bar u)=\bar d(i,i\circ u).
\]
Indeed, \(\bar d(i,i\circ u)\) is defined as the image of an element
\(d\in Z^1(K_0,\mathfrak g_0)\) such that \(iu(y)=d(y_0)i(y)\). Now
\(\bar u\) is defined as the image of an element
\(v\in Z^1(K_0,\mathfrak k_0)\) such that \(u(y)=v(y_0)y\). One can
therefore choose \(d(y_0)=iv(y_0)\), which proves the announced relation.

\item[4)]\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-38:item:4}
Let \(\bar d(i,i')\) be the image of the deviation \(d(i,i')\) in
\(H^1(\mathfrak g_0)\), where \(i':K\to G\) is a group homomorphism
lifting \(i_0\). Let \(\bar d(K,i'(K))\) be the image of the deviation
\(d(K,i'(K))\) in \(H^1(\mathbf n^{\vee}_{K_0/G_0})\).\footnote{Translator's note:
the 1970 text has \(\mathbf n\) here, without the dual, a misprint (also in
the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, which deduces the formula from
the general form of 4.27 given in the Addition after 4.27, \(d(K,i'(K))\)
being a cocycle by 4.21).} Then
\[
 d^1\bigl(\bar d(i,i')\bigr)=\bar d\bigl(K,i'(K)\bigr).
\]
This follows without difficulty from \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-8:item:ii}{4.8 (ii)} and (iii), reasoning as in the
proof of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:4-27}{4.27}.

\item[5)]\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-38:item:5}
Let finally \(K'\) be a subgroup of \(G\), flat over \(S\) and lifting
\(K_0\). We have assumed that \(K_0\) is \emph{affine}. Then we know that
\(K\) and \(K'\) are isomorphic as schemes extending \(K_0\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:remark:3-6}{3.6}), hence that there is an isomorphism of \(S\)-schemes
\[
 f:K\longrightarrow K'
\]
inducing the identity on \(K_0\). Transport by \(f\) the group structure
of \(K'\), and let \(K_1\) be the group obtained (which thus has \(K\) as
underlying scheme). Then
\[
 \partial^1\bar d(K,K')=\bar d(K,K_1).
\]
Indeed, \(\bar d(K,K_1)\) is defined as the image of a
\(b\in Z^2(K_0,\mathfrak k_0)\) such that
\(f^{-1}(f(y)f(y'))=b(y_0,y'_0)\,yy'\). On the other hand,
\(\bar d(K,K')\) is defined as the image in
\(H^1(K_0,\mathfrak g_0/\mathfrak k_0)\) of an
\(a\in C^1(K_0,\mathfrak g_0)\)\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has
\(a\in Z^1\); \(a\) is only a cochain, whose coboundary is \(b\). The
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, writes \(C^1\), and rewrites 5)
using its Proposition 4.35.1 (see the Addition after 4.35).} such that \(f(y)=a(y_0)y\). Computing \(b\),
one finds at once
\[
 b(y_0,y'_0)=a(y_0y'_0)^{-1}a(y_0)\,y\,a(y'_0)\,y'\,(yy')^{-1}
 =\partial a(y_0,y'_0).
\]
\end{enumerate}
