

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:remark:0-3}\textbf{Remark 0.3.}
Suppose that the \(X^+\)-scheme \(Y\) is flat over \(S\) (SGA~1,
IV). We may then write
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(Y,L_X)
 =
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{Y_0}}
 \bigl(g_0^*(\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0}),
       \mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{Y_0}\bigr).
\]

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:remark:0-4}\textbf{Remark 0.4.}
Suppose that \(\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\) is the inverse image on \(X_0\) of
an \(\mathscr O_{S_0}\)-module denoted \(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\) (this will be
the case in particular when \(X_0\) is an \(S_0\)-group; cf.
\SGARefLink{sga3:ii:subsection:4-11}{II~4.11}). If one defines a functor \(L'_X\) over \(S\) by the
formula
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,L'_X)
 =
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{Y_0}}
 \bigl(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{Y_0},
       \mathscr J\mathscr O_Y\bigr),
\]
then
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(Y,L_X)=\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,L'_X)
\]
for every \(X^+\)-scheme \(Y\); in other words,
\[
 L_X=L'_X\mathbin{\times_S}X^+.
\]

The morphism of \(X^+\)-group functors
\(L_X\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{X^+}(X)\) translates into a morphism
of \(S\)-group functors \(L'_X\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_S(X)\), the
actions of \(L'_X\) on \(X\) respecting the morphism \(p_X:X\to X^+\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, gives
the reason. In our words: since \(L_X\times_{X^+}X=L'_X\times_SX\), the action
of \(L_X\) on \(X\) over \(X^+\) is an action of \(L'_X\) on \(X\); and for
\(h\in X(Y)\) and \(m\in L'_X(Y)\), the points \(m\cdot h\) and \(h\) have the
same restriction to \(Y_{\mathscr J}\), the action being by liftings of a
fixed morphism \(Y_{\mathscr J}\to X\), so that \(p_X(m\cdot h)=p_X(h)\).}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:remark:0-5}\textbf{Remark 0.5.}
Under the preceding conditions, suppose moreover that
\(Y\) is an \(S\)-scheme flat over \(S\). Then
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(Y,L_X)
 =\operatorname{Hom}_S(Y,L'_X)
 =\operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(Y_0,L_{0X}),
\]
where the \(S_0\)-functor in abelian groups \(L_{0X}\) is defined by
the following identity (with \(T\) the variable \(S_0\)-scheme):
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(T,L_{0X})
 =
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_T}
 \bigl(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_T,
       \mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_T\bigr).
\]
Thus, in the notation of II~1, we have shown that \(L'_X\) and
\(\prod_{S_0/S}L_{0X}\) have the same restriction to the full
subcategory of \((\mathbf{Sch})/S\) whose objects are the \(S\)-schemes
flat over \(S\).

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:remark:0-6}\textbf{Remark 0.6.}
Suppose now that \(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\) admits a finite presentation
(EGA~0\textsubscript{I}, 5.2.5), which will be the case in particular if
\(X_0\) is \emph{locally of finite presentation} over \(S_0\) and the
structure morphism \(X_0\to S_0\) has a section.\footnote{Translator's note:
the 1970 text asks only that \(X_0\) be locally of finite presentation over
\(S_0\). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, adds a section
\(\varepsilon_0\) of \(X_0\to S_0\), as for groups: then
\(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\simeq\varepsilon_0^*(\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0})\), which is of
finite presentation by EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 16.4.22. Some such hypothesis
is needed, since \(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\) is not determined by
\(\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\) in general: if \(X_0\) is empty, every
\(\mathscr O_{S_0}\)-module has inverse image \(0=\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\).} One
may write
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(T,L_{0X})
 =
 \Gamma\!\left(
   T,
   \mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_T}
   \bigl(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\otimes\mathscr O_T,
         \mathscr J\otimes\mathscr O_T\bigr)
 \right)
\]
(the tensor products being taken over \(\mathscr O_{S_0}\)). If \(T\) is
\emph{flat} over \(S_0\), it follows from EGA~0\textsubscript{I}, 6.7.6 that
this may also be written
\[
 \Gamma\!\left(
   T,
   \mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
   \bigl(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0},\mathscr J\bigr)
   \otimes\mathscr O_T
 \right).
\]
Using the notation \(\mathbf W(\,\cdot\,)\) of \SGARefLink{sga3:i:definition:4-6-1}{I~4.6.1}, we have
therefore proved that, for every \(S_0\)-scheme \(T\) flat over
\(S_0\),
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(T,L_{0X})
 =
 \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}
 \left(T,
 \mathbf W\!\left(
   \mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
   (\omega^1_{X_0/S_0},\mathscr J)
 \right)\right).
\]

In summary, if \(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\) admits a finite presentation
and one restricts to the category of \(S\)-schemes flat over \(S\),
then
\[
 L'_X
 =
 \prod_{S_0/S}
 \mathbf W\!\left(
   \mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
   (\omega^1_{X_0/S_0},\mathscr J)
 \right)
\]
(the sheaf of which one takes the \(\mathbf W\) being quasi-coherent by
EGA~I, 9.1.1). Note finally that if, moreover, \(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\) is locally free
(of finite rank), for example if \(X_0\) is \emph{smooth} over \(S_0\) (in
which case it is automatically locally of finite presentation over
\(S_0\)), then
\[
 \mathscr{H}om_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
   (\omega^1_{X_0/S_0},\mathscr J)
 \simeq
 \underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(X_0/S_0)
 \otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr J,
\]
where, by an abuse of language (since \(X_0\) need not be an
\(S_0\)-group),
\(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(X_0/S_0)\) denotes the dual of the
\(\mathscr O_{S_0}\)-module \(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III,
explains this name. In our words: by \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:proposition:3-3}{Exposé~II, 3.3}, the
tangent space of \(X_0\) over \(S_0\) along the section \(\varepsilon_0\) is
the vector fibration
\(\mathbf V(\varepsilon_0^*\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0})=\mathbf V(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0})\),
whose module of sections over \(S_0\) is the dual of
\(\omega^1_{X_0/S_0}\); when \(X_0\) is an \(S_0\)-group and
\(\varepsilon_0\) its unit section, this is
\(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(X_0/S_0)\).}

The preceding proposition has an important corollary:

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:corollary:0-7}\textbf{Corollary 0.7.}
\textit{Let \(X\) be an \(S\)-scheme. Every \(S\)-endomorphism of \(X\)
inducing the identity on \(X_{\mathscr J}\) is an automorphism. There is an
exact sequence of groups}
\[
 0\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{X_0}}
   (\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0},\mathscr J\mathscr O_X)
 \xrightarrow{\ i\ }
 \operatorname{Aut}_S(X)
 \longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Aut}_{S_{\mathscr J}}(X_{\mathscr J}).
\]
\textit{Moreover, if one lets \(\operatorname{Aut}_S(X)\) act on the first
group by transport of structure, one has the following relation}
\[
 i(um)=u\,i(m)\,u^{-1}
\]
\textit{for all \(u\in\operatorname{Aut}_S(X)\) and
\(m\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{X_0}}
(\Omega^1_{X_0/S_0},\mathscr J\mathscr O_X)\).}

\noindent\textit{Proof.}
By \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:0-2:item:i}{(i)}, \(\operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(X,X)\) is a principal
homogeneous set under \(\operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(X,L_X)\), since it is
certainly nonempty: indeed it contains a marked point, the identity
automorphism of \(X\). Using this marked point, one defines an isomorphism
of sets
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(X,L_X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(X,X).
\]
Applying now \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:0-2:item:ii}{(ii)} to an endomorphism of \(X\) over \(X^+\), that is,
an \(S\)-endomorphism of \(X\) inducing the identity on \(X_{\mathscr J}\),
one sees easily that the preceding map respects the composition laws of the
two sides.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, writes this out. In our words: put
\(\varphi_m=m\cdot\operatorname{id}_X\); for \(f=\varphi_{m'}\), (ii) gives
\(f\circ\varphi_m=L_f(m)\cdot f\), and \(L_f(m)=m\) because \(f\) induces
the identity on \(X_0\) and \(L_f\) depends only on \(f_0\) (Addition after
0.2, part (d)); hence
\(\varphi_{m'}\circ\varphi_m=m\cdot(m'\cdot\operatorname{id}_X)
=\varphi_{m+m'}\).} It follows first that every element of
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(X,X)\) is invertible, which is the first assertion
of the statement, and then that there is an exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(X,L_X)
 \xrightarrow{\ i\ }\operatorname{Aut}_S(X)
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{S_{\mathscr J}}(X_{\mathscr J}),
\]
which is the second.

Note now that the morphism \(i\) defined above is functorial in \(X\)
for isomorphisms, because it is defined in structural terms from the action
of \(L_X\) on \(X\) over \(X^+\), itself functorial in \(X\) by assertion
\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:0-2:item:ii}{(ii)} of Proposition~0.2. Let then \(u\) be an automorphism of \(X\)
over \(S\). It defines a map
\[
 h:\operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(X,L_X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{X^+}(X,L_X)
\]
by transport of structure, and a map
\[
 f:\operatorname{Aut}_S(X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_S(X),
\]
also by transport of structure, that is, by the commutative diagram

\SGAThreeNativeDiagram[0.40\textwidth]
  {native_diagrams/exp3/diagram_023_exp3_p016_cor07_conjugation_square.tex}
  {The automorphism \(f\) acts by conjugation with \(u\).}
  {fig:exp3-cor07-conjugation}
  {LNM 151, pp.~93--94}

Writing \(i(h(m))=f(i(m))\), one finds the desired formula.
