\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:lemma:4-17}\textbf{Lemma 4.17.}
\textit{Under the preceding hypotheses, consider for each \(Y\) lifting
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\) the obstruction}
\[
 DY\in
 \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}
 \bigl(Y_0\mathbin{\times_{S_0}}Y_0,N_0\bigr)
 =C^2(Y_0,N_0)
\]
\textit{defined by \(DY=c(X,Y,\mu\circ(i,i))\):}

\begin{center}
  \SGAThreeNativeInput[0.52\textwidth]
    {native_diagrams/exp3/diagram_055_exp3_p062_4161_DY_diagram.tex}
\end{center}

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:lemma:4-17:item:i}(i)] \textit{For \(Y\) to be a sub-\(S\)-group of \(X\), it is
  necessary and sufficient that \(DY=0\).}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:lemma:4-17:item:ii}(ii)] \textit{If \(Y_0\) is made to act on \(N_0\), by
  functoriality, through the inner automorphisms of \(Y_0\), then
  \(DY\in Z^2(Y_0,N_0)\).}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:lemma:4-17:item:iii}(iii)] \textit{If \(Y\) and \(Y'\) are two subschemes of \(X\),
  flat over \(S\), lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) (so that the deviation
  \(d(Y,Y')\in C^1(Y_0,N_0)\) is defined), then}\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has
  \(DY-DY'=\partial d(Y,Y')\). In 4.17 to 4.21 it takes for \(\partial\) the
  opposite of the coboundary of Exposé~I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:5-1}{5.1}: thus 4.19 ends with
  \(-\operatorname{Ad}(x)DY(y,z)+DY(xy,z)-DY(x,yz)+DY(x,y)=\partial DY(x,y,z)\),
  and 4.21 seeks \(d(Y,Y')\) with \(DY=\partial d(Y,Y')\). The Gille--Polo
  re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, changes the signs to agree with I, 5.1,
  writing \(\partial^1\) and \(\partial^2\); we follow it in 4.17 to 4.21.}
  \[
   DY'-DY=\partial^1d(Y,Y').
  \]
\end{enumerate}

We prove these assertions in succession.

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-18}\textbf{4.18. --- Proof of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:4-17:item:i}{4.17(i)}.}
If \(DY=0\), then there is a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
 Y\times_SY\arrow[r]\arrow[d]&Y\arrow[d]\\
 X\times_SX\arrow[r]&X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
so \(Y\) is stable under the group law of \(X\). For it to be a subgroup
of \(X\), it is necessary and sufficient that it be a group for the
induced law; now this law is associative and reduces modulo
\(\mathscr J\) to the law induced on \(Y_{\mathscr J}\), which is a group
law. Since \(Y\) is flat,\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, says ``flat and
locally of finite presentation over \(S\)'', as 3.3 requires; \(Y\) is locally
of finite presentation over \(S\), being locally complete intersection in
\(X\) (4.15), which is smooth over \(S\).} one concludes by \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:3-3}{3.3}.

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-19}\textbf{4.19. --- Proof of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:4-17:item:ii}{4.17(ii)}.}
This is done by comparing the two values of
\(u=c(X,Y,\mu^2\circ(i,i,i))\) computed in the following two diagrams
\((D_j)\), \(j=1,2\):

\begin{center}
 \SGAThreeNativeInput[0.94\textwidth]
   {native_diagrams/exp3/diagram_056_exp3_p063_Dj_associativity.tex}
\end{center}

where \(f_1=(1,\mu)\), \(f_2=(\mu,1)\), and
\(\mu\circ f_j=\mu^2:X\times X\times X\to X\).

One applies \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-13}{4.13} each time.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, details
this step. Lemma 4.12 and Proposition 4.13 are applied to
\(f_j\circ(i,i,i)\colon Y\times_SY\times_SY\to X\times_SX\), with the
subscheme \(Y\times_SY\) of \(X\times_SX\) (flat over \(S\), with \(Y_0\times Y_0\)
locally complete intersection in \(X_0\times X_0\)), and to \(\mu\). As \(X_0\) and
\(Y_0\) are flat over \(S_0\), \(\mathscr N_{Y_0\times Y_0/X_0\times X_0}=
\operatorname{pr}_1^*\mathscr N_{Y_0/X_0}\oplus\operatorname{pr}_2^*
\mathscr N_{Y_0/X_0}\); by 4.16 and the flatness of \(Y\times_SY\) and
\(Y\times_SY\times_SY\), the groups involved become \(\operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}
(Y_0^n,N_0)\) or \(\operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(Y_0^3,N_0\oplus N_0)\), and
\(b_{f_{j0}}\) is the pull-back by \(f_{j0}\) (Addition after 4.12). The
obstruction for \(f_1\circ(i,i,i)\), whose first component factors through
\(Y\), is \((0,DY)\); that for \(f_2\circ(i,i,i)\) is \((DY,0)\).} One therefore has
\[
 u=a_{1,\mu_0}((0,DY))+b_{f_{10}}(DY)
  =a_{2,\mu_0}((DY,0))+b_{f_{20}}(DY).
\]
The first thing one notices is that \(b_{f_{j0}}\) is nothing but
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(f_{j0},N_0)\), that is, the morphism deduced from
\(f_{j0}\) by functoriality. The identity above therefore becomes, writing
\(a_{j,\mu_0}=a_j\),
\begin{multline*}
 a_j:\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{Y_0^3}}
 \bigl(f_{j0}^*(\mathscr N_{Y_0\times Y_0/X_0\times X_0}),
  \mathscr J\otimes\mathscr O_{Y_0^3}\bigr)\\
 \longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{Y_0^3}}
 \bigl((\mu^2_0)^*(\mathscr N_{Y_0/X_0}),
  \mathscr J\otimes\mathscr O_{Y_0^3}\bigr),
\end{multline*}
\[
 a_1((0,DY))-\operatorname{Hom}((\mu,1),N_0)(DY)
 +\operatorname{Hom}((1,\mu),N_0)(DY)-a_2((DY,0))=0.
\]
One recognizes the two middle terms: they are the parts
``\(DY(xy,z)\)'' and ``\(DY(x,yz)\)'' of the formula for the
\(2\)-coboundary. It therefore remains only to identify the other two
terms.

We must first compute the map \(a_j\). It comes, by inverse image under
\(f_{j0}\), from the morphism of \(\mathscr O_{Y_0^2}\)-modules
\[
 P:\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}\otimes\mathscr O_{Y_0^2}
 \longrightarrow
 (\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}\oplus \mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0})
 \otimes\mathscr O_{Y_0^2}
\]
induced by the product in \(Y_0\). This morphism is described as
follows: consider the vector bundle \(\mathbf V(\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0})=V\);
by duality, \(P\) gives a morphism
\[
 \mathbf V(P):
 V\times_{S_0}V\times_{S_0}Y_0\times_{S_0}Y_0
 \longrightarrow V\times_{S_0}Y_0\times_{S_0}Y_0,
\]
which is expressed set-theoretically by\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, writes
\((u+\operatorname{Ad}(a)v,ab,b)\), so as to exhibit \(\mathbf V(P)\) as
obtained, by pull-back over \(Y_0\times Y_0\), from the multiplication of the
\(S_0\)-group \(\mathbf V(\mathscr N_{Y_0/X_0})\) described below. The printed
formula, which fixes \((a,b)\), describes \(\mathbf V(P)\) itself, a morphism of
vector bundles over \(Y_0\times Y_0\).}
\[
 \mathbf V(P)(u,v,a,b)=(u+\operatorname{Ad}(a)v,a,b).
\]
This is proved exactly as is the corresponding fact for Lie algebras,
that is, for the module \(\omega^1_{Y_0/S_0}\). First observe that,
by functoriality in \(Y_0\), \(V\) is endowed with a group structure in
the category of vector bundles over \(S_0\). By the lemma already used
for Lie algebras (\SGARefLink{sga3:ii:lemma:3-10}{Expos\'e II, 3.10}), this structure coincides with the
group structure underlying its \(\mathscr O_S\)-module structure. Next,
\[
 \mathbf V\bigl(\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}
   \otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{Y_0}\bigr)
 =\mathbf V(\mathscr N_{Y_0/X_0})
\]
is itself endowed with an \(S_0\)-group structure, namely the
semidirect product of the structure on \(V\) by that on \(Y_0\). It
remains only to identify the operations of \(Y_0\) on \(V\) to obtain
the desired formula.

We now compute the two remaining terms. Consider first
\(a_1((0,DY))\). It is computed by the diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
 \mathbf n\otimes\mathscr O_{Y_0^2}\arrow[r,"P"]\arrow[d,"f_{10}^*"']
  &(\mathbf n+\mathbf n)\otimes\mathscr O_{Y_0^2}\arrow[d,"f_{10}^*"]\\
 \mathbf n\otimes\mathscr O_{Y_0^3}\arrow[dr,"{a_1((0,DY))}"']
  &(\mathbf n+\mathbf n)\otimes\mathscr O_{Y_0^3}\arrow[d,"{(0,DY)}"]\\
 &\mathscr J\otimes\mathscr O_{Y_0^3}
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Regard the vector bundles defined by these modules as schemes over
\(S_0\), and take points with values in an arbitrary scheme. If
\((u,x,y,z)\) denotes a point of
\(\mathbf V(\mathscr J)\times Y_0^3\), we obtain the following diagram:

\begin{center}
 \SGAThreeNativeInput[0.78\textwidth]
   {native_diagrams/exp3/diagram_058_exp3_p065_vector_bundle_points.tex}
\end{center}

Thus
\[
 a_1((0,DY))(x,y,z)
 =\operatorname{Ad}(x)DY(y,z),
\]
which is indeed the first term of the coboundary. Likewise,
\(a_2((DY,0))(x,y,z)=DY(x,y)\), and therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0={}&\operatorname{Ad}(x)DY(y,z)-DY(xy,z)
      +DY(x,yz)-DY(x,y)\\
   ={}&(\partial^2DY)(x,y,z).
 \end{aligned}
\]

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-20}\textbf{4.20. --- Proof of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:4-17:item:iii}{4.17(iii)}.}
One considers the diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.4em]
 Y'\times_SY'\arrow[d,hook]&Y\times_SY\arrow[d,hook]&Y\arrow[d,hook]
  &Y'\arrow[d,hook]\\
 X\times_SX\arrow[r]&X\times_SX\arrow[r]&X\arrow[r]&X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
which makes it possible to compute \(DY'\) by means of \(DY\) and of
\(d(Y,Y')\). The computation is done exactly as before; we leave the
details to the reader. One finds\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has \(d(Y,Y')(y)\) for
the third term, a misprint, and ends the next formula with
\(\partial d(Y',Y)(y)\) (on the sign, see the note to 4.17 (iii)).}
\[
 DY'(x,y)=d(Y',Y)(xy)+DY(x,y)+d(Y,Y')(x)+\operatorname{Ad}(x)d(Y,Y')(y),
\]
that is,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 DY'(x,y)-DY(x,y)
 &=-\operatorname{Ad}(x)d(Y',Y)(y)+d(Y',Y)(xy)-d(Y',Y)(x)\\
 &=\bigl(\partial^1d(Y,Y')\bigr)(x,y).
 \end{aligned}
\]

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III; our
formulation). The re-edition carries out this computation. Put
\(c=c(X,Y,i')\), so that \(c=d(Y,Y')\) by \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-8:item:ii}{4.8 (ii)}, and compute
\(v=c(X,Y,\mu\circ(i',i'))\) in two ways by 4.13, whose hypotheses hold by
4.15. First, for \(Y'\times_SY'\xrightarrow{\mu\circ(i',i')}X
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}X\), with \(Y'\subset X\) in the middle and \(Y\subset
X\) on the right: the obstruction \(c(X,Y',\mu\circ(i',i'))\) is \(DY'\), and as
\(Y'_0=Y_0\) the morphism \(b\) is the pull-back by the multiplication of
\(Y_0\); so \(v(x,y)=DY'(x,y)+c(xy)\). Second, for \(Y'\times_SY'
\xrightarrow{(i',i')}X\times_SX\xrightarrow{\mu}X\), with \(Y\times_SY\subset
X\times_SX\) in the middle: the obstruction for \((i',i')\) to factor through
\(Y\times_SY\) is \((c,c)\), and \(b\) is the identity of \(C^2(Y_0,N_0)\); so
\(v=DY+a_{\mu_0}((c,c))\), where, by the computation of \(P\) in 4.19,
\(a_{\mu_0}((c,c))(x,y)=c(x)+\operatorname{Ad}(x)c(y)\). Comparing,
\((DY'-DY)(x,y)=\operatorname{Ad}(x)c(y)-c(xy)+c(x)=(\partial^1c)(x,y)\).

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:theorem:4-21}\textbf{Theorem 4.21.}
\textit{Let \(S\) be a scheme and let \(\mathscr I\) and
\(\mathscr J\) be two nilpotent ideals on \(S\),\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, drops the
word ``nilpotent''. It is not needed: \(\mathscr J^2\subset\mathscr I\mathscr J
=0\), and the nilpotence of \(\mathscr I\) serves only through 4.8, where it can
be dispensed with (see the note there).} such that \(\mathscr I\supset\mathscr J\) and
\(\mathscr I\mathscr J=0\). Let \(X\) be an \(S\)-group smooth over
\(S\), and let \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) be a sub-\(S_{\mathscr J}\)-group of
\(X_{\mathscr J}\) (\(=\) subscheme in groups), flat and locally of
finite presentation over \(S_{\mathscr J}\). Consider the \(S_0\)-functor of
commutative groups
\(N_0\) defined by}
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(T,N_0)
 ={}&\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_T}
 \left(
  \mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_T,
  \mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_T
 \right),\\[-0.2em]
 &\hspace{17em}T\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathbf{Sch}/S_0),
 \end{aligned}
\]
\textit{on which \(Y_0\) acts through the inner automorphisms of
\(X_0\).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \textit{For there to exist a sub-\(S\)-group of \(X\), flat
  over \(S\), which reduces to \(Y_{\mathscr J}\), it is necessary and
  sufficient that the following two conditions hold:}
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item[\((i_1)\)] \textit{There exists a subscheme \(Y\) of \(X\),
    flat over \(S\), lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) (a condition automatically
    satisfied if \(Y_0\) is affine \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:remark:4-6}{4.6})}).}
  \item[\((i_2)\)] \textit{A certain canonical obstruction, an element
    of \(H^2(Y_0,N_0)\), vanishes.}
  \end{enumerate}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:theorem:4-21:item:ii}(ii)] \textit{If the conditions in (i) hold, the set of
  sub-\(S\)-groups \(Y\) of \(X\), flat over \(S\) and reducing to
  \(Y_{\mathscr J}\), is a principal homogeneous set under
  \(Z^1(Y_0,N_0)\).}
\end{enumerate}

Indeed, condition \((i_1)\) is necessary. Suppose that it holds, and let
\(Y\), flat over \(S\), lift \(Y_{\mathscr J}\). We must find a
\(Y'\) also lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) such that \(DY'=0\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:4-17:item:i}{4.17(i)}). But this amounts to finding a
\(d(Y',Y)\in C^1(Y_0,N_0)\) such that \(DY=\partial^1d(Y',Y)\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:4-17:item:iii}{4.17(iii)}).
Let \(c\in H^2(Y_0,N_0)\) be the class represented by \(DY\),
which is a cocycle by \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:4-17:item:ii}{4.17(ii)}. By \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:4-17:item:iii}{4.17(iii)}, this class does not depend
on the choice of \(Y\), and its vanishing is necessary and sufficient
for the existence of \(d(Y',Y)\) satisfying the preceding equation.
This proves (i).

If \(Y\) has now been chosen so that \(DY=0\), the equation to
be solved becomes \(\partial^1d(Y',Y)=0\), which proves \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:theorem:4-21:item:ii}{(ii)}.

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:remark:4-22}\textbf{Remark 4.22.}
Note that \(\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}\) is of finite presentation over
\(S_0\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, argues instead as
follows: by 4.15, \(Y_0\) is locally complete intersection in \(X_0\), so
\(\mathscr N_{Y_0/X_0}\), and hence \(\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}=\varepsilon_0^*
(\mathscr N_{Y_0/X_0})\), is locally free of finite type; then, writing
\(\mathbf n^\vee_{Y_0/X_0}\) for its dual,
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_Z}(\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
\mathscr O_Z,\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_Z)=\Gamma(Z,
\mathbf n^\vee_{Y_0/X_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
\mathscr O_Z)\) for every \(S_0\)-scheme \(Z\), flat or not, so that
\(N_0\simeq\mathbf W(\mathbf n^\vee_{Y_0/X_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr J)
\simeq\mathbf W(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}(\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0},
\mathscr J))\) on all \(S_0\)-schemes; it also writes the isomorphisms below
with \(\mathbf n^\vee_{Y_0/X_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr J\).} Hence, on
the category of \(S\)-flat schemes, the functor \(N_0\) can be written
\[
 N_0=\mathbf W\bigl(
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
  (\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0},\mathscr J)\bigr).
\]
There follow isomorphisms:\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, specifies that
the groups on the right are those of Exposé~I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:5-3}{5.3}, \(Y_0\) being
assumed affine over \(S_0\).}
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H^2(Y_0,N_0)
 &\simeq H^2\!\left(
   Y_0,\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
   (\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0},\mathscr J)\right),\\
 Z^1(Y_0,N_0)
 &\simeq Z^1\!\left(
   Y_0,\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
   (\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0},\mathscr J)\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
