\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:lemma:4-12}\textbf{Lemma 4.12.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \textit{Suppose that \(Y'\) is flat over \(S\). Then there is a
natural morphism}
\begin{align*}
 b_{f_0}:&\
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{Y'_0}}
 \bigl(i_0'^*g_0^*(\mathscr N_{Y/X}
   \otimes_{\mathscr O_Y}\mathscr O_{Y_0}),
   \mathscr J\mathscr O_{Y'}\bigr)\\
 &\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{T_0}}
 \bigl(f_0^*g_0^*(\mathscr N_{Y/X}
   \otimes_{\mathscr O_Y}\mathscr O_{Y_0}),
   \mathscr J\mathscr O_T\bigr).
\end{align*}
\item[(ii)] \textit{Suppose moreover that \(Y'_0\) is locally complete
intersection in \(X'_0\).}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, drops this
hypothesis, here and in 4.13: for \(Y'\) flat over \(S\), the second vertical
arrow of the second diagram below is an isomorphism without it (see the
Addition after Remark~\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-10}{4.10}).} \textit{Then there is a natural
morphism}
\begin{align*}
 a_{g_0}:&\
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{T_0}}
 \bigl(f_0^*(\mathscr N_{Y'/X'}
   \otimes_{\mathscr O_{Y'}}\mathscr O_{Y'_0}),
   \mathscr J\mathscr O_T\bigr)\\
 &\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{T_0}}
 \bigl(f_0^*g_0^*(\mathscr N_{Y/X}
   \otimes_{\mathscr O_Y}\mathscr O_{Y_0}),
   \mathscr J\mathscr O_T\bigr).
\end{align*}
\end{enumerate}

\(\mathscr O_{T_0}\)-morphism

\]

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III; our
formulation). The re-edition makes explicit two properties of these
morphisms, which are used in 4.19. Let
\(\alpha\colon i_0'^*g_0^*(\mathscr N_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathscr O_Y}\mathscr O_{Y_0})
\to\mathscr N_{Y'/X'}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{Y'}}\mathscr O_{Y'_0}\) and
\(\beta_T\colon f_0^*(\mathscr J\mathscr O_{Y'})\to\mathscr J\mathscr O_T\) be the
morphisms just constructed, so that \(a_{g_0}(c')=c'\circ f_0^*(\alpha)\) and
\(b_{f_0}(c)=\beta_T\circ f_0^*(c)\). (a) Change of \(T\). Let \(u\colon U\to T\)
be an \(S\)-morphism and \(h=f\circ u\); for an \(\mathscr O_{Y'_0}\)-module
\(\mathscr E\) and an \(\mathscr O_{T_0}\)-morphism
\(\varphi\colon f_0^*\mathscr E\to\mathscr J\mathscr O_T\), let
\(u^\natural(\varphi)\colon h_0^*\mathscr E\to\mathscr J\mathscr O_U\) be the
composite of \(u_0^*(\varphi)\) with the canonical morphism
\(u_0^*(\mathscr J\mathscr O_T)\to\mathscr J\mathscr O_U\). Since \(\alpha\) does
not depend on \(T\), and \(\beta_U\) is the composite of \(u_0^*(\beta_T)\) with
that canonical morphism, the morphisms attached to \(h\) satisfy
\(a_{g_0}(u^\natural(c'))=u^\natural(a_{g_0}(c'))\) and
\(b_{h_0}(c)=u^\natural(b_{f_0}(c))\). (b) Flat \(T\). If \(T\) is flat over
\(S\), then \(\mathscr J\mathscr O_T=\mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}
\mathscr O_{T_0}\) and \(\beta_T\) is an isomorphism; the groups of the lemma
then depend only on \(f_0\colon T_0\to Y'_0\), \(b_{f_0}\) is simply
\(c\mapsto f_0^*(c)\), and \(a_{g_0}\) is the value at \(T_0\) of the morphism of
functors \(c'\mapsto c'\circ f_0^*(\alpha)\), defined for all \(Y'_0\)-schemes
\(f_0\colon T_0\to Y'_0\).

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:4-13}\textbf{Proposition 4.13.}
\textit{Suppose that \(Y'\) is flat over \(S\) and that \(Y'_0\) is
locally complete intersection in \(X'_0\). Then one has the formula}
\[
 c(X,Y,g\circ f)=a_{g_0}\bigl(c(X',Y',f)\bigr)
   +b_{f_0}\bigl(c(X,Y,g\circ i')\bigr).
\]

Since the definitions of the various obstructions and of the morphisms
\(a_{g_0}\) and \(b_{f_0}\) are local, it plainly suffices to verify the
formula when all the schemes in question are affine. Write
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 S=\operatorname{Spec}(\Lambda),\qquad
 S_{\mathscr J}=\operatorname{Spec}(\Lambda/\mathscr J),\qquad
 S_0=\operatorname{Spec}(\Lambda/\mathscr I),\\
 T=\operatorname{Spec}(C),\qquad X=\operatorname{Spec}(A),\qquad
 Y=\operatorname{Spec}(A/I_Y)=\operatorname{Spec}(B),\\
 X'=\operatorname{Spec}(A'),\qquad
 Y'=\operatorname{Spec}(A'/I_{Y'})=\operatorname{Spec}(B').
 \end{gathered}
\]
There is therefore a diagram of rings and ideals

\begin{center}
  \SGAThreeNativeInput[0.48\textwidth]
    {native_diagrams/exp3/diagram_049_exp3_p058_413_ring_ideal_diagram.tex}
\end{center}

Let us study the various terms of the formula to be proved. First
\(c=c(X,Y,g\circ f)\). We saw that it is the unique \(C_0\)-morphism
\[
 I_Y/I_Y^2\otimes_B C_0\longrightarrow\mathscr J C
\]
such that \(c(\bar x\otimes1)=f(g(x))\) for every \(x\in I_Y\).
So let \(x\in I_Y\), and put \(x'=g(x)\in I_{Y'}+\mathscr J A'\). Write
\[
 x'=y'+\sum_i\lambda_i a'_i,
 \qquad y'\in I_{Y'},\quad \lambda_i\in\mathscr J,\quad a'_i\in A'.
\]
Hence
\[
 c(X,Y,g\circ f)(\bar x\otimes1)
 =f(x')=f(y')+\sum_i\lambda_i f(a'_i).
\]

Consider now \(a_{g_0}(c(X',Y',f))\). By the definitions laid down, it
is defined by the diagram

\begin{center}
  \SGAThreeNativeInput[0.86\textwidth]
    {native_diagrams/exp3/diagram_050_exp3_p059_413_ag0_obstruction_diagram.tex}
\end{center}

Hence \(a_{g_0}(c(X',Y',f))(\bar x\otimes1)=f(u)\), where \(u\) is an
element of \(I_{Y'}\) lifting \(g_0(x_0)=(g(x))_0=y'_0\). One therefore
takes \(u=y'\) and finds
\[
 a_{g_0}(c(X',Y',f))(\bar x\otimes1)=f(y').
\]

Consider finally \(b_{f_0}(c(X,Y,g\circ i'))\). It is defined by the
diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.2em,row sep=2.6em]
 I_Y/I_Y^2\otimes_BB'_0\arrow[r,"\bar g"]\arrow[d]
  &\mathscr JB'\arrow[d]&\\
 I_Y/I_Y^2\otimes_BC_0\arrow[r]
   \arrow[drr,"{b_{f_0}(c(X,Y,g\circ i'))}"']
  &\mathscr JB'\otimes_{B'_0}C_0
  &\mathscr J\otimes_{\Lambda_0}C_0\arrow[l,"\sim"']\arrow[d]\\
 &&\mathscr JC
\end{tikzcd}
\]
One therefore has at once\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, justifies this
step. As \(Y'\) is flat over \(S\), \(\mathscr JB'=\mathscr J\otimes_{\Lambda_0}B'_0\);
through this identification, the composite
\(\mathscr JB'\to\mathscr JB'\otimes_{B'_0}C_0\to\mathscr JC\) of the diagram is
\(\lambda\otimes b\mapsto\lambda c\), where \(c\in C\) lifts \(f_0(b)\) (the
morphism \(f_0\colon A'_0\to C_0\) factors through \(B'_0\), and \(c\) matters
only modulo \(\mathscr IC\), which \(\mathscr J\) annihilates); so it sends
\(\lambda\pi'(a')\) to \(\lambda f(a')\). Since \(y'\) vanishes in \(B'\),
\(\bar g(\bar x\otimes1)\) is the image \(\sum_i\lambda_i\pi'(a'_i)\) of \(x'\)
in \(\mathscr JB'\), whence the value \(\sum_i\lambda_if(a'_i)\), written
\(f(\bar g(x))\) in the text.}
\[
 b_{f_0}(c(X,Y,g\circ i'))(\bar x\otimes1)=f(\bar g(x))
 =\sum_i\lambda_i f(a'_i).
\]
The comparison of the three explicit results gives the required formula.

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:proposition:4-15}\textbf{4.15.}
We shall now consider the case where \(X\) is an \(S\)-group
\emph{smooth} over \(S\). Note first that every sub-\(S\)-group \(Y\)
\emph{flat} and locally of finite presentation over \(S\) is \emph{locally
complete intersection} in \(X\).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has
``every sub-\(S\)-group \(Y\) flat over \(S\)''; the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Exposé~III, adds ``and locally of finite presentation''. The addition
cannot be omitted over a general base (it is automatic when \(S\) is locally
noetherian): the ideal of a locally complete intersection is locally finitely
generated, so \(Y\) is then locally of finite presentation over \(S\), and a
flat closed subgroup need not be. For instance, let \(R=k^{\mathbf N}\) for a
field \(k\) and \(I=k^{(\mathbf N)}\), the ideal of sequences with finite
support, which is not finitely generated; every \(R\)-module is flat, and the
ideal \(xI[x]\) of \(R[x]\) defines a closed subgroup of the additive group
over \(R\) that is flat but not of finite presentation.}
Let us sketch the proof of this fact. By general results (cf. in
particular SGA~1, IV, 5.7), since \(X\) and \(Y\) are flat, one is
reduced to verifying the assertion on the geometric fibres of \(S\),
hence when \(S\) is the spectrum of an algebraically closed field. One
then remarks that one is dealing with groups, so that it suffices to
verify the assertion at the origin. Finally, to verify that \(Y\) is a
complete intersection in \(X\) at the origin, one may content oneself
with doing so for the formal groups \(\widehat X\) and \(\widehat Y\)
corresponding to \(X\) and \(Y\). Since \(X\) is smooth, the
hyperalgebra of \(\widehat X\) is a polynomial algebra, and one concludes
by means of the structure theorem of Dieudonné, which shows that the
hyperalgebra of \(\widehat Y\) is obtained by ``truncating'' that of
\(\widehat X\), hence is a complete intersection in it (cf. Exp.~VII).
Of course, when \(Y\) is also smooth over \(S\), it suffices simply to
invoke SGA~1, II, 4.15.

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III; our
formulation). The re-edition proves the first assertion of 4.15 as follows,
for \(Y\) flat and locally of finite presentation over \(S\). It suffices to
show that \(Y\to X\) is a regular immersion in the sense of
EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 16.9.2: a regular sequence has an acyclic Koszul
complex (loc.\ cit., 19.5.1), so that \(Y\) is then locally complete
intersection in the sense of the Addition after 4.6. As \(X\) and \(Y\) are
flat and locally of finite presentation over \(S\), it suffices that each
\(Y_s\to X_s\) (\(s\in S\)) be a regular immersion (loc.\ cit., 19.2.4); this
may be checked after passing to an algebraic closure of the residue field,
since quasi-regularity descends under faithfully flat quasi-compact base
change and coincides with regularity over a field (loc.\ cit., 19.1.5 (ii)
and 16.9.10). So let \(S=\operatorname{Spec}k\), \(k\) algebraically closed.
The quotient \(X/Y\) exists and is smooth over \(k\), and
\(\pi\colon X\to X/Y\) is faithfully flat (Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A},
\SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIA-3.2}{3.2}), with \(Y=X\times_{X/Y}\bar e\), where \(\bar e\) is the image of the unit
section. The local ring of \(X/Y\) at \(\bar e\) is regular, so its maximal
ideal is generated by a regular sequence and \(\bar e\to X/Y\) is a regular
immersion; hence so is its flat base change \(Y\to X\) (loc.\ cit., 19.1.5
(ii)). The re-edition also details the 1970 sketch. Over an algebraically
closed field \(k\), by loc.\ cit., 16.9.10 and 19.3.2, it suffices that every
local ring of \(Y\) be a complete intersection ring, that is, that its
completion be the quotient of a complete regular local ring by an ideal
generated by a regular sequence. The points where this holds form an open
subset of \(Y\) (loc.\ cit., 19.3.3), stable under the translations by the
points of \(Y(k)\), so it suffices to treat the origin. There the completed
local ring of \(Y\) is a quotient of that of \(X\), a power series ring
\(k[[x_1,\ldots,x_n]]\), and Dieudonné's theorem on formal groups over a
perfect field shows that it is isomorphic to
\(k[[x_1,\ldots,x_{r+s}]]/(x_1^{p^{n_1}},\ldots,x_r^{p^{n_r}})\), with
\(r+s\leq n\) and \(p\) the characteristic exponent of \(k\) (cf.
Exposé~VII\textsubscript{B}; the re-edition cites its Remark~5.5.2 (b)), a
ring of the required form.

Let us now give ourselves a sub-\(S_{\mathscr J}\)-group
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\) of \(X_{\mathscr J}\), \emph{flat} and locally of
finite presentation over \(S_{\mathscr J}\). We know first that
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\) is locally complete intersection in \(X_{\mathscr J}\), hence
(\SGARefLink{sga3:iii:remark:4-6}{4.6}) that every flat\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has
``in \(X\)'' in the preceding line, a misprint, and ``every \(S\)-prescheme
\(Y\) lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\)'' without ``flat'', which the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, adds. Flatness, assumed in 4.6, is needed:
\(Y_{\mathscr J}\) itself is a subscheme of \(X\) lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\);
for \(S=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]/(t^2)\), \(\mathscr J=t\mathscr O_S\), \(X\) the
additive group and \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) its unit section over \(S_{\mathscr J}\),
the ideal of \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) in the local ring \(k[t,x]_{(t,x)}/(t^2)\) of
\(X\) at the origin is its maximal ideal, which needs two generators, while a
sequence of that ring with acyclic Koszul complex is regular
(EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 19.5.2), hence has at most one term, the ring having
dimension one.} \(S\)-scheme \(Y\) lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\) is
locally complete intersection in \(X\), and that such a \(Y\) exists if
moreover \(Y_0\) is affine.

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-16}\textbf{4.16.}
Moreover, one has
\[
 \mathscr N_{Y/X}\otimes_{\mathscr O_Y}\mathscr O_{Y_0}
 =\mathscr N_{Y_{\mathscr J}/X_{\mathscr J}}
   \otimes_{\mathscr O_{Y_{\mathscr J}}}\mathscr O_{Y_0}
 =\mathscr N_{Y_0/X_0}.
\]
Since \(Y_0\) and \(X_0\) are \(S_0\)-groups, one sees easily that, if
one denotes by \(\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}\) the inverse image of
\(\mathscr N_{Y_0/X_0}\) under the unit section of \(Y_0\) (it is a
quasi-coherent \(\mathscr O_{S_0}\)-Module), one also has
\[
 \mathscr N_{Y_0/X_0}
 =\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{Y_0}.
\]
The deviations and obstructions of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:paragraph:4-7}{4.7} and \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-8}{4.8} are
therefore elements respectively of the groups\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~III, adds
that when \(T\) is flat over \(S\), \(\mathscr J\mathscr O_T=\mathscr J
\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{T_0}\), so that the second group is
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(T_0,N_0)\) for the functor \(N_0\) defined below; this
is what places the obstruction \(DY\) of \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:lemma:4-17}{4.17}, where
\(T=Y\times_SY\), in \(C^2(Y_0,N_0)\).}
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{Y_0}}
 \bigl(\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{Y_0},
       \mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{Y_0}\bigr)
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_{T_0}}
 \bigl(\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{T_0},
       \mathscr J\mathscr O_T\bigr).
\]
Consider the functor in commutative groups \(N_0\) over \(S_0\) defined
by
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(Z,N_0)
 =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr O_Z}
 \bigl(\mathbf n_{Y_0/X_0}\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_Z,
       \mathscr J\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_Z\bigr),
 \qquad Z\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathbf{Sch})_{/S_0}.
\]
We know that the set of sub-\(S\)-schemes of \(X\), \emph{flat} over
\(S\), lifting \(Y_{\mathscr J}\), is empty or principal homogeneous
under
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(Y_0,N_0)=C^1(Y_0,N_0).
\]
