\section*{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:section:4}4. Case of an arbitrary base. Quasi-isotriviality theorem}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{4. Case of an arbitrary base. Quasi-isotriviality theorem}

Recall, following Nagata, that a local ring \(A\) is said to be
\emph{henselian} if every algebra \(B\) finite over \(A\) is a product of local
algebras \(B_i\) finite over \(A\). It follows at once that, putting
\(S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\) and \(S_0=\operatorname{Spec}(k)\) (\(k\) the
residue field), the functor
\[
  X\longmapsto X_0=X\times_S S_0
\]
from the category of étale coverings of \(S\) to the category of étale
coverings of \(S_0=\operatorname{Spec}(k)\) is an equivalence of categories.
When \(A\) is moreover noetherian, writing \(A'\) for its completion and
\(S'=\operatorname{Spec}(A')\), and noting that a complete noetherian local
ring \(A'\) is henselian, one deduces that the functor
\[
  X\longmapsto X'=X\times_S S'
\]
from the category of étale coverings of \(S\) to the category of étale
coverings of \(S'\) is an equivalence of categories.\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, sets this recall out
as a numbered statement 4.0 followed by a Remark 4.0.1. In our words: for
\(A\) henselian the equivalence is EGA~IV\(_4\), 18.5.15, and a complete
noetherian local ring is henselian by 18.5.14. Since \(S'\) and \(S\) have the
same closed fiber \(S_0\), the functor \(X\mapsto X_0\) is the composite of
\(X\mapsto X'\) and of the analogous functor for \(S'\), which is an
equivalence; hence so is \(X\mapsto X'\). By SGA~1, V, it follows that
\(\pi_1(S_0,\xi)=\pi_1(S,\xi)=\pi_1(S',\xi)\) for a geometric point \(\xi\) of
\(S_0\); as \(S\) is connected, \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:1:2}{1.2} then shows that base change induces an
equivalence between the isotrivial groups of multiplicative type over \(S\)
and over \(S_0\) (and over \(S'\) when \(A\) is noetherian). The same composite
argument derives the second assertion of 4.1 below from the first.}

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:lemma:4-1}Lemma 4.1.}\enspace
\begingroup\itshape
Let \(A\) be a henselian local ring with residue field \(k\),
\(S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\), and \(S_0=\operatorname{Spec}(k)\). Then the
functor
\[
  H\longmapsto H_0=H\times_S S_0
\]
from the category of groups of multiplicative type finite over \(S\) to the
category of groups of multiplicative type finite over \(S_0\) is an
equivalence of categories. When \(A\) is noetherian, writing \(A'\) for its
completion and putting \(S'=\operatorname{Spec}(A')\), the functor
\[
  H\longmapsto H'=H\times_S S'
\]
from the category of groups of multiplicative type finite over \(S\) to the
category of groups of multiplicative type finite over \(S'\) is an
equivalence.
\par\endgroup

Of course, the second assertion is a consequence of the first.

We already know that the functor under consideration is essentially
surjective, since every group of multiplicative type \(H_0\) over
\(S_0=\operatorname{Spec}(k)\), being finite and hence of finite type over
\(k\), is isotrivial by \SGARefLink{sga3:x:proposition:1:4}{1.4}, and therefore comes from an isotrivial group of
multiplicative type over \(S\), as follows for example from the recall at the
beginning of this number and from \SGARefLink{sga3:x:proposition:1:1}{1.1}. It remains to prove full
faithfulness, that is, that for two groups \(G,H\) of multiplicative type
finite over \(S\), the map
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(G,H)
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0\text{-gr.}}(G_0,H_0)
\]
is bijective. For this, in view of the recalls made above, it is enough to
prove the following lemma.\footnote{Translator's note: if
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(G,H)\) is represented by a finite étale
\(S\)-scheme \(X\) (Lemma 4.2), the map in question is the map from the
\(S\)-morphisms \(S\to X\) to the \(S_0\)-morphisms \(S_0\to X_0\), which is
bijective because \(X\mapsto X_0\) is fully faithful on étale coverings, \(S\)
being one of them. The Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, spells
out this step.}

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:lemma:4-2}Lemma 4.2.}\enspace
\emph{Let \(G,H\) be two finite groups of multiplicative type over a
prescheme \(S\). Then
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(G,H)\) is representable by a finite
étale scheme over \(S\).}

The question being local on \(S\) for the faithfully flat quasi-compact
topology, we may indeed suppose \(G\) and \(H\) diagonalizable (N.B.
faithfully flat quasi-compact morphisms are morphisms of effective descent for
the fibered category of finite étale preschemes over variable preschemes, cf.\
SGA 1, VIII).\footnote{Translator's note: in more detail (the Gille--Polo
draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, writes this out; our formulation): the
functor \(F=\operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(G,H)\) is a sheaf for the
faithfully flat quasi-compact topology. If \(S'\to S\) is faithfully flat and
quasi-compact and \(F\times_SS'\) is represented by a finite étale
\(S'\)-scheme \(X'\), the descent datum on \(X'\) given by \(F\) is effective,
\(X'\) being affine over \(S'\) (SGA~1, VIII 2.1); the descended \(S\)-scheme
\(X\) represents \(F\), both being sheaves, and it is finite and étale over
\(S\), these properties descending (EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.7.1, and EGA~IV\(_4\),
17.7.3 (ii)).} We shall then have, by \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:proposition:2:1}{VIII 2.1} c),
\[
  G=D_S(M),\qquad H=D_S(N),
\]
where \(M\) and \(N\) are ordinary finite commutative groups, whence, by
Exp.~\SGARefLink{sga3:viii:corollary:1:5}{VIII 1.5}, an isomorphism
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(G,H)\simeq K_S,
  \qquad\text{with }K=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr.}}(N,M),
\]
which completes the proof.

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:lemma:4-3}Lemma 4.3.}\enspace
\begingroup\itshape
Let \(A\) be a noetherian henselian local ring, \(A'\) its completion,
\(S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\), \(S'=\operatorname{Spec}(A')\). Let \(G,H\) be
two \(S\)-preschemes in groups, with \(G\) of multiplicative type and of
finite type over \(S\), and with \(H\) locally of finite type and separated
over \(S\), \(H_0\) of multiplicative type, and \(H\) flat over \(S\) at the
points of \(H_0\) (where \(H_0=H\otimes_A k\)).

Let \(G',H'\) be obtained from \(G,H\) by the base change \(S'\to S\). Then
the following natural map is bijective:
\par\endgroup
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(G,H)
  \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S'\text{-gr.}}(G',H').
\]

Since \(S'\) is faithfully flat and quasi-compact over \(S\), we know from
SGA 1, VIII that this map identifies the first member with the subset of
the second formed by the \(u':G'\to H'\) for which the two inverse images
\(u''_1,u''_2:G''\to H''\) of \(u'\) on
\(S''=S'\times_S S'\), under the projections
\(\operatorname{pr}_1,\operatorname{pr}_2:S''\to S'\), are equal.

It therefore remains to prove that \(u''_1=u''_2\) for every homomorphism of
\(S'\)-groups \(u':G'\to H'\). By the density theorem \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:8}{IX 4.8}, it is enough
to prove that \(u''_1\) and \(u''_2\) agree on \({}_nG''\) for every integer
\(n>0\). (N.B. Here the density theorem is needed essentially in a case
where the base prescheme, namely \(S''\), is not locally noetherian.)

Replacing \(G\) by \({}_nG\), this reduces us to the case in which
\(n\cdot\operatorname{id}_G=0\) for some integer \(n>0\), and hence \(G\)
is finite over \(S\). Likewise let \({}_nH\) be the kernel of the \(n\)-th
power morphism in \(H\) (N.B. we have not assumed \(H\) commutative, so the
\(n\)-th power morphism in \(H\) need not be a group homomorphism, and hence
\({}_nH\) need not be a subgroup of \(H\)). Then \(({}_nH)_0={}_n(H_0)\) is
finite over \(k\), since \(H_0\) is of multiplicative type; it follows (by a
well-known property of henselian rings, generalizing the case of a complete
noetherian local ring) that the local rings of \({}_nH\) at the points of
\(({}_nH)_0\) are finite over \(S\), whence, by a known easy argument (using
the fact that \({}_nH\) is separated over \(S\)), a decomposition as a
disjoint sum of open subsets
\[
  {}_nH=X=X^1\amalg X^2,
\]
where \(X^1\) is finite over \(S\) and \(X^2\) is such that
\((X^2)_0=\varnothing\). Then for every finite \(S\)-scheme \(Y\) (such as
\(G\)), every \(S\)-morphism \(Y\to X\) factors through \(X^1\). On the other
hand, since \(H\) is flat over \(S\) at the points of \(H_0\), it follows that
for every integer \(m>0\), \(H_m\) is flat over \(S_m\), whence one concludes
that \(X^{1\prime}\) (whose reductions lie in the \(H_m\)) is a commutative
subgroup of \(H'\), which is moreover of multiplicative type by the criterion
\SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:3:8}{3.8} b). Consequently \(X^1\) is a finite subgroup of multiplicative type
of \(H\). Finally, by the remark made above on the decomposition of \(X\),
applying this same remark to \(X'\), one sees that \(u':G'\to H'\) takes its
values in \(X^{1\prime}\). We can then reduce, replacing \(H\) by the subgroup
\(X^1\) of multiplicative type finite over \(S\), to the case where \(H\) is
also a group of multiplicative type finite over \(S\). But then our assertion
is nothing but \SGARefLink{sga3:x:lemma:4-1}{4.1}, which completes the proof of \SGARefLink{sga3:x:lemma:4-3}{4.3}.

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X,
which expands this proof following indications of M.~Raynaud; our
formulation). The details of the preceding proof are as follows; \(\mathfrak
m\) is the maximal ideal of \(A\), and the index \(m\) denotes reduction
modulo \(\mathfrak m^{m+1}\). (a) The descent statement used at the start is
SGA~1, VIII 5.2. (b) As \({}_nH\) is the fiber product of the \(n\)-th power
morphism \(H\to H\) and of the unit section, its formation commutes with base
change, and it is closed in \(H\), \(H\) being separated. (c) The two
properties invoked are EGA~IV\(_4\), 18.5.11 c') and c), applied at the
finitely many points of \(({}_nH)_0\), at which \({}_nH\to S\) is
quasi-finite. (d) If \(Y\) is finite over \(S\) and \(f\colon Y\to X\) is an
\(S\)-morphism, then \(f^{-1}(X^2)\) is open and closed in \(Y\), hence finite
over \(S\), and has no point over the closed point; its image, closed in the
local scheme \(S\), is therefore empty. The decomposition is compatible with
the base change \(S'\to S\), which does not change the closed fiber, so the
same holds over \(S'\). (e) \(H_m\) is flat over \(S_m\) and quasi-compact
(its underlying space is that of \(H_0\)); by \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:2:4}{2.4} it is of multiplicative
type and of finite type. Hence \(({}_nH)_m={}_n(H_m)\) is a commutative
subgroup of \(H_m\), finite and flat over \(S_m\); it equals \(X^1_m\), since
\(X^2\) has no point over the closed point. (f) Write \(X^1\times_SX^1
=\operatorname{Spec}(B)\), with \(B\) finite over \(A\). The inverse image of
the closed subscheme \(X^1\) under the multiplication
\(X^1\times_SX^1\to H\) is defined by an ideal of \(B\) which, by (e), lies
in every \(\mathfrak m^{m+1}B\), hence is zero by Krull's intersection
theorem. The same argument, applied to the inverse and to the equalizer of the
multiplication and of its composite with the symmetry, shows that \(X^1\) is a
commutative closed subgroup of \(H\) (the unit section factors through \(X^1\)
by (d)); and \(X^1\) is flat over \(S\) by the local criterion of flatness
(EGA~\(0_{\mathrm{III}}\), 10.2.2), all the \(X^1_m\) being flat over \(S_m\).
(g) Over the complete ring \(A'\), \(X^{1\prime}\) is commutative, finite and
flat, its closed fiber \({}_n(H_0)\) is isotrivial by \SGARefLink{sga3:x:proposition:1:4}{1.4}, and its
subgroups \({}_r(X^{1\prime})\) are finite; so \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:3:8}{3.8} b) shows that
\(X^{1\prime}\) is of multiplicative type, and therefore so is \(X^1\). (h) As
\(G'\) is killed by \(n\), \(u'\) maps \(G'\) into \({}_n(H')=X'\), hence into
\(X^{1\prime}\) by (d), and the second assertion of 4.1 applies to the
homomorphism \(G'\to X^{1\prime}\) so obtained.

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X,
which states the following without proof; our formulation). \emph{Let \(A\)
be the union of a filtered family of subrings \(A_i\), let
\(S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\), \(S_i=\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)\), and let \(H\)
be a quasi-isotrivial \textup{(}respectively isotrivial\textup{)}
\(S\)-group of multiplicative type and of finite type. Then for some \(i\)
there is a quasi-isotrivial \textup{(}respectively isotrivial\textup{)}
\(S_i\)-group \(H_i\) of multiplicative type and of finite type such that
\(H\simeq H_i\times_{S_i}S\).} Indeed, \(S\) being quasi-compact, it is the
sum of finitely many open and closed parts on which the type of \(H\) is
constant, and such a decomposition comes from one of some \(S_i\)
(EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.3.11); so we may suppose \(H\) of constant type \(M\), a
finitely generated group (\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:proposition:2:1}{IX 2.1} b)). Choose \(T\to S\) étale and
surjective (respectively finite, étale and surjective) such that \(H_T\) is
locally diagonalizable, and finitely many affine open subsets \(U_k\) of
\(T\), whose images cover \(S\) (respectively which cover \(T\)), over which
\(H\) is diagonalizable; then \(S^\circ=\coprod_kU_k\) is affine, étale and
surjective over \(S\), and \(H_{S^\circ}\simeq D_{S^\circ}(M)\). The descent
datum of \(H\) relative to \(S^\circ\to S\) is an automorphism of \(D(M)\)
over \(S^\circ\times_SS^\circ\), that is (\SGARefLink{sga3:viii:corollary:1:5}{VIII 1.5}), a locally constant map
from this quasi-compact scheme to \(\operatorname{Aut}(M)\), satisfying the
cocycle condition. By EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.2.11, 8.3.11, 8.8.2 and 8.10.5, and
EGA~IV\(_4\), 17.7.8, for \(i\) large \(S^\circ\) (respectively \(T\) and
the \(U_k\)) and this map come from analogous data over \(S_i\), the cocycle
condition included; the descent datum so obtained on a group \(D(M)\) over an
affine scheme étale and surjective over \(S_i\) is effective (SGA~1,
VIII 2.1) and gives the desired \(H_i\).

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:theorem:4-4}Theorem 4.4.}\enspace
\begingroup\itshape
Let \(S\) be a prescheme, let \(H\) be an \(S\)-prescheme in groups of
finite presentation, and let \(s\in S\). Suppose:

\smallskip
\noindent
\(\alpha)\) \(H\) is affine over \(S\), and flat over \(S\) at the points of
\(H_s\);

\smallskip
\noindent
\(\beta)\) \(H_s\) is of multiplicative type.

\smallskip
Then there exist an étale morphism \(S'\to S\), a point \(s'\in S'\) above
\(s\) such that \(\kappa(s')/\kappa(s)\) is trivial, and an open and closed
subgroup \(G'\) of \(H'=H\times_S S'\), of multiplicative type and
isotrivial, such that \(G'_{s'}=H'_{s'}\).
\par\endgroup

One reduces at once to the case where \(S\) is the spectrum of a noetherian
local ring \(A\), and where \(s\) is the closed point of \(S\). Let us
distinguish two cases.

\par\smallskip\noindent
\(\mathbf{1^\circ}\) \emph{\(A\) is henselian.}
Let \(\widehat A\) be its completion,
\(\widehat S=\operatorname{Spec}(\widehat A)\), and
\(\widehat H=H\times_S\widehat S\). Applying \SGARefLink{sga3:x:theorem:3:7}{Theorem 3.7}, we find an
isotrivial group \(\widehat G\) of multiplicative type over \(\widehat S\)
and a homomorphism of \(\widehat S\)-groups
\[
  \widehat u:\widehat G\longrightarrow\widehat H
\]
which is an open immersion and a closed immersion, such that \(\widehat u\)
induces an isomorphism
\[
  \widehat u_0:\widehat G_0\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\widehat H_0.
\]
By the recalls at the beginning of this number on henselian rings, and the
classification in number 1 of isotrivial groups of multiplicative type, one
sees that the base change functor by \(\widehat S\to S\) induces an
equivalence between the category of isotrivial groups of multiplicative type
over \(S\) and that over \(\widehat S\); in particular, \(\widehat G\)
``comes'' from an isotrivial group of multiplicative type \(G\) over \(S\).
By \SGARefLink{sga3:x:lemma:4-3}{4.3}, \(\widehat u\) comes from a homomorphism
\[
  u:G\longrightarrow H;
\]
moreover, \(u\) is an open and closed immersion and induces an isomorphism
\(u_0:G_0\to H_0\), since this is so after the faithfully flat
quasi-compact base change \(\widehat S\to S\). This therefore proves
\SGARefLink{sga3:x:theorem:4-4}{4.4} in this case (taking of course, in the conclusion of 4.4,
\(S'=S\)).

\par\smallskip\noindent
\(\mathbf{2^\circ}\) \emph{General case.}
The reduction to the preceding case is immediate, by applying \(1^\circ\) to
the henselization \(A^h\) of \(A\). More precisely, one sees easily (using
SGA 1, I \S~5) that the local rings \(\mathcal O_{S',s'}\) of the étale
\(S\)-preschemes \(S'\) equipped with a point \(s'\) above \(s\) such that
\(\kappa(s')/\kappa(s)\) is trivial form a filtered inductive system, whose
inductive limit is a noetherian henselian local ring \(A^h\) (called the
henselization of \(A\)); for details of this construction (due to Nagata in
the normal case), cf.\ SGA 4, VIII \S~4%
\begingroup
\renewcommand{\thefootnote}{\(\ast\)}
\footnote{Or EGA IV\(_4\), \S~18.6.}
\addtocounter{footnote}{-1}
\endgroup. The standard arguments of EGA IV\(_3\), \S~8 then allow one to
deduce, from a result known over the inductive limit of the
\(\mathcal O_{S',s'}\), an analogous result over one of the \((S',s')\), which
proves \SGARefLink{sga3:x:theorem:4-4}{4.4}.

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X,
which writes out the reductions in this proof; our formulation).
(a) \emph{Reduction to \(S\) local.} Let \(T=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal
O_{S,s})\), the limit of the affine open neighborhoods \(S_i\) of \(s\), and
suppose that \(T'\to T\) étale, \(s'\in T'\) and \(G'\subset H_{T'}\) satisfy
the conclusion for \(H_T\); we may take \(T'\) affine, hence of finite
presentation over \(T\). Let \(\widetilde T\to T'\) be finite, étale and
surjective, and \((\widetilde U_k)\) a finite affine open covering of
\(\widetilde T\) over whose members \(G'\) is diagonalizable. By
EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.2.11, 8.3.11, 8.8.2 and 8.10.5, EGA~IV\(_4\), 17.7.8, and
Expos\'e~VI\(_{\mathrm B}\), 10.2 and 10.3, for \(i\) large all these data
come from analogous data over \(S_i\): an étale \(S_i\)-scheme \(T'_i\), an
open and closed subgroup \(G'_i\) of \(H\times_ST'_i\), a finite étale
surjective \(\widetilde T_i\to T'_i\) and an affine open covering of
\(\widetilde T_i\) over whose members \(G'_i\) is diagonalizable. Then
\(T'_i\to S\), the image of \(s'\) in \(T'_i\) (whose residue field is
\(\kappa(s)\)) and \(G'_i\) satisfy the conclusion of 4.4 for \(H\).
(b) \emph{Reduction to \(A\) noetherian.} If \(A\) is local, it is the
filtered union of local rings \(A_i\) which are localizations of finitely
generated \(\mathbb Z\)-algebras, hence noetherian; for \(i\) large, \(H\)
comes from an affine \(S_i\)-group \(H_i\) of finite presentation,
\(S_i=\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)\) (EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.8.2 and 8.10.5;
VI\(_{\mathrm B}\), 10.3). The set of points at which \(H\) is flat over
\(S\) is open (EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.1.1) and contains \(H_s\); a quasi-compact
open subset \(V\) of it containing \(H_s\) comes, for \(i\) large, from an
open subset \(V_i\) of \(H_i\) flat over \(S_i\) (EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.2.6), and
\(V_i\) contains the fiber of \(H_i\) at the image \(s_i\) of \(s\), since
\(V_s=H_s\) is obtained from it by the extension \(\kappa(s)/\kappa(s_i)\).
That fiber is of multiplicative type, since \(H_s\) is. So
\((S_i,H_i,s_i)\) satisfies \(\alpha)\) and \(\beta)\), and the conclusion
for it gives the conclusion for \((S,H,s)\) by base change. (c) In
\(2^\circ\), the conclusion over \(\operatorname{Spec}(A^h)\) descends to one
of the \((S',s')\) by the argument of (a), the \(\mathcal O_{S',s'}\) taking
the place of the \(\mathcal O_S(S_i)\).

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:corollary:4-5}Corollary 4.5.}\enspace
\emph{Every group of multiplicative type and of finite type over a prescheme
\(S\) is quasi-isotrivial, that is, is split by an étale surjective morphism
\(S'\to S\).}

One applies \SGARefLink{sga3:x:theorem:4-4}{4.4} in the case where \(H\) is of multiplicative type; then,
with the notation of its conclusion, one sees that \(G'=H'\) provided \(S'\)
is replaced if necessary by an open neighborhood of \(s'\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:2:9}{IX 2.9}). In
particular, one finds:\footnote{Translator's note: for the first assertion of
4.6, let \(s\) be the closed point of the henselian local scheme \(S\), and let
\(S'\to S\), \(s'\) and an open neighborhood \(U'\) of \(s'\) over which
\(H'\) is isotrivial be obtained as above. By EGA~IV\(_4\), 18.5.11 b), the
étale morphism \(S'\to S\) has a section \(\sigma\) with \(\sigma(s)=s'\);
every point of \(S\) being a generization of \(s\), \(\sigma\) maps \(S\)
into \(U'\), and \(H\simeq\sigma^*(H'|_{U'})\) is isotrivial. (One can also
see directly that \(\mathcal O_{S',s'}=A\): this local ring is finite over
\(A\) by 18.5.11 c'), with residue field \(\kappa(s)\), hence of the form
\(A/I\) by Nakayama's lemma; as it is flat and of finite presentation over
\(A\), \(I\) is finitely generated and \(I=I^2\subset\mathfrak mI\), so
\(I=0\).) The Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, gives this
argument.}

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:corollary:4-6}Corollary 4.6.}\enspace
\begingroup\itshape
Let \(A\) be a henselian local ring. Then every group of multiplicative type
and of finite type over \(S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\) is isotrivial, and the
functor \(H\mapsto H\otimes_A k\) from the category of these groups over
\(S\) to the category of algebraic groups of multiplicative type over \(k\)
is an equivalence of categories. \textup{(}In view of \SGARefLink{sga3:x:proposition:1:4}{1.4}, this therefore
gives a complete classification of the groups of multiplicative type of finite
type over \(A\) in terms of the topological Galois group of an algebraic
closure \(\Omega\) of \(k\).\textup{)}
\par\endgroup

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:corollary:4-7}Corollary 4.7.}\enspace
\begingroup\itshape
Under the hypotheses of \SGARefLink{sga3:x:theorem:4-4}{4.4}, suppose in addition that one of the
following conditions holds:

\smallskip
\noindent
a) for every generization \(t\) of \(s\), \(H_t\) is of multiplicative
type and has the same type as \(H_s\);

\smallskip
\noindent
b) \(H\) is commutative and the \({}_nH\), \(n>0\), are finite over \(S\)
in a neighborhood of \(s\);

\smallskip
\noindent
c) for every generization \(t\) of \(s\), the fiber \(H_t\) is connected.

\smallskip
Then there is an open neighborhood \(U\) of \(s\) such that \(H|_U\) is of
multiplicative type.
\par\endgroup

Since an étale morphism is open, we are reduced to proving that there exists
(with the notation of the conclusion of \SGARefLink{sga3:x:theorem:4-4}{4.4}) an open neighborhood of \(s'\)
over which \(G'=H'\). We may assume \(S'=S\), and \(S\) local with closed
point \(s\). Under these conditions the argument given in \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:3:8}{3.8} is valid in
the present case.\footnote{Translator's note: the passage to a local base is
as follows. With \(S''=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal O_{S',s'})\), an equality
\(G'\times_{S'}S''=H'\times_{S'}S''\) extends to an open neighborhood of
\(s'\), \(G'\) and \(H'\) being of finite presentation over \(S'\)
(EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.8.2). One is thus reduced to showing: if \(S\) is local with
closed point \(s\), \(H\) an \(S\)-group of finite type and \(G\) an open and
closed subgroup of multiplicative type of \(H\) with \(G_s=H_s\), then
\(G=H\) under each of the conditions a), b), c) (now for all the fibers). The
Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, states this as a Lemma
4.7.1, proved like 3.8.}

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:corollary:4-8}Corollary 4.8.}\enspace
\begingroup\itshape
Let \(S\) be a prescheme and let \(H\) be an \(S\)-prescheme in groups of
finite presentation. For \(H\) to be of multiplicative type, it is necessary
and sufficient that it be affine over \(S\), flat over \(S\), with fibers of
multiplicative type, and that it satisfy one of the following two conditions
(which are equivalent given the preceding conditions):

\smallskip
\noindent
\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:corollary:4-8:item:a}a) the type of \(H_s\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:definition:1:4}{IX 1.4}) is a locally constant function of
\(s\in S\);

\smallskip
\noindent
\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:corollary:4-8:item:b}b) \(H\) is commutative, and the \({}_nH\), \(n>0\), are finite over \(S\).

\smallskip
Moreover, these conditions a), b) are implied by the following:

\smallskip
\noindent
c) the fibers of \(H\) are connected.
\par\endgroup

In particular:

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:corollary:4-9}Corollary 4.9.}\enspace
\begingroup\itshape
Let \(S\) and \(H\) be as above. For \(H\) to be a torus, it is necessary
and sufficient that it be affine over \(S\), flat over \(S\), and that its
fibers be tori. Suppose \(H\) affine over \(S\), flat over \(S\), with
connected fibers, and let \(s\in S\) be such that \(H_s\) is a torus; then
there is an open neighborhood \(U\) of \(s\) such that \(H|_U\) is a torus.
\par\endgroup

\section*{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:section:5}5. Scheme of homomorphisms from one group of multiplicative type to
another. Twisted constant groups and groups of multiplicative type}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{5. Scheme of homomorphisms from one group of
multiplicative type to another. Twisted constant groups and groups of
multiplicative type}

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:definition:5-1}Definition 5.1.}\enspace
\begingroup\itshape
Let \(S\) be a prescheme and \(R\) a prescheme in groups over
\(S\). We say that \(R\) is a \emph{twisted constant group} over \(S\) if
it is locally isomorphic, for the faithfully flat quasi-compact topology,
to a constant group scheme, that is, one of the form \(M_S\) for an
ordinary group \(M\). We say that the twisted constant group \(R\) over
\(S\) is \emph{quasi-isotrivial}, respectively \emph{isotrivial},
respectively \emph{locally isotrivial}, respectively \emph{locally trivial},
respectively \emph{trivial}, if in the preceding definition one can replace
the faithfully flat quasi-compact topology by the étale topology,
respectively the global finite étale topology, respectively the finite
étale topology, respectively the Zariski topology, respectively the chaotic
topology (compare \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:definition:1:1}{IX 1.1} and \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:comments:1:2}{1.2}, and \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:subsection:6:5}{IV 6.5}).\footnote{Translator's
note: the 1970 text refers here to VIII 1.1 and 1.2, and below to VIII 1.4
for the type; the notions in question are those of IX 1.1 and 1.2, and the
type is defined in IX 1.4. The global finite étale topology and the chaotic
topology appear in IV 6.6. The Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009),
Expos\'e~X, corrects the first of these references and refers to IV 6.6.}
\par\endgroup

Thus, to say that \(R\) is quasi-isotrivial (respectively isotrivial) means
that there is an étale surjective (respectively also finite) morphism
\(S'\to S\) such that \(R'=R\times_S S'\) is a constant group over \(S'\);
to say that \(R\) is trivial means that \(R\) is a constant group. As in
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:definition:1:4}{IX 1.4}, one defines the \emph{type} of a twisted constant group
\(R\) over \(S\) at a point \(s\in S\). It is an isomorphism class of
ordinary groups and, as \(s\) varies, is a locally constant function of
\(s\), hence a constant function if \(S\) is connected. We also say that
\(R\) is ``of type \(M\)'' if all the fibers of \(R\) are of type \(M\).
One should beware that \(R\) is of finite type over \(S\) only if it is
finite over \(S\).\footnote{Translator's note: \(R\) being étale over \(S\),
it is of finite type over \(S\) if and only if it is quasi-compact over
\(S\), and then it is finite over \(S\) by Lemma 5.12 below (applied to
\(Z=R\)); that is, its type at every point is a finite group. The
Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, states the remark in this
form and refers to 5.12.}

The case of greatest interest to us is that in which \(R\) is
commutative. We then say that \(R\) is \emph{finitely generated} if its
type at every point \(s\in S\) is given by an ordinary commutative group
which is a finitely generated \(\mathbb Z\)-module, a notion that must not be
confused with the scheme-theoretic notion ``\(R\) of finite type over
\(S\)''.

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:remark:5-2}Remark 5.2.}\enspace
We shall also have to consider \(S\)-preschemes \(X\) which are locally
isomorphic, for the faithfully flat quasi-compact topology, to constant
preschemes, independently of any group structure. We then call \(X\) a
\emph{twisted constant bundle} over \(S\), and extend to these preschemes
the terminology introduced in \SGARefLink{sga3:x:definition:5-1}{5.1}. Of course, when \(X\) is endowed with an
\(S\)-group structure, the meaning of the expressions ``twisted constant'',
``isotrivial'', and so on changes according as one does or does not take the
group structure over \(S\) into account. The same applies when \(X\)
carries any other kind of algebraic structure (for example, that of a
Galois principal bundle, to be considered in the next section).

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:proposition:5-3}Proposition 5.3.}\enspace
\begingroup\itshape
Let \(R\) be a commutative twisted constant group over \(S\). Then
\(H=D_S(R)\) \textup{(}VIII 1\textup{)} is representable and is a group of
multiplicative type over \(S\). For every \(s\in S\), the type of \(R\) at
\(s\) equals the type of \(H\) at \(s\). For \(R\) to be quasi-isotrivial
\textup{(}respectively isotrivial, respectively trivial, respectively
locally isotrivial, respectively locally trivial\textup{)}, it is necessary
and sufficient that \(H\) be so.
\par\endgroup

Covering families for the faithfully flat quasi-compact topology are
families of effective descent for the fibered category of affine
preschemes in groups over variable base preschemes (SGA 1, VIII).
Consequently \(H\) is representable (and affine over \(S\)), since this is
true ``locally''
(when \(R\) is constant, \(H\) is a diagonalizable group).

That \(H\) is of multiplicative type is then immediate from the definition,
as is the equality of the types of \(R\) and \(H\) at \(s\in S\). The last
assertion is reduced by definition to the ``trivial'' case, namely to
checking that \(R\) is trivial if and only if \(H\) is trivial; this follows
at once from the biduality theorem \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:theorem:1:2}{VIII 1.2} (N.B. one may be concerned
that in \SGARefLink{sga3:x:proposition:5-3}{5.3} the word ``type'' is being used in two different senses,
according as it refers to \(R\) or to \(H\); fortunately, when an
\(S\)-prescheme in groups \(G\) is both a twisted constant group and a group
of multiplicative type, its type in either sense is the same, thanks to the
fact that an ordinary finite commutative group is isomorphic to its dual!).

To complete the description of the correspondence between twisted constant
groups and groups of multiplicative type, one must start with a group of
multiplicative type \(H\) and study \(R=D_S(H)\). If \(R\) is
representable, it is plainly a twisted constant group and
\(H\simeq D_S(R)\). In other words, the functor \(R\mapsto D_S(R)\) is an
anti-equivalence\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text says
``equivalence''; the functor is contravariant. The Gille--Polo draft
re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, makes this correction.} of the category of
twisted constant groups over \(S\) with the category of groups of
multiplicative type \(H\) over \(S\) such that \(D_S(H)\) is representable. I
do not know whether this condition on \(H\) is always satisfied; we shall
see, however, that it is satisfied when \(H\) is quasi-isotrivial, and in
particular when \(H\) is of finite type.

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:lemma:5-4}Lemma 5.4.}\enspace
\begingroup\itshape
Let \(S'\to S\) be a faithfully flat morphism locally of finite
presentation, and \(X'\) an \(S'\)-prescheme endowed with a descent datum
relative to \(S'\to S\). Suppose that \(X'\) is separated, locally of finite
presentation, and locally quasi-finite over \(S'\). Then the descent datum
is effective, that is, there are an \(S\)-prescheme \(X\) and an
\(S'\)-isomorphism \(X\times_S S'\simeq X'\) compatible with the descent
data.
\par\endgroup

We have already observed that the hypothesis on \(S'\to S\) implies that it
is a covering morphism for the faithfully flat quasi-compact topology
(\SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:6:3:1:item:iv:01}{IV 6.3.1 (iv)}), and hence \emph{a fortiori} a morphism of effective
descent.

When \(X'\to S'\) is quasi-compact, and hence of finite presentation and
quasi-finite, it is a quasi-affine morphism (SGA 1, VIII
6.2),\footnote{Translator's note: SGA~1, VIII 6.2 assumes the base locally
noetherian; for an arbitrary \(S'\), a separated quasi-finite morphism of
finite presentation is quasi-affine by EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.11.2. The Gille--Polo
draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, adds this reference.} and effectivity
follows in this case
from SGA 1, VIII 7.9. In the general case, one reduces at once to the case
where \(S\) and \(S'\) are affine. Cover \(X'\) by affine open subsets
\(U'_i\), and let \(V'_i\) be the saturation of \(U'_i\) under the
equivalence relation on \(X'\) defined by the descent datum:
\[
  V'_i=q_2\bigl(q_1^{-1}(U'_i)\bigr).
\]
Here \(q_1,q_2\) are the two projections onto \(X'\) of
\[
  X''_1=X'\times_{S'}(S'',p_1)
\]
(\(q_1=\operatorname{pr}_1\), and \(q_2\) is deduced from the first
projection of
\[
  X''_2=X'\times_{S'}(S'',p_2)
\]
by means of the given descent isomorphism \(X''_1\simeq X''_2\)). Since \(S'\to S\) is faithfully flat,
quasi-compact, and locally of finite presentation, the same is true of
\[
  p_1:S''=S'\times_S S'\longrightarrow S',
\]
and hence of \(q_1,q_2\). These are consequently open morphisms
(SGA 1, IV 6.6).\footnote{Translator's note: SGA~1, IV 6.6 is stated for a
locally noetherian base; in general, a flat morphism locally of finite
presentation is universally open by EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.4.6. The Gille--Polo
draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, adds this reference.} Therefore
\(V'_i\) is an
open quasi-compact part of \(X'\). By the case already proved, the induced
descent data on the \(V'_i\) are effective; hence so is the descent datum on
\(X'\) (SGA 1, VIII 7.2).

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:corollary:5-5}Corollary 5.5.}\enspace
\emph{A faithfully flat morphism of finite presentation \(S'\to S\) is a
morphism of effective descent for the fibered category of twisted constant
groups over variable base preschemes.}

Indeed, this amounts to the effectivity assertion of \SGARefLink{sga3:x:lemma:5-4}{5.4} when \(X'\) is a
constant \(S'\)-prescheme.

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:theorem:5-6}Theorem 5.6.}\enspace
\begingroup\itshape
Let \(S\) be a prescheme, and let \(G,H\) be two quasi-isotrivial
\(S\)-preschemes in groups of multiplicative type, with \(G\) of finite
type.\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has ``with \(H\) of finite
type''. What is needed is that \(G\) be of finite type: for \(G=D_S(M)\) and
\(H=D_S(N)\), \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:corollary:1:5}{VIII 1.5} identifies \(\operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(H,G)\)
with the constant group \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr.}}(M,N)_S\) when
\(M\) is finitely generated. In particular, at a point where \(G\) and \(H\)
have types \(M\) and \(N\), the type of
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(H,G)\) is
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr.}}(M,N)\). The Gille--Polo draft re-edition
(2009), Expos\'e~X, makes this correction and adds the statement on the
type.} Then \(\operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(H,G)\) is representable and
is a quasi-isotrivial twisted constant group over \(S\).
\par\endgroup

The proof proceeds as in \SGARefLink{sga3:x:lemma:4-2}{4.2}, using the fact that the assertion is known
(\SGARefLink{sga3:viii:corollary:1:5}{VIII 1.5}) when \(G\) and \(H\) are trivial. The required effectivity
criterion is provided by \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:5-5}{5.5}, applied to an étale surjective morphism
\(S'\to S\).

Taking \(G=\mathbb G_{m,S}\), one finds:

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:corollary:5-7}Corollary 5.7.}\enspace
\begingroup\itshape
For every quasi-isotrivial group of multiplicative type \(H\) over \(S\),
the \(S\)-group \(D_S(H)\) is representable and is a twisted constant group
over \(S\). The functors \(R\mapsto D_S(R)\) and \(H\mapsto D_S(H)\) are
anti-equivalences, quasi-inverse to each other, between the category of
quasi-isotrivial twisted constant groups over \(S\) and the category of
quasi-isotrivial groups of multiplicative type over \(S\). They induce
anti-equivalences between the subcategories formed by the isotrivial,
respectively locally isotrivial, respectively locally trivial, respectively
trivial groups.
\par\endgroup

The last assertion is included only for reference, since it is already
contained in \SGARefLink{sga3:x:proposition:5-3}{5.3}.

Using now \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:4-5}{4.5}, one finds:

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:corollary:5-8}Corollary 5.8.}\enspace
\emph{Let \(S\) be a prescheme, and let \(G,H\) be two \(S\)-preschemes in
groups of multiplicative type and of finite type. Then
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(H,G)\) is representable and is a
finitely generated quasi-isotrivial twisted constant \(S\)-group.}

Note that in \SGARefLink{sga3:x:proposition:5-3}{5.3}, \(R\) is finitely generated if and only if \(H\) is
of finite type (\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:proposition:2:1}{IX 2.1} b)). By \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:4-5}{4.5}, \(H\) is then
quasi-isotrivial, hence \(R\) is quasi-isotrivial. One thus finds:

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:corollary:5-9}Corollary 5.9.}\enspace
\emph{The functors of \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:5-7}{5.7} induce mutually quasi-inverse anti-equivalences
between the category of \(S\)-groups \(H\) of multiplicative type and of
finite type and the category of finitely generated twisted constant
\(S\)-groups \(R\). Every such group \(R\) is quasi-isotrivial.}

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:corollary:5-10}Corollary 5.10.}\enspace
\begingroup\itshape
Let \(H,G\) be two \(S\)-preschemes in groups of multiplicative type
and of finite type. Then \(\operatorname{Isom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(H,G)\) is
representable by an open and closed subprescheme of
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(H,G)\), and it is a twisted constant
\(S\)-prescheme. In particular, \(\operatorname{Aut}_{S\text{-gr.}}(H)\) is
representable and is a twisted constant group (in general noncommutative)
over \(S\).
\par\endgroup

This follows from \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:5-8}{5.8} and \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:corollary:1:6}{VIII
1.6}.\footnote{Translator's note: VIII 1.6 concerns the groups of sections
over a connected base. For the functors: over any \(S\)-prescheme \(T\), a
homomorphism \(D_T(N)\to D_T(M)\), \(M\) and \(N\) being finitely generated,
is given by a locally constant map
\(\varphi\colon T\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr.}}(M,N)\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:viii:corollary:1:5}{VIII 1.5}), and it is an isomorphism if and only if \(\varphi\) takes its
values in \(\operatorname{Isom}_{\mathrm{gr.}}(M,N)\), the inverse being given
by \(t\mapsto\varphi(t)^{-1}\). So, locally for the étale topology,
\(\operatorname{Isom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(H,G)\) is the open and closed
subscheme \(\operatorname{Isom}_{\mathrm{gr.}}(M,N)_S\) of
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr.}}(M,N)_S\), and the assertion follows by
descent. The Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, notes that the
reference to VIII 1.6 needs such a complement.}

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:proposition:5-11}Proposition 5.11.}\enspace
\begingroup\itshape
Let \(S\) be a prescheme, let \(R\) be a commutative twisted constant
group over \(S\), and let \(H=D_S(R)\) be the group of multiplicative type
that it defines. Consider the following conditions:

\smallskip
\noindent
\(\mathrm{(i)}\) \(H\) is isotrivial (that is, \(R\) is isotrivial);

\smallskip
\noindent
\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:proposition:5-11:item:ii}\(\mathrm{(ii)}\) \(R\) is a union of open and closed subpreschemes \(R_i\)
which are quasi-compact over \(S\) (and then necessarily finite over \(S\));

\smallskip
\noindent
\(\mathrm{(iii)}\) the connected components of \(R\) are finite over \(S\).

\smallskip
Then \(\mathrm{(i)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(ii)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(iii)}\); one
has \(\mathrm{(i)}\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{(ii)}\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{(iii)}\)
if \(S\) is locally noetherian; finally,
\(\mathrm{(i)}\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{(ii)}\) if \(R\) is finitely generated,
that is, if \(H\) is of finite type, at least if \(S\) is quasi-compact or if
its connected components are open.\footnote{Translator's note: the proof below
establishes \(\mathrm{(ii)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(i)}\) only when \(H\) is of
finite type, and without that hypothesis the implication fails even for
noetherian \(S\). Over \(S=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb F_p)\), let \(R\) be
the étale group corresponding to the Galois module
\(\bigoplus_{n\geq1}\mathbb Z[\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z]\), the Frobenius acting on
each summand by translation by \(1\). Every element has a finite orbit, so
\(R\) is the sum of spectra of finite extensions of \(\mathbb F_p\) and
satisfies (ii) and (iii); but no finite extension of \(\mathbb F_p\) splits
\(R\), so \(R\) is not isotrivial. Thus, for \(S\) locally noetherian, the
equivalence with (i) requires \(R\) finitely generated, and then follows from
the last assertion, the connected components of \(S\) being open. The
Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, notes that the proof does not
cover this case.}
\par\endgroup

First decompose \(S\) as a prescheme sum of preschemes \(S_i\) on each of
which \(H\) has constant type (cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:remark:1:4:1}{IX 1}). We are reduced to the case
where \(H\), and therefore \(R\), has constant type. We shall need:

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:lemma:5-12}Lemma 5.12.}\enspace
\emph{Let \(S\) be a prescheme and \(R\) a twisted constant prescheme over
\(S\). Every closed subprescheme \(Z\) of \(R\) which is quasi-compact
over \(S\) is finite over \(S\).}

Indeed, we reduce to the case where \(R\) is constant, hence of the form
\(I_S\) for a set \(I\). It is the filtered increasing union of the
\(J_S\), where \(J\) runs through the finite subsets of \(I\). We may also
assume that \(S\) is affine. Then \(Z\) is quasi-compact, and is therefore
contained in some \(J_S\). Since \(J_S\) is finite over \(S\), so is \(Z\).

\SGARefLink{sga3:x:lemma:5-12}{Lemma 5.12} already proves the parenthetical assertion in
\SGARefLink{sga3:x:proposition:5-11:item:ii}{5.11 (ii)}. The implication
\(\mathrm{(ii)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(iii)}\), and the converse implication when
\(S\) is locally noetherian, are then clear.\footnote{Translator's note: a
connected component of \(R\) lies in one of the open and closed \(R_i\) that
it meets, and is closed in it, hence finite over \(S\) if \(R_i\) is;
conversely, if \(S\) is locally noetherian, so is \(R\), which is étale over
\(S\), and the connected components of a locally noetherian prescheme are
open (each point has a noetherian open neighborhood, whose finitely many
connected components are open in it; cf.\ EGA~I, 6.1.9), so that \(R\) is
the union of its connected components. The Gille--Polo draft re-edition
(2009), Expos\'e~X, spells this out.}

Let us prove \(\mathrm{(i)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(ii)}\). Choose a finite
étale surjective morphism \(S'\to S\) which splits \(H\), and hence \(R\), so
that
\[
  R'\simeq M_{S'}=\coprod_{m\in M}R'_m,
\]
where the \(R'_m\) are disjoint open subsets of \(R'\), each
\(S'\)-isomorphic to \(S'\). Let \(g:R'\to R\) be the projection. It is a
finite étale surjective morphism, and hence both open and closed. The
subsets \(R_m=g(R'_m)\) are therefore open and closed parts of \(R\), and
are plainly quasi-compact over \(S\), since the \(R'_m\) are.

Finally, let us prove \(\mathrm{(ii)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(i)}\) when \(H\) is
of finite type over \(S\), \(S\) being quasi-compact or connected. Let \(M\)
be its type; one can write
\[
  M=\mathbb Z^r\times N,
\]
where \(r\) is an integer \(\geq0\) and \(N\) a finite group. Consider the
prescheme
\[
  P=\operatorname{Isom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(H,G)
  \subset
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(H,G)=Q,
\]
where \(G=D_S(M)\), cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:5-8}{5.8} and \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:5-10}{5.10}. We have an isomorphism
\[
  Q\simeq
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(\mathbb Z^r_S,R)
  \times
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(N_S,R)
  \simeq R^r\times E,
\]
where \(E=\operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(N_S,R)\) is finite over
\(S\).\footnote{Translator's note: by the duality \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:5-9}{5.9},
\(Q\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(M_S,R)\), whence the first
isomorphism; and \(E\) is a twisted constant group of type
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr.}}(N,M)=\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{gr.}}(N)\)
(cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:x:theorem:5-6}{5.6} and \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:5-8}{5.8}), a finite group, hence finite over \(S\). The
Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, gives these
justifications.} It follows that \(Q\) is a union of open and closed
subpreschemes \(Q_i\) finite over \(S\). Hence \(P\) is a union of the open
and closed subpreschemes \(P_i=P\cap Q_i\), finite over \(S\). Since they
are étale over \(S\), their images in \(S\) are open and closed parts
\(S_i\), and these cover \(S\). If \(S\) is connected or quasi-compact,
there is a finite set of indices \(i\) for which the \(S_i\) cover \(S\).
Let \(S'\) be the union of the corresponding \(P_i\). Then
\(S'\to S\) is finite étale surjective. Putting
\[
  P'=P\times_S S'
     =\operatorname{Isom}_{S'\text{-gr.}}(H',G'),
\]
we see that \(P'\) has a section over \(S'\); hence there is an isomorphism
of \(S'\)-groups
\[
  H'=H\times_S S'
  \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
  G'=G\times_S S'=D_{S'}(M).
\]
This proves that \(S'\) splits \(H\). The case where the connected
components of \(S\) are open reduces at once to the case where \(S\) is
connected. --- Let us remark, moreover, that when \(H\) is of finite type
over \(S\) and of constant type, one can give the following isotriviality
criterion (where it is no longer necessary to make any restriction on
\(S\)): \(R\) is the union of a sequence of open and closed parts finite over
\(S\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009),
Expos\'e~X, observes that the condition is necessary, the type of \(H\) being
countable, so that the proof of (i)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii) exhibits \(R\) as a
countable union of open and closed parts finite over \(S\); it leaves the
converse to be checked. The converse holds: if \(R=\bigcup_{j\geq1}R_j\) with
\(R_j\) open, closed and finite over \(S\), then, with the notation of the
proof, \(Q\) and hence \(P\) are countable unions of open and closed parts
\(P_j\), finite and étale over \(S\), whose images \(S_j\) are open and closed
and cover \(S\); the sets \(T_j=S_j-(S_1\cup\dots\cup S_{j-1})\) are open and
closed and form a partition of \(S\), and the sum of the
\(P_j\times_ST_j\) is finite, étale and surjective over \(S\) and splits
\(H\).}

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:lemma:5-13}Lemma 5.13.}\enspace
\emph{Let \(S\) be a locally noetherian connected prescheme, let \(P\) be
a quasi-isotrivial twisted constant \(S\)-prescheme, and let \(Z\) be an
open and closed part of \(P\). If \(Z_s\) is finite for some \(s\in S\),
then \(Z\) is finite over \(S\).}

First, do not assume \(S\) connected. Let \(U\) be the set of \(s\in S\)
for which \(Z_s\) is finite. We shall prove that \(U\) is both open and
closed, and that \(Z|_U\) is finite over \(U\). This statement is
essentially equivalent to \SGARefLink{sga3:x:lemma:5-13}{5.13}, but has the advantage of being local in
\(S\) for the étale topology, say. We are therefore reduced to the case
where \(P\) is constant, of the form \(I_S\) for a set \(I\). (N.B. The
locally noetherian hypothesis is preserved by étale base change; this is
where the quasi-isotriviality of \(P\) over \(S\) is used.)

We may also suppose that \(S\) is connected, since its connected components
are open (\(S\) being locally noetherian). Then necessarily \(Z=J_S\) for
a subset \(J\subseteq I\), and consequently \(U=\varnothing\) or \(U=S\)
according as \(J\) is infinite or finite. This proves the assertion.

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:corollary:5-14}Corollary 5.14.}\enspace
\emph{Let \(S\) be a locally noetherian normal prescheme, and let \(P\) be a
quasi-isotrivial twisted constant \(S\)-prescheme. Then the connected
components of \(P\) are finite over \(S\).}

Since \(P\) is étale over \(S\), \(P\) is normal (SGA 1, I 9.5), so its
connected components are its irreducible components. We may obviously
suppose \(S\) connected, hence irreducible; let \(s\) be its generic point.
Since \(P\) is flat over \(S\), its maximal points, that is, the generic
points \(x_i\) of the irreducible components \(P_i\) of \(P\), are the
maximal points of \(P_s\), and the generic fiber \((P_i)_s\) of \(P_i\) is
the irreducible component of \(P_s\) which is the closure of \(x_i\) in
\(P_s\). Now \(P_s\), being étale over \(\kappa(s)\), is a disjoint sum of
spectra of finite separable extensions of \(\kappa(s)\); hence, for every
\(i\), \((P_i)_s\) is finite (in fact reduced to a single point). Therefore
\SGARefLink{sga3:x:lemma:5-13}{5.13} applies and shows that the \(P_i\) are finite over
\(S\).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text writes \(R\) for \(P\) from
``Since \(P\) is flat'' to the end of the proof.}

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:remark:5-15}Remark 5.15.}\enspace
The argument applies, more generally, if one only assumes \(S\)
geometrically unibranch instead of normal, that is, that the canonical
morphism \(\widetilde S\to S\), where \(\widetilde S\) is the normalization
of \(S_{\mathrm{red}}\), is radicial. Hence Theorem 5.16 below is also valid
under this more general condition.\footnote{Translator's note: in more
detail: \(S\) being connected, locally noetherian and unibranch at each
point, its irreducible components are disjoint, hence open, so that \(S\) is
irreducible; and by EGA~IV\(_4\), 18.10.7 (cf.\ 17.5.7), the étale
\(S\)-prescheme \(P\) is the sum of irreducible geometrically unibranch
preschemes indexed by the points of \(P_s\), \(s\) the generic point of
\(S\). Each connected component of \(P\) therefore meets \(P_s\) in a single
point, and 5.13 applies as above. The Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009),
Expos\'e~X, states 5.14 and 5.16 directly for geometrically unibranch \(S\),
with these details (EGA~IV\(_4\), 18.10.6 and 18.10.7).}

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:theorem:5-16}Theorem 5.16.}\enspace
\emph{Let \(S\) be a locally noetherian normal prescheme. Then every group
of multiplicative type and of finite type over \(S\) is isotrivial.}

Indeed, we may suppose \(S\) connected, and hence \(H\) of constant type
\(M\). It is enough to apply \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:5-14}{5.14} to
\[
  P=\operatorname{Isom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(H,G),
  \qquad G=D_S(M),
\]
reasoning as in \SGARefLink{sga3:x:proposition:5-11}{5.11} \(\mathrm{(ii)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(i)}\); one may also apply
\SGARefLink{sga3:x:proposition:5-11}{5.11}, using \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:5-14}{5.14} for \(R=D_S(H)\).\footnote{Translator's note:
the 1970 text has ``apply 5.13'' in the first argument and ``\(R=D_S(M)\)'' in
the second; 5.14 is what is used (with 5.15 in the geometrically unibranch
case), and \(R=D_S(H)\) is quasi-isotrivial by 5.9. The Gille--Polo draft
re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, makes these corrections.}

\section*{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:section:6}6. Infinite Galois principal bundles and the enlarged fundamental group}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{6. Infinite Galois principal bundles and the
enlarged fundamental group}

(The results of the present section and of the next will not be used again
in the remainder of this Seminar.)

Let \(S\) be a prescheme. We propose to determine the principal homogeneous
bundles \(P\) over \(S\) whose structural group is of the form \(G_S\), the
constant \(S\)-group defined by an ordinary group \(G\), not necessarily
finite. We shall also call them \emph{Galois principal bundles over \(S\)
with group \(G\)}. We use ``principal bundle'' in the sense of the
faithfully flat quasi-compact topology (cf.\ Exposé IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:subsection:5:1}{5.1}). For
such a \(P\), however, the structural morphism \(P\to S\) is necessarily
étale and surjective, hence a covering for the étale topology; consequently
\(P\) is also locally trivial for the étale topology.\footnote{Translator's
note: the definition is IV \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:definition:5:1:5}{5.1.5}; and \(P\) is trivialized by the étale
covering \(P\to S\) itself, the diagonal giving a section of \(P\times_SP\)
(IV \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:5:1:6}{5.1.6}). The Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X,
gives these two references.}

We assume that \(S\) is a sum of connected preschemes, that is, that its
connected components are open; this reduces us at once to the case in which
\(S\) is connected. Choose a ``geometric point'' \(\xi\) of \(S\), that
is, an \(S\)-scheme \(\xi\) which is the spectrum of an algebraically
closed field \(\Omega=\kappa(\xi)\). For every Galois principal bundle
\(P\) over \(S\) with group \(G\), \(P_\xi\) is a Galois principal bundle
over the algebraically closed field \(\kappa(\xi)\), and is therefore
trivial. We refine the initial problem by asking for the category of Galois
principal bundles over \(S\) \emph{pointed} above \(\xi\), that is, endowed
with an \(S\)-morphism \(\xi\to P\), or equivalently with a trivialization
of \(P_\xi\). For fixed \(G\), let
\(\bar\pi^{\,1}(S,\xi;G)\) denote the set of classes of such bundles, up to
an isomorphism respecting the base point. Then the set
\(\bar\pi^{\,1}(S;G)\) of isomorphism classes of Galois principal bundles
over \(S\) with group \(G\), without a specified base point, is the set of
orbits of \(G\) in \(\bar\pi^{\,1}(S,\xi;G)\). Here \(G\) acts naturally
by translating the marked point of a pointed Galois principal bundle:
\[
  \bar\pi^{\,1}(S;G)=\bar\pi^{\,1}(S,\xi;G)/G.
\]

Let \(S'\to S\) be any morphism which is a morphism of universal effective
descent for the fibered category of twisted constant preschemes over a
variable base (for example, \(S'\to S\) faithfully flat and locally of
finite presentation, cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:x:lemma:5-4}{5.4};\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970
text has ``cf.\ 5.3''; the effective descent in question is Lemma 5.4.} for
other examples, cf.\ SGA 1 IX). We
propose to determine the subsets
\[
  \bar\pi^{\,1}(S'/S,\xi;G)
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \bar\pi^{\,1}(S'/S;G)
\]
of the preceding sets, formed by the Galois principal bundles over \(S\)
which become trivial over \(S'\), or, as one says, are ``split'' by \(S'\).
In fact, we shall determine the category of Galois principal bundles \(P\)
over \(S\) split by \(S'\). Of course,
\[
  \bar\pi^{\,1}(S,\xi;G)
  =
  \varinjlim_{S'}\bar\pi^{\,1}(S'/S,\xi;G),
\]
where \(S'\) runs through a cofinal system among the \(S'/S\) which cover
for the étale topology (for example, if \(S\) is quasi-compact, among the
quasi-compact \(S'\) over \(S\) whose structural morphism is étale and
surjective). Likewise, the category of Galois principal bundles over \(S\)
is the inductive limit of the subcategories defined by the \(S'\), formed
by the bundles split over \(S'\).

By the assumption on \(S'/S\), the category of Galois principal bundles
over \(S\) split by \(S'\) is equivalent to the category of trivial Galois
principal bundles over \(S'\), hence of the form \(G_{S'}\), with \(G\)
acting by right translations, equipped with descent data relative to
\(S'\to S\). In terms of the corresponding trivial bundle \(P'\) over
\(S'\) and its descent datum, a base point on a Galois principal bundle
\(P\) over \(S\) split by \(S'\) amounts to a trivialization of
\(P'\times_{S'}S'_\xi\) compatible with the induced descent datum relative
to \(S'_\xi\to\xi\), where \(S'_\xi=S'\times_S\xi\); equivalently, it is a
section of \(P'_\xi\) over \(S'_\xi\) compatible with the
descent datum.

It is therefore useful, for an arbitrary \(S\)-prescheme \(S'\), without
now assuming that \(S'\to S\) is a morphism of universal effective descent
for the fibered category of twisted constant bundles, to define
\(\bar\pi^{\,1}(S'/S;G)\) and
\(\bar\pi^{\,1}(S'/S,\xi;G)\) as the sets of isomorphism classes of the
structures with descent data just specified. These functors in \(G\) then
have a very simple simplicial description in terms of the fibered square
and cube \(S''\) and \(S'''\) of \(S'\) over \(S\), which we shall sketch
below.

The important conclusion to retain will be the following. Suppose that the
connected components of \(S''\) are open, and that the pointed simplicial set
\(\widetilde K_\bullet\) defined below is connected (for example, that
\(S'\to S\) is surjective and that the quotient of \(\pi_0(S')\) by the
equivalence relation induced by the two projections
\(\pi_0(S'')\to\pi_0(S')\) is a single point).\footnote{Translator's note: the
1970 text assumes only that the connected components of \(S''\) are open.
The Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, adds that those of
\(S'\) are open, which already follows (a connected component of \(S'\) is
the inverse image under the diagonal of the component of \(S''\) containing
its image), and that \(\pi_0(S')\) modulo the two projections is a single
point. Some such condition is needed, but this one is not enough by itself:
let \(S\) be the affine plane over a field, \(\xi\) a geometric point over its
generic point, and \(S'\) the normalization of a plane nodal cubic; then
\(S'_\xi\) is empty, \(S''\) is the diagonal plus two points over the node,
and \(\bar\pi^{\,1}(S'/S,\xi;G)\) is the set of conjugacy classes of \(G\),
which is not a representable functor of \(G\).} Then the functor
\[
  G\longmapsto\bar\pi^{\,1}(S'/S,\xi;G)
\]
from the category of groups to the category of sets is representable by a
group, which will be denoted \(\pi_1(S'/S;\xi)\) and called the
\emph{fundamental group of \(S\) at \(\xi\) relative to the morphism
\(S'\to S\)}. We thus have a functorial bijection
\[
  \bar\pi^{\,1}(S'/S,\xi;G)
  \simeq
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr.}}\bigl(\pi_1(S'/S;\xi),G\bigr).
\]
This group has a set of generators in one-to-one correspondence with the
elements of \(\pi_0(S'')\) (in particular, it is finitely generated if
\(S''\) has only finitely many connected components). When the connected
components of \(S'''\) are also open, \(\pi_1(S'/S;\xi)\) is completely
described, in terms of the generators coming from \(\pi_0(S'')\), by
relations in one-to-one correspondence with the elements of \(\pi_0(S''')\)
(in particular, if moreover \(\pi_0(S'')\) and \(\pi_0(S''')\) are finite,
the group \(\pi_1(S'/S;\xi)\) is finitely presented).\footnote{Translator's
note: the hypothesis on \(S'''\) is not needed, as the Gille--Polo draft
re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, observes: the cocycle condition asserts the
equality of two locally constant maps from \(S'''\) to the group, which may
be tested on each connected component of \(S'''\), open or not.}

One also obtains a description of the category of Galois principal bundles
over \(S\) split by \(S'\), pointed above \(\xi\), as equivalent to the
category of ordinary groups \(G\) endowed with a homomorphism of
\(\pi_1(S'/S;\xi)\) into \(G\).

When \(S\) is a connected locally noetherian prescheme, every étale
prescheme \(S'\) over \(S\) is locally noetherian and therefore has open
connected components. It follows from the preceding
discussion\footnote{Translator's note: explicitly, one can take for \(I\) a
cofinal set of étale surjective \(S'\to S\), ordered by refinement, and
\(\pi_i=\pi_1(S'_i/S;\xi)\): for such \(S'\), \(S\) being connected, the
hypotheses of the conclusion above hold; and when \(S'_j\) refines \(S'_i\),
the inclusions
\(\bar\pi^{\,1}(S'_i/S,\xi;G)\subset\bar\pi^{\,1}(S'_j/S,\xi;G)\) define a
homomorphism \(\pi_j\to\pi_i\), which is surjective since these maps are
injective for every \(G\). The Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009),
Expos\'e~X, indicates this.} that the functor
\[
  G\longmapsto\bar\pi^{\,1}(S,\xi;G)
\]
from ordinary groups to sets is \emph{strictly pro-representable}
(cf.\ \emph{Séminaire Bourbaki}, February 1960, No.~195,
\S\S~A.2 and A.3). Thus there is a projective system
\[
  \Pi=\Pi_1(S;\xi)=(\pi_i)_{i\in I}
\]
of ordinary groups over an increasing filtered index set \(I\), which is
``strict'', meaning that its transition morphisms
\(\pi_j\to\pi_i\) are surjective, together with an isomorphism of functors
in \(G\)
\[
  \bar\pi^{\,1}(S,\xi;G)
  \simeq
  \varinjlim_i\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr.}}(\pi_i,G).
\]
The second member is also denoted
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{pro\text{-}gr.}}(\Pi,G)\)
(cf.\ \emph{loc.\ cit.}).

If the projective limit
\[
  \pi=\varprojlim_i\pi_i
\]
is ``large enough'', more precisely if the canonical homomorphisms
\(\pi\to\pi_i\) are surjective, it is natural to equip \(\pi\) with the
projective-limit topology of the discrete topologies on the \(\pi_i\).
The preceding isomorphism can then also be written
\[
  \bar\pi^{\,1}(S,\xi;G)
  \simeq
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr.\,top.}}(\pi,G),
\]
where the right-hand side is the set of homomorphisms of topological
groups, \(G\) being given the discrete topology.

The hypothesis just formulated on the projective system \(\Pi\) holds, as
is well known, when the \(\pi_i\) are finite groups.\footnote{Translator's
note: for every \(x\in\pi_i\), the inverse images of \(x\) in the \(\pi_j\),
\(j\geq i\), form a projective system of nonempty finite sets, whose limit is
nonempty by compactness; the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009),
Expos\'e~X, refers here to N.~Bourbaki, \emph{Th\'eorie des ensembles},
chap.~III, \S~7, no.~4, th.~1.} This last condition plainly also means
that every Galois principal bundle over \(S\) is isotrivial, that is, split
by a finite étale surjective morphism. This is the case when \(S\) is
geometrically unibranch (for example normal), as follows at once from
\SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:5-14}{5.14}.\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text refers to 5.13; the
statement used is 5.14, with Remark 5.15 in the geometrically unibranch case.
The Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, corrects the
reference.}
When the \(\pi_i\) are finite, \(\pi\) also coincides with the fundamental
group \(\pi_1(S,\xi)\) introduced in SGA 1, V.

Thus, in the favorable case in which the maps \(\pi\to\pi_i\) are
surjective, one might call \(\pi\) the \emph{enlarged fundamental group of
\(S\) at \(\xi\)}. Outside this favorable case, \(\pi\) itself is of
little interest, and the role of the usual fundamental group is played by
the projective system \(\Pi\) itself, which we call the \emph{enlarged
fundamental pro-group of \(S\) at \(\xi\)}. (Any better terminological
suggestion than ``enlarged'' is welcome!) Knowledge of this
pro-group is more precise than knowledge of the usual fundamental group
\(\pi_1(S,\xi)\) of SGA 1, V; more precisely, the latter is the projective
limit of the projective system formed by the finite quotients of the
\(\pi_i\).

Let us indicate briefly the ``calculation'' of \(\pi_1(S'/S;\xi)\). Let
\(S_i\) be the \((i+1)\)-st fibered power of \(S'\) over \(S\). The evident
simplicial operations
between the \(S_i\) make \((S_i)_{i\in\mathbb N}\) a simplicial object of
\((\mathrm{Sch})/S\).

Applying to this simplicial object the functor ``set of connected
components''
\[
  \pi_0:(\mathrm{Sch})/S\longrightarrow(\mathrm{Ens}),
\]
we obtain a simplicial set \(K_\bullet=(K_i)_{i\in\mathbb N}\), with
\[
  K_i=\pi_0(S_i).
\]

Likewise, the \(S_{i,\xi}\) form a simplicial object of
\((\mathrm{Sch})/\xi\), hence of \((\mathrm{Sch})/S\), together with a
natural morphism of simplicial objects into \((S_i)_{i\in\mathbb N}\).
Thus we obtain a simplicial set \(k_\bullet\), with
\[
  k_i=\pi_0(S_{i,\xi}),
\]
and a canonical morphism
\[
  k_\bullet\longrightarrow K_\bullet.
\]
Granting this, we can form a new simplicial set, the cone of this morphism
(cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:x:definition:9-5-1}{the Addition at the end of the exposé, (b)}):
\[
  \widetilde K_\bullet
  =
  \operatorname{Cone}(k_\bullet\longrightarrow K_\bullet).
\]
\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text takes instead
\(\widetilde K_\bullet=K_\bullet/k_\bullet\), obtained by contracting the
image of each \(k_i\) in \(K_i\) to a point, and writes the result below as
\(\pi_1(K_\bullet/k_\bullet)\). This is not correct in general: \(k_\bullet\)
is contractible, but \(k_\bullet\to K_\bullet\) need not be injective, and
contracting its image can kill loops. For the nodal curve \(S\) of the
examples below, its normalization \(S'\) and \(\xi\) over the double point,
the image of \(k_\bullet\) is all of \(K_\bullet\) in degrees \(\leq1\), so
that \(K_\bullet/k_\bullet\) has trivial fundamental group, whereas
\(\pi_1(S'/S;\xi)=\mathbb Z\). The Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009),
Expos\'e~X, makes this correction. The cone is obtained from \(K_\bullet\)
and \(k_\bullet\times\Delta^1\) by attaching \(k_\bullet\times\{0\}\) along
\(k_\bullet\to K_\bullet\) and contracting \(k_\bullet\times\{1\}\) to a point,
the base point.}

In this way we obtain a ``pointed simplicial set'' \(\widetilde K_\bullet\),
that is, a simplicial set equipped with a morphism
\[
  \widetilde\xi:e_\bullet\longrightarrow\widetilde K_\bullet,
\]
where \(e_\bullet\) is the final simplicial set. We may form its familiar
combinatorial invariants
\[
  \pi_0(\widetilde K_\bullet,\widetilde\xi)
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \pi_1(\widetilde K_\bullet,\widetilde\xi),
\]
whose constructions involve only the components of degree at most \(1\)
and at most \(2\), respectively. These invariants are defined without any
restriction on \(S\) or \(S'\). When the connected components of \(S_0\)
and \(S_1\) are open and \(\widetilde K_\bullet\) is
connected,\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text does not assume
\(\widetilde K_\bullet\) connected; see the note to the conclusion stated
above. The Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, adds this
hypothesis and proves the assertion in its addenda (cf.\ the Addition at the
end of this exposé).} one checks without difficulty that
\(\pi_1(\widetilde K_\bullet,\widetilde\xi)\) represents the functor
\(G\mapsto\bar\pi^{\,1}(S'/S,\xi;G)\); that is,
\[
  \pi_1(S'/S;\xi)
  \simeq
  \pi_1(\widetilde K_\bullet,\widetilde\xi).
\]

We also note that if \(S'\to S\) is ``universally submersive''
(cf.\ SGA 1, IX 2.1) and the connected components of \(S_0=S'\) are open,
then \(\pi_0(\widetilde K_\bullet,\widetilde\xi)\) is also canonically
isomorphic to the set \(\pi_0(S,\xi)\) of connected components of \(S\),
pointed by the connected component of \(\xi\) in \(S\).\footnote{Translator's
note: the 1970 text cites SGA~1, IV 2.1 for universally submersive
morphisms, which are defined in SGA~1, IX 2.1. The assertion remains true for
the cone \(\widetilde K_\bullet\): \(S'_\xi\) is nonempty, \(S'\to S\) being
surjective, so that \(\pi_0(\widetilde K_\bullet)=\pi_0(K_\bullet)\), and the
unions of the classes of connected components of \(S'\) under the relation
defined by \(S''\) are the inverse images of the connected components of
\(S\), \(S\) carrying the quotient topology. The Gille--Polo draft re-edition
(2009), Expos\'e~X, replaces it by the statement that \(K_\bullet\), and hence
\(\widetilde K_\bullet\), is connected (\(S\) being connected).}

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:x:example:6-4}Examples.}\enspace
It remains to give examples of enlarged fundamental groups. Take for example
a complete rational curve over an algebraically closed field \(k\), having
exactly one singular point, that point being an ordinary double point, or a
curve which is the union of two irreducible components that are complete
nonsingular rational curves, meeting in exactly two points, which are
ordinary double points of the union curve. In either case, the enlarged
fundamental group of the curve \(C\) is isomorphic to \(\mathbb Z\) (compare
with the examples \SGARefLink{sga3:x:paragraph:1:6}{1.6}). We shall not give the verification of these facts
here; they are of an essentially ``combinatorial'' nature, once one admits
the descent results of SGA 1, IX and the well-known fact that a complete
nonsingular rational curve is simply connected (cf.\ for example SGA 1,
XI).\footnote{Translator's note: in outline (the Gille--Polo draft
re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, sketches this): by 5.14 and the above, every
Galois principal bundle over \(\mathbb P^1_k\) is isotrivial, hence trivial,
\(\mathbb P^1_k\) being simply connected (SGA~1, XI 1.1); so its enlarged
fundamental group is zero. Granting the descent results of SGA~1, IX (cf.\
its 5.4) for bundles with an arbitrary discrete group, a Galois principal
bundle with group \(G\) over the curve obtained from \(\mathbb P^1_k\) by
identifying two points \(a\) and \(b\) is the trivial bundle on
\(\mathbb P^1_k\) with an identification of its fibers at \(a\) and \(b\),
that is, an element of \(G\) acting by left translation; with a base point,
these are classified by \(G=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z,G)\). For two copies
of \(\mathbb P^1_k\) glued at two pairs of points, gluing one pair gives a
curve with zero enlarged fundamental group, and gluing the second pair
introduces one such element.}

More generally, one should take up, simplifying and correcting them, the
results of SGA 1, IX 5 in the setting of the enlarged fundamental group.
The examples of \emph{loc.\ cit.}, 5.5,\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970
text has ``the examples 5.5 and 5.6''; in SGA~1 as published (Lecture Notes
in Mathematics 224, 1971), IX 5.6 is a corollary, not an example. The
Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~X, corrects the reference.}
would give as many examples of enlarged fundamental pro-groups which are not
profinite. Thus, if in the first example cited above the ordinary double
point were replaced by a double point with \(n\) distinct
branches,\footnote{Translator's note: that is, a singular point with \(n\)
distinct branches, for instance an ordinary \(n\)-fold point, such as the
origin on the rational curve \(X^n-Y^n=X^{n+1}\) when the characteristic of
