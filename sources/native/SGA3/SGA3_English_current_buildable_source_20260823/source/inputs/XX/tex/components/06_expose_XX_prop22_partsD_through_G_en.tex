
\textup{D)} Suppose first that \(S\) is the spectrum of a field.
If \(F'(0)=0\), then \(F=1\). Formula \((5'')\) then gives
\[
  H(x+y)=H(x)H(y),
\]
which shows that \(H\) extends to a group morphism
\[
  \mathbb G_{a,S}\longrightarrow T
\]
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:xviii:prop:2.3}{Exposé XVIII, 2.3})}, necessarily the constant morphism
with value \(e\). We also have \(E(x)=1+bx\), but formula \((6'')\)
then gives \(b=0\),\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XX, details this step and corrects the values
``\(F,G,H\) \ldots\ \((0,e,0)\)'' of the 1970 text. In our formulation: with \(F=1\) and \(H=e\),
\((6'')\) reads \((x+y)E(x+y)^{-1}=xE(x)^{-1}+yE(y)^{-1}\) for \((x,y)\in N(S')\), and
\(E(x)=1+bx\) by Lemma 2.3. Clearing denominators turns this into
\(bxy\bigl(2+b(x+y)\bigr)=0\) on a nonempty open subset of the plane, hence as a polynomial
identity; its coefficient of \(x^2y\) is \(b^2\), so \(b=0\).} which shows that \(F,H,E\) are constant with
values \((1,e,1)\) in a neighborhood of the zero section, and hence
everywhere; by \((+)\), this shows that \(U_\alpha\) and
\(U_{-\alpha}\) commute, contrary to hypothesis \textup{(ii)}.

For arbitrary \(S\), we have therefore proved that \(F'(0)\) is
nonzero on every fiber, and hence invertible. The same is plainly
true of \(E'(0)\). \SGARefLink{sga3:xx:2.3}{Lemma 2.3} now shows that there are
\(a,b\in\mathbb G_m(S)\) such that
\[
  F(x)=1+ax,\qquad E(x)=1+bx
  \qquad\text{for }x\in W(S').
\]

\textup{E)} The rest is now easy. Substituting the preceding
results in \((3'')\), one finds at once
\[
  y\,\alpha\bigl(H(x)\bigr)
   =y\bigl(1+ax+ay(1+bx)\bigr)(1+ax)(1+ay)^{-1}.
\]
This formula holds for a section \((x,y)\) of \(N\). But since
\(\mathbb G_{a,S}\times_S\mathbb G_{m,S}\) is schematically dense
in \(\mathbb G_{a,S}^{\,2}\), one deduces
\[
  \alpha\bigl(H(x)\bigr)
   =\bigl(1+ax+ay(1+bx)\bigr)(1+ax)(1+ay)^{-1}.
\]
Setting \(y=0\), this gives \(\alpha\bigl(H(x)\bigr)=(1+ax)^2\).\footnote{Translator's note:
in this formula and the two preceding ones, the 1970 text has the factor \(1+bx\) in place of
\(1+ax\); the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XX, corrects it. With \(F(x)=1+ax\) and
\(E(x)=1+bx\), \((3'')\) gives \(\alpha(H(x))^{-1}(1+ay)^{-1}
=\bigl(1+a(x+yE(x))\bigr)^{-1}(1+ax)^{-1}\), whence the factor \(1+ax\). Since \(1+bx\) occurs on
both sides of the printed comparison, it led nevertheless to the same relation \(a^2xy=abxy\).}
Identifying the two preceding expressions, one finds
\(a^2xy=abxy\). Arguing as before, one deduces \(a^2=ab\), and, as
\(a\) is invertible, \(a=b\), whence
\(\alpha\bigl(H(x)\bigr)=(1+ax)^2\).

Because \(a\) is invertible, the map \(x\mapsto1+ax\) is an
automorphism of \(\mathbb G_{a,S}\). There are therefore an open
subset \(W'\) of \(\mathbb G_{a,S}\), containing the section \(1\),
and a morphism
\[
  P\colon W'\longrightarrow T
\]
such that
\[
  P(1+ax)=H(x).
\]
Substitution in \((2')\) gives immediately, for
\((x,y)\in M(S')\),\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text refers here to \((2'')\) and to
\((x,y)\in N(S')\). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XX, reads \((2')\), which gives the
displayed relation exactly, for \((x,y)\) in \(M\) (written \((v,w)\) above); \((2'')\) gives
equally \(P\bigl((1+ax)(1+ay)\bigr)=P(1+ax)P(1+ay)\) for \((x,y)\in N(S')\).}
\[
  P(1+ax+ay)
   =P\left(\frac{1+ax+ay}{1+ax}\right)P(1+ax).
\]
Thus there is an open neighborhood of \(1\) in
\(\mathbb G_{m,S}\) on which
\[
  P(x)P(y)=P(xy).
\]
By \SGARefLink{sga3:xviii:prop:2.3}{Exposé XVIII, 2.3}, there is a group morphism
\[
  \alpha^\ast\colon\mathbb G_{m,S}\longrightarrow T
\]
extending \(P\). Since
\(\alpha(H(x))=(1+ax)^2\) near the zero section, we have
\(\alpha(\alpha^\ast(z))=z^2\) near the section \(1\), and hence
\[
  \alpha\circ\alpha^\ast=2.
\]

\textup{F)}
Collecting the results obtained, we see that there are an open
subset \(V'\) of \(\mathbb G_{a,S}^{\,2}\) containing the unit
section, and
\[
  a\in\mathbb G_m(S),\qquad
  \alpha^\ast\in
    \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(\mathbb G_{m,S},T)
\]
such that \(\alpha\circ\alpha^\ast=2\), and such that, for
\((u,v)\in V'(S')\), the element \(1+auv\)
is invertible and
\[
  p_\alpha(u)p_{-\alpha}(v)
   =p_{-\alpha}\left(\frac{v}{1+auv}\right)
      \alpha^\ast(1+auv)
      p_\alpha\left(\frac{u}{1+auv}\right).
\]

Consider the open subset \(V\) of \(\mathbb G_{a,S}^{\,2}\) defined
by ``\(1+auv\) invertible''; that is,
\[
  V=\bigl(\mathbb G_{a,S}^{\,2}\bigr)_f,
  \qquad f(u,v)=1+auv.
\]
The two sides of the preceding formula define morphisms
\(V\to G\) which agree in a neighborhood of the zero section,
and hence agree on \(V\). Thus the formula holds for every
section \((u,v)\) of \(V\). In particular \(V\subset U\),
where \(U\) is the open subset of part A.

We prove that \(U=V\). Returning to the notation of part A, there
is a morphism
\[
  A\colon U\longrightarrow\mathbb G_{a,S}
\]
which, on \(V\), is given by
\[
  A(u,v)=v(1+auv)^{-1}.
\]
The equality \(U=V\) is a set-theoretic question and may therefore
be checked when \(S=\operatorname{Spec}(k)\) for a field \(k\).
It then reduces to the evident assertion that the domain of
definition of the rational map
\[
  \mathbb G_{a,k}^{\,2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb G_{a,k},
  \qquad
  (X,Y)\longmapsto\frac{Y}{1+aXY},
\]
is the open subset defined by \(1+aXY\).

\textup{G)} We have proved the existence of \(a\) and
\(\alpha^\ast\), as well as the two asserted supplementary
properties. It remains to prove uniqueness. Let
\(a'\) and \(\alpha^{\ast\prime}\) also satisfy the required
conditions. For \(u,v\in\mathbb G_a(S')\), we immediately have
\[
\begin{gathered}
  1+auv\text{ invertible}
    \ \Longrightarrow\
  1+a'uv\text{ invertible},\\
  \frac{v}{1+auv}=\frac{v}{1+a'uv}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Hence, for every \(u\in\mathbb G_a(S')\),
\[
  1+au\text{ invertible}
    \ \Longrightarrow\
  1+au=1+a'u,
\]
which proves \(a=a'\). With the same notation,
\[
  1+au\text{ invertible}
    \ \Longrightarrow\
  \alpha^\ast(1+au)=\alpha^{\ast\prime}(1+au),
\]
and therefore \(\alpha^\ast=\alpha^{\ast\prime}\).
\qed
