Let \(x,y\in\mathbb G_a(S')\), where \(S'\to S\), and suppose that
\(y\) and \(1+xy\) are invertible.  Applying \SGARefLink{sga3:xx:6.2.1}{Lemma 6.2.1} first with
\(z=y\), one obtains
\[
\begin{aligned}
 f_\alpha(xu)f_{-\alpha}(y\widetilde u)
 &=f_\alpha\bigl((x+y^{-1})u\bigr)\cdot
   f_T\bigl(\alpha^\ast(-y^{-1})\bigr)\cdot h\cdot f_\alpha(y^{-1}u)\\
 &=f_\alpha\bigl((x+y^{-1})u\bigr)\cdot
   f_T\bigl(\alpha^\ast(-(x+y^{-1}))\bigr)\\
 &\qquad{}\cdot
   f_T\!\left(\alpha^\ast\!\left(\frac{y^{-1}}{x+y^{-1}}\right)\right)
   \cdot h\cdot f_\alpha(y^{-1}u).
\end{aligned}
\]
Now \(x+y^{-1}=y^{-1}(1+xy)\).  Applying the lemma with
\(z=y/(1+xy)\), one finds\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XX, details these steps. In our formulation:
the second equality uses \(-y^{-1}=-(x+y^{-1})\cdot y^{-1}(x+y^{-1})^{-1}\); the next one uses
\SGARefLink{sga3:xx:6.2.1}{6.2.1} for \(z=y/(1+xy)\), whose inverse is \(x+y^{-1}\), and
\(y^{-1}/(x+y^{-1})=(1+xy)^{-1}\); then \(h^{-1}f_T(\alpha^\ast(c))h=f_T(\alpha^\ast(c^{-1}))\) by
\textup{(2)} and \textup{(3)}, since \(s_\alpha(\alpha^\ast(c))=\alpha^\ast(c^{-1})\); then
\(f_\alpha(x')f_T(\alpha^\ast(c))=f_T(\alpha^\ast(c))f_\alpha(c^{-2}x')\) by \textup{(1)}; finally
\(-(x+y^{-1})(1+xy)^{-2}+y^{-1}=x(1+xy)^{-1}\).}
\[
\begin{aligned}
 f_\alpha(xu)f_{-\alpha}(y\widetilde u)
 &=f_{-\alpha}\!\left(\frac{y}{1+xy}\widetilde u\right)\cdot
   f_\alpha\bigl(-(x+y^{-1})u\bigr)\\
 &\qquad{}\cdot h^{-1}f_T\bigl(\alpha^\ast(1+xy)^{-1}\bigr)h\cdot
   f_\alpha(y^{-1}u)\\
 &=f_{-\alpha}\!\left(\frac{y}{1+xy}\widetilde u\right)\cdot
   f_\alpha\bigl(-(x+y^{-1})u\bigr)\cdot
   f_T\bigl(\alpha^\ast(1+xy)\bigr)\cdot
   f_\alpha(y^{-1}u)\\
 &=f_{-\alpha}\!\left(\frac{y}{1+xy}\widetilde u\right)\cdot
   f_T\bigl(\alpha^\ast(1+xy)\bigr)\\
 &\qquad{}\cdot
   f_\alpha\Bigl(\bigl(-(x+y^{-1})(1+xy)^{-2}+y^{-1}\bigr)u\Bigr)\\
 &=f_{-\alpha}\!\left(\frac{y}{1+xy}\widetilde u\right)\cdot
   f_T\bigl(\alpha^\ast(1+xy)\bigr)\cdot
   f_\alpha\!\left(\frac{x}{1+xy}u\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
We have therefore proved:

\paragraph{\textbf{Lemma 6.2.2.}}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xx:6.2.2}
\emph{If \(a\in U_\alpha(S')\), \(b\in U_{-\alpha}^\times(S')\),
\(S'\to S\), are such that \(1+ab\in\mathbb G_m(S')\), then}
\[
 f_\alpha(a)f_{-\alpha}(b)
 =
 f_{-\alpha}\!\left(\frac{b}{1+ab}\right)
 f_T\bigl(\alpha^\ast(1+ab)\bigr)
 f_\alpha\!\left(\frac{a}{1+ab}\right).
\]

By schematic density, this formula remains valid for
\(b\in U_{-\alpha}(S')\), with \(1+ab\) invertible.  Define
\[
 f\colon\Omega\longrightarrow H,
 \qquad
 f(ytx)=f_{-\alpha}(y)f_T(t)f_\alpha(x).
\]
It follows at once from \SGARefLink{sga3:xx:6.2.2}{Lemma 6.2.2}, condition \textup{(i)}, and
formula \textup{(F')} of \SGARefLink{sga3:xx:2.4}{2.4} that whenever
\(g,g'\in\Omega(S')\) and \(gg'\in\Omega(S')\), one has
\[
 f(gg')=f(g)f(g').
\]
By \SGARefLink{sga3:xviii:prop:2.3}{Exposé XVIII, 2.3}\textup{\SGARefLink{sga3:xviii:prop:2.3:item:iii}{(iii)}} and \SGARefLink{sga3:xx:2.4}{2.4}, there is therefore
a group morphism \(G\to H\) extending \(f\).  Denote it again by
\(f\).  It remains only to prove that \(f(w_\alpha)=h\).  Now
\[
 w_\alpha=u\cdot(-\widetilde u)\cdot u,
\]
whence\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XX, shortens this computation: by the relation
\(f_{-\alpha}(-\widetilde u)=f_\alpha(-u)hf_\alpha(-u)\) obtained above,
\(f_\alpha(u)f_{-\alpha}(-\widetilde u)f_\alpha(u)=h\) at once. In the computation given here,
\(h^{-1}=h^3\) and \(h^2\) commutes with \(f_\alpha(u)\), by \textup{(3)} and \textup{(1)}, since
\(h^4=e\); and \(\bigl(f_\alpha(u)h\bigr)^3=e\) is \textup{(4)} conjugated by \(h\).}
\[
\begin{aligned}
 f(w_\alpha)
 &=f_\alpha(u)f_{-\alpha}(-\widetilde u)f_\alpha(u)
  =f_\alpha(u)hf_\alpha(u)h^{-1}f_\alpha(u)\\
 &=f_\alpha(u)hf_\alpha(u)hf_\alpha(u)h^2
  =\bigl(f_\alpha(u)h\bigr)^3h=h.
\end{aligned}
\]
This completes the proof of \SGARefLink{sga3:xx:6.2}{Theorem 6.2}.
