
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{4.1.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:4.1.3}

More generally, for every \(S\)-functor \(X\), define the
\(S\)-functor \(X^{(p^n)}\) recursively in \(n\) by
\[
  X^{(p)}=X^{(p/S)}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  X^{(p^n)}
  =
  \bigl(X^{(p^{n-1})}\bigr)^{(p)}.
\]
Likewise, \(\operatorname{Fr}^n(X/S)\), or simply
\(\operatorname{Fr}^n\), denotes the composite functorial
homomorphism
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:4.1.3-iterated-relative-frobenius-chain}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_041}
\end{center}
If \(G\) is a group \(S\)-functor, then \(G^{(p^n)}\) is again a group
\(S\)-functor and \(\operatorname{Fr}^n(G/S)\) is a homomorphism of
group \(S\)-functors. We denote the kernel of
\(\operatorname{Fr}^n(G/S)\) by \(\operatorname{Fr}^nG\), and we say that
\(G\) has \emph{height} \(\leq n\) if \(\operatorname{Fr}^n(G/S)\) is
zero, that is, if \(\operatorname{Fr}^nG=G\). The group subfunctor
\(\operatorname{Fr}^nG\) of \(G\) is characteristic, in the sense that,
for every \(S\)-scheme \(T\), every endomorphism \(f\) of the group
\(T\)-functor \(G_T\) induces an endomorphism of
\((\operatorname{Fr}^nG)_T\): indeed, since the construction of
\(G^{(p^n)}\) and of \(\operatorname{Fr}^n(G/S)\) commutes with base
change by \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:4.1.1}{4.1.1}, we may assume \(T=S\); in this case, the
assertion follows from the fact that \(\operatorname{Fr}^n(G/S)\) is a
functorial homomorphism.

Finally, note that \(\operatorname{Fr}(X^{(p)}/S)\) coincides with
\(\operatorname{Fr}(X/S)^{(p)}\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{4.1.4.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:4.1.4}
Here are some examples:

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{a)}
Let \(M\) first be an ``abstract'' abelian group, and let
\[
  G=D_S(M)
\]
be the diagonalizable group of type \(M\) (I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:4-4}{4.4}). For every
\(S\)-scheme \(T\),
\[
  G(T)
  =
  \operatorname{Hom}_{(\mathbf{Ab})}
    \bigl(M,\Gamma(T,\mathcal O_T)^\times\bigr).
\]
Since \(G\) is the pullback of the diagonalizable group \(D(M)\)
over \(\mathbb F_p\), \(G^{(p)}\) identifies with \(G\), and
\(\operatorname{Fr}(G/S)(T)\) identifies with the endomorphism
\(x\mapsto x^p\) of \(G(T)\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:4.1.1}{4.1.1}). In particular, when
\(M\) is equal to \(\mathbb Z\), one has \(D_S(M)=\mathbb G_{m,S}\), so
that \(\operatorname{Fr}\mathbb G_{m,S}\) is the \(S\)-group
\(\boldsymbol\mu_{p,S}\) which associates to every \(S\)-scheme \(T\) the
group of \(p\)-th roots of unity of \(\Gamma(T,\mathcal O_T)\).

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{b)}
Now let \(S\) be a scheme of characteristic \(p\), and let
\(\mathcal E\) be a sheaf of modules on \(S\). By I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:proposition:4-6-2}{4.6.2} there is
a canonical isomorphism
\[
  \mathcal W(\mathcal E)^{(p)}
  \xrightarrow{\sim}
  \mathcal W(\mathcal E^{(p)}),
\]
where \(\mathcal E^{(p)}\) is the pullback of \(\mathcal E\) along
\(\operatorname{fr}(S)\). Moreover, by \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:4.1}{4.1}, the map
\(\operatorname{Fr}(\mathcal W(\mathcal E)/S)(\pi)\) is determined, for
every \(S\)-scheme \(\pi:T\to S\), by the commutative triangle
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:4.1.4-vector-group-frobenius-triangle}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_043}
\end{center}
where \(f'\) is the map induced by \(\operatorname{fr}(T)\).

In particular, if \(\mathcal E=\mathcal O_S\), then
\(\mathcal W(\mathcal E)\) identifies with the additive group
\(\mathbb G_{a,S}\). In this case
\(\mathcal E^{(p)}=\mathcal E=\mathcal O_S\), and the Frobenius
morphism \(\operatorname{Fr}(\mathbb G_{a,S}/S)\) sends
\(x\in\Gamma(T,\mathcal O_T)\) to \(x^p\). The kernel
\(\boldsymbol\alpha_{p,S}\) of the Frobenius morphism of
\(\mathbb G_{a,S}\) therefore associates to every \(S\)-scheme \(T\) the
set of sections \(x\) of \(\mathcal O_T\) such that \(x^p=0\).

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{c)}
Similarly, for every quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_S\)-algebra
\(\mathcal A\), the scheme
\((\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A)^{(p)}\) identifies with
\(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A^{(p)}\), the spectrum of the
pullback of \(\mathcal A\) along \(\operatorname{fr}(S)\). If
\(\pi\) denotes the endomorphism \(x\mapsto x^p\) of the sheaf of
rings \(\mathcal O_S\), then
\[
  \mathcal A^{(p)}
  =
  \mathcal A\otimes_\pi\mathcal O_S,
\]
and it is clear that \(\operatorname{Fr}((\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A)/S)\)
is induced by the homomorphism
\[
  a\otimes_\pi x\longmapsto a^p x
\]
from \(\mathcal A\otimes_\pi\mathcal O_S\) to \(\mathcal A\).

Finally, for every quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_S\)-module
\(\mathcal E\), there are canonical isomorphisms
\[
  \mathcal V(\mathcal E)^{(p)}
  \xrightarrow{\sim}
  \mathcal V(\mathcal E^{(p)})
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \mathcal S(\mathcal E)^{(p)}
  \xrightarrow{\sim}
  \mathcal S(\mathcal E^{(p)}),
\]
where \(\mathcal S(\mathcal E)\) denotes the symmetric algebra of
the \(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(\mathcal E\).

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{d)}
Let \(\mathcal U\) be an \(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebra (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:3.1}{3.1}), and let
\(S\) be a scheme of characteristic \(p\).\footnote{Translator's note:
the 1970 text, followed by the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), has
``\(T\)'' for ``\(S\)'' here. The re-edition also specifies that the last
condition below means that the image of
\(\gamma\otimes_{\operatorname{fr}(T)}1\) in
\(\Gamma(T,\mathcal U_T\otimes_{\operatorname{fr}(T)}\mathcal O_T)\)
is \(1\).} If
\(\mathcal U^{(p/S)}\), or \(\mathcal U^{(p)}\), denotes the inverse
image of the coalgebra \(\mathcal U\) under \(\operatorname{fr}(S)\),
then, as in \textit{b)}, there is a canonical isomorphism
\[
  \bigl(\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U\bigr)^{(p)}
  \simeq
  \operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U^{(p)}.
\]
If \(\mathcal U\) is a coalgebra in groups, the value of
\(\operatorname{Fr}(\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U)\) at an
\(S\)-scheme \(T\) is therefore the set of elements \(\gamma\) of
\(\Gamma(T,\mathcal U_T)\) such that
\[
  \varepsilon_{\mathcal U_T}(\gamma)=1,
  \qquad
  \Delta_{\mathcal U_T}\gamma=\gamma\otimes\gamma
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \gamma\otimes_{\operatorname{fr}(T)}1=1.
\]
