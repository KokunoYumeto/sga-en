
\medskip
\noindent\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, 5.5; our formulation). Expos\'es~III and
VI\textsubscript{A} refer to the following consequences of 5.2 and 5.4. A
Noetherian local ring is a \emph{complete intersection} if its completion
is the quotient of a complete regular Noetherian local ring by an ideal
generated by a regular sequence (EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 19.3.1); a
closed immersion \(Y\to X\) of locally Noetherian schemes is
\emph{regular} at a point \(y\) of \(Y\) if the kernel of
\(\mathcal O_{X,y}\to\mathcal O_{Y,y}\) is generated by a regular
sequence, and \emph{regular} if it is so at every point
(loc.\ cit., 16.9.2 and 16.9.10).

\smallskip
\noindent\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:5.5.1}%
(a) \emph{If \(G\) is an algebraic group over a field \(k\), the local
ring \(\mathcal O_{G,e}\) is a complete intersection, and so are all the
local rings of \(G\).} By loc.\ cit., 19.3.4, this may be checked after
extending the base field to an algebraic closure of \(k\); there the
translations by rational points act transitively on the closed points,
and the points where \(G\) is a complete intersection form an open set
(loc.\ cit., 19.3.3), so that it suffices to consider \(\mathcal O_{G,e}\).
If \(k\) has characteristic \(0\), \(G\) is smooth
(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:3.3.1}{3.3.1}, or Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, \SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.6.1}{1.6.1}), so that
\(\mathcal O_{G,e}\) is regular. If \(k\) has characteristic \(p>0\),
\SGARefLink{sga3:vii:corollary:5-4}{5.4}, applied with \(H=\{e\}\), gives
\(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{G,e}\simeq
k[[X_1,\ldots,X_s]]/(X_1^{p^{n_1}},\ldots,X_r^{p^{n_r}})\), and
\(X_1^{p^{n_1}},\ldots,X_r^{p^{n_r}}\) is a regular sequence in
\(k[[X_1,\ldots,X_s]]\).

\smallskip
\noindent(b) \emph{Let \(G\) be a smooth algebraic group over a field
\(k\) and \(H\) a closed subgroup of \(G\). Then the immersion \(H\to G\)
is regular} (Expos\'e~III, \SGARefLink{sga3:iii:proposition:4-15}{4.15}). This can also be deduced from
5.4. As in Expos\'e~III, 4.15, one reduces to the case where \(k\) is
algebraically closed and to the point \(e\): it suffices to show that the
kernel \(I\) of \(\mathcal O_{G,e}\to\mathcal O_{H,e}\) is generated by a
regular sequence, and, \(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{G,e}\) being faithfully
flat over \(\mathcal O_{G,e}\), that \(I\widehat{\mathcal O}_{G,e}\) is
(EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 19.1.5 (ii), as in the proof of 19.3.2). In
characteristic \(0\), \(H\) is smooth too, and a closed immersion of
smooth \(k\)-schemes is regular (loc.\ cit., 17.12.1). In characteristic
\(p>0\), put \(\widehat A=\widehat{\mathcal O}_{G,e}\),
\(\widehat G=\operatorname{Spf}\widehat A\),
\(\widehat H=\operatorname{Spf}\widehat{\mathcal O}_{H,e}\), and let
\(B\subset\widehat A\) be the affine algebra of
\(\widehat H\backslash\widehat G\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.4}{2.4})}. As \(G\) is smooth,
\(\widehat A\) is a formal power series algebra (loc.\ cit., 17.5.3,
\(\mathrm d''\)). So \(B\), which is the completion of the local ring of
\(H\backslash G\) at \(e\) (proof of 5.4), is reduced; in the form given by
5.4 no truncated variable can then occur, and \(B\) is a formal power
series algebra \(k[[x_1,\ldots,x_m]]\). The ideal \(I\widehat A\) of
\(\widehat H\) is the closed ideal generated by the maximal ideal
\((x_1,\ldots,x_m)\) of \(B\) (2.4; see the Addition after 5.1); since
\(\widehat A\) is Noetherian, it is the ideal \((x_1,\ldots,x_m)\widehat A\).
Finally, \(\widehat A\) is topologically flat over \(B\) (2.4), hence flat
(0.3.7 and 0.3.8), so that the regular sequence \((x_1,\ldots,x_m)\) of
\(B\) remains regular in \(\widehat A\).

\smallskip
\noindent(c) \emph{Let \(k\) be a perfect field of characteristic \(p>0\),
\(G\) a smooth algebraic \(k\)-group, \(H\) a closed subgroup of \(G\), and
\(\mathfrak m\) the maximal ideal of \(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{G,e}\). There
are a system of coordinates \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\in\mathfrak m\), that is,
elements such that the homomorphism
\(k[[T_1,\ldots,T_n]]\to\widehat{\mathcal O}_{G,e}\), \(T_i\mapsto x_i\),
is an isomorphism, and integers \(r,s\geq0\) and
\(1\leq n_1\leq\cdots\leq n_r\), such that the kernel of
\(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{G,e}\to\widehat{\mathcal O}_{H,e}\) is generated by
\(x_1^{p^{n_1}},\ldots,x_r^{p^{n_r}},x_{r+s+1},\ldots,x_n\).} Indeed, by
5.4, applied to \(H\) and its unit subgroup, \(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{H,e}\)
is isomorphic to
\(k[[Y_1,\ldots,Y_{r+s}]]/(Y_1^{p^{n_1}},\ldots,Y_r^{p^{n_r}})\), with the
\(n_i\) in increasing order. Let \(y_i\) be the image of \(Y_i\), \(J\) the
kernel of \(\pi:\widehat{\mathcal O}_{G,e}\to\widehat{\mathcal O}_{H,e}\),
and choose \(x_i\in\mathfrak m\) with \(\pi(x_i)=y_i\) for \(i\leq r+s\).
The map \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\to\mathfrak m_H/\mathfrak m_H^2\),
where \(\mathfrak m_H\) is the maximal ideal of
\(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{H,e}\), is surjective with kernel the image of
\(J\), and it maps the classes of \(x_1,\ldots,x_{r+s}\) onto a basis (the
\(p^{n_i}\) being \(\geq2\)); so these classes can be completed by those
of elements \(x_{r+s+1},\ldots,x_n\) of \(J\) to a basis of
\(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\). As \(G\) is smooth,
\(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{G,e}\) is a formal power series algebra in \(n\)
variables (EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 17.5.3, \(\mathrm d''\)); the
homomorphism \(T_i\mapsto x_i\) is surjective, being so modulo
\(\mathfrak m^2\), and therefore bijective, since a nonzero prime ideal
\(P\) of the Noetherian local domain \(k[[T_1,\ldots,T_n]]\) has
\(\dim k[[T_1,\ldots,T_n]]/P<n\). Through it, \(\pi\) maps \(T_i\) to
\(y_i\) for \(i\leq r+s\) and to \(0\) for \(i>r+s\), so that its kernel is
generated by the \(T_i^{p^{n_i}}\) \((i\leq r)\) and the \(T_i\)
\((i>r+s)\). These form a regular sequence, which gives (b) at \(e\) once
more. The re-edition calls this ``Dieudonn\'e's theorem'' and compares it
with J.~Dieudonn\'e, \emph{Groupes de Lie et hyperalg\`ebres de Lie sur un
corps de caract\'eristique \(p>0\)}, Comment.\ Math.\ Helv.\ \textbf{28}
(1954), 87--118, \S\,19, Theorem~6, and with his \emph{Introduction to the
theory of formal groups}, Marcel Dekker, 1973, chap.~II, \S\,3.2,
Proposition~3.
