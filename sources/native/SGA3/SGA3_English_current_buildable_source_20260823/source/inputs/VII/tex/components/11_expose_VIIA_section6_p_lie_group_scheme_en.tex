
\section*{6. The \(p\)-Lie Algebra of an \(S\)-Group Scheme}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:6}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{6. The p-Lie Algebra of an S-Group Scheme}

Let \(S\) be a scheme of characteristic \(p>0\). In
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5}{Section~5} we associated with every quasi-coherent
\(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra \(\mathcal G\) an \(S\)-functor in
groups \(\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G)\).
We shall now see that, for every \(S\)-group scheme \(G\), the
\(\mathcal O_S\)-Lie algebra \(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\) defined
in II, \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:subsection:4-11}{4.11} is naturally endowed with the structure of an
\(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{6.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:6.1}

First identify \(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)(S)\) and
\(\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{Aut}G/S)(S)\), respectively,
with Lie subalgebras of \(\mathrm U(G)\) and
\(\operatorname{Dif}_{G/S}\) by means of the injections
\(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:2.5}{2.5}. The Lie algebra of
\(\operatorname{Aut}G\) is thus identified with the Lie algebra of
\(S\)-derivations of \(\mathcal O_G\). By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.2}{5.2}, the latter is a
restricted \(p\)-Lie subalgebra of \(\operatorname{Dif}_{G/S}\).

On the other hand, by \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:theorem:4-1-4}{II~4.1.4}, the image of
\(L=\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)(S)\) under right translation
\(r:\mathrm U(G)\to\operatorname{Dif}_{G/S}\) consists of the
right-invariant derivations. If \(x\) belongs to \(L\), \(r(x)^p\) is
none other than \(r(x^p)\), by \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:2.2}{2.2}. Since \(r(x)^p\) is again a
derivation, \(x^p\) belongs to \(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)(S)\), which is
therefore a restricted \(p\)-Lie subalgebra of the infinitesimal algebra
\(\mathrm U(G)\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{6.1.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:6.1.1}

Let \(\varphi:G\to H\) be a homomorphism of \(S\)-group schemes.
The homomorphisms
\(\operatorname{Lie}(\varphi/S)(S)\) and \(\mathrm U(\varphi)\)
are compatible with the identifications of
\(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)(S)\) and
\(\operatorname{Lie}(H/S)(S)\) with restricted \(p\)-Lie
subalgebras of \(\mathrm U(G)\) and \(\mathrm U(H)\). Since
\(\mathrm U(\varphi)\) is an algebra homomorphism,
\(\operatorname{Lie}(\varphi/S)(S)\) is a homomorphism of
restricted \(p\)-Lie algebras.

Likewise, if \(s:T\to S\) is a base change, the map from
\(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)(S)\) to
\(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)(T)\) induced by \(s\) is a
homomorphism of restricted \(p\)-Lie algebras. Equivalently, the
functor \(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\) is endowed with a structure
of \(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra. In particular, as \(T\)
ranges over the open subsets of \(S\), the sheaf
\(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\) is endowed with a structure of
\(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{6.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:6.2}

Following an idea of Demazure, we shall generalize the preceding
construction to certain group \(S\)-functors which need not be
representable. We first give another definition of the symbolic
\(p\)-th power in the Lie algebra of an \(S\)-group scheme \(G\).

Let \(D\) be a derivation of \(G\) at the origin, that is, the
deviation of the origin obtained by composing the canonical deviation
\(\delta:S\to I_S\) of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.5}{1.5} with an extension \(x\) to \(I_S\) of the
unit section \(\varepsilon:S\to G\). By the definition given in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:2.1}{2.1},
\(D^p\) is the following composite deviation:
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:6.2-demazure-pfold-composite}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_057}
\end{center}
Here \(m^{(p)}\) is the morphism induced by the multiplication
\(m:G\times G\to G\). Since \(I_S\times\cdots\times I_S\) is affine
over \(S\), with affine algebra
\(\mathcal O_S[d_1,\ldots,d_p]/(d_1^2,\ldots,d_p^2)\), the deviation
\(\delta\times\cdots\times\delta\) is defined by a morphism of
\(\mathcal O_S\)-modules
\[
  \mathcal O_S[d_1,\ldots,d_p]/(d_1^2,\ldots,d_p^2)
  \longrightarrow\mathcal O_S
\]
which sends the monomial \(d_1d_2\cdots d_p\) to \(1\), and the other
monomials \(d_{i_1}\cdots d_{i_r}\) to \(0\) (\(r<p\)). On the other
hand, if \(\operatorname{pr}_i\) denotes the projection of \(I_S^p\)
onto the \(i\)-th factor, and if \(x_i\) is the image of \(x\) in
\(G(I_S^p)\) under \(G(\operatorname{pr}_i)\), then
\[
  m^{(p)}\circ(x\times\cdots\times x)
\]
is precisely the product \(x_1x_2\cdots x_p\). Consequently,
\(D^p\) is also the composite deviation
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:6.2-demazure-product-composite}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_058}
\end{center}

This description gives another proof that \(D^p\) is a
derivation of \(G\) at the origin. Since \(G\) is a very good
group (II, \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:subsection:4-11}{4.11}), the images \(G(\operatorname{pr}_1)(x)\) and
\(G(\operatorname{pr}_2)(x)\) of \(x\) in
\(G(I_S\times I_S)\) commute. It follows that the elements
\(x_i\in G(I_S^p)\) commute pairwise. Hence, for every
permutation \(\gamma\) of the factors of \(I_S^p\),
\[
  (x_1\cdots x_p)\circ\gamma=x_1\cdots x_p.
\]
Thus \(x_1\cdots x_p\) factors through the canonical projection
from \(I_S^p\) to the symmetric product \(\Sigma^p I_S\)
(cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:4.2}{4.2}).

The symmetric product \(\Sigma^p I_S\) has as affine algebra a
subalgebra \(\mathcal A\) of
\(\mathcal O_S[d_1,\ldots,d_p]/\allowbreak(d_1^2,\ldots,d_p^2)\) which has as an
\(\mathcal O_S\)-basis the elementary symmetric functions
\(1=\sigma_0,\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_p\) of \(d_1,\ldots,d_p\). We denote by
\(q\) the homomorphism from \(\mathcal A\) to \(\mathcal O_S[d]/(d^2)\)
which kills \(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_{p-1}\) and sends \(\sigma_p\) to
\(d\), and by \(i\) the closed immersion of \(I_S\) in
\(\Sigma^p I_S\) associated with \(q\). We then have a commutative
diagram
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:6.2-symbolic-power-commutative-diagram}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_059}
\end{center}
which shows that \(D^p\) has the form \(y\circ\delta\), and is
therefore indeed a derivation of \(G\) at the origin.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{6.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:6.3}

Let \(\mathfrak S_p\) be the symmetric group of degree \(p\),
and let \(I_S^p\times\mathfrak S_p\) be the direct sum of a family of
copies of \(I_S^p\) indexed by \(\mathfrak S_p\). We denote by
\(\pi:I_S^p\times\mathfrak S_p\to I_S^p\) the canonical projection and
by \(\mu:I_S^p\times\mathfrak S_p\to I_S^p\) the morphism defining the
action of \(\mathfrak S_p\) on \(I_S^p\) (if \(\rho\) is an element of
\(\mathfrak S_p\), the restriction of \(\mu\) to \(I_S^p\times\rho\) has
\(\operatorname{pr}_{\rho(j)}\) as its \(j\)-th component). This being
so, we say that a functor
\(X:(\operatorname{Sch}/S)^\circ\to(\operatorname{Ens})\) satisfies
\emph{condition (F)} if \(X\) transforms finite direct sums into direct
products and if, for every \(S\)-scheme \(T\), the sequence
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:6.3-conditionF-equalizer}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_060}
\end{center}

Every \(S\)-scheme satisfies (F). If \(\mathcal F\) is an
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module, then \(\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\)
satisfies (F). Every projective limit of functors satisfying
(F) again satisfies (F). Finally, if \(Y\) satisfies (F) and
\(X\) is an arbitrary \(S\)-functor, then
\(\operatorname{Hom}_S(X,Y)\) satisfies (F).

Let \(X\) be a very good group (II, \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:definition:4-10}{4.10}) satisfying condition
(F). Let \(x:I_S\to X\) be a morphism extending the unit section
of \(X\), and use the notation of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:6.2}{6.2}. As above,
\[
  x_1\cdots x_p:I_S^p\longrightarrow X
\]
factors through \(\Sigma^p I_S\):
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:6.3-symbolic-power-factorization}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_061}
\end{center}
and therefore defines, by composition, a morphism
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:6.3-symbolic-pth-power-composite}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_062}
\end{center}
which we call the \emph{symbolic \(p\)-th power} of \(x\).

The endomorphism
\[
  x\longmapsto x^{(p)}
\]
of \(\operatorname{Lie}(X/S)(S)\) is plainly compatible with
base change and functorial in \(X\). It would be interesting to
know for which \(X\) this endomorphism makes
\(\operatorname{Lie}(X/S)(S)\) into a restricted \(p\)-Lie
algebra.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{6.4.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:6.4}

The preceding definition of the symbolic \(p\)-th power is
particularly well suited to computation. Here are some
examples.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{6.4.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:6.4.1}

Let \(M\) be an ``abstract'' abelian group, and let \(D_S(M)\)
be the diagonalizable \(S\)-group of type \(M\) (I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:paragraph:4-4-2}{4.4.2}). For
every \(S\)-scheme \(T\),
\[
  D_S(M)(T)
  =
  \operatorname{Hom}_{(\mathrm{Ab})}
  \bigl(M,\mathcal O(T)^\times\bigr).
\]
Let \(x\in\operatorname{Lie}(D_S(M)/S)(S)\), that is, an
abelian-group homomorphism
\[
  x:M\longrightarrow
  \Gamma(S,\mathcal O_S+d\mathcal O_S)^\times
\]
of the form
\[
  m\longmapsto1+d\xi(m),
  \qquad
  \xi\in
  \operatorname{Hom}_{(\mathrm{Ab})}(M,\mathcal O(S)).
\]
With the notation of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:6.2}{6.2} and \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:6.3}{6.3}, the product
\(x_1\cdots x_p\) associates with \(m\in M\) the expression
\[
  (1+d_1\xi(m))\cdots(1+d_p\xi(m)),
\]
that is,
\[
  1+\sigma_1\xi(m)+\sigma_2\xi(m)^2+\cdots+
  \sigma_p\xi(m)^p.
\]
This expression belongs to \(\mathcal O(\Sigma^p I_S)\).
Projecting it to \(\mathcal O(S)[d]/(d^2)\), killing
\(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_{p-1}\), and sending \(\sigma_p\) to
\(d\), we see that \(x^{(p)}\) is the homomorphism
\[
  m\longmapsto1+d\xi(m)^p.
\]
Thus, under the identification
\[
  \operatorname{Lie}(D_S(M)/S)(S)
  \simeq
  \operatorname{Hom}_{(\mathrm{Ab})}(M,\mathcal O(S))
\]
of II, \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:proposition:5-1}{5.1}, the symbolic \(p\)-th power sends \(\xi\) to
\[
  \xi^{(p)}:m\longmapsto\xi(m)^p.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{6.4.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:6.4.2}

Let \(\mathcal F\) be an \(\mathcal O_S\)-module, and let \(G\) be the
\(S\)-functor in abelian groups \(\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\) (cf.\ I,
\SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:4-6}{4.6}). Let \(y\) be an element of
\(\mathcal W(\mathcal F)(S)=\Gamma(S,\mathcal F)\) and \(y'\) the
canonical image of \(y\) in \(\mathcal W(\mathcal F)(I_S)\). It is known
that the map \(y\mapsto dy'\) is a bijection from
\(\mathcal W(\mathcal F)(S)\) onto
\(\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal W(\mathcal F)/S)(S)\).\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A},
refers for this to Expos\'e~II, 4.4.2 and 4.5.1, and corrects ``the
canonical image of \(x\)'' of the 1970 text to ``of \(y'\)'' below (the
image of \(x=dy'\) would be \(d\,y''\)). The 1970 text also writes ``the
morphism \(i\) of 6.1'' for the \(i\) defined in 6.2.} If we put
\(x=dy'\), the element \(x_i\) of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:6.2}{6.2} is none other than \(d_iy''\),
where \(y''\) denotes the canonical image of \(y'\) in
\(\mathcal W(\mathcal F)(I_S^p)\). Consequently the product
\(x_1\cdots x_p\) is equal to \((d_1+\cdots+d_p)y''\) and belongs to
\(\mathcal W(\mathcal F)(\Sigma^p I_S)\). Since the map from
\(\mathcal O(\Sigma^p I_S)\) to \(\mathcal O(I_S)\) which defines the
morphism \(i\) of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:6.2}{6.2} kills \(d_1+\cdots+d_p\), we see that \(x^{(p)}\)
is zero.

For every \(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(\mathcal F\), the symbolic \(p\)-th
power operation in the Lie algebra of \(\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\) is
therefore zero.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{6.4.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:6.4.3}

Let \(X\) be an \(S\)-scheme, let
\(G=\operatorname{Aut}_S X\), and let \(D\) be an
\(S\)-derivation of the structure sheaf \(\mathcal O_X\). By
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.5}{1.5},\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text refers to 6.1,
which treats only \(\operatorname{Aut}G\) for a group \(G\); the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, corrects
the reference to 1.5. The 1970 text (and the re-edition) also writes
\(\mathcal O_S[d]/(d^2)\) and \(\mathcal O_S[d_1,\ldots,d_p]/(d_1^2,\ldots,d_p^2)\)
for the rings \(\mathcal O_X[\ldots]\) on which the endomorphisms below
act.} \(D\) may be identified with an \(I_S\)-automorphism \(x\)
of \(X_{I_S}\), which can be described as follows: if \(f\) is a
section of \(\mathcal O_X[d]/(d^2)\) of the form \(a+bd\), put
\[
  D_{I_S}f=Da+(Db)d;
\]
in other words, \(D_{I_S}\) is obtained from \(D\) by the base change
\(I_S\to S\); the automorphism in question of \(X_{I_S}\) is then
associated with the endomorphism
\[
  f\longmapsto f+(D_{I_S}f)d
\]
of \(\mathcal O_X[d]/(d^2)\).

Similarly, let \(D_{I_S^p}\) be the differential operator on
\(X_{I_S^p}\) obtained from \(D\) by the base change
\(I_S^p\to S\). With the notation of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:6.2}{6.2}, the automorphism
\(x_i\) of \(X_{I_S^p}\) is associated with the endomorphism
\[
  f\longmapsto f+(D_{I_S^p}f)d_i
\]
of
\[
  \mathcal O_X[d_1,\ldots,d_p]/(d_1^2,\ldots,d_p^2).
\]
The product \(x_1\cdots x_p\) is therefore associated with
\[
  (1+d_1D_{I_S^p})(1+d_2D_{I_S^p})\cdots
  (1+d_pD_{I_S^p}),
\]
that is, with
\[
  1+\sigma_1D_{I_S^p}
  +\sigma_2D_{I_S^p}^{\,2}
  +\cdots+
  \sigma_pD_{I_S^p}^{\,p}.
\]
The coefficient of \(\sigma_p\) is \(D_{I_S^p}^{\,p}\), which means
that the bijection \(D\mapsto x\) from the Lie algebra of
\(S\)-derivations of \(\mathcal O_X\) onto the Lie algebra of
\(\operatorname{Aut}_S X\) is an isomorphism of restricted \(p\)-Lie
algebras.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{6.4.4.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:6.4.4}

The same method shows that, for every \(\mathcal O_S\)-module
\(\mathcal F\), the bijection described in \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:proposition:4-5}{II~4.5} is an isomorphism of
restricted \(p\)-Lie algebras from
\(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\)
onto
\(\operatorname{Lie}\bigl(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}
\mathcal W(\mathcal F)/S\bigr)(S)\).

Likewise, if \(\mathcal U\) is a quasi-coherent
\(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebra in groups and \(G\) is the functor in groups
\(\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U\), one sees easily that the
canonical injection of \(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)(S)\) into
\(\Gamma(S,\mathcal U)\), which identifies \(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)(S)\)
with the set of primitive elements of \(\Gamma(S,\mathcal U)\), is
compatible with the \(p\)-th power.\footnote{Translator's note: in
the manner of 6.4.1: if \(x\) corresponds to \(1+du\), \(u\) primitive,
the \(x_i\) correspond to \(1+d_iu\), whose product is
\(\sum_k\sigma_ku^k\); its image under \(i\) is \(1+du^p\). The
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, adds
(its 6.4.5) that when \(G\) is representable, the restricted structure
of 6.1 on \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)(T)\), given by \(p\)-th powers in
\(\mathrm U(G_T)\), coincides with the one given by \(p\)-th powers of
primitive elements of \(\Gamma(T,\mathcal U_T)\). In our words: by 6.2
both are the symbolic \(p\)-th power of 6.3, the first by the
description of \(D^p\) in 6.2, the second by the computation just
given, made over \(T\).}
