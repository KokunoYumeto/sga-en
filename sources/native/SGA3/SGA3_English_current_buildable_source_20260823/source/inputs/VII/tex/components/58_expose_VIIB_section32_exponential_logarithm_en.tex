
\subsection*{3.2}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:3.2}

Suppose now that \(C\) is Artinian, with radical \(\mathfrak r\).
For every \(C\)-algebra \(U\) \textup{(associative and unital)}, the
product \(\mathfrak rU\) therefore consists of nilpotent elements. If
\(x\in\mathfrak rU\), put
\[
  \exp_U(x)
  =
  1+x+\frac{x^2}{2!}+\cdots.
\]
This gives a bijection from \(\mathfrak rU\) onto
\(1+\mathfrak rU\); its inverse sends \(1+y\in1+\mathfrak rU\) to
\[
  \log_U(1+y)
  =
  y-\frac{y^2}{2}+\frac{y^3}{3}-\cdots.
\]
Moreover, the map \(\exp_U\) is plainly functorial in
\(U\).\footnote{Translator's note: that is, \(\varphi(\exp_U(x))=\exp_V(\varphi(x))\) for every homomorphism
of \(C\)-algebras \(\varphi\colon U\to V\); applied to \(\Delta_U\) and to \(u\mapsto u\otimes1\),
\(u\mapsto1\otimes u\), this gives the first and fourth equalities below. The third uses
\(\exp(y+y')=\exp(y)\exp(y')\) for commuting \(y,y'\), here \(x\otimes1\) and \(1\otimes x\). The
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, states these two facts.}

Still assuming \(C\) Artinian, suppose that \(U\) is endowed with a
bialgebra structure over \(C\) \textup{(cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.2}{2.2})}. For every
primitive element \(x\in\mathfrak rU\)
\textup{(cf.\ VII\textsubscript{A}, \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:3.2.3}{3.2.3})}, we then have
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \Delta_U\bigl(\exp_U(x)\bigr)
  &=
  \exp_{U\otimes_CU}\bigl(\Delta_U(x)\bigr)
  \\
  &=
  \exp_{U\otimes_CU}(x\otimes1+1\otimes x)
  \\
  &=
  \exp_{U\otimes_CU}(x\otimes1)\,
  \exp_{U\otimes_CU}(1\otimes x)
  \\
  &=
  \bigl(\exp_U(x)\otimes1\bigr)
  \bigl(1\otimes\exp_U(x)\bigr)
  \\
  &=
  \exp_U(x)\otimes\exp_U(x).
\end{aligned}
\]
We therefore see that the bijection \(\exp_U\) transforms a primitive
element of \(\mathfrak rU\) into an element \(z\) of \(1+\mathfrak rU\) such
that \(\Delta_U(z)=z\otimes z\), and the converse is clear.\footnote{Translator's note: if
\(z\in1+\mathfrak rU\) and \(\Delta_U(z)=z\otimes z\), put \(x=\log_U(z)\); the same computation gives
\(\exp_{U\otimes U}(x\otimes1+1\otimes x)=z\otimes z=\exp_{U\otimes U}(\Delta_U(x))\), whence
\(\Delta_U(x)=x\otimes1+1\otimes x\), \(\exp_{U\otimes U}\) being injective on \(\mathfrak r(U\otimes U)\).
Moreover such a \(z\) has augmentation \(1\): applying \(\varepsilon_U\otimes\varepsilon_U\) gives
\(\varepsilon_U(z)^2=\varepsilon_U(z)\), and \(\varepsilon_U(z)\in1+\mathfrak r\) is invertible. The
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, writes this out.}

In particular, let \(L\) be a Lie algebra flat over \(C\), take for
\(U\) the enveloping algebra \(U(L)\) of \(L\) over \(C\), and
identify \(L\) with its canonical image in \(U\). By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:3.1:lemma}{Lemma~3.1},
\(L\) is the set of primitive elements of \(U\): indeed, on every
local component of \(C\), \(L\) is a free module. Consider on the other hand the quotient \(\overline C=C/\mathfrak m\) of
\(C\) by a maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\). Since the \(\overline C\)-formal group
\(\mathscr G(L\otimes_C\overline C)\), which has \(U(L\otimes_C\overline C)\) for
bialgebra, is infinitesimal \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.6.2:prop}{2.6.2})}, the unit element of
\(U(L\otimes_C\overline C)\) is the only element \(\overline z\) such that
\(\varepsilon(\overline z)=1\) and
\(\Delta_{U(L\otimes_C\overline C)}(\overline z)=\overline z\otimes\overline z\).\footnote{Translator's note: the
1970 text omits the condition \(\varepsilon(\overline z)=1\), here and in the next sentence; without it both
statements fail, since \(0\) also satisfies \(\Delta(\overline z)=\overline z\otimes\overline z\). With it, the
elements in question are the points of \(\mathscr G(L\otimes_C\overline C)\) with values in the field
\(\overline C\), and those of \(\mathscr G(L)(C)\) (2.6.2). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, adds the condition.} It follows
that the elements \(z\) of \(U(L)\) such that \(\varepsilon_U(z)=1\) and
\(\Delta_U(z)=z\otimes z\) necessarily belong to \(1+\mathfrak rU\).

Finally, since \(L\cap\mathfrak rU\) is identified with
\(L\otimes_C\mathfrak r\), we see finally that \emph{\(\exp_U\) defines a
bijection of \(L\otimes_C\mathfrak r\) onto the group
\(\mathscr G(L)(C)\)}. We summarize:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 3.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:3.2:prop}
{\itshape Let \(k\) be a pseudocompact ring containing \(\mathbb Q\),
\(\mathbf L\) a Lie algebra flat over \(\mathbf O_k\), \(C\) a profinite
\(k\)-algebra of finite length and \(\mathfrak r(C)\) the radical of \(C\).
The map
\[
  \exp_{U(\mathbf L(C))}:
  \mathbf L(C)\otimes_C\mathfrak r(C)
  \longrightarrow
  \mathscr G(\mathbf L)(C)
\]
is bijective and functorial in \(C\) and \(\mathbf L\).}

\par\medskip\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIB:3.2:prop:item:ii}%
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation). Under the hypotheses of the
proposition, the morphism of \(\mathbf O_k\)-Lie algebras
\(\tau_{\mathbf L}\colon\mathbf L\to\operatorname{Lie}(\mathscr G(\mathbf L))\) of the
Addition after \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.6.2:prop:item:iii}{2.6.2} is an isomorphism. Indeed,
\(\operatorname{Lie}(\mathscr G(\mathbf L))(C)\) is the set of primitive elements of
\(U(\mathbf L(C))\), and \(\mathbf L(C)\) is free over each local component
\(C'\) of \(C\), \(U(\mathbf L(C))\) being the product of the
\(U(\mathbf L(C'))\); so Lemma \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:3.1:lemma}{3.1}, applied over each \(C'\), shows that
these primitive elements are exactly the image of \(\mathbf L(C)\). This is
what is used, as ``\(\mathbf L=\operatorname{Lie}(\mathscr G(\mathbf L))\)'', in
3.3, (iii)~\(\Rightarrow\)~(i), and in 3.3.2.
