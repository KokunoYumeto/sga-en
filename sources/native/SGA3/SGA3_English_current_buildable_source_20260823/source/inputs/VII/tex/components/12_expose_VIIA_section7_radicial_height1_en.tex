
\section*{7. Radicial Groups of Height 1}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:7}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{7. Radicial Groups of Height 1}

Let \(S\) be a scheme of characteristic
\(p>0\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, refers for the results of this section also
to M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel, \textit{Groupes alg\'ebriques}, tome~I,
Masson and North-Holland, 1970, II, \S7, nos.~3--4.}
We shall say that an \(\mathcal O_S\)-algebra \(\mathcal A\)
(respectively, an \(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra
\(\mathcal L\)) is \emph{finite locally free} if its underlying
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module is locally free and of finite type. If
\(\mathcal L\) is a finite locally free
\(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra, we know that the group
\(S\)-functor
\[
  \mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)=\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U_p(\mathcal L)
\]
is representable by a finite, locally free \(S\)-scheme (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.4.1}{5.4.1}). We
shall see that this \(S\)-scheme is the solution of a universal problem,
and we shall characterize the \(S\)-group schemes of the form
\(\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U_p(\mathcal L)\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{7.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:7.1}

Consider first a quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra
\(\mathcal L\). As \(V\) ranges over the open subsets of \(S\), the maps
\[
  j:\Gamma(V,\mathcal L)\longrightarrow U_p\bigl(\Gamma(V,\mathcal L)\bigr)
\]
of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.3}{5.3} define a morphism
\(j:\mathcal L\to\mathcal U_p(\mathcal L)\). On the other hand, by
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:3.2.3}{3.2.3}, the \(\mathcal O_S\)-Lie algebra of the group
\(S\)-functor \(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\) is the kernel of the morphism
\[
  \Delta-\mathrm{in}_1-\mathrm{in}_2:
  \mathcal U_p(\mathcal L)
  \longrightarrow
  \mathcal U_p(\mathcal L)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal U_p(\mathcal L),
\]
where \(\Delta\) denotes the diagonal morphism and \(\mathrm{in}_1\),
\(\mathrm{in}_2\) are the injections \(x\mapsto x\otimes1\) and
\(x\mapsto1\otimes x\). It is clear that the image of \(j\) is contained in
the kernel \(\operatorname{Lie}\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\) of
\(\Delta-\mathrm{in}_1-\mathrm{in}_2\); this is why we shall denote by
\[
  j_{\mathcal L}:\mathcal L\longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Lie}\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)
\]
the morphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebras which is induced by
\(j\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:6.4.4}{6.4.4}).

Now consider a very good group \(S\)-functor \(G\) satisfying
condition (F) of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:6.3}{6.3}, and let
\[
  h:\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\longrightarrow G
\]
be a homomorphism of group \(S\)-functors. By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:6.3}{6.3}, the morphism
\[
  \operatorname{Lie}h:
  \operatorname{Lie}\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Lie}G
\]
is a homomorphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-Lie algebras which is compatible
with raising to the symbolic \(p\)-th power. The same therefore holds for
the composite morphism \((\operatorname{Lie}h)\circ j_{\mathcal L}\). If
we denote by \(\operatorname{Hom}_p(\mathcal L,\operatorname{Lie}G)\) the
set of homomorphisms of \(\mathcal O_S\)-Lie algebras which are
compatible with raising to the symbolic \(p\)-th power, we therefore have
a map
\[
  J(\mathcal L,G):
  h\longmapsto(\operatorname{Lie}h)\circ j_{\mathcal L}
\]
from \(\operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-Gr.}}\bigl(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L),G\bigr)\)
to \(\operatorname{Hom}_p(\mathcal L,\operatorname{Lie}G)\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{7.2. Theorem.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:7.2}

\textit{If \(\mathcal L\) is a finite locally free
\(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra, the map}
\[
  J(\mathcal L,G):
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}
    \bigl(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L),G\bigr)
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Hom}_p
    \bigl(\mathcal L,\operatorname{Lie}G\bigr)
\]
\textit{is bijective in each of the following cases:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textit{(i)}]
  \textit{\(G\) is an \(S\)-group scheme;}
  \item[\textit{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIA:7.2:item:ii}(ii)}]
  \textit{\(G\) is of the form \(\operatorname{Aut}_S X\), where
  \(X\) is an \(S\)-scheme;}
  \item[\textit{(iii)}]
  \textit{\(G\) is of one of the forms
  \(\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\) or
  \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}
  \mathcal W(\mathcal F)\), where \(\mathcal F\) is a
  quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_S\)-module.}
\end{enumerate}

The proof of the theorem rests on the following lemma.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Lemma.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:7.2-frobenius-lemma}

\textit{If \(\mathcal L\) is a finite locally free
\(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra, the \(S\)-group
\(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\) is annihilated by the Frobenius morphism
of \(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\) relative to \(S\).}

Indeed, let \(\mathcal U\) be the restricted enveloping algebra of
\(\mathcal L\), and put
\[
  \mathcal A=\mathcal U^{*}
  =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal U,\mathcal O_S).
\]
Then \(\mathcal A\) is the affine algebra of
\(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\). Moreover, if \(\mathcal J\) is the
augmentation ideal of \(\mathcal U\), that is, the ideal generated by the
image of \(j_{\mathcal L}:\mathcal L\to\mathcal U\), we denote by
\(\mathcal I\) the orthogonal in \(\mathcal A\) of the unit section of
\(\mathcal U\), that is, the augmentation ideal of
\(\mathcal A\).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text calls
\(\mathcal I\) the orthogonal of \(\mathcal J\) in \(\mathcal A\). Since
\(\mathcal J\) is the kernel of the augmentation of \(\mathcal U\), which
is the unit element \(1_{\mathcal A}\) of \(\mathcal A\), that orthogonal
is the submodule \(\mathcal O_S\cdot1_{\mathcal A}\), not an ideal. The
reading above is that of the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}: \(\mathcal I\) is the kernel of the
augmentation of \(\mathcal A\), which is evaluation at
\(1_{\mathcal U}\). The decompositions
\(\mathcal A=\mathcal O_S\cdot1_{\mathcal A}\oplus\mathcal I\) and
\(\mathcal U=\mathcal O_S\cdot1_{\mathcal U}\oplus\mathcal J\) are dual to
each other, so that \(\mathcal J\) is the dual of \(\mathcal I\).} Thus
\(\mathcal A/\mathcal I\simeq\mathcal O_S\), and the ideal \(\mathcal I\)
defines the unit section of \(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\).

If \(\pi\) is the endomorphism \(x\mapsto x^p\) of \(\mathcal O_S\), we
must show that the morphism
\[
  \Phi:a\otimes_{\pi}x\longmapsto a^px
\]
from \(\mathcal A\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal O_S\) to \(\mathcal A\) vanishes on
\(\mathcal I\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal O_S\). Now \(\Phi\) is none other than
the following composite:
\[
  \mathcal A\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal O_S
  \xrightarrow{j(\mathcal A)}
  \mathrm S^p\mathcal A
  \xrightarrow{b(\mathcal A)}
  \mathcal A,
\]
where \(b(\mathcal A)\) and \(j(\mathcal A)\) are defined as in
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:4.3.3}{4.3.3}. Since the \(\mathcal O_S\)-module dual to
\(\mathrm S^p\mathcal A\) is none other than the submodule
\(\Sigma^p\mathcal U\) of \(\bigotimes^p\mathcal U\) formed by the
sections invariant under the action of the symmetric group of degree
\(p\), one sees that the transpose \(\Phi^{*}\) of \(\Phi\) is the
following composite:
\[
  \mathcal U
  \xrightarrow{a(\mathcal U)}
  \Sigma^p\mathcal U
  \xrightarrow{r(\mathcal U)}
  \mathcal U\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal O_S,
\]
where \(a(\mathcal U)\) is induced by the morphism
\((\Delta\otimes\mathcal U\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal U)\cdots
(\Delta\otimes\mathcal U)\Delta\) from \(\mathcal U\) to
\(\bigotimes^p\mathcal U\) (\(\Delta\) is the diagonal morphism of
\(\mathcal U\)); likewise, \(r(\mathcal U)\) vanishes on the symmetrized
tensors and sends a section \(x\otimes\cdots\otimes x\) to
\(x\otimes_{\pi}1\) (confer \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:4.3.3}{4.3.3}). It now remains to show
that \(\Phi^{*}\) annihilates the augmentation ideal \(\mathcal J\).
Since \(\Phi^{*}\) is a homomorphism of algebras, it suffices to see that
\(\Phi^{*}\) vanishes on \(\operatorname{Im}j_{\mathcal L}\). This
follows from the formula \(\Delta s=s\otimes1+1\otimes s\) for
\(s\in\operatorname{Im}j_{\mathcal L}\).\footnote{Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, spells this
out. By the formula, \(a(\mathcal U)(s)\) is the sum of the \(p\) tensors
\(1\otimes\cdots\otimes s\otimes\cdots\otimes1\) with \(s\) in one place;
multiplied by \((p-1)!\), a unit (equal to \(-1\)) in characteristic
\(p\), this sum is the symmetrization
\(\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_p}\sigma(s\otimes1\otimes\cdots\otimes1)\), on
which \(r(\mathcal U)\) vanishes. The re-edition also cites 4.1.4~c) for
the description of the Frobenius morphism by \(\Phi\), and refers for
another proof to M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel, \textit{Groupes
alg\'ebriques}, tome~I, II, \S7, 3.9.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{7.2.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:7.2.1}

Put \(G_p=\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\). We shall first prove assertion
(ii) of Theorem \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.2}{7.2}, keeping the notation above. Since every element
of \(\mathcal I\) has zero \(p\)-th power and \(\mathcal I\) is an
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module of finite type, \(\mathcal I\) is locally
nilpotent. Hence \((G_p)_{\mathrm{red}}=S_{\mathrm{red}}\).\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A},
moves this to the lemma of 7.2 and makes it explicit: if, over an affine
open subset \(V\) of \(S\), \(\mathcal I(V)\) is generated by \(r\)
elements, then \(\mathcal I(V)^{r(p-1)+1}=0\), since every monomial of
degree \(r(p-1)+1\) in these generators contains one of them at least
\(p\) times. It calls a finite locally free \(S\)-group whose augmentation
ideal is locally nilpotent, that is, whose unit section is a
homeomorphism, \emph{infinitesimal} (cf.\ M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel,
\textit{Groupes alg\'ebriques}, tome~I, II, \S4, 7.1), and it carries out
the following reduction to the case where \(X\) is affine over \(S\) for
every infinitesimal group in place of \(G_p\); the argument is the same.}
Now the
homomorphisms \(h\) from \(G_p\) to \(\operatorname{Aut}X\) correspond
bijectively to the left operations \(h':G_p\times X\to X\) of \(G_p\) on
\(X\). For such an operation, if \(\varepsilon\) is the unit section of
\(G_p\), the composite morphism
\[
  X\simeq S\times X
  \xrightarrow{\varepsilon\times X}
  G_p\times X
  \xrightarrow{h'}
  X
\]
must be the identity. Since \((G_p\times X)_{\mathrm{red}}\) is identified
with \(X_{\mathrm{red}}\), one sees that \(h'\) must induce the identity on
the associated reduced schemes. In particular, \(h'\) induces an operation
of \(G_p\) on every open subset of \(X\), so that one easily reduces to the
case where \(S\) and \(X\) are affine, or more generally to the case where
\(X\) is affine over \(S\). In the latter case, one applies the following
lemma.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Lemma.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:7.2.1-action-representation-lemma}

\textit{Let \(X\) be an affine \(S\)-scheme with algebra \(\mathcal C\),
and \(G_p\) a finite locally free \(S\)-group scheme with algebra
\(\mathcal A\). If we put
\(\mathcal U=\mathcal A^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal A,\mathcal O_S)\),
the left operations of \(G_p\) on \(X\) correspond bijectively to the
representations of the algebra \(\mathcal U\) in the
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(\mathcal C\) such that}
\begin{align*}
  &u(1_{\mathcal C})=\varepsilon(u)\cdot1_{\mathcal C}
  \\
  \textit{and}\qquad&
  u(xy)=\sum_i v_i(x)w_i(y)
  \quad\textit{if}\quad
  \Delta u=\sum_i v_i\otimes w_i.
\end{align*}

In these formulas, \(u\) denotes an arbitrary section of \(\mathcal U\)
over an affine open subset \(V\), and \(x\) and \(y\) sections of
\(\mathcal C\) over \(V\); we denote by \(1_{\mathcal C}\) the unit
section of \(\mathcal C\), and by \(\varepsilon\) and \(\Delta\) the
augmentation and the diagonal morphism of \(\mathcal U\). A left operation
\(h'\) of \(G_p\) on \(X\) is defined by a homomorphism of algebras
\(\lambda:\mathcal C\to\mathcal A\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal C\). We
shall denote by \(\mu\) the composite morphism
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:7.2.1-action-contraction-composite}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_067}
\end{center}
where \(\gamma\) is the ``contraction'' from
\(\mathcal A^{*}\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal A\) to \(\mathcal O_S\).
One knows that the map
\(\lambda\mapsto(\gamma\otimes\mathcal C)(\mathcal U\otimes\lambda)\) is a
bijection from
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal C,\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal C)\)
onto
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal U\otimes\mathcal C,\mathcal C)\).
Moreover, one easily sees that \(\lambda\) is a homomorphism of unital
algebras defining an operation of \(G_p\) on \(X\) if and only if \(\mu\)
satisfies the conditions of the lemma.\footnote{Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, separates the
two parts: by duality, \(\lambda\) makes \(\mathcal C\) a comodule over
\(\mathcal A\) if and only if \(\mu\) is a representation of
\(\mathcal U\), and \(\lambda\) is a homomorphism of unital algebras if
and only if \(\mu\) satisfies the two displayed conditions.}

It is moreover clear that, for every representation of \(\mathcal U\) in
the \(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(\mathcal C\), the sections \(u\) of
\(\mathcal U\) which satisfy the conditions of the preceding lemma form a
subalgebra of \(\mathcal U\). In the particular case which concerns us,
these conditions are therefore satisfied for all the sections \(u\) if
they hold for the sections \(u\) of \(\operatorname{Im}j_{\mathcal L}\). If
\(u\) is a section of \(\operatorname{Im}j_{\mathcal L}\), these
conditions mean that \(u(1_{\mathcal C})=0\) and that
\(u(xy)=u(x)y+xu(y)\). Every homomorphism \(h\) from
\(G_p=\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\) to \(\operatorname{Aut}X\) therefore
defines a homomorphism \(H\) from \(\mathcal U\) to
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal C,\mathcal C)\) which sends
\(\operatorname{Im}j_{\mathcal L}\) into the set of
\(\mathcal O_S\)-derivations of \(\mathcal C\). The map
\(H\circ j_{\mathcal L}\) is a homomorphism of \(p\)-Lie algebras from
\(\mathcal L\) to the sheaf \(\operatorname{Der}_{X/S}\) of
\(\mathcal O_S\)-derivations of \(\mathcal C\). Moreover, the map
\(h\mapsto H\circ j_{\mathcal L}\) is evidently bijective; it would remain
to verify that, when \(\operatorname{Der}_{X/S}\) is identified with
\(\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{Aut}X/S)\) as in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:2.5}{2.5}, the map
\(h\mapsto H\circ j_{\mathcal L}\) is identified with that of Theorem
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.2}{7.2}.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, concludes directly that assertion~(ii)
follows. The verification is short. A section \(x\) of \(\mathcal L\),
viewed through \(j_{\mathcal L}\) as a morphism \(I_S\to G_p\) extending
the unit section, is the homomorphism
\(a\mapsto\varepsilon_{\mathcal A}(a)+\langle x,a\rangle t\) from
\(\mathcal A\) to \(\mathcal O_S[t]/(t^2)\). Hence \(h(x)\) is the
automorphism of \(I_S\times X\) which acts on the sections of
\(\mathcal C\) by \(c\mapsto c+H(j_{\mathcal L}(x))(c)\,t\), since the
unit of \(G_p\) acts trivially. By 1.5, it corresponds to the derivation
\(H(j_{\mathcal L}(x))\), so that
\(\operatorname{Lie}(h)\circ j_{\mathcal L}\) and
\(H\circ j_{\mathcal L}\) agree.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{7.2.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:7.2.2}

We now show how assertion (i) of Theorem \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.2}{7.2} follows from (ii). If
\(T\) is an \(S\)-scheme and \(x\) an element of \(G(T)\), let
\(\ell_x^T\) (respectively, \(r_x^T\)) denote the left
(respectively, right) translation of \(G_T\) defined by \(x\).
The maps
\[
  \ell^T:x\longmapsto\ell_x^T
\]
therefore determine a homomorphism
\(\ell:G\to\operatorname{Aut}G\).

On the other hand, let \(f\) be a \(T\)-automorphism of \(G_T\),
and define
\[
  {}^x f=(r_x^T)^{-1}fr_x^T;
\]
in this way \(G\) acts on the left on the \(S\)-functor
\(\operatorname{Aut}G\), and hence also on the functors
\[
  T\longmapsto
  \operatorname{Hom}_{T\text{-Gr.}}
    \bigl(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L_T),
          \operatorname{Aut}G_T\bigr)
\]
and
\[
  T\longmapsto
  \operatorname{Hom}_p
    \bigl(
      \mathcal L_T,
      \operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{Aut}G_T/T)
    \bigr).
\]

On the other hand, the homomorphism \(\ell\) induces the commutative
squares
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:7.2.2-lie-invariant-commutative-squares}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_068}
\end{center}
The images of the two vertical arrows are the subfunctors
formed by the invariants under the action of the \(S\)-group
\(G\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, gives the reason, which in our words is
the following. An automorphism \(f\) of the \(T\)-scheme \(G_T\) which
commutes with all right translations satisfies \(f(g)=f(e)g\), so it is
the left translation by \(f(e)\); thus \(\ell\) identifies \(G\) with the
invariants of \(\operatorname{Aut}G\), and a homomorphism into
\(\operatorname{Aut}G_T\) is invariant exactly when it factors through
\(\ell^T\). Likewise \(\operatorname{Lie}(\ell^T)\) identifies
\(\operatorname{Lie}G_T\) with the derivations of \(\mathcal O_{G_T}\)
which commute with right translations (II, 4.11.1), that is, with the
invariants of \(\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{Aut}G_T/T)\).} Since the second horizontal arrow is invertible by \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.2.1}{7.2.1}
and is compatible with the action of \(G\), the first
horizontal arrow is invertible as well.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{7.2.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:7.2.3}

Consider finally the case of
\(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\)
(the case of \(\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\) is analogous). Put
\(G_p=\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\). A homomorphism from \(G_p\) to
\(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\) is
a multiplicative homomorphism from \(G_p\) to
\(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\)
which is compatible with the unit sections of \(G_p\) and of
\(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\).
Now a morphism of \(S\)-functors
\(h:G_p\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\)
is by definition an endomorphism of
\(\mathcal W(\mathcal O_{G_p}\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal F)\); by I,
\SGARefLink{sga3:i:proposition:4-6-2}{4.6.2}~(ii), such an endomorphism is induced by an endomorphism of
\(\mathcal O_{G_p}\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal F\), that is, by an
\(\mathcal A\)-linear endomorphism of the sheaf
\(\mathcal A\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal F\), where \(\mathcal A\) is
the affine \(\mathcal O_S\)-algebra of \(G_p\). Such an endomorphism is of
the form \(a\otimes x\mapsto a\lambda(x)\), where \(\lambda\) is a
morphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules from \(\mathcal F\) to
\(\mathcal A\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal F\). If one puts
\(\mu=(\gamma\otimes\mathcal F)(\mathcal U\otimes\lambda)\) as in
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.2.1}{7.2.1}, \(h\) is finally determined by
\(\mu:\mathcal U\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal F\to\mathcal F\). The
hypotheses made on \(h\) translate into saying that \(\mu\) defines a
structure of \(\mathcal U\)-module on \(\mathcal F\).\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A},
derives this from VIB, \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-11.6.1}{11.6.1}: as \(G_p\) is affine over \(S\), a
homomorphism of \(S\)-groups from \(G_p\) to
\(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\) is
the same thing as a comodule structure
\(\mathcal F\to\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal A\), which,
\(\mathcal A\) being finite locally free, amounts to a structure of
\(\mathcal U\)-module on \(\mathcal F\).} Such a module
structure is defined by a homomorphism of \(p\)-Lie algebras from
\(\mathcal L\) to \(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal F)\)~\dots\footnote{Translator's
note: the sentence ends with these dots in the 1970 printing. By
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:6.4.4}{6.4.4}, \(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal F)\) is the
\(p\)-Lie algebra
\(\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}\mathcal W(\mathcal F))\),
and the bijection obtained is the map \(J(\mathcal L,G)\) of 7.1: for a
section \(x\) of \(\mathcal L\), viewed as a morphism \(I_S\to G_p\),
\(h(x)\) acts on \(\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal O_S[t]/(t^2)\)
by \(y\mapsto y+\mu(x\otimes y)\,t\).}

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, 7.2.3; our formulation). The case
\(G=\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\). Let \(\mathcal U=\mathcal U_p(\mathcal L)\),
let \(\mathcal A=\mathcal U^{*}\) be the affine algebra of \(G_p\), and
let \(\mathcal J\) be the augmentation ideal of \(\mathcal U\). A morphism
of \(S\)-functors \(h:G_p\to\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\) is a section of
\(\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal A\), that is, since
\(\mathcal A\) is finite locally free, a morphism of
\(\mathcal O_S\)-modules \(\theta:\mathcal U\to\mathcal F\). As the
comultiplication of \(\mathcal A\) is the transpose of the multiplication
of \(\mathcal U\), \(h\) is a homomorphism if and only if
\(\theta(uv)=\varepsilon(u)\theta(v)+\varepsilon(v)\theta(u)\) for all
local sections \(u\), \(v\) of \(\mathcal U\); writing
\(\mathcal U=\mathcal O_S\cdot1\oplus\mathcal J\), this means that
\(\theta\) vanishes on \(1\) and on \(\mathcal J^2\). Hence
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}\bigl(G_p,\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\bigr)
  =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal J/\mathcal J^2,\mathcal F).
\]
On the other hand, \(\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal W(\mathcal F)\) is
\(\mathcal F\) with zero bracket and zero symbolic \(p\)-th power
(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:6.4.2}{6.4.2}), so that
\(\operatorname{Hom}_p(\mathcal L,\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal W(\mathcal F))
=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal L/\mathcal L',\mathcal F)\),
where \(\mathcal L'\) is the \(\mathcal O_S\)-submodule of \(\mathcal L\)
generated by the brackets \([x,y]\) and the symbolic \(p\)-th powers
\(x^{(p)}\) of its sections. Now \(\mathcal L\), identified with its image
in \(\mathcal U\), maps onto \(\mathcal J/\mathcal J^2\), since the
monomials of degree \(\geq2\) of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.3.3}{5.3.3} lie in \(\mathcal J^2\); and
the kernel of \(\mathcal L\to\mathcal J/\mathcal J^2\) is
\(\mathcal L'\). Indeed, it contains \(\mathcal L'\), as \([x,y]=xy-yx\)
and \(x^{(p)}=x^p\) in \(\mathcal U\); conversely, put
\(\mathcal M=\mathcal L/\mathcal L'\) and let
\(\mathcal O_S\oplus\mathcal M\) be the algebra in which
\(\mathcal M^2=0\): the projection \(\mathcal L\to\mathcal M\) is a
homomorphism of \(p\)-Lie algebras into
\((\mathcal O_S\oplus\mathcal M)_{\mathrm{Lie}}\), hence extends to a
homomorphism of algebras \(\mathcal U\to\mathcal O_S\oplus\mathcal M\),
which maps \(\mathcal J\) into \(\mathcal M\) and \(\mathcal J^2\) to
\(0\). Thus \(\mathcal J/\mathcal J^2\simeq\mathcal L/\mathcal L'\).
Finally, if \(h\) corresponds to \(\theta\) and \(x\) is a section of
\(\mathcal L\), viewed as a morphism \(I_S\to G_p\), then \(h\circ x\) is
the element \(\theta(x)t\) of \(\mathcal W(\mathcal F)(I_S)\), so that
\(J(\mathcal L,\mathcal W(\mathcal F))(h)\) is the restriction of
\(\theta\) to \(\mathcal L\), and \(J(\mathcal L,\mathcal W(\mathcal F))\)
is bijective. (The re-edition states that the kernel of
\(\mathcal L\to\mathcal J/\mathcal J^2\) is \([\mathcal L,\mathcal L]\);
the symbolic \(p\)-th powers must be added: for
\(\mathcal L=\mathcal O_Sx\) with \(x^{(p)}=x\), one has
\(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)=\boldsymbol\mu_{p,S}\) and
\(\mathcal J=\mathcal J^2\), while \([\mathcal L,\mathcal L]=0\).)

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{7.3. Lemma.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:7.3}

\textit{If \(\mathcal L\) is a finite locally free
\(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra, the morphism}
\[
  j_{\mathcal L}:
  \mathcal L
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Lie}\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)
\]
\textit{of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.1}{7.1} is invertible.}

Indeed, the problem is local on \(S\). We may therefore suppose that
\(S\) is affine with ring \(R\), and that \(\mathcal L\) is the sheaf
associated with an \(R\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra with basis
\(x_1,\ldots,x_r\). We may then use the notation of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.3.3}{5.3.3} and put
\(z^n=\prod_iz_i^{n_i}\) for every \(r\)-tuple \(n=(n_1,\ldots,n_r)\) of
natural numbers such that \(0\leq n_i<p\). Putting moreover
\(n!=\prod_i(n_i)!\) and endowing the monoid \(\mathbb N^r\) with the
product order, one easily sees that
\[
  \Delta\frac{z^n}{n!}
  =
  \sum\frac{z^m}{m!}\otimes\frac{z^{n-m}}{(n-m)!},
\]
the sum being extended over all \(m\) in \(\mathbb N^r\) such that
\(0\leq m\leq n\) (\(\Delta\) is the diagonal morphism of
\(\mathcal U\)). Since the \(z^n\) form a basis of \(U_p(\mathfrak g)\), it
is clear that \(\Delta x=x\otimes1+1\otimes x\) if and only if \(x\) is a
linear combination of \(z_1,\ldots,z_r\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{7.4.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:7.4}

To conclude the expos\'e, we shall give a characterization of the
\(S\)-group schemes of the form \(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\), where
\(\mathcal L\) is a finite locally free \(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra.

Let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group scheme, \(\varepsilon_G\) its unit section,
and \(\mathcal I'\) the kernel of the morphism from
\(\varepsilon_G^{-1}(\mathcal O_G)\) to \(\mathcal O_S\) which defines
\(\varepsilon_G\). The canonical image of
\(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)(S)\) in \(\mathrm U(G)\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:2.5}{2.5}) is
identified, by \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.3}{1.3}, with the morphisms of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules
from \(\varepsilon_G^{-1}(\mathcal O_G)\) to \(\mathcal O_S\) which vanish
on the unit section of \(\varepsilon_G^{-1}(\mathcal O_G)\) and on
\(\mathcal I'^{\,2}\). One thus recovers the canonical isomorphism from
\(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)(S)\) onto
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal I'/\mathcal I'^{\,2},\mathcal O_S)\)
of Expos\'e~II.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, gives the places: Expos\'e~II,
\SGARefLink{sga3:ii:proposition:3-3}{3.3} and \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:remark:4-11-4}{4.11.4}.} We shall moreover put
\[
  \omega_{G/S}=\mathcal I'/\mathcal I'^{\,2}
\]
as in Expos\'e~II, so that the sheaf \(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\) is
identified with
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}(\omega_{G/S},\mathcal O_S)\).\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A},
adds that when \(G\) is affine over \(S\), with affine algebra
\(\mathcal A\) and augmentation ideal \(\mathcal I\subset\mathcal A\),
\(\mathcal I'/\mathcal I'^{\,2}\) is identified with
\(\varepsilon_G^{*}(\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2)\), that is, with
\(\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2\) regarded as a module over
\(\mathcal A/\mathcal I=\mathcal O_S\); this is how \(\omega_{G/S}\) is
computed in 7.4.1 and 7.4.2.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Theorem.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:7.4-theorem}

\textit{If \(G\) is a group scheme over a scheme \(S\) of
characteristic \(p>0\), the following assertions are
equivalent:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textit{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIA:7.4-theorem:item:i}(i)}]
  \textit{There exists a finite locally free
  \(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra \(\mathcal L\) such that
  \(G\simeq\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\).}

  \item[\textit{(ii)}]
  \textit{\(G\) is affine over \(S\), the
  \(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(\omega_{G/S}\) is locally free of
  finite type, and the affine algebra of \(G\) is locally
  isomorphic to the quotient of the symmetric algebra
  \(\mathrm S_{\mathcal O_S}(\omega_{G/S})\) by the ideal
  generated by the \(p\)-th powers of the sections of
  \(\omega_{G/S}\).}

  \item[\textit{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIA:7.4-theorem:item:iii}(iii)}]
  \textit{\(G\) is locally of finite presentation over \(S\),
  is of height \(\leq1\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:4.1.3}{4.1.3})}, and \(\omega_{G/S}\) is locally free.}\footnote{When \(G\) is flat over \(S\), the
  condition on \(\omega_{G/S}\) is in fact unnecessary, as one easily sees
  by reducing to the case where \(S\) is local with residue field \(k\),
  and applying the theorem to the group \(G_k\).}\footnote{Translator's
  note: the 1970 footnote has no flatness hypothesis. O.~Gabber observed
  that one is needed (Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
  Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}): if \(A\) is an Artinian local ring of
  characteristic \(p\) and \(\mathfrak a\) a nonzero proper ideal of \(A\),
  the subgroup \(H=\operatorname{Spec}A[x]/(x^p,\mathfrak ax)\) of
  \(\boldsymbol\alpha_{p,A}\) is finite and of finite presentation over
  \(A\), of height \(\leq1\), but \(\omega_{H/A}\simeq A/\mathfrak a\) is
  not free, and \(H\) is not flat over \(A\), whereas every
  \(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\) is flat. The re-edition adds the flat case
  to the theorem as a fourth equivalent assertion; see the Addition after
  7.4.2.}
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{7.4.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:7.4.1}

The implication (ii) \(\Rightarrow\) (iii) being clear, let us first show
that (i) implies (ii). For this we consider the exact sequence
\begin{equation}
  0\longrightarrow\mathcal L
  \xrightarrow{j_{\mathcal L}}\mathcal J
  \xrightarrow{\delta}\mathcal J\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal J,
  \tag{\(*\)}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal J\) is the augmentation ideal of
\(\mathcal U=\mathcal U_p(\mathcal L)\) and \(\delta\) is the morphism
induced by \(\Delta-\mathrm{in}_1-\mathrm{in}_2\). If \(q\) is the
projection of \(\mathcal U\) onto \(\mathcal J\) which vanishes on the
unit section of \(\mathcal U\), \(\delta\) can also be characterized by
the commutative square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2.8em,column sep=3.6em]
  \mathcal U \arrow[r,"\Delta"] \arrow[d,swap,"q"]
  & \mathcal U\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal U \arrow[d,"q\otimes q"] \\
  \mathcal J \arrow[r,"\delta"]
  & \mathcal J\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal J
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Moreover, the sequence \((*)\) remains exact after every change of base;
consequently (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg\`ebre commutative}, II, \S3,
Proposition~6), the sequence \((*)\) splits and gives by duality an exact
sequence
\[
  \mathcal I\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal I
  \xrightarrow{m}
  \mathcal I
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal L,\mathcal O_S)
  \longrightarrow0,
\]
where \(\mathcal I\) still denotes the augmentation ideal of the affine
algebra \(\mathcal A\) of \(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\), and \(m\) is
induced by the multiplication of \(\mathcal A\). This shows that
\(\omega_{G/S}\) \emph{is the dual of} \(\mathcal L\), and is therefore
finite locally free.

Suppose now that \(S\) is affine. There is then a section
\(\sigma:\omega_{G/S}\to\mathcal I\) of the canonical projection of
\(\mathcal I\) onto \(\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2\); such a section induces
(Lemma \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.2-frobenius-lemma}{7.2}) a homomorphism of algebras
\[
  h:\mathrm S_{\mathcal O_S}[\omega_{G/S}]/(x^p)_{x\in\omega_{G/S}}
  \longrightarrow\mathcal A.
\]
If one filters \(\mathcal A\) (respectively,
\(\mathrm S_{\mathcal O_S}[\omega]/(x^p)_{x\in\omega}\)) by the powers of
\(\mathcal I\) (respectively, of the ideal generated by
\(\omega_{G/S}\)), it is clear that \(h\) induces an epimorphism of the
associated graded modules. Hence \(h\) is an epimorphism of locally free
\(\mathcal O_S\)-modules of the same rank; hence \(h\) is an
isomorphism.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, refers here to 5.3.3: if
\(\mathcal L\) has rank \(r\), both modules are locally free of rank
\(p^r\).}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{7.4.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:7.4.2}

Let us finally show that (iii) implies (i). Since the Frobenius morphism
annihilates \(G\), it is clear that the unit section of \(G\) induces a
homeomorphism from the topological space underlying \(S\) onto the space
underlying \(G\). We may therefore identify \(S\) with the closed
subscheme of \(G\) defined by a certain ideal \(\mathcal I\) of
\(\mathcal O_G\). Since \(G\) is locally of finite presentation over \(S\)
and every section of \(\mathcal I\) has zero \(p\)-th power,
\(\mathcal I\) is locally nilpotent and \(G\) is affine over \(S\)
(EGA~I, 5.1.9), hence finite over \(S\).\footnote{Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, argues
instead that the Frobenius morphism \(G\to G^{(p)}\) is integral and
factors through the unit section of \(G^{(p)}\), so that \(G\) is
integral, hence affine, over \(S\). It also supplies the following step:
\(G\) being finite and of finite presentation over \(S\), \(\mathcal A\)
is an \(\mathcal O_S\)-module of finite presentation
(EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.4.7), hence so are \(\mathcal I\), a direct summand of
\(\mathcal A\), and \(\omega_{G/S}=\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2\); thus, under
(iii), \(\omega_{G/S}\) is locally free of finite type and its dual
\(\mathcal L=\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\) is finite locally free, as Theorem
7.2 requires.}

Let then \(\mathcal A\) be the affine \(\mathcal O_S\)-algebra of \(G\);
put \(\mathcal L=\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\),
\(\mathcal A_p=\mathcal U_p(\mathcal L)^{*}\), and let
\(G_p=\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\) be the spectrum of \(\mathcal A_p\). By
Theorem \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.2}{7.2}, the identity of \(\mathcal L\) is defined by a
homomorphism of groups \(h:\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\to G\), hence by a
homomorphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-algebras
\(a:\mathcal A\to\mathcal A_p\). We must show that \(a\), which by
definition induces an isomorphism from \(\omega_{G/S}\) onto
\(\omega_{G_p/S}\), is an isomorphism.

For this, we may restrict to the case where \(S\) is affine. There is
then a section \(\tau\) of the canonical projection of \(\mathcal I\) onto
\(\omega_{G/S}\). Since every section of \(\mathcal I\) has zero
\(p\)-th power, \(\tau\) induces a homomorphism of
\(\mathcal O_S\)-algebras
\[
  b:\mathrm S_{\mathcal O_S}[\omega_{G/S}]/(x^p)_{x\in\omega_{G/S}}
  \longrightarrow\mathcal A.
\]
It is clear that \(b\) is an epimorphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules
(confer \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.4.1}{7.4.1}). On the other hand, we saw in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.4.1}{7.4.1} that \(ab\) is
an isomorphism. The same therefore holds for \(a\).

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, 7.4, 7.4.3 and 7.5; our formulation). The
re-edition adds to the theorem of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.4}{7.4} three equivalent assertions, the
last two pointed out by O.~Gabber:
(i\('\)) \(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\) is a finite locally free
\(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra and
\(G\simeq\mathfrak G_p(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S))\);
(iii\('\)) \(G\) is locally of finite type over \(S\), of height
\(\leq1\), and \(\omega_{G/S}\) is locally free;
(iv) \(G\) is locally of finite presentation and flat over \(S\), and of
height \(\leq1\). Here are proofs.
(a) If \(G\simeq\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\), Lemma 7.3 identifies
\(\mathcal L\) with \(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\) as a \(p\)-Lie algebra;
hence (i) \(\Rightarrow\) (i\('\)), and the converse is trivial.
(b) Clearly (iii) \(\Rightarrow\) (iii\('\)). Conversely, 7.4.2 uses the
finite presentation of \(G\) only to see that the ideal \(\mathcal I\) is
locally of finite type, hence locally nilpotent. This holds as soon as
\(G\) is locally of finite type: if an \(R\)-algebra \(B\) is generated by
\(b_1,\ldots,b_n\) and \(\varepsilon:B\to R\) is a retraction, then
\(\operatorname{Ker}\varepsilon\) is generated by the
\(b_i-\varepsilon(b_i)\). The affine algebra \(\mathcal A\) is then
locally generated by finitely many sections of \(\mathcal I\) of zero
\(p\)-th power, so it is a finite \(\mathcal O_S\)-module, \(\omega_{G/S}\)
is of finite type, and the rest of 7.4.2 applies.
(c) (i) \(\Rightarrow\) (iv): \(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\) is finite and
locally free (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.4.1}{5.4.1}) and of height \(\leq1\) (lemma of 7.2).
(d) (iv) \(\Rightarrow\) (iii): it suffices to show that
\(\omega_{G/S}\) is locally free. As in 7.4.2, \(G\) is finite over \(S\),
and its affine algebra is an \(\mathcal O_S\)-module of finite
presentation, hence locally free since it is flat. We may therefore
suppose that \(S\) is the spectrum of a local ring \(R\) with residue field
\(k\), and that \(G=\operatorname{Spec}A\) with \(A\) free over \(R\); let
\(I\) be the augmentation ideal of \(A\). Choose
\(x_1,\ldots,x_r\in I\) whose images form a basis of
\(\omega_{G_k/k}=I_k/I_k^2\), let \(N\) be the free \(R\)-module with
basis \(x_1,\ldots,x_r\), and put \(B=\mathrm S_R(N)/(x^p)_{x\in N}\).
Since every element of \(I\) has zero \(p\)-th power, the map
\(N\to I\) induces a homomorphism of augmented \(R\)-algebras
\(\psi:B\to A\). The \(k\)-group \(G_k\) satisfies (iii), hence (i); so,
by 7.4.1, \(\psi\otimes_Rk\) is an isomorphism. By Nakayama's lemma,
\(\psi\) is surjective; as \(A\) is flat over \(R\), the sequence
\(0\to(\operatorname{Ker}\psi)\otimes_Rk\to B\otimes_Rk\to A\otimes_Rk\to0\)
is exact, so \((\operatorname{Ker}\psi)\otimes_Rk=0\); and
\(\operatorname{Ker}\psi\) is of finite type, \(A\) being of finite
presentation, so \(\operatorname{Ker}\psi=0\) by Nakayama's lemma. Thus
\(\psi\) is an isomorphism of augmented algebras, and
\(\omega_{G/S}=I/I^2\) is isomorphic to \(N\), hence free.
(e) It follows (7.5 of the re-edition) that the functors
\(G\mapsto\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\) and
\(\mathcal L\mapsto\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\) are quasi-inverse
equivalences between the category of \(S\)-groups that are locally of
finite presentation, flat, and of height \(\leq1\), and the category of
finite locally free \(\mathcal O_S\)-\(p\)-Lie algebras: by (c), (d) and
(a), both functors take values in the stated categories;
\(j_{\mathcal L}\) (7.3) is an isomorphism
\(\mathcal L\to\operatorname{Lie}\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\), natural in
\(\mathcal L\); and the homomorphism
\(\mathfrak G_p(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S))\to G\) of 7.4.2, which is natural
in \(G\) by Theorem 7.2, is an isomorphism.
