
\subsection*{5.1}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:5.1}

Let \(G\) be an infinitesimal formal \(k\)-group with affine algebra
\(A\), \(B\) a closed subalgebra of \(A\), \(I_B\) the intersection
\(B\cap I_A\) of \(B\) with the augmentation ideal of \(A\),
\(\overline{AI_B}\) the closed ideal of \(A\) generated by \(I_B\),
\(X\) the formal spectrum of \(B\), and \(r:G\to X\) the epimorphism
induced by the inclusion of \(B\) in \(A\). We propose to see under
what condition \(r\) makes \(X\) the left quotient of \(G\) by a
subgroup \(F\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.4}{2.4})}:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:5.1:proposition}
{\itshape With the notation above, the following two assertions are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] For every \(x\in B\),
\[
  \Delta_Ax-1\widehat\otimes x
  \in
  \overline{AI_B}\widehat\otimes_kA.
\]

\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIB:5.1:proposition:item:ii}(ii)}] The diagonal morphism of \(A\) induces, by passage to the
quotient, a diagonal morphism on the algebra \(A/\overline{AI_B}\)
which makes the formal spectrum of this algebra a formal subgroup of
\(G\). Moreover, if \(q\) denotes the canonical projection of \(A\)
onto \(A/\overline{AI_B}\), the sequence
\[
  \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.4em]
    B \arrow[r,"\mathrm{incl.}"]
    & A
      \arrow[r,shift left=0.7ex,"\mathrm{in}_2"]
      \arrow[r,shift right=0.7ex,"(q\widehat\otimes A)\Delta_A"']
    & (A/\overline{AI_B})\widehat\otimes_kA
  \end{tikzcd}
  \tag{*}
\]
is exact; in other words, \(r\) induces an isomorphism of
\(F\backslash G\) onto \(X\).
\end{enumerate}}

Assertion \textup{(i)} simply means that \(B\) is contained in the
kernel of the pair \((\mathrm{in}_2,(q\widehat\otimes A)\Delta_A)\).
Consequently, \textup{(ii)} implies \textup{(i)}. The proof of the
converse occupies \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.1.1}{5.1.1} to \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.1.5}{5.1.5}.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, 5.0; our formulation). Let \(G\) be any
formal \(k\)-group, with affine algebra \(A\), and \(H=\mathbf H(G)\) its
bialgebra (2.2.1), a cocommutative Hopf algebra whose dual is \(A\)
(0.2.2). If \(F\) is a formal subgroup of \(G\), defined by the closed
ideal \(J\), the affine algebra of \(F\backslash G\) (2.4) is the closed
subalgebra \(B\) of the \(b\in A\) such that
\(\Delta_Ab-1\widehat\otimes b\in J\widehat\otimes_kA\); it satisfies
\(\Delta_A(B)\subset B\widehat\otimes_kA\), and \(J=\overline{A(B\cap
I_A)}\), \(F\) being the fiber of \(G\to F\backslash G\) at the unit
element (2.4). Thus \(F\mapsto B\) is injective. Conversely, if \(B\) is
a closed subalgebra of \(A\) with \(\Delta_A(B)\subset
B\widehat\otimes_kA\), condition \textup{(i)} of the proposition holds
(write \(\Delta_Ab\) as \(1\widehat\otimes a\) plus an element of
\((B\cap I_A)\widehat\otimes_kA\), and apply \(\varepsilon_A\widehat\otimes
A\) to see that \(a=b\)); so, when \(G\) is infinitesimal, the
proposition makes \(F\mapsto B\) a bijection onto these subalgebras. Dually,
\(F\) corresponds to the Hopf subalgebra \(K\) of \(H\) orthogonal to
\(J\), and \(B\) to its orthogonal, a coideal of \(H\) which is an ideal
on one side, generated on that side by the augmentation ideal of \(K\).
For every cocommutative Hopf algebra over a field, the map from Hopf
subalgebras to such one-sided ideals that are coideals is bijective:
K.~Newman, \emph{A correspondence between bi-ideals and sub-Hopf algebras
in cocommutative Hopf algebras}, J.\ Algebra \textbf{36} (1975), 1--15,
using the Cartier--Gabriel--Kostant theorem and M.~E.~Sweedler,
\emph{Hopf algebras with one grouplike element}, Trans.\ Amer.\ Math.\
Soc.\ \textbf{127} (1967), 515--526; a shorter proof is due to
H.-J.~Schneider, \emph{Principal homogeneous spaces for arbitrary Hopf
algebras}, Israel J.\ Math.\ \textbf{72} (1990), 167--195, and
A.~Masuoka, \emph{On Hopf algebras with cocommutative coradicals},
J.\ Algebra \textbf{144} (1991), 451--466, extended it to Hopf algebras
with cocommutative coradical. For the commutative Hopf algebra \(A\) of
an affine group scheme \(G\) over \(k\), where \(G/F\) need not be
affine, M.~Takeuchi showed that the normal subgroups correspond to the
subalgebras \(B\) with \(\Delta(B)\subset A\otimes B\) stable under the
antipode (\emph{A correspondence between Hopf ideals and sub-Hopf
algebras}, Manuscripta Math.\ \textbf{7} (1972), 251--270), and the
subgroups \(F\) with \(G/F\) affine to such subalgebras \(B\) over which
\(A\) is faithfully flat (\emph{Relative Hopf modules---equivalences and
freeness criteria}, J.\ Algebra \textbf{60} (1979), 452--471).
