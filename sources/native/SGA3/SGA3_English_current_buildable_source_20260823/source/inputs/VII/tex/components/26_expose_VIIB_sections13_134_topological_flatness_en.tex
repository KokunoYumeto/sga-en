
\subsection*{1.3}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.3}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{1.3. Monomorphisms and topological flatness}

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.3.prop}
{\itshape Let \(f:X\to Y\) be a morphism of formal varieties over
\(k\), let \(A\) and \(B\) be the affine algebras of \(X\) and \(Y\),
and let \(g:B\to A\) be the morphism induced by \(f\). Then \(f\) is a
monomorphism in \(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\) if and only if \(g\) is
surjective.}

Indeed, \(A\) is a pseudocompact \(k\)-module and \(g\) makes \(A\) a
pseudocompact \(B\)-module (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.4}{0.4}); one may therefore suppose that
\(B\) equals \(k\).\footnote{Translator's note: by 1.1, \(f\) is a monomorphism if and only if \(g\) is an
epimorphism in \(\operatorname{Alp}/k\), which is the case if \(g\) is surjective; what is proved here is
the converse. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, expands the first steps of
the proof in this way.} Moreover, since \(B\) and \(A\) are the products of
the local components \(B_{\mathfrak n}\) and \(A_{\mathfrak n}\)
(\(\mathfrak n\in\Upsilon(B)\)), one may suppose \(B\) local. If
\(\mathfrak n\) is then the maximal ideal of \(B\), it is enough to show
that \(g\widehat{\otimes}_B(B/\mathfrak n)\) is surjective (Nakayama's
lemma, \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.3}{0.3.3}); now \(g\widehat{\otimes}_B(B/\mathfrak n)\) is deduced
from \(g\) by base change, hence is an epimorphism of
\(\operatorname{Alp}/k\). One may therefore suppose that \(B=k\) is a
field. Now \(f\) is a monomorphism if and only if the diagonal morphism
\[
  X\longrightarrow X\times_YX
\]
is an isomorphism, that is, if the homomorphism
\(x\widehat{\otimes}_Bx'\mapsto xx'\) is an isomorphism from
\(A\widehat{\otimes}_kA\) onto \(A\). Since \(k\) is a field, this implies
\(A=k\).\footnote{Translator's note: so a monomorphism \(f\) is an isomorphism from \(X\) onto the closed
formal subvariety \(\operatorname{Spf}(B/\operatorname{Ker}g)\) of \(Y\) (\(\operatorname{Ker}g\) is closed,
and \(B/\operatorname{Ker}g\to A\) is an isomorphism of pseudocompact \(B\)-modules by the Addition in 0.2,
(d)). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, adds this remark.}

\subsubsection*{1.3.1}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.3.1}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsubsection}{\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.1}{1.3.1}. Effective epimorphisms}

The preceding proposition implies, in particular, that every
monomorphism of \(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\) is effective
\textup{(cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:definition:1:3}{IV 1.3})}.
The same is not true of epimorphisms, as one easily sees by slightly
modifying the counterexample of Expos\'e V, \S~2.c);\footnote{Translator's note: for instance, over a field
\(k\), let \(A=k[[T]]\) and let \(B\) be the closed subalgebra topologically generated by \(T^3\) and
\(T^4\) (the series without terms in \(T\), \(T^2\), \(T^5\)). Then \(\operatorname{Spf}A\to\operatorname{Spf}B\)
is an epimorphism of \(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\), \(B\to A\) being injective, but it is not effective:
\(T^5\notin B\), and yet in \(A\widehat\otimes_BA\) one has
\(T^5\otimes1=T\otimes T^4=T^4\otimes T=1\otimes T^5\), since \(T^3,T^4\in B\). The Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, gives this example.} this is why we shall consider a
convenient class of effective epimorphisms:

A morphism \(f:X\to Y\) of formal \(k\)-varieties is said to be
\emph{surjective} if it induces a surjection of the underlying sets; it
is said to be \emph{topologically flat} if, for every point \(x\) of
\(X\), the homomorphism
\[
  f_x^\natural:
  \mathcal O_{Y,f(x)}
  \longrightarrow
  \mathcal O_{X,x}
\]
induced by \(f\) makes \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\) a topologically free module
over \(\mathcal O_{Y,f(x)}\).\footnote{Translator's note: equivalently, the affine algebra \(A\) of \(X\) is
a projective pseudocompact module over the affine algebra \(B\) of \(Y\), or again
\(M\mapsto M\widehat\otimes_BA\) is exact (0.3.7). Indeed, the local component of \(A\) at \(y\in Y\) is
\(\prod_{f(x)=y}\mathcal O_{X,x}\); it is projective over \(B_y=\mathcal O_{Y,y}\) if and only if each
\(\mathcal O_{X,x}\) is, and over the local ring \(B_y\) projective means topologically free (0.2.1). This
explains the terminology; the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, states it as a
lemma.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.3.1.effective-epi}
{\itshape Let \(f:X\to Y\) be a surjective, topologically flat
morphism of formal \(k\)-varieties. Then \(f\) is an effective
epimorphism \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:iv:definition:1:3}{IV 1.3})}.}

Indeed, let \(A\) and \(B\) be the affine algebras of \(X\) and \(Y\),
and let \(g:B\to A\) be the morphism induced by \(f\). By what we saw in
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2}{1.2}, one may suppose \(B\) local. Our hypothesis then means that
\(g\) makes \(A\) a nonzero topologically free \(B\)-module. If
\(\mathfrak n\) is the maximal ideal of \(B\),
\(A/\overline{\mathfrak nA}\) is therefore not zero, so that the map
\[
  g':B/\mathfrak n\longrightarrow
     A/\overline{\mathfrak nA}
\]
deduced from \(g\) is injective. By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.2}{Lemma 1.3.2} below, the
\(B\)-module \(A\) therefore identifies with the direct sum of \(B\) and of
a complement \(A'\) of the image of \(B\) in \(A\). It plainly follows
that \(g\) is an isomorphism from \(B\) onto the part of \(A\) formed by
the \(a\) such that
\[
  a\widehat{\otimes}_B1
  =
  1\widehat{\otimes}_B a.
\]

\subsubsection*{1.3.2}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.3.2}

\noindent\textbf{Lemma 1.3.2.}
{\itshape Let \(B\) be a local pseudocompact ring with maximal ideal
\(\mathfrak n\), let \(M\) and \(N\) be two projective pseudocompact
\(B\)-modules, and let \(g\) be a morphism from \(M\) to \(N\). Then
\(g\) is an isomorphism from \(M\) onto a direct factor of \(N\) if
\[
  (B/\mathfrak n)\widehat{\otimes}_B g
\]
is an injection.}

Indeed, put
\[
  g'=(B/\mathfrak n)\widehat{\otimes}_B g.
\]
Then \(g'\) has a retraction \(r'\). If \(p\) and \(q\) are the
canonical projections from \(M\) and \(N\) onto
\[
  M/\overline{\mathfrak nM}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  N/\overline{\mathfrak nN},
\]
there is therefore a morphism \(r\) such that
\[
  p\circ r=r'\circ q;
\]
consequently \(r'\) is deduced from \(r\) by passage to the quotient,
and \(r\circ g\) is an isomorphism because \(r'\circ g'\) is
(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.4}{0.3.4}). If \(s\) is the isomorphism inverse to \(r\circ g\),
then \(g\) has \(s\circ r\) as a retraction.

\subsubsection*{1.3.3}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.3.3}

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1.3.3.}
{\itshape Let \(f:X\to Y\) and \(g:Y\to Z\) be morphisms of formal
\(k\)-varieties.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textup{(i)}]
  {\itshape If \(f\) and \(g\) are topologically flat, so is
  \(g\circ f\).}

  \item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIB:1.3.3:item:ii}(ii)}]
  {\itshape If \(f\) and \(g\circ f\) are topologically flat and
  \(f\) is surjective, then \(g\) is topologically flat.}\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has ``Si
  \(g\) et \(gf\) sont topologiquement plats''; the hypothesis concerns \(f\), as the proof shows. The
  Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, corrects this.}

  \item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIB:1.3.3:item:iii}(iii)}]
  {\itshape If \(f\) is topologically flat, then so is
  \(f\times_YY'\) after every base change \(Y'\to Y\).}
\end{enumerate}

Assertions \textup{(i)} and \textup{\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.3:item:iii}{(iii)}} are clear. To prove
\textup{\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.3:item:ii}{(ii)}}, let \(A,B,C\) be the affine algebras of \(X,Y,Z\), and
let \(f':B\to A\), \(g':C\to B\) be the homomorphisms induced by
\(f,g\). Since \(g\circ f\) is topologically flat,
\(f'\circ g'\) makes \(A\) a projective pseudocompact \(C\)-module.
Likewise, \(f'\) makes \(A\) a faithful pseudocompact
\(B\)-module. Thus, as \(P\) ranges over pseudocompact \(C\)-modules
and \(N\) over pseudocompact \(B\)-modules, the functors
\[
  P\longmapsto P\widehat{\otimes}_C A,
  \qquad
  N\longmapsto N\widehat{\otimes}_B A
\]
are exact; the second is also faithful. Hence the functor
\[
  P\longmapsto P\widehat{\otimes}_C B
\]
is exact, and \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.7}{0.3.7} implies that \(B\) is a projective
pseudocompact \(C\)-module.

\subsubsection*{1.3.4}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.3.4}

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1.3.4.}
{\itshape Let \(S\) be a scheme, \(Y\) a locally Noetherian
\(S\)-scheme, and \(f:X\to Y\) an \(S\)-morphism locally of finite type
and faithfully flat. Then
\[
  \widehat f:
  \widehat X/\widehat S
  \longrightarrow
  \widehat Y/\widehat S
\]
is a surjective and topologically flat morphism of formal
\(\widehat S\)-varieties \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2.6}{1.2.6})}. Moreover, the sequence}

\end{center}

{\itshape deduced from the exact sequence}

\begin{center}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viib_15_95/diag_085}
\end{center}

{\itshape is exact.}\footnote{Translator's note: the sequence \((\ast)\) is exact, that is, \(f\) is an
effective epimorphism of schemes, because \(f\), faithfully flat and locally of finite presentation (\(Y\)
being locally Noetherian), is a covering for the faithfully flat quasi-compact topology (Expos\'e~IV,
\SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:6:3:1}{6.3.1}~(iv), and \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:definition:1:3}{1.3}). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, states this in the proposition.}

Indeed, let \(y\) be a point of \(Y\), with image \(s\) in \(S\), such
that \(\kappa(y)\) is a finite extension of the residue field
\(\kappa(s)\) of \(s\). Since \(f\) is locally of finite type,
\(f^{-1}(y)\) is locally of finite type over \(\kappa(y)\), and the
points of \(\widehat X/\widehat S\) lying over \(y\) are the closed points
of \(f^{-1}(y)\); this shows that \(\widehat f\) is surjective.

If \(x\) is a closed point of \(f^{-1}(y)\), the local rings of \(x\) and
\(y\) on \(\widehat X/\widehat S\) and \(\widehat Y/\widehat S\) are the
completions of \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\) and \(\mathcal O_{Y,y}\) for the
linear topologies defined by the powers of the maximal
ideals.\footnote{Translator's note: \(\mathcal O_{Y,y}\) and \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\) are Noetherian, so their
ideals of finite colength are the ideals containing a power of the maximal ideal. The Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, makes this explicit.} By SGA~1, IV, 5.8,
\(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X,x}\) is therefore flat over
\(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{Y,y}\). By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.8}{0.3.8},
\(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X,x}\) is a topologically free
\(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{Y,y}\)-module.

Finally, we saw in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2.6}{1.2.6}\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has
``1.2.4''; the statement is in 1.2.6. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B},
moves this part of the argument into 1.2.6.} that the functor
\(T\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Vaf}/\widehat S}(T,\widehat X/\widehat S)\)
is naturally isomorphic to the functor
\(T\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{(\operatorname{Sch}/S)}(T,X)\) (\(T\) being
of finite length). Since a projective limit of left exact functors is
computed ``argument by argument'' and \(\operatorname{Vaf}/\widehat S\)
is equivalent to the category of left exact contravariant functors from
\(\operatorname{Vaf}^{\ell f}/\widehat S\) to \((\mathbf{Sets})\), one
sees that the functor \(X\mapsto\widehat X/\widehat S\) commutes with
projective limits. In particular,
\(\widehat{X\times_YX}/\widehat S\) identifies with
\(\widehat X/\widehat S\times_{\widehat Y/\widehat S}\widehat X/\widehat S\),
so that our last assertion says simply that \(\widehat f\) is an
effective epimorphism (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.1}{1.3.1}).
