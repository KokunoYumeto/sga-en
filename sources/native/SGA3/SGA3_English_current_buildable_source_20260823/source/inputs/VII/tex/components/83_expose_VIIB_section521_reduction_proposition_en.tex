
\subsubsection*{5.2.1}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:5.2.1}

Let \(I\) be the maximal ideal of \(B\), and put
\[
  \omega=I/\overline{I^2}.
\]
Denote by \(I_r\) the closed vector subspace of \(I\) formed by the
elements \(x\) such that \(x^{p^r}=0\), and by \(\omega_r\) the
canonical image of \(I_r\) in \(\omega\).
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:5.2.1:section-proposition}
We shall see that \(B\) is isomorphic to
\[
  k[[\omega]]
  \big/
  \bigl((x^{p^r})\bigr)_{x\in\omega_r}
\]
whenever there exists a section \(\sigma:\omega\to I\) of the canonical
projection of \(I\) onto \(I/\overline{I^2}\) such that
\(\sigma(\omega_r)\subset I_r\) for every \(r\): such a section indeed
extends to a continuous homomorphism \(k[[\omega]]\to B\), which
factors through
\[
  B'
  =
  k[[\omega]]
  \big/
  \bigl((x^{p^r})\bigr)_{x\in\omega_r}.
\]
We prove in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.2.2}{5.2.2} to \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.2.5}{5.2.5} that the homomorphism
\[
  \varphi:B'\longrightarrow B
\]
which it induces is bijective.

What precedes applies in particular when the \emph{filtration} of
\(\omega\) is \emph{stationary}, that is, when there exists an integer
\(n_0\) such that \(\omega_n=\omega_{n_0}\) for \(n_0\leq n<+\infty\).
In this case, indeed, one can construct \(\sigma\) first on
\(\omega_1\), then on a complement \(\omega'_2\) of \(\omega_1\) in
\(\omega_2\), then on a complement \(\omega'_3\) of \(\omega_2\) in
\(\omega_3\), \ldots, and finally on a complement \(\omega'_\infty\) of
\(\omega_{n_0}\) in \(\omega\). It is clear that one thus obtains an
isomorphism of \(k[[\omega]]/((x^{p^r}))_{x\in\omega_r}\) onto the
completed tensor product of the algebras
\[
\begin{gathered}
  k[[\omega_1]]/\bigl((x^p)\bigr)_{x\in\omega_1},\quad
  k[[\omega'_2]]/\bigl((x^{p^2})\bigr)_{x\in\omega'_2},\quad\ldots,
  \\
  k[[\omega'_{n_0}]]/\bigl((x^{p^{n_0}})\bigr)_{x\in\omega'_{n_0}},\quad
  k[[\omega'_\infty]].
\end{gathered}
\]

The filtration of \(\omega\) is evidently stationary if
\(\omega_r=\omega\) for \(r\) large enough (case \textup{(i)}), or if
\(\omega\) is finite-dimensional (case \textup{(ii)}). The statement
above therefore implies our theorem.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, 5.2.1.A--5.2.1.B; our formulation). A
section \(\sigma\) of the required kind also exists in the following
case, where the filtration need not be stationary. Let
\(\omega_\infty\) be the closure of \(\bigcup_r\omega_r\). For
\(r\geq1\), let \(S_r\) be a closed complement in \(I_r\) of
\(I_r\cap(\overline{I^2}+I_{r-1})\); the projection \(I\to\omega\) maps
\(S_r\) isomorphically onto a complement \(\omega'_r\) of
\(\omega_{r-1}\) in \(\omega_r\). Let \(\theta\) be the morphism into
\(\omega_\infty\), defined on the direct sum \(\widehat\bigoplus_rS_r\)
of the \(S_r\) in the category of linearly compact vector spaces, which
is the projection on each \(S_r\). If \(\theta\) is an isomorphism, one
defines \(\sigma\) on \(\omega_\infty\) by composing \(\theta^{-1}\) with
the morphism \(\widehat\bigoplus_rS_r\to I\) given by the inclusions,
and on a complement of \(\omega_\infty\) in \(\omega\) by any continuous
section; then \(\sigma(\omega_r)\subset S_1+\cdots+S_r\subset I_r\). By
duality \textup{(0.2.2)}, \(\theta\) is an isomorphism if and only if the
vector space \(V=\omega_\infty^\dagger\), filtered by the orthogonals
\(V_r\) of the \(\omega_r\), is complete, that is,
\(V\to\varprojlim_rV/V_r\) is bijective. The re-edition also asserts
that \(\sigma\) exists when this filtration of \(V\) splits, that is,
when \(V\) is the direct sum of subspaces \(F_r\) with
\(V_r=\bigoplus_{i\geq r}F_i\); such a splitting exists when \(V\) has
countable dimension (by an inductive choice of the \(F_r\), a proof
which the re-edition credits to E.~Bouscaren), but not always: the space
of all sequences \((u_i)_{i\in\mathbb N}\) of elements of \(k\),
filtered by the subspaces of sequences vanishing in degrees \(<r\), has
successive quotients of dimension \(1\) and is not of countable
dimension. We have not been able to check that a section \(\sigma\) of
the required kind exists in this second case.
