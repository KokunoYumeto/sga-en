
Let us denote by \(\operatorname{Vaf}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}/k\) the full
subcategory of \(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\) formed by the étale formal
varieties. The inclusion of \(\operatorname{Vaf}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}/k\)
in \(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\) then has a left adjoint \(X\mapsto X_e\),
which can be described as follows: the formal variety \(X_e\) has the
same points as \(X\); moreover, if \(x\) is a point of \(X\), with
residue field \(\kappa(x)\) and projection \(s\) on
\(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\), the local algebra \(\mathcal O_{X_e,x}\) is
a projective pseudocompact \(k\)-module, and
\(\kappa(s)\widehat\otimes_k\mathcal O_{X_e,x}\) is isomorphic to the
largest separable extension of \(\kappa(s)\) contained in
\(\kappa(x)\). For every isomorphism \(q_x\) of
\(\kappa(s)\widehat\otimes_k\mathcal O_{X_e,x}\) into \(\kappa(x)\),
there is one and only one morphism of profinite algebras
\(p_x:\mathcal O_{X_e,x}\to\mathcal O_{X,x}\) compatible with \(q_x\)
and with the canonical projections of \(\mathcal O_{X_e,x}\) and
\(\mathcal O_{X,x}\) onto
\(\kappa(s)\widehat\otimes_k\mathcal O_{X_e,x}\) and \(\kappa(x)\).
These morphisms \(p_x\) define a canonical morphism \(p\) from \(X\) to
\(X_e\), which is functorial in \(X\). It follows from SGA~1 that every
morphism from \(X\) to an étale formal variety factors in one and only
one way through \(p\).

\textbf{(g)} \emph{The functor \(X\mapsto X_e\).} For \(x\in X\) over
\(s\), let \(\kappa_e(x)\) be the separable closure of \(\kappa(s)\)
in \(\kappa(x)\), and \(E_x\) the local étale \(k_s\)-algebra with
\(E_x\widehat\otimes_k\kappa(s)=\kappa_e(x)\), which exists and is
unique up to unique isomorphism by \textup{(f)}; it is free of finite
rank over \(k_s\). Then \(X_e=\operatorname{Spf}\bigl(\prod_xE_x\bigr)\),
and \(p_x:E_x\to\mathcal O_{X,x}\) is the unique continuous lift of
\(E_x\to\kappa_e(x)\subset\kappa(x)\), which exists by \textup{(a)}, the
maximal ideals of the quotients of finite length of
\(\mathcal O_{X,x}\) being nilpotent. If \(Y\) is étale, composition
with \(p\) is a bijection
\(\operatorname{Hom}(X_e,Y)\to\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)\). Indeed, by
\textup{(e)} both sides may be computed after the base change
\(k\to\kappa(k)\), where
\((X_e)_\kappa=\operatorname{Spf}\bigl(\prod_x\kappa_e(x)\bigr)\) and
\(Y_\kappa=\operatorname{Spf}\bigl(\prod_y\kappa(y)\bigr)\). A morphism
\(X_\kappa\to Y_\kappa\) amounts to the choice, for every \(x\), of a
point \(y\) over the same \(s\) and of a continuous
\(\kappa(s)\)-morphism
\(\kappa(y)\to\mathcal O_{X,x}\widehat\otimes_k\kappa(s)\), that is, by
\textup{(a)}, of a \(\kappa(s)\)-embedding of \(\kappa(y)\) in
\(\kappa(x)\), whose image lies in \(\kappa_e(x)\); and these are also
the data of a morphism \((X_e)_\kappa\to Y_\kappa\), compatibly with
\(p\). So \(X\mapsto X_e\) is left adjoint to the inclusion, \(p\)
being the unit of the adjunction.

When \(X\) is topologically flat over \(k\), \(p_x\) is injective,
\(\mathcal O_{X,x}\) being then a nonzero topologically free
\(E_x\)-module \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.6.1}{1.6.1} below)}. It need not be injective in
general: for \(k=\kappa[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\) and
\(X=\operatorname{Spf}(\kappa)\), one has \(X_e=\operatorname{Spf}(k)\),
and \(p_x\) is the projection \(k\to\kappa\).

Finally, \(X\mapsto X_e\) commutes with finite products \textup{(this is
used in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.5.1}{2.5.1})}. Let \(X\) and \(X'\) be formal \(k\)-varieties.
The morphism \((X\times X')_e\to X_e\times X'_e\) deduced from
\(p\times p'\) is a morphism of étale formal varieties \textup{(f)}, so
it is an isomorphism if it becomes one after base change to
\(\kappa(k)\); as \((X_e)_\kappa=(X_\kappa)_e\), we may suppose that
\(k\) is a field. The points of \(X\times X'\) over \(x\in X\) and
\(x'\in X'\) correspond to the prime ideals of
\(\kappa(x)\otimes_k\kappa(x')\), with the same residue fields, and
those of \(X_e\times X'_e\) to the prime ideals of the étale algebra
\(\kappa_e(x)\otimes_k\kappa_e(x')\). The former algebra is radicial
over the latter, a \(p^n\)-th power of each of its elements lying in
the latter, \(p\) being the characteristic exponent of \(k\); so their
prime ideals correspond, and the residue fields of the latter are the
separable closures of \(k\) in those of the former. Thus
\((X\times X')_e\to X_e\times X'_e\) is bijective and induces
isomorphisms of residue fields, so that it is an isomorphism, by
\textup{(d)}.
