
\subsubsection*{2.5.1}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:2.5.1}

Suppose again that the pseudocompact ring \(k\) is arbitrary. It is
clear that the functor \(G\mapsto G_e\) of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.6.1}{1.6.1} commutes with
finite products.\footnote{Translator's note: the functor \(X\mapsto X_e\) and the morphism
\(p\colon X\to X_e\) are defined in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.6}{1.6}. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, refers here to a paragraph it adds to 1.6 (its \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.6.F}{1.6.F}), which
proves that this functor commutes with finite products.} It therefore transforms a formal group into an étale
formal group. Moreover, since the morphism \(p:G\to G_e\) of
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.6}{1.6} is functorial in \(G\), it is a homomorphism of formal groups
when \(G\) is endowed with a group structure. Let us consider the
kernel of this homomorphism: since \(p\) induces a bijection on the
underlying sets, \(\ker(p)\) has for underlying set the image of
\(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\) under the unit section; if we identify the
underlying sets of \(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\) and \(\ker(p)\) by means of
this unit section, \(G_e\) has the same local algebra as
\(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\) at a point \(g\) of \(\ker(p)\); consequently,
\(\ker(p)\) has for local algebra at \(g\) the tensor product
\(k\widehat\otimes_k\mathcal O_{G,g}\), that is,
\(\mathcal O_{G,g}\).\footnote{Translator's note: in detail, \(\ker(p)\) is the fibre product of
\(p\) and of the unit section \(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\to G_e\). If \(g\) is a point of the unit section of
\(G\) over the point \(s\) of \(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\), and \(k_s\) is the local component of \(k\) at
\(s\), then \(g\) has residue field \(\kappa(s)\), so that \(\mathcal O_{G_e,g}=k_s\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.6}{1.6}), and
the local algebra of \(\ker(p)\) at \(g\) is
\(\mathcal O_{G,g}\widehat\otimes_{\mathcal O_{G_e,g}}k_s=\mathcal O_{G,g}\). The Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, writes these steps out.} For these reasons we shall say that \(\ker(p)\)
is the \emph{infinitesimal neighborhood of the origin} of \(G\), and we
shall write \(\ker(p)=G_0\), thus obtaining an exact sequence

In what follows, we shall say that \(G\) is infinitesimal if
\(G=G_0\).\footnote{Translator's note: equivalently, for every field \(K\) with the discrete
topology and every continuous homomorphism \(k\to K\), the unit is the only element of \(G(K)\). Indeed,
a point \(g\) of \(G\) defines an element of \(G(\kappa(g))\), which is the unit only if \(g\) lies on the unit
section; conversely, if \(G=G_0\), an element of \(G(K)\) factors through the residue field of a point of
the unit section, which is that of its image in \(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\), and is therefore the unit. The
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, adds this characterization, also with \(K\)
restricted to the residue fields \(\kappa(s)\) of \(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\); that is not enough: the étale
group \(\mu_3\) over \(\mathbb Q\) has no \(\mathbb Q\)-point other than \(1\).}

\noindent
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:2.5.1:corollary}%
\emph{When \(G\) is a formal group topologically flat over \(k\),}
\(p:G\to G_e\) is surjective and topologically flat by
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.6.1}{1.6.1}, so that \(G_e\) is identified with the quotient
\(G/G_0\) and \emph{we have an exact sequence}\footnote{Translator's note: a surjective and
topologically flat morphism of formal varieties is an effective epimorphism (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.1}{1.3.1}),
so that \(G_e\) is the quotient of \(G\) by the equivalence relation \(G\times_{G_e}G\), that is, by
\(\ker(p)=G_0\); the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, makes this step
explicit.}
