
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.3}
Let \(M\) and \(N\) be two pseudocompact \(A\)-modules, and let
\(\operatorname{Hom}_c(M,N)\) be the group of morphisms from \(M\) to \(N\).
Denote moreover by \(h_c^M\) the functor which associates with every
pseudocompact \(A\)-module \(N\) of \emph{finite length} the abelian group
\(\operatorname{Hom}_c(M,N)\). It is then known that the functor
\(M\mapsto h_c^M\) is an anti-equivalence from the category of
pseudocompact \(A\)-modules to the category of left exact functors
(\textup{[CA]}, II, Th.~1). If \(L\) and \(M\) are two pseudocompact
\(A\)-modules, one therefore defines, up to isomorphism, a pseudocompact
\(A\)-module \(L\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M\) by requiring the functor
\(N\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{c}(L\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M,N)\) to be
isomorphic to the functor \(N\mapsto\operatorname{Bil}_{c}(L\times M,N)\),
where \(\operatorname{Bil}_{c}(L\times M,N)\) denotes the set of continuous
\(A\)-bilinear maps from \(L\times M\) to a module \(N\) of finite length.

\medskip\noindent
\phantomsection\label{sga3:VIIB:0.2.G}%
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
\emph{Some consequences of this anti-equivalence} (cf.\ \textup{[CA]}, II, \S~4, Lemma~4 and
Corollary~1). (i) A pseudocompact \(A\)-module \(P\) is projective if and only if the functor
\(h_c^P\) is exact (loc.\ cit.). (ii) If \((M_i)\) is a filtered projective system of pseudocompact
\(A\)-modules and \(N\) has finite length, then
\(\operatorname{Hom}_c(\varprojlim_iM_i,N)=\varinjlim_i\operatorname{Hom}_c(M_i,N)\): the
anti-equivalence turns projective limits into inductive limits of left exact functors, and a
filtered inductive limit of left exact functors with values in abelian groups, formed argument by
argument, is left exact. (iii) By (i) and (ii), a filtered projective limit of projective objects is
projective, a filtered inductive limit of exact functors being exact; so is a product, which is the
filtered projective limit of its finite partial products. As every factor of a product is a direct
factor, \(\prod_iM_i\) is therefore projective if and only if every \(M_i\) is.

One can describe \(L\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M\) as the projective limit of
the (discrete) \(A\)-modules
\[
  (L/L')\otimes_{A}(M/M'),
\]
where \(L'\) and \(M'\) range respectively over the open submodules of
\(L\) and of \(M\). Indeed, let
\(\varphi:L\times M\to N\) be a continuous bilinear map from
\(L\times M\) to a (discrete) \(A\)-module of finite length. By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.1}{Lemma~0.3.1} below, there exist
open submodules \(L'\) and \(M'\) of \(L\) and \(M\) such that
\[
  \varphi(L'\times M)=\varphi(L\times M')=\{0\}.
\]
This means that the map
\[
  \overline{\varphi}:L\otimes_{A}M\longrightarrow N
\]
induced by \(\varphi\) has the form \(\varphi'\circ p\), where \(p\) is
the canonical projection from \(L\otimes_{A}M\) onto
\((L/L')\otimes_{A}(M/M')\). If \(\widehat{\varphi}\) is obtained by
composing \(\varphi'\) with the canonical projection from the projective
limit \(L\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M\) onto \((L/L')\otimes_{A}(M/M')\), it is
clear that the map \(\varphi\mapsto\widehat{\varphi}\) is a bijection
from \(\operatorname{Bil}_{c}(L\times M,N)\) onto
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{c}(L\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M,N)\).

The pseudocompact module \(L\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M\) is therefore the
separated completion of \(L\otimes_{A}M\) for the linear topology
defined by the kernels of the canonical projections
\[
  L\otimes_{A}M
  \longrightarrow
  (L/L')\otimes_{A}(M/M').
\]
If \(x\in L\) and \(y\in M\), the image of \(x\otimes_{A}y\) in the
\emph{completed tensor product} \(L\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M\) will be
denoted by \(x\widehat{\otimes}_{A}y\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 0.3.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.3.1}
\textit{Let \(L\), \(M\), and \(N\) be pseudocompact \(A\)-modules,
with \(N\) of finite length. If
\(\varphi:L\times M\to N\) is a continuous \(A\)-bilinear map, there
exist open submodules \(L'\) and \(M'\) of \(L\) and \(M\) such that}
\[
  \varphi(L'\times M)=\varphi(L\times M')=\{0\}.
\]

\noindent\textit{Proof.}
The inverse image \(\varphi^{-1}(0)\) is an open neighborhood of
\((0,0)\), and therefore contains an open set of the form
\(L_{1}\times M_{1}\), where \(L_{1}\) and \(M_{1}\) are open
submodules of \(L\) and \(M\). Since \(L/L_{1}\) has finite length,
there are elements \(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\) of \(L\) such that
\[
  L_{1}+Ax_{1}+\cdots+Ax_{r}=L.
\]
If \(M'\subset M_{1}\) is sufficiently small, then
\(\varphi(x_i,M')=0\) for every \(i\), since the map
\(y\mapsto\varphi(x_i,y)\) is continuous. It follows that
\(\varphi(L,M')=\{0\}\). Likewise,
\(\varphi(L',M)=\{0\}\) when \(L'\) is sufficiently small.
\hfill\(\square\)

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
Two consequences of Lemma~0.3.1. (a) \emph{Every open submodule \(M'\) of a pseudocompact \(A\)-module
\(M\) contains \(\overline{\mathfrak lM}\) for some open ideal \(\mathfrak l\) of \(A\); hence \(M\) is
the projective limit of the pseudocompact modules \(M/\overline{\mathfrak lM}\), with their quotient
topologies, \(\mathfrak l\) ranging over the open ideals of \(A\).} Indeed, the lemma applied to the
map \(A\times M\to M/M'\), \((a,m)\mapsto am+M'\), gives an open ideal \(\mathfrak l\) with
\(\mathfrak lM\subset M'\), and \(M'\) is closed. The canonical map
\(M\to\varprojlim_{\mathfrak l}M/\overline{\mathfrak lM}\) is then injective, and it is surjective by
the Addition in 0.2, (c); being a bijective morphism of pseudocompact modules, it is an isomorphism
(ibid., (d)). One may also use 0.3.2 and 0.3.5 below:
\(M\simeq M\widehat\otimes_A\varprojlim_{\mathfrak l}A/\mathfrak l
\simeq\varprojlim_{\mathfrak l}M/\overline{\mathfrak lM}\).
(b) \emph{The completed tensor product is associative}: there is a canonical isomorphism
\((L\widehat\otimes_AM)\widehat\otimes_AP\simeq L\widehat\otimes_A(M\widehat\otimes_AP)\), since both
modules represent the functor which associates with every module \(N\) of finite length the group of
continuous trilinear maps from \(L\times M\times P\) to \(N\). For the left-hand side, a continuous
bilinear map \(\beta\colon(L\widehat\otimes_AM)\times P\to N\) gives the trilinear map
\((x,y,z)\mapsto\beta(x\widehat\otimes y,z)\), and conversely a continuous trilinear map vanishes, by
the argument of Lemma~0.3.1, as soon as one variable lies in a suitable open submodule, so that it
factors through some \((L/L')\otimes_A(M/M')\times(P/P')\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.3.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.3.2}
Let
\[
  L'\xrightarrow{\,f\,}L\xrightarrow{\,g\,}L''\longrightarrow0
\]
be an exact sequence of pseudocompact \(A\)-modules. It is clear that
the induced sequence
\[
  0\longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Bil}_{c}(L''\times M,N)
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Bil}_{c}(L\times M,N)
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Bil}_{c}(L'\times M,N)
\]
is exact for every pseudocompact \(A\)-module \(M\) and every \(N\) of
finite length. This means that the sequence of left exact functors
represented by \(L''\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M\), \(L\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M\)
and \(L'\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M\) is exact; in other words, the sequence
\begin{equation}
  L'\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M
  \xrightarrow{\,f\widehat{\otimes}M\,}
  L\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M
  \xrightarrow{\,g\widehat{\otimes}M\,}
  L''\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M
  \longrightarrow0
  \tag{\(*\)}
  \label{sga3:VIIB:0.3.2.star}
\end{equation}
is exact: \emph{the completed tensor product functor is right exact}.

In particular, take \(L=A\), let \(f\) be the inclusion of a closed
ideal \(\mathfrak a\) into \(A\), and let \(g\) be the canonical
projection of \(A\) onto \(A/\mathfrak a\). We may then identify
\(A\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M\) with \(M\) by means of the map
\[
  x\widehat{\otimes}_{A}m\longmapsto xm.
\]
Since the image of
\(\mathfrak a\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M\) is closed in \(M\)
(\textup{[CA]}, IV, \S~3), and the image of
\(\mathfrak a\otimes_{A}M\) is dense in
\(\mathfrak a\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M\), the image of
\(f\widehat{\otimes}M\) is none other than the closure
\(\overline{\mathfrak aM}\) of the product \(\mathfrak aM\) in \(M\).
The sequence \(\textup{(\(*\))}\) therefore shows that
\((A/\mathfrak a)\widehat{\otimes}_{A}M\) identifies with
\(M/\overline{\mathfrak aM}\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.3.3. Nakayama's Lemma.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.3.3}
\textit{Let \(A\) be a pseudocompact ring, \(M\) a pseudocompact
\(A\)-module, and \(\mathfrak a\) an ideal of \(A\) contained in the
radical \(r(A)\). Then}
\[
  \overline{\mathfrak aM}=M
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  M=0.
\]

\noindent\textit{Proof.}
Let \(M'\) be an open submodule of \(M\), and let \(M''\) be the
quotient \(M/M'\). Since \(M''\) is discrete, \(\mathfrak aM''\) is
closed in \(M''\), and hence equals \(\overline{\mathfrak aM''}\). By
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.2}{0.3.2}, the canonical map
\[
  M/\overline{\mathfrak aM}
  \longrightarrow
  M''/\overline{\mathfrak aM''}
\]
is surjective, so that
\[
  \mathfrak aM''
  =
  \overline{\mathfrak aM''}
  =
  M''
\]
if \(\overline{\mathfrak aM}=M\); but this implies \(M''=0\), since
\(M''\) is a module of finite type over \(A\) (Nakayama). Consequently,
every open submodule \(M'\) of \(M\) is equal to \(M\), which is
therefore zero.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, adds two remarks. One may replace \(\mathfrak a\) by its closure, so that
0.3.2 applies: the closure is still contained in \(r(A)\), which is closed, and has the same product
closure \(\overline{\mathfrak aM}\), because \(\overline{\mathfrak a}M\subset\overline{\mathfrak aM}\) by
continuity of the multiplication. And \(M\) is zero because it is separated, the intersection of its open
submodules being \(0\).}
\hfill\(\square\)

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.3.4.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.3.4}
The usual consequences follow from Nakayama's lemma: if
\(\mathfrak a\) is a closed ideal contained in the radical of \(A\) and
\(f:M\to N\) is a morphism of pseudocompact \(A\)-modules, then \(f\) is
surjective if the induced map
\[
  f':
  M/\overline{\mathfrak aM}
  \longrightarrow
  N/\overline{\mathfrak aN}
\]
is surjective (apply \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.3}{Lemma~0.3.3} to \(\operatorname{Coker}f\)).
Likewise, if \(N\) is projective, \(f\) is invertible if \(f'\) is
(indeed \(f\) is surjective, hence has a section; then apply
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.3}{0.3.3} to \(\operatorname{Ker}f\)).

When \(A\) is local with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\), one may also
deduce from \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.3}{0.3.3} the following \emph{exchange theorem}:
if \(M\) is a topologically free \(A\)-module with pseudobasis
\((m_i)_{i\in I}\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.2.1}{0.2.1})} and \(N\) is a (closed)
direct factor of \(M\), there is a pseudobasis of \(M\) consisting of
elements of \(N\) and of some of the \(m_i\). Indeed, this is clear when
\(A\) is a field (use the duality of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.2.2}{0.2.2} and apply the
usual exchange theorem); consequently, \(N/\overline{\mathfrak mN}\) has
as a complement a topologically free \(A/\mathfrak m\)-module with
pseudobasis \((\overline{m}_i)_{i\in J}\), where \(\overline{m}_i\) is
the image of \(m_i\) in \(M/\overline{\mathfrak mM}\) and \(J\) is a
subset of \(I\).\footnote{Translator's note: here the 1970 text writes \(N/\mathfrak mN\) and
\(M/\mathfrak mM\). The quotients by the closures, which are the pseudocompact \(A/\mathfrak m\)-modules
\((A/\mathfrak m)\widehat\otimes_AN\) and \((A/\mathfrak m)\widehat\otimes_AM\) (0.3.2), are meant, as in the
first part of 0.3.4; the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, corrects this.} If
\(P\) denotes the direct product
\(\prod_{i\in J}Am_i\), the canonical map \(N\oplus P\to M\) is
``bijective modulo \(\mathfrak m\)''; it is therefore bijective by what
precedes (for another proof see \textup{[CA]}, IV, \S~2,
Proposition~8).
