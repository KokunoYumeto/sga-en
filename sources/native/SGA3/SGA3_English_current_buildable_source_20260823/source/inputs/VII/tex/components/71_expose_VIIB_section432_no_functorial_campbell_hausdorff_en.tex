
\subsubsection*{4.3.2}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:4.3.2}

``There is no Campbell--Hausdorff formula in characteristic
\(p>0\)''; let us explain: the functorial isomorphism of
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:4.3.1}{4.3.1} depends on the choice of the bases
\(({}^C x_i)_{i\in I}\). Unlike what happens in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:3.2:prop}{3.2}
\textup{(the case of characteristic \(0\))}, there is in general no
bijection
\[
  \mathbf L(C)\otimes_C\sqrt[p]{0_C}
  \longrightarrow
  \mathscr G_p(\mathbf L)(C)
\]
that is ``functorial both in \(C\) and in \(\mathbf L\).''
The following argument shows, for example, that no such
isomorphism exists when \(k\) is a field containing the
\((p^2-1)\)-st roots of unity.

Choose indeed \(\mathbf L\) so that, for every
\(C\in\operatorname{Alf}/k\), \(\mathbf L(C)\) is the
abelian \(p\)-Lie algebra over \(C\) generated by an element \(x\)
subject to the relation
\[
  x^{(p^2)}=0.
\]
One thus has
\[
  \mathbf L(C)
  =
  Cx\oplus Cx^{(p)}.
\]

We shall show that the only functorial morphism
\[
  \chi_C:
  \mathbf L(C)\otimes_C\sqrt[p]{0_C}
  \longrightarrow
  U_p\bigl(\mathbf L(C)\bigr)
\]
which is compatible with the endomorphisms of \(\mathbf L\) and
sends \(\mathbf L(C)\otimes_C\sqrt[p]{0_C}\) into
\(\mathscr G_p(\mathbf L)(C)\) is the constant morphism with value
\(1\). Let indeed \(\psi_C\) be the map of
\(\mathbf L(C)\otimes_C\sqrt[p]{0_C}\) into \(U_p(\mathbf L(C))\)
which sends
\[
  x\otimes a+x^{(p)}\otimes b
  \quad\text{to}\quad
  \sum_{0\leq i,j<p}
  \frac{a^i b^j}{i!\,j!}\,x^{\,i+pj}.
\]
\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text omits the factors \(1/(i!\,j!)\) here and in the formula for
\(\chi_C\) below. Without them \(\psi_C\) is not the bijection of 4.3.1 (for the basis \(x\), \(x^{(p)}\)),
and its values are not group-like when \(p\geq3\).}
Composing \(\chi_C\) with \(\psi_C^{-1}\), one obtains a morphism,
functorial in \(C\),
\[
  \mathbf L(C)\otimes_C\sqrt[p]{0_C}
  \longrightarrow
  \mathbf L(C)\otimes_C\sqrt[p]{0_C}
\]
which sends \(x\otimes a+x^{(p)}\otimes b\) to
\(x\otimes P(a,b)+x^{(p)}\otimes Q(a,b)\). Functoriality in \(C\)
implies that \(P(a,b)\) and \(Q(a,b)\) are the values at \((a,b)\) of
two polynomials \(P,Q\in k[X,Y]\), which one may suppose of degree
less than \(p\) in \(X\) as well as in \(Y\). If \(\alpha\) is an
element of \(k\) and \(\ell_\alpha\) is the endomorphism of
\(\mathbf L\) which sends \(x\) to \(\alpha x\), one sees at once that
the square

  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIB:4.3.2:naturality-endomorphism-square}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viib_15_95/diag_123}
\end{center}
is commutative. Taking into account the compatibility of \(\chi_C\)
with \(\ell_\alpha\), one deduces the equalities
\[
  P(\alpha a,\alpha^p b)=\alpha P(a,b),
  \qquad
  Q(\alpha a,\alpha^p b)=\alpha^p Q(a,b).
\]
Taking for \(\alpha\) a primitive \((p^2-1)\)-st root of unity and
for \(C\) the algebra \(k[X,Y]/(X^p,Y^p)\), one deduces that \(P\) and
\(Q\) are of the form
\[
  P=\lambda X,
  \qquad
  Q=\mu Y,
  \qquad
  \lambda,\mu\in k.
\]
Consequently, \(\chi_C\) sends \(x\otimes a+x^{(p)}\otimes b\) to
\[
  \sum_{0\leq i,j<p}
  \frac{(\lambda a)^i(\mu b)^j}{i!\,j!}\,x^{\,i+pj}.
\]

This last map ``commutes'' with the endomorphism of \(\mathbf L\)
which sends \(x\) to \(x^{(p)}\) only if \(\lambda=\mu=0\).

\noindent\textit{Translator's note.}\enspace This last assertion is not
correct, and this choice of \(\mathbf L\) does not prove the claim. Here
\(U_p(\mathbf L(C))=C[x]/(x^{p^2})\); writing
\(E(u)=\sum_{0\leq i<p}u^i/i!\), one has
\(\psi_C(x\otimes a+x^{(p)}\otimes b)=E(ax)E(bx^p)\). The endomorphisms
of \(\mathbf L\) are the maps \(x\mapsto\alpha x+\beta x^{(p)}\)
\((\alpha,\beta\in k)\), and since \(a^p=b^p=0\) and \(x^{p^2}=0\), one
has \(E(\alpha ax+\beta ax^p)=E(\alpha ax)E(\beta ax^p)\) and
\(E(\beta ax^p)E(\alpha^pbx^p)=E((\beta a+\alpha^pb)x^p)\). Hence
\(\psi_C\), and every \(\chi_C\) as above with \(\lambda=\mu\), commutes
with all the endomorphisms of \(\mathbf L\); the endomorphism
\(x\mapsto x^{(p)}\) only gives \(\lambda=\mu\). The claim becomes
correct if \(\mathbf L(C)\) is generated by an element \(x\) subject to
\(x^{(p^3)}=0\) and \(k\) contains the \((p^3-1)\)-st roots of unity:
the same scaling argument shows that \(\chi_C\) sends
\(\sum_{j<3}x^{(p^j)}\otimes a_j\) to \(\prod_jE(\lambda_ja_jx^{p^j})\);
the endomorphism \(x\mapsto x^{(p)}\) gives
\(\lambda_0=\lambda_1=\lambda_2=\lambda\); and for the endomorphism
\(x\mapsto x+x^{(p)}\), the algebra \(C=k[a,b]/(a^p,b^p)\) and the
element \(x\otimes a+x^{(p)}\otimes b\), the coefficient of
\(ab^{p-1}x^{p^2}\) is \(0\) on one side and \(\lambda^p/(p-1)!\) on the
other, whence \(\lambda=0\). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, replaces the last step by a comparison of
coefficients for \(x\mapsto x+x^{(p)}\) and \(C=k[a]/(a^3)\); that
computation uses \(\psi_C\) without the factors \(1/(i!\,j!)\), and once
they are restored it only gives \(\lambda=\mu\).
