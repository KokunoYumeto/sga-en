\subsection*{2.1}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:2.1}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{2.1. Formal groups and cogroups}

Let \(k\) be a pseudocompact ring and let \(G\) be a formal
\(k\)-group, that is, a group object in the category
\(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\) of formal varieties over \(k\). Let \(A\)
be the affine algebra of \(G\). The composition law of \(G\) plainly
defines a diagonal morphism, namely a homomorphism of profinite
\(k\)-algebras
\[
  \Delta_A:A\longrightarrow A\widehat\otimes_kA.
\]
This homomorphism has the following properties.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIB:2.1:item:i}(i)}] The diagram
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIB:2.1:coassociativity-square}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viib_15_95/diag_099}
\end{center}
is commutative.

\item[\textup{(ii)}] There is a necessarily unique
\emph{augmentation}, that is, a homomorphism of profinite
\(k\)-algebras
\[
  \varepsilon_A:A\longrightarrow k,
\]
such that the two composites displayed below are the identity maps of
\(A\):
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIB:2.1:counit-compositions}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viib_15_95/diag_100}
\end{center}

\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIB:2.1:item:iii}(iii)}] There is a necessarily unique \emph{antipode},
that is, a homomorphism of profinite \(k\)-algebras
\[
  c_A:A\longrightarrow A,
\]
such that the composite
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIB:2.1:antipode-composition}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viib_15_95/diag_101}
\end{center}
equals \(\eta_A\circ\varepsilon_A\). Here \(m_A\) is the linear map
that sends \(a\widehat\otimes b\) to \(ab\), and
\(\eta_A:k\to A\) sends \(\lambda\) to \(\lambda1_A\).
\end{enumerate}

It is clear that, conversely, every diagonal morphism \(\Delta_A\)
satisfying \textup{\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.1:item:i}{(i)}}, \textup{(ii)} and \textup{\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.1:item:iii}{(iii)}} induces a
morphism from \(G\times G\) to \(G\) that makes \(G\) a formal group
over \(k\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VII\textsubscript{B}, adds two remarks here. First, for every profinite
\(k\)-algebra \(B\), the product
\(\phi\cdot\psi=m_B\circ(\phi\widehat\otimes\psi)\circ\Delta_A\) makes the set of
continuous \(k\)-linear maps \(A\to B\) an associative monoid with unit
element \(\eta_B\circ\varepsilon_A\); the continuous algebra homomorphisms,
which are the points of \(G\) with values in \(\operatorname{Spf}(B)\), form a
group in it, \(\phi\) having the inverse \(\phi\circ c_A\) (the re-edition
calls the whole monoid a group, but a linear map such as \(0\) has no inverse).
Second, if \(K\) is a formal \(k\)-group with affine algebra \(B\), a
morphism of formal varieties \(K\to G\), with comorphism \(f:A\to B\), respects
the group laws if and only if
\((f\widehat\otimes f)\circ\Delta_A=\Delta_B\circ f\); then
\(\varepsilon_B\circ f=\varepsilon_A\) and \(c_B\circ f=f\circ c_A\), as for
any map of groups that respects products. The re-edition writes
\(\operatorname{Grf}/k\) for the category of formal \(k\)-groups.}

In what follows, we shall call the \emph{augmentation ideal} of \(A\)
the ideal \(I_A=\varepsilon_A^{-1}(0)\). We shall write
\(\omega_{G/k}\) for the pseudocompact \(k\)-module
\(I_A/\overline{I_A^2}\), that is, the quotient of \(I_A\) by the
closed ideal generated by the products \(x\cdot y\), with
\(x,y\in I_A\).
