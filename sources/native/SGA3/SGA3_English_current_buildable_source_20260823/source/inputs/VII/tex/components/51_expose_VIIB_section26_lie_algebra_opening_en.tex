
\subsection*{2.6}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:2.6}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.6}{2.6}. Lie algebras of formal groups}

We shall now study infinitesimal formal groups, to which the sections
that follow are devoted. In this study, Lie algebras play a fundamental
role.

Suppose first that the base ring \(k\) is Artinian, and let \(G\) be a
formal group over \(k\). The Lie algebra of \(G\) admits three different
interpretations, all of which we shall use:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{a)}
Let
\[
  D=k[d]/(d^2)
\]
be the algebra of dual numbers over \(k\), and let
\[
  \delta:D\longrightarrow k
\]
be the homomorphism that annihilates \(d\). For every formal group
\(G\) over \(k\), \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\) is the kernel of
\(G(\delta)\), so that by definition there is an \emph{exact sequence of
groups}
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIB:2.6:dual-numbers-exact-sequence}
  \(
  0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Lie}(G)
  \longrightarrow G(D)
  \xrightarrow{\ G(\delta)\ }G(k)
  \longrightarrow1.
  \)
\end{center}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{b)}
If \(G\) has \(A\) for affine algebra, the group \(G(D)\) has for
elements the homomorphisms of profinite algebras
\[
  f:A\longrightarrow D.
\]
The condition \(G(\delta)(f)=1\) is equivalent to
\[
  \delta\circ f=\varepsilon_A.
\]
For every \(x\in A\), we therefore have the relation
\[
  f(x)
  =
  \varepsilon_A(x)\cdot1_D+f'(y)\cdot d,
\]
where \(f'\) is a continuous linear map from \(I_A/\overline{I_A^2}\) to
\(k\), and \(y\) is the class of \(x-\varepsilon_A(x)\cdot1_A\) modulo
\(\overline{I_A^2}\).\footnote{Translator's note: indeed \(x-\varepsilon_A(x)\cdot1_A\) lies in
\(I_A=\ker\varepsilon_A\), and \(\delta\circ f=\varepsilon_A\) means that \(f\) maps \(I_A\) into
\(k\cdot d\); since \(d^2=0\), \(f\) vanishes on \(I_A^2\), hence, being continuous, on its closure. The
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, writes these steps out.} It is clear that \emph{the map \(f\mapsto f'\) is a
bijection from \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\) onto the topological dual of
\(\omega_{G/k}\)} \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.2.2}{0.2.2} and \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.1}{2.1})}. This bijection evidently
respects the group structures: indeed, let \(f\) and \(g\) be two
elements of \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\). Their product is the composite
\(h\circ\Delta_A\), where \(h:A\widehat\otimes A\to D\) is such that
\(h(a\widehat\otimes b)=f(a)\cdot g(b)\). But it follows from
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.1}{2.1}~(ii) that
\[
  \Delta_A a-a\widehat\otimes1-1\widehat\otimes a
  \in
  I_A\widehat\otimes I_A
\]
when \(a\in I_A\); if \(a\in I_A\), we therefore have
\[
  (f\cdot g)(a)=f(a)+g(a)
\]
\textup{(confer also II, \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:lemma:3-10}{3.10})}.

In what follows, we identify \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\) with
\(\omega_{G/k}^{\dagger}\) by means of the bijection \(f\mapsto f'\)
described above. \emph{The group \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\) is therefore
endowed with a structure of \(k\)-module.}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIB:2.6:item:c}\textbf{c)}
Let \(A^\dagger\) and \(D^\dagger\) be the \(k\)-modules dual to
\(A\) and \(D\), and let \(\{1_D^\dagger,d^\dagger\}\) be the basis dual to
the basis \(\{1_D,d\}\) of \(D\) over \(k\) \textup{(}\(1_D^\dagger=\delta\)\textup{)}.
Since \(D\) is free of finite rank over \(k\), the canonical map
\(f\mapsto{}^t f\) from the set \(\operatorname{Hom}_c(A,D)\) of morphisms
of pseudocompact \(k\)-modules from \(A\) to \(D\) to
\(\operatorname{Hom}_k(D^\dagger,A^\dagger)\) is bijective. On the other
hand, \({}^t f\) is determined by the values \({}^t f(1_D^\dagger)\) and
\({}^t f(d^\dagger)=x\). The condition \(G(\delta)(f)=1\) is equivalent to
the equality \({}^t f(1_D^\dagger)=\varepsilon_A\). One sees easily, on
the other hand, that \(f\) is compatible with multiplication if and only
if
\[
  \tag{*}
  \label{sga3:VIIB:2.6:primitive-relation}
  \delta_G(x)=\varphi_G(x\otimes1+1\otimes x)
  \qquad\textup{(cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.3}{2.3})}.
\]

Finally, it is clear that a continuous linear map \(f:A\to D\) which is
compatible with multiplication and such that
\(\delta\circ f=\varepsilon_A\) sends unit element to unit element.\footnote{Translator's note: indeed
\(\delta(f(1))=\varepsilon_A(1)=1\), so that \(f(1)=1+\lambda d\) with \(\lambda\in k\); then
\(1+\lambda d=f(1)=f(1)^2=1+2\lambda d\), whence \(\lambda=0\). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, gives this argument.} The
map \(f\mapsto x\) therefore allows us to identify \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\)
with the part of \(\mathbf H(G)\) formed by the elements satisfying
relation \textup{(*)}. If \(x\) and \(y\) are two elements of
\(\mathbf H(G)\) satisfying \textup{(*)}, we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \delta_G(xy)
  &=
  \delta_G(x)\delta_G(y)
  =
  \varphi_G\bigl(
    (x\otimes1+1\otimes x)
    (y\otimes1+1\otimes y)
  \bigr)
  \\
  &=
  \varphi_G\bigl(
    xy\otimes1+x\otimes y+y\otimes x+1\otimes xy
  \bigr),
\end{aligned}
\]
whence
\(\delta_G(xy-yx)=\varphi_G\bigl((xy-yx)\otimes1+1\otimes(xy-yx)\bigr)\).

This shows that the \(k\)-module \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\) is identified
with a Lie subalgebra of \(\mathbf H(G)\): we shall say that
\(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\) \emph{is the Lie algebra of} \(G\).\footnote{Translator's note: let
\(K\) be a \(k\)-group scheme of finite type and \(G\) its formal completion at the origin, whose affine
algebra is the completion of \(\mathcal O_{K,e}\) for the topology of its augmentation ideal
\(\mathfrak m\). Then \(\mathbf H(G)\), the union of the duals of the \(\mathcal O_{K,e}/\mathfrak m^{n}\),
is the infinitesimal algebra of \(K\) (Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:2.1}{2.1}), and
\(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\), the dual of \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\), is \(\operatorname{Lie}(K)\): as
\(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) has finite length, its topological and ordinary duals coincide (compare
VII\textsubscript{A}, \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:2.5}{2.5}). In particular, if \(\mathcal A(G)\) is a finite \(k\)-module, the Lie
algebra defined here is that of the \(k\)-group scheme \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A(G)\). The
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, adds this comparison.}

\subsubsection*{2.6.1}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:2.6.1}

When \(k\) is an arbitrary pseudocompact ring and \(G\) a formal group
over \(k\), we call \emph{\(\mathbf O_k\)-Lie algebra of \(G\)} the functor
\(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\) that assigns to every profinite \(k\)-algebra of
finite length \(C\) the Lie algebra \(\operatorname{Lie}(G\widehat\otimes_kC)\)
of \(G\widehat\otimes_kC\) over \(C\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2.3}{1.2.3})}; this Lie algebra is flat
over \(\mathbf O_k\) when \(\omega_{G/k}\) is a projective pseudocompact
\(k\)-module.\footnote{Translator's note: indeed, \(A=k\cdot1_A\oplus I_A\), so that the augmentation
ideal of \(A\widehat\otimes_kC\) is \(I_A\widehat\otimes_kC\) and
\(\omega_{(G\widehat\otimes_kC)/C}=\omega_{G/k}\widehat\otimes_kC\). By 2.6~b),
\(\operatorname{Lie}(G)(C)=\operatorname{Hom}_c(\omega_{G/k}\widehat\otimes_kC,C)\): the \(\mathbf O_k\)-module
underlying \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\) is the dual \(\mathbf V_k^f(\omega_{G/k})\) of \(\omega_{G/k}\) (1.2.3),
which is flat when \(\omega_{G/k}\) is projective. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, writes this out and adds the converse (by its
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2.3.E}{1.2.3.E}): if \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\) is flat over \(\mathbf O_k\), then \(\omega_{G/k}\)
is projective.}
