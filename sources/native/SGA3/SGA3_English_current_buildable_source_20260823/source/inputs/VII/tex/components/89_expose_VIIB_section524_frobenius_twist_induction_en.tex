
\subsubsection*{5.2.4}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:5.2.4}

For every linearly compact vector space \(V\) over \(k\), we denote
by
\[
  {}_{\pi}V
  =
  V\widehat\otimes_{\pi}k
\]
the space deduced from \(V\) by the extension \(x\mapsto x^p\) of the
field of scalars. One then has a commutative exact diagram

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viib_15_95/diag_135}
\end{figure}

\noindent
where one has put
\[
  \overline\omega=J/\overline{J^2}
\]
and where the maps \(u,v,w\) are induced by the linear map
\(x\widehat\otimes a\mapsto x^pa\) of \({}_{\pi}B\) into \(B^p\). If one
puts
\[
  J_n=\{\,x\in J\mid x^{p^n}=0\,\}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \overline\omega_n=\delta(J_n),
\]
it is clear that
\[
  J_n=v({}_{\pi}I_{n+1})
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \overline\omega_n=w({}_{\pi}\omega_{n+1}).
\]

\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:5.2.4:delta-source-note}
\noindent\textit{Translator's note.}\enspace The French text prints
\(\overline\omega_n=\delta(I_n)\), although the displayed exact
row defines \(\delta\) on \(J\), and the immediately preceding
definition introduces \(J_n\subset J\). The type-correct
\(\delta(J_n)\) is used above; all subsequent identities agree
with this reading.

Moreover, since \(u\) and \(v\) are surjections, \(w\) is a surjection
and has as kernel the image \({}_{\pi}\omega_1\) of the kernel of \(v\).\footnote{Translator's
note: here and below the 1970 text prints \(w(\omega_{n+1})\) for \(w({}_{\pi}\omega_{n+1})\), \(\omega_0\)
for \({}_{\pi}\omega_1\), and \(((X^{p^r}))_{x\in{}_r\overline\omega}\) for
\(((x^{p^n}))_{x\in\overline\omega_n}\) in the definition of \(B''\). Since \(k\) is perfect, \({}_{\pi}V\) may
be identified with \(V\), on which \(\lambda\in k\) acts by \(\lambda^{1/p}\); then \(v\) becomes
\(y\mapsto y^p\), whose kernel is \({}_{\pi}I_1\). The induction is on the height \(r\): \(B^p\), the affine
algebra of a homogeneous space of \(G\) (5.2.3), has height at most \(r-1\). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, corrects the misprints and makes these points explicit.}
The section
\[
  {}_{\pi}\sigma:{}_{\pi}\omega\longrightarrow{}_{\pi}I,
\]
which is induced by the section \(\sigma\) of 5.2.1, therefore defines
by passage to the quotient a section
\[
  \tau:\overline\omega\longrightarrow J
\]
which is compatible with the filtrations of \(J\) and
\(\overline\omega\). By the induction hypothesis, this section induces
an isomorphism
\[
  \psi:
  B''
  =
  k[[\overline\omega]]
  \big/
  \bigl((x^{p^n})\bigr)_{x\in\overline\omega_n}
  \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
  B^p.
\]
Since \(B''\) is manifestly identified with \(B'^p\), and \(\psi\) with
\(\varphi^p\), \emph{our theorem is therefore proved when \(B\) has
finite height.}
