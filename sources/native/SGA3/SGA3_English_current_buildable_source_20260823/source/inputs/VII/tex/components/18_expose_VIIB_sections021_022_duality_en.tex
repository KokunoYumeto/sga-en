
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.2.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.2.1}
Each local component \(A_{\mathfrak m}\) of \(A\) is a direct factor
of \(A\), hence a projective object of the category of pseudocompact
\(A\)-modules (\(A\) is manifestly projective). It follows from [CA] (IV,
\S~3, Corollary~1 of Theorem~3) that every projective pseudocompact
\(A\)-module is a (topological) direct product of such \(A_{\mathfrak m}\).

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
\emph{The decomposition is unique, and the \(A_{\mathfrak m}\) are, up to isomorphism, the indecomposable
projective objects.} Every simple pseudocompact \(A\)-module \(S\) is discrete and cyclic, with open
annihilator; so \(S\simeq A/\mathfrak m=A_{\mathfrak m}/\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}\) for a unique
\(\mathfrak m\in\Upsilon(A)\). A continuous linear map from a product \(\prod_iP_i\) to a discrete module
factors through a finite partial product, and \(\operatorname{Hom}_c(A_{\mathfrak m'},A/\mathfrak m)\) is
\(A/\mathfrak m\) for \(\mathfrak m'=\mathfrak m\) and \(0\) otherwise. Hence, if
\(P\simeq\prod_{i\in I}A_{\mathfrak m_i}\), the dimension of the \(A/\mathfrak m\)-vector space
\(\operatorname{Hom}_c(P,A/\mathfrak m)\) is the number of indices \(i\) with \(\mathfrak m_i=\mathfrak m\):
the factors are determined by \(P\) up to isomorphism, and \(P\) is indecomposable only if it is
isomorphic to a single \(A_{\mathfrak m}\). Conversely each \(A_{\mathfrak m}\) is indecomposable, since a
decomposition of it as a product would come from an idempotent of the local ring \(A_{\mathfrak m}\).

A pseudocompact \(A\)-module \(M\) is said to be
\emph{topologically free} if it is isomorphic to the product of a
family \((A_i)\) of copies of \(A\). A family \((m_i)\) of elements of
\(M\) is called a \emph{pseudobasis} of \(M\) if the \(A\)-linear maps
\(A_i\longrightarrow M\) sending the unit element of \(A\) to
\(m_i\) extend to an isomorphism
\[
  \prod_i A_i\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }M.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.2.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.2.2}
If \(M\) is a pseudocompact \(A\)-module, \(M^\dagger\) denotes the
\(A\)-module \(\operatorname{Hom}_c(M,A)\) formed by the continuous
\(A\)-linear maps from \(M\) to \(A\). When the ring \(A\) is Artinian, the
functor \(P\mapsto P^\dagger\) is an anti-equivalence from the category of
projective pseudocompact \(A\)-modules to the category of projective
\(A\)-modules. In particular, when \(A\) is a field, \(P\mapsto P^\dagger\)
is an anti-equivalence from the category of all pseudocompact \(A\)-modules
(one also speaks of \emph{linearly compact vector spaces} over \(A\)) to
the category of \(A\)-vector spaces.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
\emph{The quasi-inverse functor.} For an \(A\)-module \(N\), let \(N^*=\operatorname{Hom}_A(N,A)\) carry
the topology of pointwise convergence, for which the submodules
\(\{f\mid f(x_1),\ldots,f(x_s)\in\mathfrak l\}\), with \(x_1,\ldots,x_s\in N\) and \(\mathfrak l\) an open
ideal of \(A\), form a basis of neighborhoods of \(0\). Then \(N^*\) is a pseudocompact \(A\)-module: for
\(N=A^{(I)}\), \(N^*\) is \(A^I\) with the product topology; a homomorphism \(\varphi\colon N_1\to N_2\) has
a continuous transpose \({}^t\varphi\colon N_2^*\to N_1^*\); and a presentation
\(A^{(J)}\xrightarrow{\varphi}A^{(I)}\to N\to0\) identifies \(N^*\), with its topology, with the closed
submodule \(\operatorname{Ker}({}^t\varphi)\) of \(A^I\). When \(A\) is Artinian (so that one may take
\(\mathfrak l=0\)), the functors \(P\mapsto P^\dagger\) and \(Q\mapsto Q^*\) are quasi-inverse
anti-equivalences between projective pseudocompact \(A\)-modules and projective \(A\)-modules: the
canonical maps \(Q\to(Q^*)^\dagger\) and \(P\to(P^\dagger)^*\) are bijective for \(Q=A^{(I)}\) and
\(P=A^I\), a continuous linear map from \(A^I\) to the discrete ring \(A\) factoring through a finite
partial product; hence they are bijective for direct summands of these, which are all the projective
objects (0.2.1). For instance, when \(A\) is a field, the direct sum in \(\operatorname{PC}(A)\) of a
family \((V_i)\) is \(\bigl(\prod_iV_i^\dagger\bigr)^*\).
