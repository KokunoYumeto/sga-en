
\subsubsection*{5.2.3}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:5.2.3}

Suppose now that \(B\) has finite height at most \(r\): let
\[
  \pi:k\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }k,
  \qquad
  x\longmapsto x^p,
\]
be the isomorphism of \(k\) onto \(k\). The linear map of
\(B\widehat\otimes_{\pi}k\) into \(B\) which sends
\(b\widehat\otimes_{\pi}x\) to \(b^px=(bx^{1/p})^p\) has a closed image,
which is none other than the closed subalgebra
\[
  B^p=\{\,b^p\mid b\in B\,\}
\]
of \(B\). By Proposition \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.1:proposition}{5.1}, if one puts
\[
  J=B^p\cap I=B^p\cap I_A,
\]
\(B^p\) is the affine algebra of the left quotient of \(G\) by a
subgroup \(H'\) with affine algebra \(A/\overline{AJ}\).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970
text prints \(J=B^p\cap I=A\cap I_A\), calls this subgroup \(H\), like the subgroup of the theorem, and below
writes \(B=B/\overline{BJ}\) for \(B_1=B/\overline{BJ}\). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, has \(J=B^p\cap I_A\) and \(B_1\).} Since \(A\) is
topologically free over \(B\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.4.thm}{1.4})}, \(B\) is a direct factor
of the pseudocompact \(B\)-module \(A\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.2}{1.3.2})}, so that one can
identify
\[
  B_1=B/\overline{BJ}
\]
with a closed subalgebra of \(A/\overline{AJ}\).

By Proposition 5.1, \(B_1\) is the affine algebra of a homogeneous
space of \(H'\) and has height at most \(1\). By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.2.2}{5.2.2}, the
homomorphism \(\varphi\) of 5.2.1 therefore induces an isomorphism of
\(B'_1\) onto \(B_1\). If we denote by \(I'\) the maximal ideal of
\(B'\), by \(B'^p\) the subalgebra \(\{\,x^p\mid x\in B'\,\}\) of
\(B'\), and by \(J'\) the maximal ideal of \(B'^p\), this means that the
homomorphism \(\varphi:B'\to B\) of 5.2.1 induces an isomorphism of
\(B'/\overline{B'J'}\) onto \(B/\overline{BJ}\). Now, since \(A\) is
topologically free over \(B\) and over \(B^p\) \textup{(5.1 and 1.4)},
\(B\) is topologically free over \(B^p\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.3}{1.3.3})}; by
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.4}{0.3.4}, \(\varphi\) is therefore an isomorphism if the homomorphism
\(\varphi^p:B'^p\to B^p\), which is induced by \(\varphi\), is an
isomorphism. This is proved in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.2.4}{5.2.4} below.\footnote{Translator's
note: Proposition 5.1 applies to \(B^p\) because condition \textup{(i)} holds for it: if \(b\in B\), then
\(\Delta_Ab-1\widehat\otimes b\in\overline{AI}\widehat\otimes_kA\), and, \(A\widehat\otimes_kA\) being
commutative of characteristic \(p\),
\(\Delta_A(b^p)-1\widehat\otimes b^p=(\Delta_Ab-1\widehat\otimes b)^p\in\overline{AJ}\widehat\otimes_kA\),
since the \(p\)-th power of an element of \(\overline{AI}\) lies in \(\overline{AJ}\). Likewise for \(B_1\) in
\(A/\overline{AJ}\). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, argues instead without
the implication \textup{(i)} \(\Rightarrow\) \textup{(ii)} of 5.1: \(H'\) is the inverse image of \(H^{(p)}\)
under \(\operatorname{Fr}:G\to G^{(p)}\), and, the formation of the quotient commuting with the base change
\(\pi\), \(B^p\) is the affine algebra of the quotient by \(H'\) and \(B_1\) that of the homogeneous space
of \(H'\) defined by \(H\).}
