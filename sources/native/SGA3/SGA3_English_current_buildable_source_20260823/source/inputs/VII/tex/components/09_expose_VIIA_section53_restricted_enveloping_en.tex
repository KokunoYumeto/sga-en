
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{5.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:5.3}

We are now interested in the left adjoint of the functor
\(A\mapsto A_{\mathrm{Lie}}\) of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.2}{5.2}: let \(\mathfrak g\) be a restricted
\(p\)-Lie algebra over the ring \(R\) of characteristic \(p\), let
\(U(\mathfrak g)\) be the enveloping algebra of the Lie algebra
underlying \(\mathfrak g\) (cf.\ Bourbaki, \emph{Alg\`ebres de Lie},
\S2), and let \(i_{\mathfrak g}\), or \(i:\mathfrak g\to U(\mathfrak g)\),
be the canonical map. It is known that every homomorphism of Lie
algebras \(\varphi\) from \(\mathfrak g\) to the Lie algebra underlying a
unital \(R\)-algebra \(A\) extends in one and only one way to a
homomorphism of unital \(R\)-algebras \(\psi\) from
\(U(\mathfrak g)\) to \(A\) (\(\psi i=\varphi\)). Moreover,
\(\varphi\) is a homomorphism of restricted \(p\)-Lie algebras if and
only if \(\psi\) vanishes on the elements
\(i(x)^p-i\bigl(x^{(p)}\bigr)\) as \(x\) ranges over \(\mathfrak g\).
Consequently, if \(U_p^R(\mathfrak g)\) or \(U_p(\mathfrak g)\) denotes
the quotient of \(U(\mathfrak g)\) by the two-sided ideal generated by the
elements \(i(x)^p-i\bigl(x^{(p)}\bigr)\), and if \(j_{\mathfrak g}\), or
\(j:\mathfrak g\to U_p(\mathfrak g)\), is the composite of
\(i:\mathfrak g\to U(\mathfrak g)\) and of the canonical map from
\(U(\mathfrak g)\) to \(U_p(\mathfrak g)\), one sees that every
homomorphism of restricted \(p\)-Lie algebras \(\varphi\) from
\(\mathfrak g\) to \(A_{\mathrm{Lie}}\) extends in one and only one way
to a homomorphism of unital algebras
\(\psi:U_p(\mathfrak g)\to A\) (\(\psi j=\varphi\)).

We say that \(U_p(\mathfrak g)\) is the \emph{restricted enveloping
algebra} of \(\mathfrak g\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{5.3.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:5.3.1}

With the notation of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.3}{5.3}, put
\[
  \beta(x)=i(x)^p-i\bigl(x^{(p)}\bigr).
\]
For every element \(y\) of \(\mathfrak g\), one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \beta(x)i(y)
  &=
  i(y)\beta(x)+[\beta(x),i(y)]\\
  &=
  i(y)\beta(x)
  +(\operatorname{ad}i(x))^p i(y)
  -i\bigl((\operatorname{ad}x)^p y\bigr)\\
  &=
  i(y)\beta(x).
\end{aligned}
\]
so that \(\beta(x)\) belongs to the center of \(U(\mathfrak g)\);
in particular, the left ideal generated by the elements
\(\beta(x)\) is already two-sided. Moreover,
\[
  \beta(\lambda x)=\lambda^p\beta(x)
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \beta(x+y)=\beta(x)+\beta(y)
  \qquad(\lambda\in R,\ x,y\in\mathfrak g);
\]
in particular, if \((x_\alpha)\) is a family of generators of the
\(R\)-module \(\mathfrak g\), the left ideal generated by the elements
\(\beta(x)\) is already generated by the \(\beta(x_\alpha)\).\footnote{Translator's
note: as the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, indicates, the centrality of \(\beta(x)\)
comes from 5.1~(i) and 5.2~(i), and the additivity from 5.1~(ii) and
5.2~(ii), the bracket terms of the two formulas cancelling. The 1970 text
writes the middle term of the computation above with the opposite sign,
\(i((\operatorname{ad}x)^py)-(\operatorname{ad}i(x))^p(i(y))\); the
two terms are equal, so either expression vanishes.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{5.3.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:5.3.2}
When \(\mathfrak g\) is an \(R\)-Lie algebra whose underlying
\(R\)-module is free with basis \((x_\alpha)\), one can define a
restricted \(p\)-Lie algebra structure on \(\mathfrak g\) as follows:
identify \(\mathfrak g\) with a submodule of \(U(\mathfrak g)\) by means
of \(i\) (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg\`ebres de Lie}, I, \S2.7), and let \(\pi\)
be the map \(r\mapsto r^p\) from \(R\) to \(R\). If \((y_\alpha)\) is an
arbitrary family of elements of \(\mathfrak g\) such that
\(\operatorname{ad}y_\alpha=(\operatorname{ad}x_\alpha)^p\), there then
exists an \(R\)-linear map \(\gamma\) from \(\mathfrak g\otimes_\pi R\)
to \(U(\mathfrak g)\) which sends \(x_\alpha\otimes_\pi1\) to
\(x_\alpha^p-y_\alpha\); moreover, since
\[
  (\operatorname{ad}x_\alpha^p)(x)
  =
  (\operatorname{ad}x_\alpha)^p(x)
  =
  (\operatorname{ad}y_\alpha)(x)
\]
for every \(x\in\mathfrak g\), \(\gamma\) maps
\(\mathfrak g\otimes_\pi R\) into the center of \(U(\mathfrak g)\).
Setting then
\[
  x^{(p)}
  =
  x^p-\gamma(x\otimes_\pi1)
\]
for every \(x\in\mathfrak g\), one checks without difficulty that
\(x^{(p)}\) belongs to \(\mathfrak g\) and that the map
\(x\mapsto x^{(p)}\) makes \(\mathfrak g\) a restricted \(p\)-Lie
algebra. In other words, if \((x_\alpha)\) is a basis of the module
underlying a Lie algebra \(\mathfrak g\) over \(R\), the restricted
\(p\)-Lie algebra structures on \(\mathfrak g\) correspond bijectively to
the families \((y_\alpha)\) of elements of \(\mathfrak g\) such that
\(\operatorname{ad}y_\alpha=(\operatorname{ad}x_\alpha)^p\).

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, 5.3.2; our formulation). The verification.
The map \(i\) is injective because \(\mathfrak g\) is free
(Poincar\'e--Birkhoff--Witt), and the image of \(\gamma\) is central
because \(U(\mathfrak g)\) is generated by \(\mathfrak g\). As
\(\lambda x\otimes_\pi1=x\otimes_\pi\lambda^p\), for
\(x=\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha x_\alpha\) one has
\(\gamma(x\otimes_\pi1)=\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha^p(x_\alpha^p-y_\alpha)\).
On the other hand, by 5.1~(ii), \((a+b)^p-a^p-b^p\) is a sum of iterated
brackets of \(a\) and \(b\), hence lies in \(\mathfrak g\) when
\(a,b\in\mathfrak g\); by induction on the number of nonzero
\(\lambda_\alpha\), \(z=x^p-\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha^px_\alpha^p\) lies in
\(\mathfrak g\), and so does
\(x^{(p)}=\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha^py_\alpha+z\); also
\(x_\alpha^{(p)}=y_\alpha\). Condition 5.2~(0) is clear. Since
\(\gamma(x\otimes_\pi1)\) is central,
\(\operatorname{ad}x^{(p)}=\operatorname{ad}(x^p)=(\operatorname{ad}x)^p\)
by 5.1~(i), which is 5.2~(i); and since
\(x\mapsto\gamma(x\otimes_\pi1)\) is additive, 5.2~(ii) follows from
5.1~(ii). Conversely, a restricted \(p\)-Lie algebra structure gives
elements \(y_\alpha=x_\alpha^{(p)}\) with
\(\operatorname{ad}y_\alpha=(\operatorname{ad}x_\alpha)^p\) by 5.2~(i),
and by 5.2~(0) and~(ii) it is determined by them; whence the bijection.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{5.3.3. Proposition.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:5.3.3}
\textit{Let \(\mathfrak g\) be a restricted \(p\)-Lie algebra
over \(R\) whose underlying module is free with basis
\((x_\alpha)\). Then
\[
  j:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow U_p(\mathfrak g)
\]
is injective. If \(z_\alpha=j(x_\alpha)\), then
\(U_p(\mathfrak g)\) has as a basis the monomials
\[
  \prod_\alpha z_\alpha^{n_\alpha},
  \qquad
  0\le n_\alpha<p.
\]
Here the \(n_\alpha\) are zero except for finitely many
\(\alpha\); the basis is assumed to be totally ordered, and the
products are taken in increasing order.}

Indeed, let \(n=(n_\alpha)\) be a family of nonnegative integers,
all but finitely many zero. Identify \(\mathfrak g\) with a submodule of
the enveloping algebra \(U(\mathfrak g)\) by means of the canonical map
\(i\); put
\[
  |n|=\sum_\alpha n_\alpha,
  \qquad
  x^n=\prod_\alpha x_\alpha^{n_\alpha},
\]
and let \(U^r\) be the submodule of \(U(\mathfrak g)\) generated by the
\(x^n\) such that \(|n|\le r\). Finally, put \(s=|n|\),
\(n_\alpha=m_\alpha+p\ell_\alpha\) with \(0\le m_\alpha<p\), and
\[
  T_n
  =
  \prod_\alpha
  x_\alpha^{m_\alpha}
  \beta(x_\alpha)^{\ell_\alpha}
\]
(confer \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.3.1}{5.3.1}). It is clear that
\(T_n-\prod_\alpha x_\alpha^{n_\alpha}\) belongs to
\(U^{s-1}\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, gives the reason: the graded
ring \(\bigoplus_rU^r/U^{r-1}\) is commutative (Bourbaki,
\emph{Groupes et alg\`ebres de Lie}, chapter~I, \S2, no.~6), and
\(\beta(x_\alpha)=x_\alpha^p-x_\alpha^{(p)}\) differs from
\(x_\alpha^p\) by an element of \(U^1\).} By the
Poincar\'e--Birkhoff--Witt theorem, the \(T_n\) such that \(|n|=s\)
therefore form a basis of \(U^s\) modulo \(U^{s-1}\). As \(s=|n|\)
varies, the \(T_n\) form a basis of \(U(\mathfrak g)\). Now the kernel
\(J\) of the canonical map from \(U(\mathfrak g)\) to
\(U_p(\mathfrak g)\) is the left ideal of \(U(\mathfrak g)\) generated
by the central elements \(\beta(x_\alpha)\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.3.1}{5.3.1}); consequently, the
\(T_n\) such that \(\ell=(\ell_\alpha)\ne0\) form a basis of \(J\), and
the \(T_n\) such that \(n_\alpha<p\) for every \(\alpha\) form a basis of
\(U(\mathfrak g)\) modulo \(J\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{5.3.3 bis.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:5.3.3bis}

Let \(\mathfrak g\) be a restricted \(p\)-Lie algebra over \(R\),
and let \(f:R\to R'\) be an extension of the base ring. We claim
that the \(R'\)-module
\[
  R'\otimes_R\mathfrak g
\]
admits one and only one restricted \(p\)-Lie algebra structure
such that
\[
  [\lambda\otimes x,\mu\otimes y]
  =
  \lambda\mu\otimes[x,y],
  \qquad
  (\lambda\otimes x)^{(p)}
  =
  \lambda^p\otimes x^{(p)}.
\]
In particular, it will follow that the functor
\[
  \mathfrak g\longmapsto R'\otimes_R\mathfrak g
\]
is left adjoint to restriction of scalars from \(R'\) to \(R\).

The uniqueness of the restricted \(p\)-Lie algebra structure of
\(R'\otimes_R\mathfrak g\) being clear, we prove its existence: when
\(\mathfrak g\) is free with basis \((x_\alpha)\), \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.3.2}{5.3.2}\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text refers to 5.3.1; the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, corrects
the reference to 5.3.2, where the restricted structures on a free Lie
algebra are described.} gives a unique restricted
\(p\)-Lie algebra structure on the Lie algebra
\(R'\otimes_R\mathfrak g\) such that
\[
  (1\otimes x_\alpha)^{(p)}
  =
  1\otimes x_\alpha^{(p)}.
\]
This is the structure sought.

Suppose now that \(\mathfrak g\) is arbitrary. There is a
restricted \(p\)-Lie algebra \(L_0\), free as an \(R\)-module,
together with a surjective homomorphism
\[
  q_0:L_0\longrightarrow\mathfrak g.
\]
For example, we may take
\[
  L_0=R\otimes_{\mathbb F_p}\mathfrak g
\]
and let \(q_0\) be the homomorphism
\[
  \lambda\otimes x\longmapsto\lambda x;
\]
here \(\mathfrak g\) is free over the prime field
\(\mathbb F_p\). The kernel of \(q_0\) is a \(p\)-ideal of
\(L_0\), that is, an ideal of the Lie algebra \(L_0\) stable
under \(x\mapsto x^{(p)}\). Hence there is also a restricted
\(p\)-Lie algebra \(L_1\), free as an \(R\)-module, and a
homomorphism \(q_1:L_1\to L_0\) whose image is
\(\operatorname{Ker}q_0\). This gives the exact sequence

and therefore an exact sequence of \(R'\)-Lie algebras

Since \(R'\otimes_Rq_1\) is manifestly a homomorphism of
restricted \(p\)-Lie algebras, the kernel of
\(R'\otimes_Rq_0\) is a \(p\)-ideal. Thus the symbolic \(p\)-th
power operation on \(R'\otimes_RL_0\) induces, by passage to the
quotient, a map from \(R'\otimes_R\mathfrak g\) to itself (use
formula (ii) of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.2}{5.2}). This map equips \(R'\otimes_R\mathfrak g\)
with the required restricted \(p\)-Lie algebra structure.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{5.3.4.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:5.3.4}

The canonical map
\[
  j_{\mathfrak g}:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow U_p(\mathfrak g)
\]
induces, for every extension \(R\to R'\) of the base ring, a
homomorphism
\[
  R'\otimes_Rj_{\mathfrak g}:
  R'\otimes_R\mathfrak g
  \longrightarrow
  R'\otimes_RU_p(\mathfrak g).
\]
It therefore induces a homomorphism
\[
  h:
  U_p(R'\otimes_R\mathfrak g)
  \longrightarrow
  R'\otimes_RU_p(\mathfrak g)
\]
such that
\[
  h\circ j_{R'\otimes_R\mathfrak g}
  =
  R'\otimes_Rj_{\mathfrak g}.
\]
The universal properties of \(R'\otimes_R\mathfrak g\) and of
the restricted enveloping algebra show immediately that \(h\) is
an isomorphism. We may therefore identify
\[
  U_p(R'\otimes_R\mathfrak g)
  \simeq
  R'\otimes_RU_p(\mathfrak g).
\]

In particular, let \(r\in R\), and let \(R'=R_r\) be the
localized ring. Then
\[
  \mathfrak g_r=R_r\otimes_R\mathfrak g
\]
has a canonical restricted \(p\)-Lie algebra structure over
\(R_r\). Consequently, the sheaf \(\widetilde{\mathfrak g}\) on
\(\operatorname{Spec}R\) is a quasi-coherent restricted
\(p\)-Lie algebra on \(\operatorname{Spec}R\). Moreover,
\[
  U_p^{R_r}(\mathfrak g_r)
  \simeq
  U_p^R(\mathfrak g)_r,
\]
so the sheaf associated with the presheaf
\[
  V\longmapsto
  U_p\bigl(\Gamma(V,\widetilde{\mathfrak g})\bigr)
\]
is quasi-coherent. More generally, let \(S\) be a scheme of
characteristic \(p\), and let \(\mathcal G\) be a quasi-coherent
restricted \(p\)-Lie algebra over \(\mathcal O_S\). The sheaf associated
with the presheaf
\[
  V\longmapsto U_p\bigl(\Gamma(V,\mathcal G)\bigr)
\]
is quasi-coherent. It is denoted
\(\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G)\) and is called the
\emph{restricted enveloping algebra} of \(\mathcal G\). If \(V\)
is affine, then
\[
  U_p\bigl(\Gamma(V,\mathcal G)\bigr)
  \simeq
  \Gamma\bigl(V,\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G)\bigr).
\]
