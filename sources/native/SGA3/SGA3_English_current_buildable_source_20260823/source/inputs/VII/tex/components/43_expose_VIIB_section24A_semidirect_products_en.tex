\label{sga3:VIIB:2.4.A}%
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation). The re-edition adds here two
paragraphs on semidirect products of formal groups, its 2.4.A and 2.4.B,
which it uses in its 5.1.3 and 2.9. Let \(G\) and \(Q\) be topologically
flat formal \(k\)-groups, with affine algebras \(A\) and \(A_Q\), and let
\(\sigma:Q\to G\) and \(\pi:G\to Q\) be morphisms of formal groups with
\(\pi\circ\sigma=\operatorname{id}_Q\); let \(N=\operatorname{Ker}(\pi)\),
with inclusion \(j:N\to G\) and affine algebra \(A_N\). On points with
values in any formal \(k\)-variety, \(N\) is a normal subgroup of \(G\),
and every point \(g\) of \(G\) can be written in one and only one way as
\(g=y\,\sigma(q)\), with \(y\) a point of \(N\) and \(q\) a point of
\(Q\), namely \(q=\pi(g)\) and \(y=r(g)=g\,\sigma(\pi(g))^{-1}\)
\textup{(Exposé~I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:proposition:2-3-8}{2.3.8})}. Thus
\((y,q)\mapsto y\,\sigma(q)\) is an isomorphism of formal varieties
\(N\times Q\to G\), with inverse \(g\mapsto(r(g),\pi(g))\), and the
morphism of formal varieties \(r:G\to N\) is a retraction of \(j\).

\textup{(a)} \(N\) is topologically flat over \(k\): through
\(r^\natural\) and \(j^\natural\), \(A_N\) is a direct factor of the
projective pseudocompact \(k\)-module \(A\).

\textup{(b)} The map \(A_N\widehat\otimes_kA_Q\to A\),
\(a\widehat\otimes b\mapsto r^\natural(a)\,\pi^\natural(b)\), is an
isomorphism of profinite \(k\)-algebras, with inverse
\((j^\natural\widehat\otimes\sigma^\natural)\circ\Delta_A\). The
projection \(r^\natural\circ j^\natural\) of \(A\) onto the subalgebra
\(r^\natural(A_N)\) is
\(m_A\circ(\operatorname{id}\widehat\otimes\pi^\natural\sigma^\natural c_A)\circ\Delta_A\),
since \(r(g)=g\,\sigma(\pi(g^{-1}))\).

\textup{(c)} Let \(G/Q\) be the quotient of \(G\) by \(Q\) acting by right
translations through \(\sigma\) \textup{(2.4)}. Under the isomorphism of
\textup{(b)}, the two morphisms of 2.4 from \(A\) to
\(A\widehat\otimes_kA_Q\) become
\(a\widehat\otimes b\mapsto a\widehat\otimes b\widehat\otimes1\) and
\(\operatorname{id}\widehat\otimes\Delta_{A_Q}\); as an element \(b\) of
\(A_Q\) with \(\Delta_{A_Q}(b)=b\widehat\otimes1\) is the scalar
\(\varepsilon(b)\), and \(A_N\) is projective, they agree exactly on
\(A_N\widehat\otimes1\). So \(r^\natural\) identifies \(A_N\) with the
affine algebra of \(G/Q\), and \(p\circ j:N\to G/Q\) is an isomorphism.

\textup{(d)} \(\Delta_A\) maps \(r^\natural(A_N)\) into
\(A\widehat\otimes_kr^\natural(A_N)\), and, on \(r^\natural(A_N)\),
\((r^\natural j^\natural\widehat\otimes\operatorname{id})\circ\Delta_A\)
is the comultiplication of \(A_N\) transported by \(r^\natural\); in the
same way \(r^\natural j^\natural\circ c_A\) induces on \(r^\natural(A_N)\)
the antipode of \(A_N\). This follows from the identities
\(r(gh)=r(g)\,\sigma(\pi(g))\,r(h)\,\sigma(\pi(g))^{-1}\) and
\(r(yh)=y\,r(h)\), for points \(g\), \(h\) of \(G\) and \(y\) of \(N\).
