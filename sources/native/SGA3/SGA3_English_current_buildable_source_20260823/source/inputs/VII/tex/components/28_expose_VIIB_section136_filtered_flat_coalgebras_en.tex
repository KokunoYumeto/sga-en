
\subsubsection*{1.3.6}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.3.6}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsubsection}{\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.6}{1.3.6}. Filtered flat coalgebras}

In the remainder of this Expos\'e, we shall several times define a
topologically flat formal variety \(X\) by exhibiting the coalgebra
\(\mathbf H(X)\). We shall then have to interpret, by means of
\(\mathbf H(X)\), certain geometric properties of \(X\). We give here an
example of this situation: suppose for simplicity that \(k\) is Artinian,
and put \(H=\mathbf H(X)(k)\); suppose moreover that a section \(\sigma\)
of the canonical projection of \(X\) onto \(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\) is
given, and ask under what condition \(\sigma\) induces an isomorphism of
the underlying topological spaces. For this, denote by \(H^+\) the kernel
of the augmentation \(\varepsilon_H\) (VII\textsubscript{A}, 3.1), and by
\(\eta_H\) the section of \(\varepsilon_H\) induced by \(\sigma\). The map
\((x,y)\mapsto\eta_H(x)+y\) then allows us to identify \(k\oplus H^+\)
with \(H\), and to define submodules \(H_n\) of \(H\) by induction:
\[
  H_0=\eta_H(k),
  \qquad\ldots,\qquad
  H_{n+1}=\{u\in H\mid\Delta_Hu-u\otimes1\in H_n\otimes H^+\}.
\]

We shall say that the sequence \(H_0\subset H_1\subset\cdots\) is the
\emph{natural filtration} of \(H\). Under the hypotheses above, one then
sees easily that \emph{the section \(\sigma\) of the canonical projection
of \(X\) onto \(k\) induces an isomorphism of the underlying topological
spaces if and only if \(H\) is the union of the \(H_n\)}: indeed, let
\(A\) be the affine algebra of \(X\), \(\varepsilon_A:A\to k\) the
homomorphism induced by \(\sigma\), and \(I\) the kernel of
\(\varepsilon_A\), that is, the orthogonal of \(H_0\) in \(A\). Denote
moreover by \(A'\) (respectively \(A''\)) the ring of sections of the
structure sheaf of \(X\) over the image of \(\sigma\) (respectively over
the complement of this image in \(X\)). The algebra \(A\) is then
canonically isomorphic to \(A'\oplus A''\), and \(I\) identifies with a
part of \(A'\oplus A''\) of the form \(I'\oplus A''\). Since \(A''\) is a
projective pseudocompact \(k\)-module and the ideals \(I'^r\) tend to
\(0\), it is clear that \(A''\) is zero if and only if every continuous
linear map from \(A\) to \(k\) vanishes on an ideal \(I^{n+1}\), hence if
and only if \(H\) is the union of the \(k\)-modules
\((A/\overline{I^{n+1}})^\dagger\). Let us show that
\(H_n=(A/\overline{I^{n+1}})^\dagger\).

This is clear for \(n=0\). Suppose it proved for \(n<r\): to the exact
sequences
\[
  0\longrightarrow\overline{I^r}\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow
  A/\overline{I^r}\longrightarrow0
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \overline{I^r}\widehat{\otimes}I\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow
  A/\overline{I^{r+1}}\longrightarrow0
\]
there correspond by duality the exact sequences
\[
  (\overline{I^r})^\dagger\longleftarrow H\longleftarrow H_{r-1}
  \longleftarrow0
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  (\overline{I^r})^\dagger\otimes H^+\xleftarrow{\ \delta\ }H\longleftarrow
  (A/\overline{I^{r+1}})^\dagger\longleftarrow0,
\]
where \(\delta\) is obtained by composing \(\Delta_H\) with the canonical
projection of \(H\otimes H\) onto \((\overline{I^r})^\dagger\otimes H^+\).
The first sequence shows that \(H/H_{r-1}\) injects into
\((\overline{I^r})^\dagger\), hence that \((H/H_{r-1})\otimes H^+\) injects
into \((\overline{I^r})^\dagger\otimes H^+\) (\(H^+\) is flat). On the
other hand, \(\Delta_Hu-u\otimes1\) belongs to \(H\otimes H^+\) for every
\(u\in H\), hence is the projection of \(u\) onto \(H\otimes H^+\). The
second exact sequence then shows that \(u\) belongs to
\((A/\overline{I^{r+1}})^\dagger\) if and only if \(\Delta_Hu-u\otimes1\)
belongs to the kernel of the canonical map from \(H\otimes H^+\) onto
\((H/H_{r-1})\otimes H^+\), that is, to \(H_{r-1}\otimes H^+\).

\phantomsection\label{sga3:VIIB:1.3.6.A}%
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
Keep the notation, \(k\) Artinian, and write \(\varphi=\eta_H(1)\) and, for \(x\in H^+\),
\(\overline\Delta(x)=\Delta_H(x)-x\otimes\varphi-\varphi\otimes x\in H^+\otimes H^+\). Then
\(H_n=k\varphi\oplus H_n^+\), with \(H_n^+=H_n\cap H^+\), and, for \(n\ge1\), \(x\in H^+\) lies in
\(H_n\) if and only if \(\overline\Delta(x)\in H_{n-1}^+\otimes H^+\).

(a) \emph{Another proof of the criterion.} If \(\sigma\) is bijective, \(I\) lies in all the closed maximal
ideals of \(A\), so the \(\overline{I^n}\) tend to \(0\) (0.1.2), and every \(x\in H\), being a continuous linear
form on \(A\), vanishes on some \(\overline{I^n}\). Conversely, if every \(x\in H\) vanishes on some
\(\overline{I^n}\), the \(\overline{I^n}\) tend to \(0\); an open prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\) of \(A\) then
contains some \(I^n\), hence a product of powers of the inverse images in \(A\) of the maximal ideals of
\(k\) (whose product is nilpotent), hence one of these, which is maximal. This argument does not use the
commutativity of \(A\): if the diagonal of the flat \(k\)-module \(H\) is coassociative and counital but
not cocommutative (coalgebras being cocommutative by VII\textsubscript{A}, 3.1), the open two-sided ideals of
finite colength of the dual algebra \(A=H^*\) form a basis of neighborhoods of \(0\), and \(H\) is
the union of the \((A/\overline{I^n})^\dagger\) if and only if the open prime ideals of \(A\) are the inverse
images of the maximal ideals of \(k\).

(b) \emph{Description by iterated diagonals.} Let \(\overline\Delta^{(0)}=\mathrm{id}_{H^+}\) and
\(\overline\Delta^{(n)}=(\overline\Delta^{(n-1)}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\overline\Delta\colon H^+\to
(H^+)^{\otimes(n+1)}\). Since \(H^+\) is flat, induction on \(n\) gives
\(H_n^+=\operatorname{Ker}\overline\Delta^{(n)}\).

(c) \emph{If moreover every \(H/H_n\) is flat over \(k\), then
\(\Delta_H(H_n)\subset\sum_{i+j=n}H_i\otimes H_j\); in particular every \(H_n\) is a subcoalgebra.} By
coassociativity, \(\overline\Delta^{(n)}=(\overline\Delta^{(i)}\otimes\overline\Delta^{(n-1-i)})
\circ\overline\Delta\) for \(0\le i\le n-1\); the flatness of \(H^+/H_i^+\) and of the
\((H^+)^{\otimes m}\) gives \(\operatorname{Ker}(\overline\Delta^{(i)}\otimes\overline\Delta^{(n-1-i)})
=H_i^+\otimes H^++H^+\otimes H_{n-1-i}^+\), so \(\overline\Delta(H_n^+)\) lies in the intersection of
these modules, which is \(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}H_i^+\otimes H_{n-i}^+\) by the following fact. \emph{If
\(0=E_0\subset E_1\subset\cdots\subset E_m\subset M\) are modules with every \(M/E_i\) flat, then
\(\bigcap_{i=0}^m(E_i\otimes M+M\otimes E_{m-i})=\sum_{i=1}^mE_i\otimes E_{m-i+1}\).} The right-hand
side lies in the left-hand side; conversely, if \(z\) lies in the left-hand side and in
\(E_i\otimes M\), its image in \((E_i/E_{i-1})\otimes M\) dies in
\((M/E_{i-1})\otimes(M/E_{m-i+1})\), hence lies in \((E_i/E_{i-1})\otimes E_{m-i+1}\), \(E_i/E_{i-1}\)
and \(M/E_{m-i+1}\) being flat; so \(z\) differs by an element of \(E_i\otimes E_{m-i+1}\) from an element
of the left-hand side lying in \(E_{i-1}\otimes M\), and one concludes by descending induction on \(i\).
Without the flatness of the \(H/H_n\), \(H_n\) need not be a subcoalgebra: for
\(k=k_0[t]/(t^m)\), with \(k_0\) a field and \(m\ge4\), and \(H=k\varphi\oplus kx\), with \(\varepsilon(x)=0\) and
\(\Delta(x)=x\otimes\varphi+\varphi\otimes x+t\,x\otimes x\), one finds
\(H_i=k\varphi\oplus kt^{m-i}x\), and \(\Delta(t^{m-i}x)\) has the component \(t^{m-i+1}x\otimes x\),
which does not lie in the image of \(H_i\otimes H_i\) for \(2\le i\le m-2\).

(d) \emph{An arbitrary pseudocompact ring \(k\).} Let \(\mathbf H=\mathbf H(X)\) be the coalgebra of a
topologically flat formal \(k\)-variety \(X\), and let the section \(\sigma\) be given by a morphism of
\(\mathbf O_k\)-coalgebras \(\mathbf O_k\to\mathbf H\); for \(C\) in \(\operatorname{Alf}/k\), let
\((\mathbf H_n(C))\) be the filtration of \(\mathbf H(C)\) defined as above by the image of \(1\in C\).
Then \(\sigma\) induces an isomorphism
of the underlying topological spaces if and only if \(\mathbf H(C)=\bigcup_n\mathbf H_n(C)\) for every
\(C\). Indeed, every point of \(X\) lies over a point of \(\operatorname{Spf}k\) whose ideal contains an
open ideal \(\mathfrak l\), and the points of \(X\widehat\otimes_kk/\mathfrak l\) are the points of \(X\)
over those of \(\operatorname{Spf}(k/\mathfrak l)\); so \(\sigma\) is bijective if and only if each
\(\sigma\widehat\otimes k/\mathfrak l\) is, and the case of \(k/\mathfrak l\) is the Artinian case, while
for \(C\) annihilated by \(\mathfrak l\) the filtration of \(\mathbf H(C)=C\otimes\mathbf H(k/\mathfrak l)\)
contains the image of that of \(\mathbf H(k/\mathfrak l)\).
