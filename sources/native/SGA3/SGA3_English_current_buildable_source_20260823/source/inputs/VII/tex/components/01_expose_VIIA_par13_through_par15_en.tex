
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:1.3}
Suppose now that \(Y\) is equal to \(S\); then \(u:S\to X\) is a
section of \(p:X\to S\), that is, an immersion, and the
\(S\)-deviations of \(u\) admit the following interpretation. Let
\(\mathcal I_u\) be the kernel of the homomorphism from
\(u^{-1}(\mathcal O_X)\) to \(\mathcal O_S\) which defines \(u\), let
\(d\) be a morphism of \(p^{-1}(\mathcal O_S)\)-modules from
\(\mathcal O_X\) to \(u_*(\mathcal O_S)\), and let
\(d':u^{-1}(\mathcal O_X)\to\mathcal O_S\) be the morphism canonically
associated with \(d\). Then \(d\) is an \(S\)-deviation of \(u\) of
order \(\leq n\) if and only if \(d'\) vanishes on
\(\mathcal I_u^{\,n+1}\).

This interpretation generalizes as follows. Let \(u:Y\to X\) be any
\(S\)-morphism, and let \(\Gamma_u\) be the graph of \(u\), that is,
the morphism \(Y\to Y\times X\) with components \(\operatorname{id}_Y\)
and \(u\). Every \(S\)-deviation \(d\) of \(u\) of order \(\leq n\)
gives, by composition,
\[
  Y\xrightarrow{\mathrm{diag}}Y\times Y
   \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{d_Y}_{u_Y}Y\times X,
\]
a \(Y\)-deviation of \(\Gamma_u\) of order \(\leq n\), denoted
\(\Gamma_d\) (the graph of \(d\)). We thus obtain a bijection
\(d\mapsto\Gamma_d\) from the set of \(S\)-deviations of \(u\) of order
\(\leq n\) onto the set of \(Y\)-deviations of \(\Gamma_u\) of order
\(\leq n\). The inverse bijection associates with a \(Y\)-deviation
\(Y\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{e}_{\Gamma_u}Y\times X\) the composite
\(S\)-deviation
\[
 Y\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{e}_{\Gamma_u}Y\times X
  \xrightarrow{\operatorname{pr}_2}X.
\]

Let \(\mathcal J\) be the kernel of the homomorphism of algebras
\[
  (\Gamma_u)^{-1}(\mathcal O_{Y\times X})\longrightarrow\mathcal O_Y
\]
which defines \(\Gamma_u\). Taking into account what precedes, one
then sees that the \(S\)-deviations of \(u\) of order \(\leq n\)
correspond canonically to the morphisms of \(\mathcal O_Y\)-modules from
\((\Gamma_u)^{-1}(\mathcal O_{Y\times X})\) to \(\mathcal O_Y\) which
vanish on \(\mathcal J^{\,n+1}\).

\par\medskip\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIA:1.3.1}%
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, 1.3 and 1.3.1; our formulation).
(a) For local sections \(f_0,\ldots,f_n,g\) of \(\mathcal O_X\), write
\(\bar f\) for the image of \(f\) in \(u_*(\mathcal O_Y)\) and
\(f_I=\prod_{i\in I}f_i\). Induction on \(n\) gives
\[
  (\operatorname{ad}f_0)\cdots(\operatorname{ad}f_n)(d)(g)
  =\sum_{I\subset\{0,\ldots,n\}}(-1)^{|I|}\,
   \bar f_{\{0,\ldots,n\}\smallsetminus I}\;d(f_Ig).
\]
If the \(f_i\) lie in the kernel \(\mathcal J_u\) of
\(\mathcal O_X\to u_*(\mathcal O_Y)\), only the term
\(I=\{0,\ldots,n\}\) survives; hence an \(S\)-deviation of \(u\) of
order \(\leq n\) vanishes on \(\mathcal J_u^{\,n+1}\).
(b) Proof of the first statement of 1.3. Through \(p\),
\(u^{-1}(\mathcal O_X)\) is an \(\mathcal O_S\)-algebra, and since
\(pu=\mathrm{id}_S\) the homomorphism
\(\varphi:u^{-1}(\mathcal O_X)\to\mathcal O_S\) defining \(u\) is a
retraction of its structure map; thus
\(u^{-1}(\mathcal O_X)=\mathcal O_S\oplus\mathcal I_u\), with
\(f=\varphi(f)+(f-\varphi(f))\). The map \(d'\) and the maps
\((\operatorname{ad}f_1)\cdots(\operatorname{ad}f_k)(d')\) are
\(\mathcal O_S\)-linear, so \(\operatorname{ad}f\) acts on them as
\(\operatorname{ad}(f-\varphi(f))\), and for \(f_i\in\mathcal I_u\) the
formula of (a), written for \(d'\), reduces to
\((-1)^{n+1}d'(f_0\cdots f_ng)\). Hence \(d'\) satisfies the identities
\((\ast)\) for all local sections of \(u^{-1}(\mathcal O_X)\) if and only
if it vanishes on \(\mathcal I_u^{\,n+1}\); by the note on \((\ast)\)
in 1.1, the former condition is equivalent to \((\ast)\) for \(d\).
(c) In the generalization, \(\Gamma_d\) sends \(\lambda\otimes f\) to
\(\lambda\,d(f)\), and the composite \(e_X\) of \(e\) with
\(\operatorname{pr}_2\) sends \(f\) to \(e(1\otimes f)\). Hence
\((\Gamma_d)_X=d\); and \(\Gamma_{e_X}=e\), because a \(Y\)-deviation is
linear over the functions on \(Y\), so that
\(e(\lambda\otimes f)=\lambda\,e(1\otimes f)\), while the sections
\(\lambda\otimes f\) generate \(\mathcal O_{Y\times X}\) locally as an
abelian group. The bijection is \(\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)\)-linear, and
\(d\) is an \(S\)-derivation of \(u\) (1.2.1) if and only if
\(\Gamma_d\) is a \(Y\)-derivation of \(\Gamma_u\).
(d) Derivations. Let \(u\) be a section of \(p\) as in (b). By (b), an
\(S\)-deviation of \(u\) of order \(\leq1\) amounts to an
\(\mathcal O_S\)-linear map on
\(u^{-1}(\mathcal O_X)/\mathcal I_u^2=\mathcal O_S\oplus
\mathcal I_u/\mathcal I_u^2\), that is, to an element \(d'(1)\) of
\(\Gamma(S,\mathcal O_S)\) together with an element of
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal I_u/\mathcal I_u^2,
\mathcal O_S)\). Writing \(a=\varphi(a)+a'\) and \(b=\varphi(b)+b'\),
where \(a'b'\in\mathcal I_u^2\), one finds
\(d'(ab)-\varphi(a)d'(b)-\varphi(b)d'(a)=-\varphi(a)\varphi(b)d'(1)\).
So the \(S\)-derivations of \(u\) are exactly the \(S\)-deviations of
order \(\leq1\) which vanish on \(1\); they correspond to
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal I_u/\mathcal I_u^2,
\mathcal O_S)\), and the \(S\)-deviations of \(u\) of order \(\leq1\)
form the direct sum of \(\Gamma(S,\mathcal O_S)\) and of the
\(S\)-derivations. For an arbitrary \(u\), (c) and this result, applied
over the base \(Y\) to the section \(\Gamma_u\) of
\(\operatorname{pr}_1:Y\times X\to Y\), identify the \(S\)-derivations
of \(u\) with the \(Y\)-derivations of \(\Gamma_u\), and both with
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_Y}(\mathcal J/\mathcal J^2,\mathcal O_Y)\).
For \(u=\mathrm{id}_X\), whose graph is the diagonal (1.4.1), the
\(p^{-1}(\mathcal O_S)\)-derivations of \(\mathcal O_X\) are thereby
identified with the \(X\)-derivations of \(\Delta_{X/S}\) and with
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_X}(\mathcal I_{X/S}/\mathcal I_{X/S}^2,
\mathcal O_X)\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.4.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:1.4}
Let \(X\) be an \(S\)-scheme. An \(S\)-differential operator
(respectively, an \(S\)-differential operator of order \(\leq n\)) on
\(X\) is an \(S\)-deviation (respectively, an \(S\)-deviation of order
\(\leq n\)) of the identity morphism of \(X\). By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.1}{1.1}, an \(S\)-differential operator of order \(\leq n\) is therefore
an endomorphism of the \(p^{-1}(\mathcal O_S)\)-module
\(\mathcal O_X\) satisfying the identities \((\ast)\) of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.1}{1.1}. We
write \(\operatorname{Dif}^n_{X/S}\) for the
\(\Gamma(\mathcal O_S)\)-module
of \(S\)-differential operators of order \(\leq n\), and
\(\operatorname{Dif}_{X/S}\) for the module of all
\(S\)-differential operators.

As observed in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.2}{1.2}, \(S\)-deviations of \(\operatorname{id}_X\) can be
composed. This makes \(\operatorname{Dif}_{X/S}\) a
\(\Gamma(\mathcal O_S)\)-algebra, called the \emph{algebra of
differential operators of \(X/S\)}.

Similarly, we denote by \(\mathcal{Dif}_{X/S}\) the sheaf
\(U\mapsto\operatorname{Dif}_{X\times U/U}\), where \(U\) ranges over the
open subsets of \(S\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, uses instead the sheaf
of \(\mathcal O_X\)-modules \(V\mapsto\operatorname{Dif}_{V/S}\) on \(X\)
(\(V\) open in \(X\)), the sheaf of \(S\)-differential operators on
\(X\). The sheaf above is its direct image under \(p\): for \(U\) open in
\(S\), \(X\times U=p^{-1}(U)\), and a differential operator on
\(p^{-1}(U)\) relative to \(U\) is the same thing as one relative to
\(S\), the two structural sheaves of rings on \(p^{-1}(U)\) coming from
\(\mathcal O_U\) and from \(\mathcal O_S\) being the same.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.4.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:1.4.1}
As in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.3}{1.3}, differential operators on \(X/S\) may be interpreted using
the graph of the identity morphism of \(X\), namely the diagonal
\[
  \Delta=\Delta_{X/S}:X\longrightarrow X\times X.
\]
Let us translate the statements of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.3}{1.3} into the present context.
Endow \(\mathcal O_{X\times X}\) with the
\(\operatorname{pr}_1^{-1}(\mathcal O_X)\)-algebra structure defined
by \(\operatorname{pr}_1\). Then
\(\Delta^{-1}\mathcal O_{X\times X}\) is an algebra over
\[
  \mathcal O_X=\Delta^{-1}\operatorname{pr}_1^{-1}\mathcal O_X.
\]
Let \(\mathcal I_{X/S}\) be the kernel of the homomorphism
\[
 \Delta^a_{X/S}:\Delta^{-1}\mathcal O_{X\times X}
 \longrightarrow
 \mathcal O_X
\]
defining \(\Delta\), and let \(\mathcal P^m_{X/S}\) be the
\(\mathcal O_X\)-algebra
\[
  \mathcal P^m_{X/S}
  =
  \Delta^{-1}\mathcal O_{X\times X}/\mathcal I_{X/S}^{\,m+1}.
\]
If \(V\) is an affine open subset of \(S\) and \(U\) an affine open
subset of \(X\) above \(V\), the set of sections of
\(\mathcal P^m_{X/S}\) over \(U\) is therefore equal to
\[
  (A\otimes_kA)/I^{m+1},
\]
where \(k=\Gamma(V,\mathcal O_S)\), \(A=\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_X)\), and
\(I\) is the ideal generated by the elements \(a\otimes1-1\otimes a\),
\(a\in A\).

This being so, by \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.3}{1.3} there is a canonical isomorphism
\[
 j_X:\operatorname{Dif}^m_{X/S}
 \xrightarrow{\sim}
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_X}
   (\mathcal P^m_{X/S},\mathcal O_X).
\]
It is given explicitly as follows: if
\(d\in\operatorname{Dif}^m_{X/S}\) and a section \(c\) of
\(\mathcal P^m_{X/S}\) over \(U\) has the form
\[
  c=a\otimes b+I^{m+1},
\]
then \(j_X(d)(c)=a\,d(b)\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.4.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:1.4.2}
Let \(d\) be a differential operator and \(u\) a section of \(X\) over
\(S\). The \emph{value of \(d\) at \(u\)} is the composite
\(S\)-deviation
\[
  S\xrightarrow{u}X
  \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{d}_{\operatorname{id}_X}X.
\]
By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.3}{1.3} and \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.4.1}{1.4.1}, if \(d\) is a differential operator of order \(\leq m\), then
\(du\) and \(d\) are canonically associated with morphisms
\[
 d':u^{-1}\mathcal O_X/\mathcal I_u^{\,m+1}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 d'':\mathcal P^m_{X/S}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_X.
\]

The morphism \(d'\) may be recovered from \(d''\) as follows. The square
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:1.4.2-value-cartesian-square}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_004_value_cartesian_square}
\end{center}
is cartesian. Hence \(X\) identifies with
\(S\times_X(X\times X)\), \(u\) with \(S\times_X\Delta\), and
\(u^*(\mathcal P^m_{X/S})\) with
\(u^{-1}\mathcal O_X/\mathcal I_u^{\,m+1}\). Under these
identifications, \(u^*(d'')\) is the morphism
\[
  u^{-1}\mathcal O_X/\mathcal I_u^{\,m+1}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S
\]
which is precisely \(d'\).

\noindent\textbf{1.5.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:1.5}
Set, as usual,
\[
  I_S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_S[T]/(T^2).
\]
Let \(s:S\to I_S\) be the zero section (\SGARefLink{sga3:ii:definition:2-1}{II~2.1}), and let \(\sigma\) be the
canonical deviation of \(s\) that we defined in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.2.1}{1.2.1}: the deviation \(\sigma\) is
therefore the homomorphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules which vanishes on the unit section
of \(\mathcal O_S[T]/(T^2)\) and sends the class \(t\) of \(T\) modulo
\(T^2\) to the unit section of \(\mathcal O_S\).

Let \(X\) be an \(S\)-scheme. Every \(I_S\)-automorphism \(u\) of
\(I_S\times X\) inducing the identity on \(X\) determines, by
composition, a differential operator \(D_u\) on \(X\):
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:1.5-infinitesimal-automorphism-sequence}

By \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:subsection:4-11}{II~4.11}, the map \(u\mapsto D_u\) is an isomorphism from the
Lie algebra of the group functor \(\operatorname{Aut}X\) onto the Lie
algebra of \(p^{-1}(\mathcal O_S)\)-derivations of \(\mathcal O_X\).\footnote{Translator's
note: the derivations are bracketed here as elements of
\(\operatorname{Dif}_{X/S}\), whose product (1.2, 1.4) is ``\(D\)
followed by \(D'\)'', that is, the map \(D'\circ D\). With the bracket of
Expos\'e~II (II~4.7.3), if \(u\) and \(v\) act on
\(\mathcal O_X[T]/(T^2)\) by \(\mathrm{id}+tD_u\) and
\(\mathrm{id}+t'D_v\) (\(t^2=t'^2=0\)), the commutator \(uvu^{-1}v^{-1}\)
acts by \(\mathrm{id}+tt'(D_v\circ D_u-D_u\circ D_v)\), since the passage
from automorphisms of \(I_S\times X\) to those of the structure sheaf
reverses composition. So \(u\mapsto D_u\) respects the bracket of
\(\operatorname{Dif}_{X/S}\); for the commutator \(D\circ D'-D'\circ D\)
of derivations taken as maps, the isomorphism is \(u\mapsto-D_u\)
(compare II~4.11, where \(x\mapsto-d_x\) appears for translations).} The
inverse isomorphism sends a derivation \(D\) to the automorphism of
\(I_S\times X\) corresponding to the automorphism
\[
  a+bt\longmapsto a+(Da+b)t
\]
of \(\mathcal O_X[T]/(T^2)\).
