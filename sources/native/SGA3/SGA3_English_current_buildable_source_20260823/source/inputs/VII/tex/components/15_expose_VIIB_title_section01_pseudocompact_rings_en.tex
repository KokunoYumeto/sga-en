\section*{B) Formal Groups}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:B}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{B) Formal Groups}

\noindent
The study of formal groups is usually extremely simple. If this does not
appear clearly in the pages that follow, the responsibility lies with an
arithmetician who claims to know formal groups over ``something other than
fields.''
We have therefore developed, for formal groups ``locally free over
projective limits of Artinian rings,'' the generalities ordinarily stated
for formal groups defined over a field. For a more detailed study of the
latter, we refer to the 1964/65 Heidelberg--Strasbourg algebraic geometry
seminar.

\section*{0. Review of Pseudocompact Rings and Modules}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{0. Review of Pseudocompact Rings and Modules}

This section contains a few technical preliminaries. We recall and
complete here several results from \textup{[CA]} (\emph{Des cat\'egories
ab\'eliennes}, Bull.\ Soc.\ Math.\ France \textbf{90} (1962)).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.1}
A \emph{left pseudocompact ring} is a separated, complete topological
ring with identity which has a basis of neighborhoods of \(0\) consisting
of left ideals \(\mathfrak l\) of finite colength, that is,
\[
  \operatorname{length}_A(A/\mathfrak l)<+\infty.
\]
We shall assume here that \(A\) is commutative, so that no distinction
need be made ``between left and right.''

In particular, the quotients \(A/\mathfrak l\) are endowed with
structures of Artinian rings, and
\(A\) identifies with the topological projective limit of these rings,
each endowed with the discrete topology.

A complete Noetherian local ring is plainly pseudocompact.\footnote{Translator's note: for its
\(\mathfrak m\)-adic topology, \(\mathfrak m\) being the maximal ideal: the ideals \(\mathfrak m^n\) form a
basis of neighborhoods of \(0\), and each \(A/\mathfrak m^n\) is a Noetherian ring of dimension \(0\), hence
of finite length. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, names the topology.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.1.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.1.1}
A closed ideal of \(A\) is the intersection of the open ideals that
contain it.\footnote{Translator's note: if \(I\) is a closed ideal and \(x\notin I\), there is an open ideal
\(\mathfrak l\) with \((x+\mathfrak l)\cap I=\varnothing\), and \(I+\mathfrak l\) is then an open ideal
containing \(I\) but not \(x\). Moreover an ideal containing an open ideal is open, and an open ideal is
closed, its complement being a union of cosets of it; so the closed maximal ideals of \(A\) are exactly its
open maximal ideals, and the maximal ideals containing an open ideal are all among them. The Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, makes these points explicit.} A closed maximal ideal is
therefore open. Moreover, if
\(\mathfrak l\) is an open ideal of \(A\), the maximal ideals of
\(A/\mathfrak l\) correspond bijectively to the closed maximal ideals
\(\mathfrak m\) that contain \(\mathfrak l\). The localization
\((A/\mathfrak l)_{\mathfrak m}\) of \(A/\mathfrak l\) at a closed maximal
ideal \(\mathfrak m\) is therefore a local ring if \(\mathfrak m\) contains
\(\mathfrak l\), and is zero otherwise. Since the ring \(A/\mathfrak l\) is
Artinian, it is a direct product of finitely many local rings, which may be
written
\[
  A/\mathfrak l
  \simeq
  \prod_{\mathfrak m\in\Upsilon(A)}
    (A/\mathfrak l)_{\mathfrak m},
\]
where \(\Upsilon(A)\) denotes the set of closed maximal ideals of \(A\).
It follows that there are ``canonical'' isomorphisms
\[
  A
  \simeq \varprojlim_{\mathfrak l}(A/\mathfrak l)
  \simeq \varprojlim_{\mathfrak l}
    \prod_{\mathfrak m}(A/\mathfrak l)_{\mathfrak m}
  \simeq \prod_{\mathfrak m}
    \varprojlim_{\mathfrak l}(A/\mathfrak l)_{\mathfrak m}
  \simeq \prod_{\mathfrak m}A_{\mathfrak m},
\]
where
\[
  A_{\mathfrak m}
  =
  \varprojlim_{\mathfrak l}(A/\mathfrak l)_{\mathfrak m}.
\]
This \emph{local component} \(A_{\mathfrak m}\) is a filtered
projective limit of local rings; it is therefore a local ring which is
pseudocompact for the projective-limit topology (the rings
\((A/\mathfrak l)_{\mathfrak m}\) are Artinian and endowed with the discrete
topology).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B},
adds that \(A_{\mathfrak m}\) is also the localization of \(A\) at \(\mathfrak m\). Indeed, let \(e\) be the
idempotent of \(A=\prod A_{\mathfrak m'}\) with component \(1\) in \(A_{\mathfrak m}\) and \(0\) elsewhere.
For an open ideal \(\mathfrak l\subset\mathfrak m\), the image of \(e\) in \(A/\mathfrak l\) is the unit
element of \((A/\mathfrak l)_{\mathfrak m}\), which does not lie in \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak l\); so
\(e\notin\mathfrak m\), and \(1-e\in\mathfrak m\) because \(e(1-e)=0\). Hence
\(\mathfrak m=\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}\times(1-e)A\). In the localization of \(A\) at \(\mathfrak m\) the
element \(1-e\) becomes \(0\), and the elements of \(A_{\mathfrak m}\) outside \(\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}\)
are already invertible in \(A_{\mathfrak m}\); this localization is therefore \(A_{\mathfrak m}\).}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.1.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.1.2}
Let \(r(A)\) be the intersection of the closed maximal ideals of \(A\),
that is, the cartesian product of the ideals
\(\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}\) under the identification
\[
  A=\prod_{\mathfrak m}A_{\mathfrak m}.
\]
For every open ideal \(\mathfrak l\) of \(A\), the image of \(r(A)\) in
\(A/\mathfrak l\) is contained in the radical of \(A/\mathfrak l\).
Some power of this image is therefore zero, so
\(r(A)^n\subset\mathfrak l\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). Thus the
sequence \(r(A)^n\) tends to \(0\); the same is true of \(x^n\) when
\(x\in r(A)\). In other words, every
element of \(r(A)\) is topologically nilpotent, and the converse is
clear. It follows that the sequence with general term
\[
  1+x+\cdots+x^n
\]
converges, and converges to \(1/(1-x)\), whenever \(x\in r(A)\). This
shows that \(r(A)\) is the Jacobson radical of \(A\) (Bourbaki,
\emph{Alg\`ebre}, Chap.~8, \S6, Th.~1).\footnote{Translator's note: directly, if \(x\in r(A)\)
and a maximal ideal \(\mathfrak n\) of \(A\) did not contain \(x\), there would be \(y\in A\) with
\(1-xy\in\mathfrak n\); but \(xy\in r(A)\), so \(1-xy\) is invertible. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, gives this argument.}

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
\emph{Comparison with the formal schemes of EGA~I, \S~10.} (a) An open prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\) of \(A\)
contains an open ideal \(\mathfrak l\); then \(A/\mathfrak p\) is a quotient of the Artinian ring
\(A/\mathfrak l\) without zero divisors, hence a field. So \(\Upsilon(A)\) is also the set of open prime
ideals of \(A\).

(b) For \(\mathfrak m\in\Upsilon(A)\), the maximal ideal \(\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}\) of
\(A_{\mathfrak m}\) is the inverse image of the maximal ideal of the discrete ring
\((A/\mathfrak l)_{\mathfrak m}\) for any open \(\mathfrak l\subset\mathfrak m\), hence is open, and its
powers tend to \(0\) by 0.1.2 applied to \(A_{\mathfrak m}\). It is thus an ideal of definition in the
sense of EGA~0\textsubscript{I}, 7.1.2, and \(A_{\mathfrak m}\) is the ring of an affine formal scheme
with one point (EGA~I, 10.1.2).

(c) \(A\) is admissible in the sense of EGA~0\textsubscript{I}, 7.1.2, if and only if \(\Upsilon(A)\) is
finite. An ideal of definition consists of topologically nilpotent elements, hence lies in \(r(A)\), which
is then open; the elements of \(\Upsilon(A)\) all contain an open ideal \(\mathfrak l\subset r(A)\), and
correspond to maximal ideals of the Artinian ring \(A/\mathfrak l\), so there are finitely many.
Conversely, if \(\Upsilon(A)\) is finite, \(r(A)=\prod_{\mathfrak m}\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}\) is open,
and its powers tend to \(0\). In that case the elements of \(\Upsilon(A)\) are all the maximal ideals of
\(A\): a maximal ideal contains \(r(A)\), the Jacobson radical, hence the product of the elements of
\(\Upsilon(A)\), hence one of them. The affine formal scheme of EGA~I, 10.1.2, defined by \(A\) then has
\(\operatorname{Spec}(A/r(A))=\Upsilon(A)\), with the discrete topology, as underlying space, and
\(\prod_{\mathfrak m\in E}A_{\mathfrak m}\) as ring of sections over \(E\subset\Upsilon(A)\).

(d) For an arbitrary \(A\), the formal spectrum \(\operatorname{Spf}A\) of 1.1 below is covered by the
one-point affine formal schemes of (b), hence is a formal scheme (EGA~I, 10.4.2). It is quasi-compact,
and so can be affine, only if \(\Upsilon(A)\) is finite, and then it is the formal scheme of (c).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.1.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.1.3}
If \(A\) and \(B\) are pseudocompact rings, a \emph{homomorphism} from
\(A\) to \(B\) is, by definition, a continuous map compatible with
addition, multiplication, and the identity elements. Such a
homomorphism sends a topologically nilpotent element of \(A\) to a
topologically nilpotent element of \(B\); it therefore maps the radical
of \(A\) into the radical of \(B\).
