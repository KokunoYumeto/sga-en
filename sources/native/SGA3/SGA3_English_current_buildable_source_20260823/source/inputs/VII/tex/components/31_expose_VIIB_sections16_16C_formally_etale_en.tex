
\subsection*{1.6}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.6}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.6}{1.6}. Étale formal varieties}

To complete these generalities on formal varieties, it remains to
define briefly the étale formal varieties:

A formal variety \(X\) over \(k\) is said to be \emph{étale} if it is
topologically flat over \(k\) and the diagonal morphism
\(\Delta_X:X\to X\times X\) is a local isomorphism, that is, if
\(\Delta_X\) induces an isomorphism of
\(\mathcal O_{X\times X,\Delta_X(x)}\) onto \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\) for
every point \(x\) of \(X\).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text
does not require \(X\) to be topologically flat in this definition; the
condition is added in the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VII\textsubscript{B}. It is needed for the equivalence with the two
formulations that follow: if \(\kappa\) is a field,
\(k=\kappa[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\) and
\(X=\operatorname{Spf}(\kappa)\), then
\(\kappa\widehat\otimes_k\kappa=\kappa\), so that \(\Delta_X\) is an
isomorphism, while \(\kappa\) is not flat over \(k\), and \(X\) satisfies
neither of those formulations.} One sees easily with the aid of SGA~1 that
this formulation is equivalent to each of the following two: the formal
variety \(X\) is topologically flat and, for every point \(x\in X\)
with projection \(s\in\operatorname{Spf}(k)\),
\(\mathcal O_{X,x}\widehat\otimes_k\kappa(s)\) is a finite separable
extension of the residue field \(\kappa(s)\) of \(s\); or again, if
\(A\) denotes the affine algebra of \(X\), the local components
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.1}{0.1})} of \(A\widehat\otimes_k(k/\mathfrak l)\) are finite étale
algebras over \(k/\mathfrak l\), for every open ideal \(\mathfrak l\)
of \(k\).

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation). The re-edition proves
these equivalences and the properties of the functor \(X\mapsto X_e\)
stated below (its 1.6.A--1.6.F). Here is an account, in seven steps
\textup{(a)}--\textup{(g)}. Let \(X\) be a formal \(k\)-variety with
affine algebra \(A\). For a point \(x\) of \(X\), that is, an open
maximal ideal of \(A\), with image \(s\) in \(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\),
we write \(k_s\) for the local component of \(k\) at \(s\) and
\(\mathfrak n_s\) for its maximal ideal; the local component
\(\mathcal O_{X,x}\) of \(A\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.1.1}{0.1.1})} is a \(k_s\)-algebra.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(a)}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.6.A}
\emph{Formally étale profinite algebras.} We say that \(A\) is
\emph{formally étale over \(k\)} if, for every \(k\)-algebra \(C\)
with the discrete topology for which \(k\to C\) is continuous, and
every nilpotent ideal \(J\) of \(C\), every continuous morphism of
\(k\)-algebras \(A\to C/J\) is the reduction of exactly one continuous
morphism of \(k\)-algebras \(A\to C\) \textup{(compare
EGA~\(0_{\mathrm{IV}}\), 19.10.2)}. It is enough to consider ideals
\(J\) with \(J^2=0\), since \(C\to C/J\) is a composite of finitely
many surjections with square-zero kernels. If \(k\to k'\) is a
morphism of pseudocompact rings and \(C\) is a discrete
\(k'\)-algebra, the continuous \(k'\)-morphisms
\(A\widehat\otimes_kk'\to C\) are the continuous \(k\)-morphisms
\(A\to C\); hence \(A\widehat\otimes_kk'\) is formally étale over
\(k'\) when \(A\) is formally étale over \(k\).

Let \(F\in k[T]\) be monic of degree \(d\geq1\) and \emph{separable},
that is, \(GF+HF'=1\) for some \(G,H\in k[T]\). Then
\(B=k[T]/(F)\), which is free of rank \(d\), is formally étale over
\(k\) for the product topology. Indeed, with \(C\) and \(J\) as above
and \(J^2=0\), the morphisms \(B\to C\) lifting a given
\(\phi:B\to C/J\) correspond to the roots of \(F\) in \(C\) that lift
\(\phi(t)\), where \(t\) is the class of \(T\); they are continuous,
since their kernels contain \(\mathfrak lB\), which is open,
\(\mathfrak l\) being the kernel of \(k\to C\). If \(u\in C\) lifts
\(\phi(t)\), then \(F(u)\in J\) and \(H(u)F'(u)\in1+J\), so \(F'(u)\)
is invertible; as \(F(u+h)=F(u)+F'(u)h\) for \(h\in J\), the element
\(u-F'(u)^{-1}F(u)\) is the only root of \(F\) of the form \(u+h\),
\(h\in J\). Without the topologies, the same argument shows that \(B\)
is an étale \(k\)-algebra \textup{(EGA~\(\mathrm{IV}_4\), 17.3.1)}.

If \(A=A_1\times A_2\), then \(A\) is formally étale over \(k\) if and
only if \(A_1\) and \(A_2\) are. Indeed, the preceding argument,
applied to \(T^2-T\), for which \((2T-1)^2-4(T^2-T)=1\), shows that
every idempotent of \(C/J\) is the reduction of exactly one idempotent
of \(C\). If \(e\) is the idempotent with \(A_1=Ae\), a continuous
morphism \(\phi:A\to C/J\) and all its lifts \(\Phi\) therefore satisfy
\(\Phi(e)=f\), where \(f\) is the idempotent lifting \(\phi(e)\), and to
lift \(\phi\) amounts to lifting separately its restrictions
\(A_1\to(C/J)\phi(e)\) and \(A_2\to(C/J)(1-\phi(e))\) to \(Cf\) and
\(C(1-f)\). In the same way, if \(e\) is an idempotent of \(k\) with
\(eA=A\), then \(A\) is formally étale over \(k\) if and only if it is
so over the factor \(ek\) of \(k\). \textup{(EGA~\(0_{\mathrm{IV}}\),
19.3.5~(v), states the analogue for formally smooth algebras; the
argument given there treats a homomorphism defined on a product as the
family of its restrictions to the factors, which is incorrect, as the
re-edition points out. The argument by idempotents proves that
statement as well.)}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(b)}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.6.B}
\emph{Reductions.} The following conditions are equivalent:
\textup{(\(\alpha\))} \(A\) is formally étale over \(k\);
\textup{(\(\beta\))} every local component \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\) of
\(A\) is formally étale over \(k\), or, equivalently, over \(k_s\);
\textup{(\(\gamma\))} \(A\widehat\otimes_k(k/\mathfrak l)\) is
formally étale over \(k/\mathfrak l\) for every open ideal
\(\mathfrak l\) of \(k\). Indeed, in \textup{(a)} the kernel
\(\mathfrak l\) of \(k\to C\) is open, and continuous morphisms from
\(A\) to \(C\) or to \(C/J\) are the same as continuous morphisms from
\(A\widehat\otimes_k(k/\mathfrak l)\); this gives
\textup{(\(\alpha\))}\(\Leftrightarrow\)\textup{(\(\gamma\))}. Next, a
continuous morphism \(\phi:A\to C/J\) has an open kernel, which
contains all but finitely many factors of
\(A=\prod_x\mathcal O_{X,x}\); so \(\phi\) factors through the
projection of \(A\) onto a finite product of local components, which
is the quotient of \(A\) by an idempotent, and by \textup{(a)} so do the
lifts of \(\phi\); this gives
\textup{(\(\alpha\))}\(\Leftrightarrow\)\textup{(\(\beta\))}.

Now let \(R\) be a local ring of finite length, with maximal ideal
\(\mathfrak n_R\) and residue field \(\kappa\), and let \(B\) be a
local profinite \(R\)-algebra, with residue field \(L\), finite over
\(\kappa\). \emph{Then \(B\) is formally étale over \(R\) if and only
if \(B\) is a finite étale \(R\)-algebra, and then
\(B\simeq R[T]/(F)\) for a monic separable \(F\).} A finite étale
\(R\)-algebra has finite length, hence the discrete topology, and is
formally étale \textup{(EGA~\(\mathrm{IV}_4\), 17.3.1)}. Conversely,
let \(B\) be formally étale over \(R\). By base change,
\(B_0=B\widehat\otimes_R\kappa\) is formally étale over \(\kappa\),
and so is \(B_0\otimes_\kappa K\) over \(K\), where \(K\) is a finite
normal extension of \(\kappa\) containing \(L\). The local components
\(B'\) of \(B_0\otimes_\kappa K\), finite in number, have residue
field \(K\), because \(K\) is normal and contains \(L\); thus
\(B'=K\oplus\mathfrak n'\), \(\mathfrak n'\) being the maximal ideal.
For every open ideal \(\mathfrak N\neq B'\) of \(B'\), the projection
\(B'\to B'/(\mathfrak n'^2+\mathfrak N)\) and the composite of
\(B'\to K\) with the structure map
\(K\to B'/(\mathfrak n'^2+\mathfrak N)\) are two continuous
\(K\)-morphisms that lift \(B'\to K\); hence they coincide, and
\(\mathfrak n'\subset\mathfrak n'^2+\mathfrak N\). So
\(\mathfrak n'\) is the closure of \(\mathfrak n'^2\), and
\(\mathfrak n'=0\) by Nakayama's lemma \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.3}{0.3.3})}. Thus
\(B_0\otimes_\kappa K\) is a finite product of copies of \(K\); hence
\(B_0\) is finite over \(\kappa\) and reduced, so \(B_0=L\), and
\(L\otimes_\kappa K\) is reduced, so that \(L\) is separable over
\(\kappa\), \(K\) containing the roots of the minimal polynomials of
the elements of \(L\). If \(M\) is the submodule of \(B\) generated by
liftings of a basis of \(L\) over \(\kappa\), then
\(B=M+\mathfrak n_RB\), the ideal \(\mathfrak n_RB\) being closed since
\(\mathfrak n_R\) is finitely generated; hence \(B=M\), \(\mathfrak n_R\)
being nilpotent. So \(B\) is finite over \(R\), discrete and formally
étale, that is, étale. Finally, let \(F\in R[T]\) be monic, lifting the
minimal polynomial of a primitive element of \(L\) over \(\kappa\).
Then \(F'\) is invertible modulo \(F\), since it is so modulo
\((F)+\mathfrak n_RR[T]\), so \(F\) is separable, and \(R[T]/(F)\) is
local with residue field \(L\). By formal étaleness, the residue maps
of \(R[T]/(F)\) and \(B\) onto \(L\) lift to morphisms
\(R[T]/(F)\to B\) and \(B\to R[T]/(F)\), whose composites, which lift
the identity of \(L\), are the identities.
