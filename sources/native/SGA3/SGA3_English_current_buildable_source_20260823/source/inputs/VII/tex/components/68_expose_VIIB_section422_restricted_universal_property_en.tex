
\subsubsection*{4.2.2}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:4.2.2}

When \(\mathbf L\) is a flat \(p\)-Lie algebra over \(\mathbf O_k\),
\(U_p(\mathbf L)\) is flat over \(\mathbf O_k\) by
VII\textsubscript{A}, \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.3.3}{5.3.3}; consequently,
\(\mathscr G_p(\mathbf L)\) is a formal group topologically flat over
\(k\) which has \(U_p(\mathbf L)\) as its \(\mathbf O_k\)-bialgebra.
This formal group can be characterized by a universal property:
consider indeed a homomorphism
\[
  h:\mathscr G_p(\mathbf L)\longrightarrow G
\]
into a formal group \(G\); such an \(h\) induces a homomorphism
\(\operatorname{Lie}h\) from the Lie Algebra of
\(\mathscr G_p(\mathbf L)\) into that of \(G\). On the other hand, one
sees as in VII\textsubscript{A}, \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.3}{7.3}, that the canonical morphism of
\(\mathbf L\) into the restricted enveloping Algebra
\(U_p(\mathbf L)\) defines an isomorphism of \(\mathbf L\) onto the
\(p\)-Lie algebra of \(\mathscr G_p(\mathbf L)\);\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, details this: for \(C\) in \(\operatorname{Alf}/k\),
\(\operatorname{Lie}(\mathscr G_p(\mathbf L))(C)\) is the set of primitive elements of
\(U_p(\mathbf L(C))\) (2.6, VII\textsubscript{A}, \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:3.2.3}{3.2.3}), and these primitive elements are
the elements of \(\mathbf L(C)\), by the argument of VII\textsubscript{A}, 7.3, applied on each local factor of
\(C\), over which \(\mathbf L(C)\) is free (for a module free of any rank see the
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.5.3}{Addition after VII\textsubscript{A}, 5.4.1}); this isomorphism respects the
\(p\)-th power operations. The analogue in characteristic \(0\) is 3.1.} composing this
isomorphism with \(\operatorname{Lie}h\), one obtains a homomorphism
\[
  h':\mathbf L\longrightarrow\operatorname{Lie}G.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:4.2.2:universal-property}
{\itshape If \(k\) is a pseudocompact ring of characteristic
\(p>0\), \(G\) a formal \(k\)-group, and \(\mathbf L\) a flat
\(p\)-Lie algebra over \(\mathbf O_k\), the map \(h\mapsto h'\)
defined above is a bijection
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Grf}/k}
    \bigl(\mathscr G_p(\mathbf L),G\bigr)
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Hom}_{p\text{-}\operatorname{Lie}}
    \bigl(\mathbf L,\operatorname{Lie}G\bigr)
\]
of the set of homomorphisms of formal \(k\)-groups from
\(\mathscr G_p(\mathbf L)\) to \(G\) onto the set of morphisms of
\(p\)-Lie algebras from \(\mathbf L\) to \(\operatorname{Lie}G\).}

\textit{Proof.}
We may indeed immediately reduce to the case in which \(k\) is
Artinian. In this case, put
\[
  L=\mathbf L(k);
\]
the set
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Grf}/k}
    \bigl(\mathscr G_p(\mathbf L),G\bigr)
\]
is in canonical one-to-one correspondence \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.3.1}{2.3.1})} with the
homomorphisms of unital algebras
\[
  h:U_p(L)\longrightarrow\mathbf H(G)
\]
such that the following diagram commutes:
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIB:4.2.2:restricted-universal-diagram}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viib_15_95/diag_122}
\end{center}
Now \(h\) is defined by its restriction to \(L\), which is a
homomorphism of \(p\)-Lie algebras from \(L\) to the \(p\)-Lie algebra
underlying \(\mathbf H(G)\). The commutativity of the diagram therefore
simply means that \(h\) maps \(L\) into the part \(\operatorname{Lie}G\)
of \(\mathbf H(G)\) formed by the elements \(x\) such that
\[
  \varphi_G(x\otimes1+1\otimes x)
  =
  \delta_G(x).
\]
