
\section*{8. The Case of a Base Field}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:8}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{8. The Case of a Base Field}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{8.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:8.1}

We now summarize the results obtained when \(S\) is the spectrum of a
field \(k\) of characteristic \(p>0\). Let us then say that an
\(S\)-group scheme is \emph{algebraic} if its underlying scheme is of
finite type over \(S\). By Theorem \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.2}{7.2}, the functor \(\mathfrak G_p\), which
associates with every finite-dimensional \(k\)-\(p\)-Lie algebra
\(\mathcal L\) the \(k\)-group \(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\), is then left
adjoint to the functor which associates with every algebraic \(k\)-group
its \(p\)-Lie algebra over \(k\). By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.3}{7.3} and the theorem of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.4}{7.4},\footnote{Translator's note: the
1970 text refers to ``Theorem 7.4.1''; the theorem is the one stated in
7.4, whose proof occupies 7.4.1 and 7.4.2. The Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, cites Theorem 7.4.} the functor
\(\mathfrak G_p:\mathcal L\mapsto\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L)\) induces an
equivalence of the category of finite-dimensional \(k\)-\(p\)-Lie algebras
with that of algebraic \(k\)-groups of height \(\leq1\). Since
\(\mathfrak G_p\) is a left adjoint functor, it commutes with inductive
limits; since the inclusion of the category of algebraic \(k\)-groups of
height \(\leq1\) into that of all algebraic groups obviously commutes with
finite projective limits,\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, gives the reason: the
Frobenius morphism commutes with fibre products, so a fibre product of
algebraic \(k\)-groups of height \(\leq1\) is again of height \(\leq1\);
in particular kernels are the same in both categories.} one finally sees that \(\mathfrak G_p\) is an
exact functor. For example, if \(i:\mathcal L_0\to\mathcal L_1\) and
\(q:\mathcal L_1\to\mathcal L_2\) are homomorphisms of \(k\)-\(p\)-Lie
algebras and if the sequence
\[
  0\longrightarrow\mathcal L_0\xrightarrow{i}\mathcal L_1
  \xrightarrow{q}\mathcal L_2\longrightarrow0,
\]
formed by the underlying vector spaces, is exact, then
\(\mathfrak G_p(i)\) is an isomorphism of \(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L_0)\)
onto the kernel of \(\mathfrak G_p(q)\); the image of
\(\mathfrak G_p(i)\) is therefore a normal subgroup of
\(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L_1)\), and \(\mathfrak G_p(q)\) induces an
isomorphism of the quotient of \(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L_1)\) by this
normal subgroup onto \(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L_2)\).\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A},
states this as a corollary, for a surjective \(q\): the sequence
\(1\to\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L_0)\to\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L_1)\to
\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L_2)\to1\) of algebraic \(k\)-groups is exact, and
\(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L_2)\) represents the (fppf) quotient sheaf of
\(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L_1)\) by \(\mathfrak G_p(\mathcal L_0)\) (VIA,
\SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIA-3.2}{3.2}).}

\noindent\textbf{8.2. Proposition.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:8.2}
\textit{Consider an exact sequence of algebraic groups
over a field \(k\) of characteristic \(p>0\):}\footnote{Translator's note:
the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, makes
explicit that \(\pi\) is then faithfully flat and \(\tau\) identifies
\(G'\) with \(\operatorname{Ker}\pi\), so that \(G''\) represents the (fppf)
quotient sheaf of \(G\) by \(G'\) (VIA, \SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIA-3.2}{3.2} and \SGARefLink{par:sga3-VIA-5.2}{5.2}), and that the
epimorphisms in this proposition are epimorphisms of (fppf) sheaves.}

  \item[\textup{(ii)}] \(G'\) is smooth;

\textit{\noindent is exact;
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIA:8.2:item:iv}(iv)}] the morphism
  \[
    \operatorname{Fr}\pi:
    \operatorname{Fr}G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Fr}G''
  \]
  is an epimorphism;
  \item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIA:8.2:item:v}(v)}] the morphism
  \[
    \operatorname{Lie}(\pi):
    \operatorname{Lie}(G)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Lie}(G'')
  \]
  is surjective (II, \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:subsection:4-11}{4.11}).
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
  \textup{(i)}\Longleftrightarrow\textup{(ii)}
  \Longrightarrow\textup{(iii)}
  \Longrightarrow\textup{(iv)}
  \Longleftrightarrow\textup{(v)},
\]
and all five assertions are equivalent when \(G\) is smooth
over \(k\).}

Indeed, (i) is equivalent to (ii) by Expos\'e~VI: recall indeed that (i)
implies (ii) by SGA~II~1.3; on the other hand, (ii) means that \(\pi\) is
smooth at the origin (SGA~II~2.1; \(\pi\) is flat because it is
epimorphic), hence everywhere.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, refers for this
equivalence to VIB, \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-9.2}{9.2}~(vii), and notes that (iii) implies (iv), by
taking \(n=1\).}

Likewise, the equivalence of (iv) and (v) follows from the equivalence,
defined in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:8.1}{8.1}, between the category of algebraic \(k\)-groups of
height \(\leq1\) and that of finite-dimensional \(p\)-Lie algebras over
\(k\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{8.3. Proposition.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:8.3}
\textit{If \(G\) is an algebraic group over a field \(k\) of
characteristic \(p>0\), there exists an integer \(n_0\) such that
\(G/\operatorname{Fr}^nG\) is smooth over \(k\) for \(n\ge n_0\).}\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A},
states 8.3 and 8.3.1 for a group \(G\) locally of finite type over \(k\).
The proofs apply unchanged: when \(k\) is perfect,
\(G_{\mathrm{red}}\backslash G\) is then still the spectrum of a finite
local \(k\)-algebra with residue field \(k\) (VIA, \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-5.6.1}{5.6.1}). In the proof
below, the re-edition refers to VIA, \SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIA-3.3.2}{3.3.2} for the base change and to VIA,
\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-5.4.1}{5.4.1} for the homomorphism \(h\).}

Since the construction of \(G/\operatorname{Fr}^nG\) commutes with
extension of the base field (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:4.1.1}{4.1.1} and VIA, \SGARefLink{par:sga3-VIA-4.7}{4.7}), we may suppose
that \(k\) is perfect (SGA~II~5.5). In this case \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is
an algebraic subgroup of \(G\) (VIA, \SGARefLink{sga3:VIA:0.2}{0.2}), and we have the commutative
exact diagram
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:8.3-reduced-quotient-frobenius-diagram}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_075}
\end{center}
where we have put \(H=G_{\mathrm{red}}\backslash G\). Now \(H\) is the
spectrum of a finite local \(k\)-algebra with residue field \(k\) (VIB).
Consequently, there exists an integer \(n_0\) such that
\(\operatorname{Fr}^n(H/k)\) factors through the unique section of
\(\operatorname{Spec}k\) in \(H^{(p^n)}\) when \(n\ge n_0\). It follows
that, for \(n\ge n_0\), \(\operatorname{Fr}^n(G/k)\) factors through
\(G_{\mathrm{red}}^{(p^n)}\); the homomorphism
\[
  h:G/\operatorname{Fr}^nG\longrightarrow G_{\mathrm{red}}^{(p^n)}
\]
defined by this factorization is a monomorphism (VIA, \SGARefLink{par:sga3-VIA-5.4}{5.4}) and
induces a homeomorphism of the underlying topological spaces; it is
therefore an isomorphism (VIB; \(G_{\mathrm{red}}^{(p^n)}\) is reduced).
Since \(G_{\mathrm{red}}^{(p^n)}\) is smooth over \(k\)
(VIA, \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-1.3.1}{1.3.1}), \(G/\operatorname{Fr}^nG\) is smooth over \(k\) when
\(n\ge n_0\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{8.3.1. Corollary.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:8.3.1}
\textit{Let \(n\) be an integer \(\geq1\). Then \(G\) is smooth over \(k\)
if and only if \(\operatorname{Fr}^n(G/k)\) is an epimorphism.}

If \(G\) is smooth over \(k\), \(G\) is reduced and
\(\operatorname{Fr}^n(G/k)\) is surjective, hence is an epimorphism.
Conversely, since \(\operatorname{Fr}^n(G/k)^{(p^n)}\) coincides with
\(\operatorname{Fr}^n(G^{(p^n)}/k)\) (confer \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:4.1.3}{4.1.3}),
\(\operatorname{Fr}^{nm}(G/k)\) is an epimorphism for every \(m\) if
\(\operatorname{Fr}^n(G/k)\) is one. One then has
\(G/\operatorname{Fr}^{nm}G\simeq G^{(p^{nm})}\). Since
\(G/\operatorname{Fr}^{nm}G\) is smooth over \(k\) for \(m\) large,
\(G^{(p^{nm})}\) and \(G\) are smooth as well.

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, 8.1.4 and 8.3.1; our formulation). The
re-edition adds to 8.3.1 a third equivalent condition:
\(\operatorname{Fr}^n(G/k)\) \emph{is faithfully flat}. It rests on the
following remark of O.~Gabber. Let \(\varphi:G\to H\) be a homomorphism of
\(k\)-groups with kernel \(K\). If \(\varphi\) is an epimorphism of
(fpqc) sheaves, it is faithfully flat: \(G\) is then a torsor under \(K\)
over \(H\) (IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:corollary:5:1:7:1}{5.1.7.1}), and \(H\) is covered by affine open subsets
\(S_i\), each with an affine faithfully flat morphism \(T_i\to S_i\) which
factors through \(\varphi\) (IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:6:3:1}{6.3.1}); then
\(G\times_HT_i\simeq K\times T_i\) is faithfully flat over \(T_i\), hence
\(G\times_HS_i\) is faithfully flat over \(S_i\) by descent. Conversely, a
faithfully flat morphism which is quasi-compact, respectively locally of
finite presentation, is a covering for the (fpqc), respectively (fppf),
topology (IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:6:3:1}{6.3.1}), hence an epimorphism of the corresponding sheaves
(IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:4:4:3}{4.4.3}). Now \(\operatorname{Fr}^n(G/k)\) is quasi-compact, being a
homeomorphism, and locally of finite presentation, \(G\) and
\(G^{(p^n)}\) being locally of finite type over \(k\); so it is an
epimorphism if and only if it is faithfully flat. The re-edition also
gives references for two steps of the proof: a surjective homomorphism of
groups locally of finite type over \(k\) with reduced target is
faithfully flat (VIA, \SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIA-6.2}{6.2}, or VIB, \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-1.3}{1.3}); and \(G\) is smooth when
\(G^{(p^{nm})}\) is, because \(G^{(p^{nm})}\) is deduced from \(G\) by
the faithfully flat base change \(\operatorname{Spec}k\to\operatorname{Spec}k\)
defined by \(x\mapsto x^{p^{nm}}\) (EGA~IV\(_4\), 17.7.1).
