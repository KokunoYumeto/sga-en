
\subsubsection*{4.4.2}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:4.4.2}

Continue to take for \(k\) a field of characteristic \(p\). For every
infinitesimal formal \(k\)-group \(G\), the affine algebra \(A\) of
\(G\) is the projective limit of the affine algebras
\[
  A/I_A^{\{p^n\}}
\]
of the groups \(\operatorname{Fr}_nG\)
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:4.1.2}{4.1.2})}. An infinitesimal formal \(k\)-group is therefore
an inductive limit of formal \(k\)-groups of finite height.

Suppose now that \(G\) has height at most \(n\). Since
\(\operatorname{Fr}_1G\) is the kernel of
\(\operatorname{Fr}:G\to G^{(p)}\), \(\operatorname{Fr}\) induces a
monomorphism \(i\) of \(H=G/\operatorname{Fr}_1G\) into \(G^{(p)}\).
Since
\[
  \operatorname{Fr}^{n}(G/k)
  =
  \operatorname{Fr}^{n-1}\bigl(G^{(p)}/k\bigr)\circ\operatorname{Fr}(G/k)
\]
is zero, \(\operatorname{Fr}^{n-1}(G^{(p)}/k)\circ i\) is zero, and so
is \(i^{(p^{\,n-1})}\circ\operatorname{Fr}^{n-1}(H/k)\). Since \(i\) is a
monomorphism \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.4.1}{2.4.1})}, one sees that \(H\) has height at most
\(n-1\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, makes
the steps explicit: \(\operatorname{Fr}(G/k)=i\circ\pi\), where \(\pi:G\to H\) is the canonical projection,
an epimorphism (2.4), so that \(\operatorname{Fr}^{n-1}(G^{(p)}/k)\circ i\circ\pi=0\) implies
\(\operatorname{Fr}^{n-1}(G^{(p)}/k)\circ i=0\); this composite equals
\(i^{(p^{n-1})}\circ\operatorname{Fr}^{n-1}(H/k)\) because the Frobenius morphism is functorial; and
\(i^{(p^{n-1})}\), obtained from the monomorphism \(i\) by base change, is again a monomorphism, since
\(X\mapsto X^{(p)}\) commutes with fiber products.} One thus sees that a group of finite height has a
composition series whose quotients have height at most \(1\), and can
therefore be described by \(p\)-Lie algebras over \(k\).

Finally, when \(G\) is an infinitesimal formal \(k\)-group such that
\(\omega_{G/k}\) is finite-dimensional over \(k\), the affine algebra
\(A\) of \(G\) is a quotient of \(k[[\omega]]\),\footnote{Translator's note: \(\omega\) stands for
\(\omega_{G/k}\). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, justifies this in a remark
added after 4.4 (our formulation): as \(k\) is a field, the projection of \(I_A\) onto \(\omega_{G/k}\) has a
continuous \(k\)-linear section, which extends to a continuous homomorphism \(k[[\omega_{G/k}]]\to A\)
(1.2.5); as in the proof of \textup{(iii)} \(\Rightarrow\) \textup{(i)} in 3.3, this homomorphism induces a
surjection of the graded algebras associated with the filtrations by the powers of the augmentation ideals,
the graded algebra of \(A\) being generated by its terms of degree~\(1\), and it is therefore surjective
\textup{([CA], V, \S\,7, Lemma~1)}, \(A\) being separated for its filtration since \(G\) is infinitesimal.} so
that all the
algebras
\[
  A/I_A^{\{p^n\}}
\]
are finite-dimensional \(k\)-vector spaces. One sees consequently
that an infinitesimal formal group \(G\) over a field of
characteristic \(p>0\) is an inductive limit of finite formal groups
\textup{(that is, of finite length, \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2.6}{1.2.6})} as soon as
\(\omega_{G/k}\) is finite-dimensional over \(k\).
