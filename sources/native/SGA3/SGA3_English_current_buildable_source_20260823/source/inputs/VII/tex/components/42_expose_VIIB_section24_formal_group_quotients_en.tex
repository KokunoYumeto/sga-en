\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{2.4. Quotients of formal groups}

Let us now return to an arbitrary pseudocompact ring \(k\), in order to
apply to formal groups the results of \S\,\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1}{1} on passage to the
quotient by a topologically flat equivalence relation.

Let \(u:H\to G\) be a monomorphism of formal \(k\)-groups,
\(\mu:G\times G\to G\) the ``multiplication'' morphism of \(G\), and
\(\lambda\) the composite

VI\textsubscript{A}, \SGARefLink{par:sga3-VIA-3.1}{3.1})}.\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text writes \(F\) for
the subgroup and \(F\backslash G\), the variety of left classes, for the

\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:2.4:theorem}%
If \(H\) is topologically flat over \(k\), then
\(\operatorname{pr}_1\) is topologically flat, and we can apply
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.4.thm}{Theorem~1.4}: the canonical projection \(p\) of \(G\) onto the
formal variety \(G/H\) of the classes of \(G\) modulo \(H\) is therefore
topologically flat; on the other hand, the morphism
\(G\times H\to G\times_{G/H}G\) with components
\(\operatorname{pr}_1\) and \(\lambda\) is invertible. When \(T\) is a
formal variety of finite length over \(k\) and \(x\), \(y\) are two
elements of \(G(T)\), \(x\) and \(y\) therefore have the same image in
\((G/H)(T)\) if and only if there is a \(z\) in \(H(T)\) such that
\(y=x\cdot u(z)\). Taking for \(x\) the null morphism, which factors
through the unit section of \(G\), one sees that \(y\) has the same
image in \((G/H)(T)\) as the unit element of \(G(T)\) if and only if
\(y\) belongs to the image of \(H(T)\) under \(u(T)\).

In particular, if \(u\) is an isomorphism of \(H\) onto a normal
subgroup of \(G\), the structural morphism from \(G\times G\) to \(G\)
manifestly induces, by passage to the quotient, a structure of formal
group on \(G/H\); what precedes then shows that \(u\) is an isomorphism
of \(H\) onto \(\operatorname{Ker}p\).

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation). The re-edition states the
preceding discussion as a theorem, with the following complements.
\textup{(1)} As a cokernel in \(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\)
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2}{1.2})}, \(G/H\) has for affine algebra the set of the
\(a\in\mathcal O(G)\) such that \(\Delta(a)-a\widehat\otimes1\) lies in
the image of \(\mathcal O(G)\widehat\otimes_kI\), where \(\Delta\) is the
diagonal morphism of \(\mathcal O(G)\) and \(I\) the kernel of the
surjection \(u^\natural:\mathcal O(G)\to\mathcal O(H)\)
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.prop}{1.3})}: these are the elements on which
\(\operatorname{pr}_1^\natural:a\mapsto a\widehat\otimes1\) and
\(\lambda^\natural=(\operatorname{id}\widehat\otimes u^\natural)\circ\Delta\)
agree. \textup{(2)} When \(H\) is topologically flat over \(k\), \(p\) is
also surjective \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.4.thm}{1.4})}, and \(G/H\) represents the quotient
sheaf of \(G\) by \(H\) for the flat topology \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.5}{1.5})}. The
morphism \(G\times G\to G/H\), \((g,x)\mapsto p(gx)\), therefore factors
through \(\operatorname{id}_G\times p\); this defines an action of \(G\) on
\(G/H\) for which \(p\) is equivariant, and, when \(H\) is normal, the
formal group structure on \(G/H\) obtained above is the one for which \(p\)
is a morphism of groups. The re-edition refers for this to Exposé~IV,
\SGARefLink{sga3:iv:corollary:5:2:2}{5.2.2} and \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:corollary:5:2:4}{5.2.4}. \textup{(3)} Put \(A=\mathcal O(G)\),
\(B=\mathcal O(G/H)\subset A\), and let \(I_A\) be the augmentation ideal of
\(A\). Then, when \(H\) is topologically flat over \(k\), the ideal \(I\) of
\(H\) in \(A\) is the closed ideal \(\overline{A(B\cap I_A)}\) generated by
\(B\cap I_A\). Indeed, \(B\cap I_A\) is the ideal of the unit point
\(p(e)\) of \(G/H\), and, by the isomorphism
\(G\times H\simeq G\times_{G/H}G\), the fibre of \(p\) at \(p(e)\) is
\(u(H)\); its affine algebra is
\(\bigl(B/(B\cap I_A)\bigr)\widehat\otimes_BA=A/\overline{A(B\cap I_A)}\).
