
\subsection*{5.3. Remarks}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:5.3}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{5.3. Remarks}

Call a profinite \(k\)-algebra \emph{stationary} if it is the
completed tensor product of a family of truncated formal power
series algebras. Let \(G\) be an infinitesimal formal \(k\)-group and let \(B\)
be the affine algebra of a homogeneous space of \(G\). It follows
from \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.2:theorem}{Theorem~5.2} that, for every \(r\in\mathbb N\), the algebra
\[
  B
  \big/
  \bigl((x^{p^r})\bigr)_{x\in\mathfrak r(B)}
\]
is stationary. In particular, \(B\) is an inverse limit of
stationary algebras.

I do not know whether, with the notation of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.2.1:section-proposition}{5.2.1}, \(G\) and
\(B\) can be chosen so that there exists no section
\[
  \sigma:\omega\longrightarrow I
\]
compatible with the filtrations.
Nevertheless, \(\omega\) may be any linearly compact vector space
filtered by an increasing sequence of closed subspaces. Indeed,
given
\[
  \omega_1\subset\omega_2\subset\cdots\subset\omega,
\]
one can define on the algebra
\[
  A
  =
  k[[\omega]]
  \big/
  \bigl((x^{p^r})\bigr)_{x\in\omega_r}
\]
a diagonal morphism
\[
  \Delta_A:A\longrightarrow A\widehat\otimes_kA
\]
satisfying conditions \textup{\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.1:item:i}{(i)}}, \textup{(ii)}, and
\textup{\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.1:item:iii}{(iii)}} of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.1}{2.1}. It suffices to put
\[
  \Delta_A(y)
  =
  y\widehat\otimes1+1\widehat\otimes y
\]
when \(y\) is the image in \(A\) of an element of \(\omega\).\footnote{Translator's note: here
\(G=\operatorname{Spf}A\), and \(B=A\) is the affine algebra of \(G\) as a homogeneous space of itself;
the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, writes \(B=A\). The filtration that 5.2.1
attaches to \(B\) is the given one: if \(y\in I\) has image \(\ell\) in \(\omega\) and \(y^{p^n}=0\), then
\(\ell^{p^n}=0\) in \(A\), by comparison of the homogeneous terms of degree \(p^n\) (the relations are
homogeneous); and \(\ell^{p^n}=0\) forces \(\ell\in\omega_n\), as one sees in the quotient of \(A\) defined
in the same way by \(\omega/U\), for \(U\) open in \(\omega\), with a basis of \(\omega/U\) adapted to the
filtration (\(\omega_n\) is closed). So the identity of \(\omega\) is a section of the required kind. For a
case where such a section always exists, see the Addition at the end of 5.2.1.}

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, 5.2.6 and 5.3; our formulation).
(1) \emph{Completed tensor products of infinitely many factors.} Let
\((A_\lambda)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\) be a family of profinite
\(k\)-algebras, each with a homomorphism of profinite algebras
\(\varepsilon_\lambda:A_\lambda\to k\) (for instance, \(A_\lambda\) local
with residue field \(k\)). For finite subsets \(F\subset F'\) of
\(\Lambda\), put
\(A_F=\mathop{\widehat{\bigotimes}}\nolimits_{\lambda\in F}A_\lambda\),
and let \(A_{F'}\to A_F\) be the homomorphism which applies
\(\varepsilon_\lambda\) to the factors with \(\lambda\in F'\setminus F\);
it is surjective. The completed tensor product
\(\mathop{\widehat{\bigotimes}}\nolimits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_\lambda\) is
the projective limit of the \(A_F\). A continuous homomorphism of it into
a \(k\)-algebra \(C\) of finite length factors through one of the
\(A_F\); so, if \(X_\lambda=\operatorname{Spf}A_\lambda\), the points of
its formal spectrum with values in \(C\) are the families
\((x_\lambda)\in\prod_\lambda X_\lambda(C)\) in which \(x_\lambda\) is the
composite of \(\varepsilon_\lambda\) and \(k\to C\) for all but finitely
many \(\lambda\).
(2) \emph{Stationary algebras.} For a linearly compact vector space
\(W\) and \(h\in\overline{\mathbb N}\), \(h\geq1\), put
\(T_h(W)=k[[W]]/((x^{p^h}))_{x\in W}\). Comparing points as in (1), one
sees that
\(\mathop{\widehat{\bigotimes}}\nolimits_\lambda T_{h_\lambda}(W_\lambda)\)
is isomorphic to \(k[[\omega]]/((x^{p^r}))_{x\in\omega_r}\), where
\(\omega=\prod_\lambda W_\lambda\) and \(\omega_r\) is the product of the
\(W_\lambda\) with \(h_\lambda\leq r\); by the note to the remark above,
\(\omega\) and this filtration are those that 5.2.1 attaches to the
algebra. Grouping the factors of equal height, write
\(\omega=\prod_hW'_h\), so that \(\omega_r=\prod_{h\leq r}W'_h\). The
closure \(\omega_\infty\) of \(\bigcup_r\omega_r\) is
\(\prod_{h<\infty}W'_h\); its dual \(V=\omega_\infty^\dagger\) is the
direct sum of the \((W'_h)^\dagger\), \(h<\infty\), and the orthogonal of
\(\omega_r\) in \(V\) is the sum of those with \(h>r\): the filtration of
\(V\) splits, which is the second case of the Addition at the end of
5.2.1. The re-edition suggests calling these algebras \emph{stable},
following J.~Dieudonn\'e, \emph{Introduction to the theory of formal
groups}, Marcel Dekker, 1973, chap.~II, \S\,2.9, and relates them, in
the dual setting of connected cocommutative Hopf algebras, to the work
of M.~E.~Sweedler, \emph{Hopf algebras with one grouplike element},
Trans.\ Amer.\ Math.\ Soc.\ \textbf{127} (1967), 515--526, Theorem~3.
(3) \emph{A projective limit of stationary algebras need not be
stationary.} Let \(\omega=S^*\) be the dual \textup{(0.2.2)} of the vector
space \(S\) of all sequences \((u_n)_{n\geq1}\) of elements of \(k\),
\(x_n\in\omega\) the linear form \(u\mapsto u_n\), and
\(\omega_r=kx_1+\cdots+kx_r\). The algebra
\(A=k[[\omega]]/((x^{p^r}))_{x\in\omega_r}\), that is,
\(k[[\omega]]/((x_n^{p^n}))_{n\geq1}\), is the affine algebra of an
infinitesimal formal group (remark above), hence a projective limit of
stationary algebras; its points with values in \(C\) are all the
sequences \((c_n)\) of elements of \(C\) with \(c_n^{p^n}=0\). It is not
stationary. Here \(\omega_\infty=\omega\), \(V=S\), and the orthogonal of
\(\omega_r\) is the space of sequences vanishing in degrees \(\leq r\), of
codimension \(r\); for a stationary algebra, (2) would then make \(V\) the
union of the finite-dimensional subspaces
\(\bigoplus_{h\leq r}(W'_h)^\dagger\), hence of countable dimension, which
\(S\) is not (Addition at the end of 5.2.1).

\subsection*{5.4. Corollary}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:5.4}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{5.4. Corollary}

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:vii:corollary:5-4}\textbf{Corollary 5.4.}
{\itshape Let \(G\) be an algebraic group over a perfect field
\(k\) of characteristic \(p>0\), let \(H\) be an algebraic
subgroup of \(G\), let \(e\) be the image of the identity element
of \(G\) in \(H\backslash G\), and let \(A\) be the local ring of \(H\backslash G\)
at \(e\). Then \(\widehat A\) is isomorphic to an algebra of the
form
\[
  k[[X_1,\ldots,X_r,\ldots,X_s]]
  \big/
  (X_1^{p^{n_1}},\ldots,X_r^{p^{n_r}}).
\]}

This follows from \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.2:theorem}{5.2}~\textup{\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.2:theorem:item:ii}{(ii)}} and \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.4}{1.3.4}.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, spells this out; in our words: put
\(\widehat G=\operatorname{Spf}\widehat{\mathcal O}_{G,e}\) and
\(\widehat H=\operatorname{Spf}\widehat{\mathcal O}_{H,e}\). Applied to the faithfully flat morphism
\(G\to H\backslash G\), whose kernel pair is \(H\times G\), 1.3.4 identifies, on the components at the unit
points, \(\widehat A\) with the affine algebra of the homogeneous space \(\widehat H\backslash\widehat G\); and
\(\widehat A\) is a complete Noetherian local ring, so that 5.2 (ii) applies. The re-edition also refers to
M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel, \emph{Groupes alg\'ebriques}, tome~I, Masson and North-Holland, 1970, III,
\S\,3, 6.1.}
