
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{8.4.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:8.4}

In the two statements which conclude this Expos\'e, we return to
the case of a field \(k\) of arbitrary characteristic.

If \(k\) has characteristic \(0\), let \(n\ge1\). If \(k\) has
characteristic \(p>0\), let \(n\ge1\) be prime to \(p\). In both
cases we simply say that \(n\) is \emph{prime to the
characteristic of \(k\)}. If \(G\) is a group scheme over \(k\),
we denote by
\[
  n_G:G\longrightarrow G
\]
the morphism of \(k\)-schemes which sends
\(x\in G(T)\) to \(x^n\in G(T)\), for every \(k\)-scheme \(T\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:8.4-proposition}
\textit{Let \(G\) be an algebraic group over a field \(k\), and
let \(n\) be prime to the characteristic of \(k\). Then
\[
  n_G:G\longrightarrow G
\]
is a morphism \'etale at the origin.}\footnote{Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, states that
\(n_G\) is \'etale, deducing this from the case of the origin by VIB,
\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-1.3}{1.3}. That proposition concerns homomorphisms of groups, and \(n_G\) is one
when \(G\) is commutative; then the stronger statement holds. In general
it fails: for \(G=\mathrm{SL}_{2,\mathbb C}\) and \(n=2\), the fibre of
\(n_G\) over \(-1\) consists of the matrices of trace \(0\) (as
\(x^2=(\operatorname{tr}x)x-1\)), a surface containing
\(\operatorname{diag}(i,-i)\), so \(n_G\) is not \'etale at that point.}

Indeed, let \(A\) be the local ring of \(G\) at the origin and \(I\) its
maximal ideal. By II, \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:proposition:3-9}{3.9}, the map
\[
  \operatorname{Lie}(n_G):
  \operatorname{Lie}(G)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Lie}(G)
\]
induced by \(n_G\) is multiplication by \(n\). It is therefore an
isomorphism, as is the endomorphism induced by \(n_G\) on \(I/I^2\).

If \(k\) has characteristic \(0\), \(G\) is smooth over \(k\) (VIB,
\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.6.1}{1.6.1}; see also VIIB, \S3); hence \(A\) is regular, and \(n_G\) induces an
automorphism of the graded ring associated with \(A\), hence also an
automorphism of the completion \(\widehat A\) of \(A\).\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A},
specifies that the regularity of \(A\) means that the canonical
homomorphism from the symmetric algebra of \(I/I^2\) to the graded ring
associated with \(A\) is an isomorphism, and that \(n_G\), inducing an
automorphism of \(\widehat A\), is \'etale at the origin
(EGA~IV\(_4\), 17.6.3).}

If the characteristic is \(p>0\) and \(G\) is of height \(\leq1\), \(A\)
is isomorphic to the quotient of the symmetric algebra of
\(\omega_{G/k}=I/I^2\) by the ideal generated by the \(p\)-th powers of
the elements of \(\omega_{G/k}\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.4}{7.4}); one can then apply the ``same''
argument as in characteristic \(0\).

If \(G\) is of height \(\leq r\) and if we suppose our assertion proved for
groups of height \(\leq r-1\), let \(B\), \(A\) and \(C\) be the affine
algebras of \(\operatorname{Fr}G\), \(G\) and
\(G_{\mathrm{Fr}}=\operatorname{Fr}G\backslash G\). Let us call \(n_B\),
\(n_A\) and \(n_C\) the endomorphisms of \(B\), \(A\) and \(C\) which are
induced by \(n_{\operatorname{Fr}G}\), \(n_G\) and
\(n_{G_{\mathrm{Fr}}}\). Since \(n_C\) is an isomorphism by the induction
hypothesis and \(A\) is flat over \(C\) (VIA, \SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIA-3.2}{3.2}), \(n_A\) is a
bijection if and only if \(n_A\otimes_C(C/\mathfrak r)\) is one
(\(\mathfrak r\) denotes the radical of \(C\)); but
\(n_A\otimes_C(C/\mathfrak r)\) is none other than \(n_B\)!\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A},
details these steps: \(\operatorname{Fr}G\) is the fibre of
\(G\to G_{\mathrm{Fr}}\) over the origin, so that
\(B=A\otimes_C(C/\mathfrak r)\); since \(n_A\) extends \(n_C\), an
automorphism, \(n_A(A)\) is a \(C\)-submodule of \(A\), and if
\(n_A\otimes_C(C/\mathfrak r)=n_B\) is bijective, Nakayama's lemma (\(A\)
being finite over \(C\)) shows that \(n_A\) is surjective, hence bijective,
\(A\) being finite-dimensional over \(k\).}

Finally, let \(G\) be any algebraic group over a field of
characteristic \(p>0\). The preceding argument shows that
\(n_G\) induces automorphisms of the \(k\)-schemes
\(\operatorname{Fr}^rG\). These schemes are affine over \(k\),
and their algebras are the quotients of the local algebra \(A\)
by the ideal
\[
  I^{\{p^r\}}
\]
generated by the \(p^r\)-th powers of the elements of \(I\).
Since \(n_G\) induces automorphisms of all the algebras
\(A/I^{\{p^r\}}\), passage to the projective limit shows that it
induces an automorphism of \(\widehat A\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{8.5. Proposition.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:8.5}
\textit{Let \(G\) be a finite algebraic group of rank \(n\) over
the field \(k\). Then
\[
  n_G:G\longrightarrow G
\]
is the zero morphism of \(G\) \textup{(confer \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:8.4}{8.4})}.}\footnote{Translator's note:
the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, states
the consequence this reference points to: if \(n\) is prime to the
characteristic of \(k\), then \(G\) is \'etale over \(k\). Indeed
\(n_G=0\) factors through \(\operatorname{Spec}k\), and it is \'etale at
the origin by 8.4, so that the completed local ring of \(G\) at the origin
is \(k\); thus \(G\) is \'etale at the origin, hence \'etale. The
re-edition refers for another proof to J.~Tate and F.~Oort, Group schemes
of prime order, Ann. Sci. \'Ecole Norm. Sup. (4) \textbf{3} (1970), 1--21,
Lemma~5.}

Let \(H\) be a normal subgroup of \(G\) of rank \(m\) over \(k\). With the
notation of VIA, \SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIA-3.2}{3.2}, the square
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:8.5-normal-subgroup-cartesian-square}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_078}
\end{center}
is cartesian. Since \(G\to H\backslash G\) is faithfully flat and
quasi-compact (VIA, \SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIA-3.2}{3.2}), and \(\operatorname{pr}_2\) is locally free of
rank \(m\), it follows from EGA~IV, 2.5.2 that \(G\to H\backslash G\) is
locally free of rank \(m\). One therefore has
\[
  \operatorname{rk}_k(H\backslash G)\times\operatorname{rk}_kH
  =\operatorname{rk}_kG.
\]

On the other hand, one has an exact sequence of ``abstract'' groups
\[
  1\longrightarrow H(T)\longrightarrow G(T)
  \longrightarrow(H\backslash G)(T)
\]
for every \(k\)-scheme \(T\); it is therefore clear that \(n_G\) is zero
if \(m_H\) and \((nm^{-1})_{H\backslash G}\) are. If \(H\) is the
connected component of the origin of \(G\), \(H\backslash G\) is \'etale
(VIB), so that one may suppose \(G\) \'etale over \(k\) or connected.

If \(G\) is \'etale, extension of the base field reduces us to
the case where \(k\) is algebraically closed. Then \(G\) is a
constant group (I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:4-1}{4.1}), and the assertion is classical.

If \(G\) is connected and nonzero, the characteristic \(p\) of \(k\) is
necessarily \(>0\) (VIB; VIIB, \S3); the subgroups
\(\operatorname{Fr}^rG\) then form a composition series of \(G\), whose
quotients are of height \(\leq1\).

This reduces us to the case where \(G\) is of height \(\leq1\): let then
\(A\) be the affine algebra of \(G\) and \(L\) its Lie algebra; if
\([L:k]=r\), the rank of \(G\) over \(k\) is \(p^r\) (VIIA, \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.3.3}{5.3.3}); we
shall therefore study the morphism \(p_G:G\to G\) defined by raising to
the \(p\)-th power.

This morphism \(p_G\) defines endomorphisms \(p_A\) and \(p_U\) of \(A\)
and of the restricted enveloping algebra \(U=U_p(L)\) of \(L\). The map
\(p_U\) decomposes as follows:
\[
  U\xrightarrow{\Delta_U^p}\bigotimes\nolimits_k^pU\xrightarrow{m_U^p}U,
\]
where \(\Delta_U^p\) denotes the homomorphism of algebras which sends
\(x\in L\subset U\) to
\(x\otimes1\otimes\cdots\otimes1+1\otimes x\otimes\cdots\otimes1+\cdots
+1\otimes1\otimes\cdots\otimes x\), while \(m_U^p\) is the linear map which
sends \(u_1\otimes u_2\otimes\cdots\otimes u_p\) to the product
\(u_1u_2\cdots u_p\). If \(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_t\) are \(t\) elements of
\(L\subset U\), one therefore has
\[
  p_U(x_1x_2\cdots x_t)
  =
  m_U^p\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^t\sum_{i=1}^p
    1\otimes\cdots\otimes
    \underset{\text{\scriptsize position \(i\)}}{x_j}
    \otimes\cdots\otimes1\Bigr).
\]

It is clear that the expression
\(\prod_j\sum_i1\otimes\cdots\otimes x_j\otimes\cdots\otimes1\) is a sum of
\(p^t\) terms \(x_h\) indexed by the maps \(h\) from
\(\{1,2,\ldots,t\}\) to \(\{1,2,\ldots,p\}\). Such a map defines a
preorder on \(\{1,2,\ldots,t\}\) such that \(i\leq j\) if and only if
\(h(i)\leq h(j)\); moreover, \(m_U^p(x_h)=m_U^p(x_\ell)\) if \(h\) and
\(\ell\) define the same preorder, so that we may write
\(m_U^p(x_h)=x_{\mathfrak o}\), where \(\mathfrak o\) is the preorder
defined by \(h\). Consequently,
\[
  p_U(x_1x_2\cdots x_t)
  =
  \sum_{\mathfrak o}\binom{p}{s(\mathfrak o)}\cdot x_{\mathfrak o},
\]
where \(\mathfrak o\) ranges over the preorder relations on
\(\{1,\ldots,t\}\) whose associated ordered set has at most \(p\)
elements, and \(s(\mathfrak o)\) is the cardinality of the ordered set
associated with \(\mathfrak o\).

When \(t<p\), all the terms \(\binom{p}{s(\mathfrak o)}\) are zero, so
that \(p_U(x_1\cdots x_t)=0\). In other words, \(p_U\) vanishes on the
vector subspace \(U_{p-1}^+\) of \(U\) which is spanned by the products
\(x_1\cdots x_t\), \(t<p\), \(x_i\in L\). Now it follows easily from
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.3}{7.3} that the canonical isomorphism of the dual \(U^*\) onto \(A\),
which is described in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.4}{7.4}, identifies the orthogonal of
\(U_{p-1}^+\) with \(I_A^p\) (\(I_A\) the augmentation ideal of \(A\);
confer also VIIB, \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.6}{1.3.6} and \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:4.3}{4.3}).\footnote{Translator's note:
the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, details
this. As the multiplication of \(A\) is the transpose of the
comultiplication of \(U\), an element \(u\) of \(U\) is orthogonal to
\(I_A^s\) if and only if its iterated comultiplication, projected onto
\(J^{\otimes s}\) (\(J\) the augmentation ideal of \(U\)), is zero; by the
formula for \(\Delta(z^n/n!)\) in 7.3, this holds exactly for the linear
combinations of the \(z^n\) with \(n_1+\cdots+n_r<s\), that is, of \(1\)
and of the products of fewer than \(s\) elements of \(L\). For \(s=p\),
since \(I_A\) and \(J\) are dual to each other, this gives the
assertion.} The isomorphism of \(U^*\) onto
\(A\) also allows one to identify \(p_A\) with the transpose of \(p_U\),
so that the composite map
\[
  I_A\xrightarrow{p_A}I_A\xrightarrow{\text{can.}}I_A/I_A^p
\]
is zero. Hence \(p_A\) maps \(I_A\) into \(I_A^p\), and \((p_A)^r\) maps
\(I_A\) into \(I_A^{p^r}\). Since \(I_A^{r(p-1)+1}\) is zero by Theorem
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.4}{7.4}, the inequality \(p^r>r(p-1)\) shows that \(p_A^r\) annihilates
\(I_A\).

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, 8.5.2 and 8.5.3; our formulation). The
re-edition extends 8.5 to a reduced base, as noted in Expos\'e~VIII,
Remark~\SGARefLink{sga3:viii:remark:7:3:1}{7.3.1}: \emph{if \(S\) is a reduced scheme and \(G\) a finite
locally free \(S\)-group of rank \(n\), then \(n_G\) is the zero
morphism.} Let \(S'\) be the disjoint union of the spectra of the local
rings \(\mathcal O_{S,\eta}\) at the maximal points \(\eta\) of \(S\); they
are fields, \(S\) being reduced, so that \(n_{G_{S'}}=0\) by 8.5. As
\(S'\to S\) is schematically dominant and \(G\) is finite and locally free
over \(S\), \(G_{S'}\to G\) is schematically dominant (EGA~IV\(_3\),
11.10.5). Since \(G\) is separated over \(S\), the equalizer of \(n_G\)
and the zero morphism is a closed subscheme of \(G\) through which
\(G_{S'}\to G\) factors; it is therefore \(G\). The re-edition also recalls
a theorem of P.~Deligne: if \(G\) is a commutative finite locally free
group of rank \(n\) over an arbitrary base \(S\), then \(n_G=0\)
(J.~Tate and F.~Oort, Group schemes of prime order, Ann. Sci. \'Ecole
Norm. Sup. (4) \textbf{3} (1970), 1--21, p.~4).
