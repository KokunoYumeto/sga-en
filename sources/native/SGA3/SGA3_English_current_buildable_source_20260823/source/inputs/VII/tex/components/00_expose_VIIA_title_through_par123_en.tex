In Expos\'e II we restricted ourselves to the study of first-order
differential invariants, and we did not address certain phenomena special
to characteristic \(p>0\) or to characteristic zero. Our purpose in
Part~A of this expos\'e is to fill that gap.

Moreover, the infinitesimal study of a group scheme to arbitrary order is
related to that of the associated formal group; the purpose of the second
part of this expos\'e is to present the first definitions and properties
concerning formal groups.

\section*{1. Differential Operators}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:1}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{1. Differential Operators}

In Sections~1, \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:2}{2} and~3 below, \(S\) denotes a
scheme, and all products under consideration are cartesian products in
the category of \(S\)-schemes.\footnote{Translator's note: thus
\(X\times Y\) stands for \(X\times_SY\). For Sections~1 and~2 the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, refers to
M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel, \textit{Groupes alg\'ebriques}, tome~I,
Masson and North-Holland, 1970, II, \S4, nos.~5--6, and to
J.~C.~Jantzen, \textit{Representations of Algebraic Groups}, second
edition, American Mathematical Society, 2003, Part~I, Chapter~7.}
If \(X\) is an \(S\)-scheme, we write \(p_{X/S}\), \(p_X\), or simply
\(p\) for the structural morphism from \(X\) to \(S\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:1.1}
Let \(u:Y\to X\) be a morphism of \(S\)-schemes, and endow the direct
image \(u_*(\mathcal O_Y)\) of the structure sheaf of \(Y\) with the
\(\mathcal O_X\)-module structure induced by \(u\). The sheaf
\[
  \mathcal H=
  \mathcal Hom_{p_X^{-1}(\mathcal O_S)}
    \bigl(\mathcal O_X,u_*(\mathcal O_Y)\bigr)
\]
of homomorphisms of \(p_X^{-1}(\mathcal O_S)\)-modules from
\(\mathcal O_X\) to \(u_*(\mathcal O_Y)\) is therefore naturally an
\(\mathcal O_X\)-bimodule. If \(U\) is an open subset of \(X\), and
\(f\) and \(d\) are sections over \(U\) of \(\mathcal O_X\) and
\(\mathcal H\), respectively, then \(fd\) and \(df\) are the morphisms
\[
  g\longmapsto f\,d(g)
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  g\longmapsto d(fg)
\]
from \(\mathcal O_X\) to \(u_*(\mathcal O_Y)\). From now on we write
\((\operatorname{ad}f)(d)\) in place of \(fd-df\).

\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:1.1.1}
An \(S\)-deviation of order \(\leq n\) is by definition a pair
\(D=(u,d)\), consisting of a morphism of \(S\)-schemes \(u:Y\to X\)
and a morphism of \(p_X^{-1}(\mathcal O_S)\)-modules
\(d:\mathcal O_X\to u_*(\mathcal O_Y)\) such that
\begin{equation}
  (\operatorname{ad}f_0)(\operatorname{ad}f_1)\cdots
  (\operatorname{ad}f_n)(d)=0
  \tag{\(\ast\)}
\end{equation}
for every open subset \(U\) of \(X\) and every sequence of \(n+1\)
sections \(f_0,\ldots,f_n\) of \(\mathcal O_X\) over \(U\). If the
equalities \((\ast)\) hold, we also say that \(d\) is an
\(S\)-deviation of \(u\) of order \(\leq n\).\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A},
adds two reformulations of \((\ast)\); in our words: since
\(\mathcal H\) is a sheaf and \(\operatorname{ad}f\) commutes with
restriction to smaller opens, \((\ast)\) can be checked on stalks, that
is, it holds if and only if
\((\operatorname{ad}f_0)\cdots(\operatorname{ad}f_n)(d_x)(g)=0\) for
every \(x\in X\) and all germs \(f_0,\ldots,f_n,g\in\mathcal O_{X,x}\).
Further, let \(d':u^{-1}(\mathcal O_X)\to\mathcal O_Y\) correspond to
\(d\) under the adjunction between \(u^{-1}\) and \(u_*\), and let
\(u^{-1}(\mathcal O_X)\) act on \(\mathcal O_Y\) through \(u\). As the
counit \(u^{-1}u_*(\mathcal O_Y)\to\mathcal O_Y\) is
\(u^{-1}(\mathcal O_X)\)-linear, \((\operatorname{ad}f)(d)\) corresponds
to \((\operatorname{ad}u^{-1}f)(d')\); hence \(d\) satisfies \((\ast)\)
if and only if \(d'\) satisfies the same identities for all local
sections of \(u^{-1}(\mathcal O_X)\).} In particular, an
\(S\)-deviation of order \(\leq0\) is a morphism of
\(\mathcal O_X\)-modules from \(\mathcal O_X\) to
\(u_*(\mathcal O_Y)\).

A morphism of \(p^{-1}(\mathcal O_S)\)-modules \(d\) from
\(\mathcal O_X\) to \(u_*(\mathcal O_Y)\) is an \(S\)-deviation of
\(u\) if, for every point \(y\) of \(Y\), there exist an open
neighborhood \(U\) of \(u(y)\) in \(X\) and an open neighborhood \(V\)
of \(y\) in \(Y\) satisfying the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{a)}] \(u(V)\subset U\);
\item[\textup{b)}] if \(v:V\to U\) is the morphism induced by \(u\),
there is an integer \(n\) such that the morphism
\(\mathcal O_U\to v_*(\mathcal O_V)\) induced by \(d\) is an
\(S\)-deviation of order \(\leq n\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, observes that if \(X\) and \(u\) are
quasi-compact, every \(S\)-deviation of \(u\) has order \(\leq n\) for
some integer \(n\). Indeed \(Y\) is then quasi-compact and is covered by
finitely many of the opens \(V\); the identities \((\ast)\) are
equalities of sections of \(\mathcal O_Y\) over opens \(u^{-1}(U')\),
which may be checked on the members of an open covering of \(Y\), so the
largest of the corresponding integers \(n\) will do.}
\end{enumerate}

If \(d\) is an \(S\)-deviation of \(u\), we also call the pair
\(D=(u,d)\) an \(S\)-deviation, and we may write
\[
  Y\xrightarrow{D}X
  \qquad\text{or}\qquad
  Y\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{d}_{u}X.
\]
When \(d\) is the homomorphism of algebras that defines \(u\), we also
write \(u\) in place of \(D\).\footnote{Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, adds
remarks here (its 1.1.3, used in its 1.3, 1.4 and 2.1); in our
formulation: an \(S\)-deviation of order \(\leq0\) is determined by
\(d(1)\in\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)\). If \(\lambda\in\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)\)
and \(d\) is an \(S\)-deviation of \(u\) (of order \(\leq n\)), so is
\(\lambda d:f\mapsto\lambda d(f)\), since
\((\operatorname{ad}f)(\lambda d)=\lambda\,(\operatorname{ad}f)(d)\);
thus the \(S\)-deviations of \(u\) (of order \(\leq n\)) form a
\(\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)\)-module. As the defining conditions are local
on \(Y\), the \(S\)-deviations of the restrictions \(u|_V\), \(V\) open
in \(Y\), form a sheaf of \(\mathcal O_Y\)-modules on \(Y\); by
adjunction it is a subsheaf of
\(\mathcal Hom_{p_Y^{-1}(\mathcal O_S)}(u^{-1}(\mathcal O_X),\mathcal O_Y)\).}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:1.2}
Consider two \(S\)-deviations \(D=(u,d)\) and \(E=(v,e)\):
\[
  Z\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{e}_{v}Y
  \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{d}_{u}X.
\]
As \(U\) ranges over the open subsets of \(X\), the composites
\[
 \Gamma(U,\mathcal O_X)
 \xrightarrow{d(U)}
 \Gamma(u^{-1}U,\mathcal O_Y)
 \xrightarrow{e(u^{-1}U)}
 \Gamma(v^{-1}u^{-1}U,\mathcal O_Z)
\]
define an \(S\)-deviation of \(uv\), denoted by \(de\). If \(d\) has
order \(\leq m\) and \(e\) has order \(\leq n\), then \(de\) has order
\(\leq m+n\). We also write \(D\circ E=(uv,de)\), and call
\(D\circ E\), or \(DE\), the \emph{composite \(S\)-deviation}. When
\(d\) is the homomorphism of algebras defining \(u\) (\(D=u\) with the
convention above), we also say that \(DE\) is the \emph{image of \(E\)
under \(u\)}.

The map \((D,E)\mapsto D\circ E\) allows us to speak from now on of the
\emph{category of \(S\)-deviations}, whose objects are \(S\)-schemes
and whose morphisms are \(S\)-deviations.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.2.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:1.2.1}
Suppose, for example, that \(Y\) is equal to
\(I_Z=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_Z[T]/(T^2)\), and that \(v\) is the
section defined by the homomorphism of algebras from
\(\mathcal O_Z[T]/(T^2)\) to \(\mathcal O_Z\) which vanishes on the class
\(t\) of \(T\) modulo \(T^2\). We may then take for \(e\) the morphism of
\(\mathcal O_Z\)-modules which vanishes on the unit section of
\(\mathcal O_Z[T]/(T^2)\) and sends \(t\) to the unit section of
\(\mathcal O_Z\). If we set \(D=u\) and \(w=uv\), then \(de\) is simply
an \(S\)-derivation of \(\mathcal O_X\) into \(w_*(\mathcal O_Z)\); for
every \(S\)-morphism \(w:Z\to X\) we thus obtain a one-to-one
correspondence between the \(S\)-derivations of \(\mathcal O_X\) into
\(w_*(\mathcal O_Z)\) and the \(S\)-deviations of \(w\) of the form
\(u\circ E\), where \(u\) ranges over the morphisms from \(I_Z\) to
\(X\) extending \(w\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, details this
paragraph as a definition and a lemma (its 1.2.0 and 1.2.1). In our
formulation: write \(\bar f\) for the image in \(w_*(\mathcal O_Z)\) of a
local section \(f\) of \(\mathcal O_X\). An \(S\)-derivation is a
\(p^{-1}(\mathcal O_S)\)-linear map \(\delta\) with
\(\delta(fg)=\bar f\delta(g)+\bar g\delta(f)\); then \(\delta(1)=0\),
and \((\operatorname{ad}f)(\delta)\) is the \(\mathcal O_X\)-linear map
\(g\mapsto-\bar g\,\delta(f)\), so \(\delta\) is an \(S\)-deviation of
\(w\) of order \(\leq1\). The \(S\)-derivations form a
\(\Gamma(Z,\mathcal O_Z)\)-module. As \(I_Z\) and \(Z\) have the same
underlying space, an \(S\)-morphism \(u:I_Z\to X\) with \(uv=w\) amounts
to a \(p^{-1}(\mathcal O_S)\)-linear map
\(f\mapsto\bar f+\delta(f)t\) into \(w_*(\mathcal O_Z[T]/(T^2))\); since
\(t^2=0\), it is multiplicative if and only if \(\delta\) is an
\(S\)-derivation, and then \(de=\delta\).}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.2.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:1.2.2}
If \(d\) is an \(S\)-deviation of \(u\), then \(d\) is plainly an
\(S'\)-deviation of \(u\) for every morphism \(s:S\to S'\). On the other
hand, let \(t:T\to S\) be a morphism with target \(S\), let
\(u_T:Y_T\to X_T\) be obtained from \(u\) by base change, and let
\(t_Y\), \(t_X\) be the canonical projections of \(Y_T\), \(X_T\) to
\(Y\), \(X\). There exists a unique \(T\)-deviation of \(u_T\), denoted
\(d_T\) or \(d\times T\), which satisfies the equality
\(t_Xd_T=dt_Y\).\footnote{Translator's note: the composites are taken
as in 1.2, so the condition says that \(d_T\) sends the pull-back to
\(X_T\) of a local section \(f\) of \(\mathcal O_X\) to the pull-back to
\(Y_T\) of \(d(f)\). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, describes \(d_T\) explicitly; in our
formulation: if \(V\subset S\), \(U\subset X\) and \(U'\subset T\) are
affine opens, \(U\) and \(U'\) lying over \(V\), then
\(\mathcal O_{X_T}(U\times_VU')
=\mathcal O_X(U)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S(V)}\mathcal O_T(U')\), and on
this ring \(d_T\) is \(d(U)\otimes\mathrm{id}\) followed by the
canonical map to \(\mathcal O_{Y_T}(u^{-1}(U)\times_VU')\). This makes
sense because \(d(U)\) is \(\mathcal O_S(V)\)-linear, and it is forced
by the condition above and the \(\mathcal O_T\)-linearity of \(d_T\);
these local maps glue.}
If \(D=(u,d)\), we also write \(D_T=(u_T,d_T)\), and say that \(d_T\)
and \(D_T\) are obtained from \(d\) and \(D\) by base change.

For example, let \(u:Y\to X\) and \(v:Z\to T\) be two \(S\)-morphisms,
and let \(d\) and \(e\) be \(S\)-deviations of \(u\) and \(v\). We denote
by \(d\times e\) (the \emph{product of \(d\) and \(e\)}) the
\(S\)-deviation of \(u\times v\) equal to
\[
  d_T\circ e_Y=e_X\circ d_Z.
\]
If \(D=(u,d)\) and \(E=(v,e)\), we also write
\[
  D\times E=(u\times v,d\times e).
\]
\footnote{Translator's note: the French sentence at this point prints
\(E=(v,d)\); the established notation is \(E=(v,e)\).}
