
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.3.5.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.3.5}
Consider now three pseudocompact \(A\)-modules \(L\), \(M\), and \(N\),
\(N\) being of finite length. Let \(\psi\) be an element of the set
\(\operatorname{Hom}_c\bigl(L,\operatorname{Hom}_c(M,N)\bigr)\) of
continuous \(A\)-linear maps from \(L\) to the module
\(\operatorname{Hom}_c(M,N)\) endowed with the discrete topology. Such a
\(\psi\) defines a continuous bilinear map
\[
  \psi':L\times M\longrightarrow N,
  \qquad
  (\ell,m)\longmapsto\psi(\ell)(m).
\]
One thus obtains another characterization of
\(\operatorname{Hom}_c(L\widehat{\otimes}_A M,N)\), which we shall use
when \(M\) is the projective limit of a filtered projective system
\((M_i)\) of pseudocompact \(A\)-modules. There are then natural
isomorphisms
\begin{align*}
  \operatorname{Hom}_c
    \left(L\widehat{\otimes}_A\varprojlim_iM_i,N\right)
  &\simeq
  \operatorname{Hom}_c
    \left(L,\operatorname{Hom}_c(\varprojlim_iM_i,N)\right)
  \simeq
  \operatorname{Hom}_c
    \left(L,\varinjlim_i\operatorname{Hom}_c(M_i,N)\right)
  \\
  &\simeq
  \varinjlim_i
  \operatorname{Hom}_c
    \bigl(L,\operatorname{Hom}_c(M_i,N)\bigr)
  \simeq
  \varinjlim_i
  \operatorname{Hom}_c
    \bigl(L\widehat{\otimes}_A M_i,N\bigr)
  \\
  &\simeq
  \operatorname{Hom}_c
    \left(\varprojlim_i
      (L\widehat{\otimes}_A M_i),N\right),
\end{align*}
which allow one to identify \(L\widehat{\otimes}_A(\varprojlim_iM_i)\)
with \(\varprojlim_i(L\widehat{\otimes}_A M_i)\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, details these isomorphisms. The first and the fourth are the
characterization just obtained, the second and the last come from (ii) of the Addition in 0.3. For the
third: the modules \(\operatorname{Hom}_c(M_i,N)\) are annihilated by the annihilator \(\mathfrak l\) of
\(N\), an open ideal; a continuous linear map from \(L\) to a discrete module annihilated by
\(\mathfrak l\) factors through a quotient of \(L\) of finite length annihilated by \(\mathfrak l\), which
is finitely presented over the Artinian ring \(A/\mathfrak l\); and \(\operatorname{Hom}\) from a finitely
presented module commutes with filtered inductive limits.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.3.6.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.3.6}
In particular, \emph{the completed tensor product commutes with
infinite products}.\footnote{Translator's note: an infinite product is the filtered projective limit of
its finite partial products, and the completed tensor product, being additive, commutes with finite
products; the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, notes this.} For example,
since \(A\) is the product of its local components \(A_{\mathfrak m}\)
(\S~\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.1}{0.1}), every pseudocompact \(A\)-module
\[
  M\simeq A\widehat{\otimes}_A M
\]
identifies with the product of the modules
\[
  M_{\mathfrak m}
  =
  A_{\mathfrak m}\widehat{\otimes}_A M
\]
(the \emph{local components} of \(M\)).

Likewise, let \(M\) and \(N\) be two pseudocompact \(A\)-modules, and
consider the map
\[
  \varphi:
  M^\dagger\otimes_A N^\dagger
  \longrightarrow
  (M\widehat{\otimes}_A N)^\dagger
  \qquad(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.2.2}{0.2.2})
\]
which sends an element \(f\otimes g\) of \(M^\dagger\otimes_AN^\dagger\)
to the map
\[
  m\widehat{\otimes}n\longmapsto f(m)g(n)
\]
from \(M\widehat{\otimes}_A N\) to \(A\). This map \(\varphi\) is
bijective when \(M\) is isomorphic to \(A\). Since the functors
\(M\mapsto M^\dagger\otimes_A N^\dagger\) and
\(M\mapsto(M\widehat{\otimes}_A N)^\dagger\) transform a direct product
into a direct sum when \(A\) is Artinian,\footnote{Translator's note: \(A\) being discrete, a continuous
linear map from a product to \(A\) factors through a finite partial product, so that
\((\prod_iM_i)^\dagger=\bigoplus_iM_i^\dagger\); together with the commutation of the completed tensor
product with products, this gives the assertion for both functors. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, gives this reason.} one sees that \(\varphi\)
is an isomorphism whenever \(A\) is Artinian and \(M\) is topologically
free (or, more generally, projective).

\medskip\noindent
\phantomsection\label{sga3:VIIB:0.3.6.A}%
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
In the same way, if \(A\) is Artinian, \(M\) and \(Q\) are pseudocompact \(A\)-modules with \(Q\)
projective, and \(N\) is a pseudocompact \(A\)-module of finite length, the natural maps
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_c(M,Q)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_A(Q^\dagger,M^\dagger),
  \quad u\longmapsto(\lambda\mapsto\lambda\circ u),
\]
\[
  Q^\dagger\otimes_AN\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_c(Q,N),
  \quad\lambda\otimes n\longmapsto(x\mapsto\lambda(x)n),
\]
are bijective. They are when \(Q=A\); in \(Q\), both sides of the first transform a product into a
product, and both sides of the second transform it into a direct sum, a continuous linear map from a
product to a discrete module factoring through a finite partial product; and a projective \(Q\) is a
direct factor of a product of copies of \(A\) (0.2.1).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.3.7.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.3.7}
For every closed maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) of \(A\), the functor
\[
  M\longmapsto A_{\mathfrak m}\widehat{\otimes}_A M
\]
is plainly exact. Since every projective pseudocompact \(A\)-module
\(P\) is a product of modules of the form \(A_{\mathfrak m}\), it
follows that the functor
\[
  M\longmapsto P\widehat{\otimes}_A M
\]
is exact when \(P\) is projective. The converse is true:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.3.7-proposition}
\textit{Let \(A\) be a pseudocompact ring and \(P\) a pseudocompact
\(A\)-module. The functor \(M\mapsto P\widehat{\otimes}_A M\) is exact
if and only if \(P\) is projective.}

Since \(P\widehat{\otimes}_A M\) is the product of its local components
\[
  (P\widehat{\otimes}_A M)_{\mathfrak m}
  \simeq
  P_{\mathfrak m}
    \widehat{\otimes}_{A_{\mathfrak m}}M_{\mathfrak m},
\]
one is reduced to the case in which the ring \(A\) is local. We shall
then prove that \(P\) is \emph{topologically free}.\footnote{Translator's note: hence \(P\) is projective
if and only if each local component \(P_{\mathfrak m}\) is a topologically free
\(A_{\mathfrak m}\)-module; the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, states this
as a third equivalent condition.} For this, let \(\mathfrak m\) be the
maximal ideal of \(A\); then \(P/\overline{\mathfrak mP}\) is a linearly
compact vector space over \(A/\mathfrak m\), hence a product of copies
of \(A/\mathfrak m\). There is therefore a family \((A_i)_{i\in I}\) of
copies of \(A\) and an isomorphism
\[
  \phi:
  \prod_{i\in I}(A_i/\mathfrak m)
  \xrightarrow{\sim}
  P/\overline{\mathfrak mP}.
\]
Since \(\prod_{i\in I}A_i\) is projective, there is a commutative
square
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIB:0.3.7-projective-lift-square}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viib_15_95/diag_082}
\end{center}
where \(p\) and \(q\) denote the canonical projections. Applying
Nakayama's lemma to \(\operatorname{Coker}\psi\), and noting that
\[
  (A/\mathfrak m)\widehat{\otimes}_A\psi
\]
is none other than \(\phi\), one sees that \(\psi\) is
surjective.\footnote{Translator's note: this part of the argument does not use the exactness of
\(M\mapsto P\widehat\otimes_AM\). So every pseudocompact module over a local pseudocompact ring is a
quotient of a topologically free module; over an arbitrary \(A\), the same follows by taking the product
over the local components, each \(A_{\mathfrak m}\) being a direct factor of \(A\). The Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, notes this consequence, which is used in 1.4.3.}

Setting
\[
  B=\prod_{i\in I}A_i
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  N=\ker\psi,
\]
one has the following commutative and exact diagram:

The snake lemma, applied to the first two rows, then shows that the
morphism
\[
  (A/\mathfrak m)\widehat{\otimes}_A N
  \longrightarrow
  (A/\mathfrak m)\widehat{\otimes}_A B
\]
is a monomorphism. Since \(\phi\) is an isomorphism and the last row is
exact, \((A/\mathfrak m)\widehat{\otimes}_A N\) is zero; hence \(N=0\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.3}{0.3.3}) and \(\psi\) is an isomorphism.\footnote{Translator's note: the
same argument, with the Nakayama lemma for a nilpotent ideal and the vanishing of
\(\operatorname{Tor}_1\), shows that a flat module over an Artinian local ring is free (cf.\
N.~Bourbaki, \emph{Alg\`ebre commutative}, Chap.~II, \S~3, no.~2, Cor.~2 of Prop.~5); hence flat modules
over an Artinian ring are projective. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B},
notes this.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.3.8. Corollary.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.3.8}
\textit{Let \(A\) be a complete Noetherian local ring and \(P\) a
pseudocompact \(A\)-module. Then \(P\) is topologically free if and
only if \(P\) is flat over \(A\).}

Indeed, the canonical map
\[
  M\otimes_A P\longrightarrow M\widehat{\otimes}_A P
\]
is bijective when \(M=A\), hence when \(M\) is Noetherian (take a
finite presentation of \(M\) and use the right exactness of the tensor
product and of the completed tensor product).

Now \(P\) is flat if and only if
\[
  M\longmapsto M\otimes_A P
\]
is exact on Noetherian modules. Likewise, we saw in the proof of the
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.7}{proposition of 0.3.7}\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has
``la proposition 0.3.5''; the proposition is that of 0.3.7. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, corrects the number.} that \(P\) is topologically free if the sequence
\[
  0\longrightarrow
  \mathfrak m\widehat{\otimes}_A P
  \longrightarrow
  A\widehat{\otimes}_A P
  \longrightarrow
  (A/\mathfrak m)\widehat{\otimes}_A P
  \longrightarrow0
\]
is exact. Thus \(P\) is topologically free if and only if the functor
\[
  M\longmapsto M\widehat{\otimes}_A P
\]
is exact on Noetherian modules. The corollary therefore follows from
the equality
\[
  M\otimes_A P=M\widehat{\otimes}_A P
\]
above.
