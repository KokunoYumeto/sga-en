\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation). \emph{Pointed cocommutative
Hopf algebras over a field.} Let \(k\) be a field and \(C\) a \(k\)-coalgebra,
and let \(A=C^*\) be the dual profinite algebra, as in the preceding
Addition, so that \(C=A^\dagger\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.2.2}{0.2.2})}; \(D\mapsto D^\perp\) is a bijection
from the subcoalgebras of \(C\) onto the closed two-sided ideals of \(A\),
the simple subcoalgebras, which are finite-dimensional, corresponding to
the open maximal ideals. One says that \(C\) is \emph{pointed} if its
simple subcoalgebras all have dimension \(1\), that is, if
\(A/\mathfrak m=k\) for every open maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\). If \(C\) is
cocommutative, \(A\) is commutative and is the product of its local
components \(A_{\mathfrak m}\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.1.1}{0.1.1})}, so that
\(C=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak m}A_{\mathfrak m}^\dagger\); the summand
\(A_{\mathfrak m}^\dagger\) is the largest subcoalgebra of \(C\) whose only
simple subcoalgebra is \(\mathfrak m^\perp\), the \emph{irreducible component}
of \(\mathfrak m^\perp\).

\emph{Let \(G\) be a formal \(k\)-group all of whose residue fields are equal
to \(k\). Then \(G_e\) is the constant group defined by \(M=G(k)\), the
projection \(p\colon G\to G_e\) has a section which is a homomorphism, so
that \(G=G_0\rtimes G_e\), and \(\mathbf H(G)\) is the semidirect product of
\(\mathbf H(G_0)\) by the group algebra \(kM\)
\textup{(cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.4.B}{2.4.B})}. Equivalently, if \(H\) is a pointed cocommutative Hopf
algebra over \(k\), \(H_1\) the irreducible component of \(k\cdot1_H\), and \(M\)
the group of the \(x\in H\) with \(\varepsilon(x)=1\) and
\(\Delta(x)=x\otimes x\), then \(H\) is the semidirect product of \(H_1\) by
\(kM\).} Indeed, \(G_e\) has the points of \(G\), with residue fields \(k\)
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.6}{1.6})}, and the residue maps \(\mathcal O_{G,g}\to k\) define a section
\(s\colon G_e\to G\) of \(p\). The morphisms \(s\circ m_{G_e}\) and
\(m_G\circ(s\times s)\) from \(G_e\times G_e\) to \(G\) agree on points, since
\(p\) is bijective on points; as \(G_e\times G_e\) is a sum of copies of
\(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\) and a morphism \(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\to G\) is
determined by its image point, whose residue field is \(k\), they are equal:
\(s\) is a homomorphism. Since \(G_0\) is infinitesimal, \(p\) induces a
bijection \(G(k)\to G_e(k)\), with inverse induced by \(s\); hence
\(G_e=M_k\), \(\mathbf H(G_e)=kM\) (Addition in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.5.A}{2.5}), and
\(G\simeq G_0\times G_e\) as formal varieties, by \((x,y)\mapsto x\,s(y)\), so
that the product of \(\mathbf H(G)\) induces an isomorphism of coalgebras
\(\mathbf H(G_0)\otimes kM\to\mathbf H(G)\). For the second form, take for \(G\)
the formal group with \(\mathbf H(G)=H\), whose affine algebra is \(H^*\)
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.2.2}{2.2.2})}: \(H\) is pointed if and only if the residue fields of
\(G\) are equal to \(k\); \(1_H\) is the counit of \(H^*\), that is, the unit
point of \(G\), whose local algebra is the affine algebra of \(G_0\)
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.5.1}{2.5.1})}, so that \(H_1=\mathbf H(G_0)\); and the elements of \(M\) are the
points of \(G\) with values in \(k\).

The re-edition notes that this result, implicit in 2.5.2, was obtained
independently by B.~Kostant (see M.~E.~Sweedler, \emph{Hopf algebras},
Benjamin, 1969, Preface), and that with Cartier's theorem 3.3 below
(P.~Cartier, \emph{Groupes alg\'ebriques et groupes formels}, in
\emph{Colloque sur la th\'eorie des groupes alg\'ebriques}, Bruxelles,
1962, Gauthier-Villars, 1962, pp.~87--111, \S~12, Th.~3), also obtained by
Kostant, it is often called the \emph{Cartier--Gabriel--Kostant theorem}.
R.~G.~Heyneman and M.~E.~Sweedler extended the second form to pointed
Hopf algebras that are the direct sum of their irreducible components, not
necessarily cocommutative (\emph{Affine Hopf algebras~I}, J.~Algebra
\textbf{13} (1969), Th.~3.5.8).
