\noindent\textbf{Theorem 1.4.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.4.thm}
{\itshape Let \(k\) be a pseudocompact ring and let
\[
  d_0,d_1:X_1\rightrightarrows X
\]
be an equivalence pair of \(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\)
\textup{(V, 2.b)} such that \(d_1\) is topologically flat. Then the
canonical projection from \(X\) onto
\(X/X_1\) \(({}=\operatorname{Coker}(d_0,d_1))\) is surjective and
topologically flat, and the morphism
\[
  X_1\longrightarrow X\times_{X/X_1}X
\]
with components \(d_0\) and \(d_1\) is an isomorphism.}\footnote{Translator's note: consequently, if
\(X\) is topologically flat over \(k\), so is \(X/X_1\), by 1.3.3~(ii) applied to
\(X\to X/X_1\to\operatorname{Spf}k\). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, adds
this as a second assertion of the theorem.}

The proof of this theorem occupies paragraphs \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.4.1}{1.4.1},
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.4.2}{1.4.2}, and \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.4.3}{1.4.3}:

\subsubsection*{1.4.1}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.4.1}

We first show that we may reduce to the case in which \(X\) has only
one point. Since we are dealing with an equivalence pair, one sees,
as in Expos\'e V, \S~3.e), that an equivalence relation is defined on
the underlying set of \(X\) by declaring two points \(x,y\) to be
equivalent if there exists a point \(z\in X_1\) such that
\[
  d_0(z)=y
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  d_1(z)=x.
\]
We may plainly suppose, without loss of generality, that \(X\)
contains a single equivalence class for this relation; in other words,
that \(X/X_1\) has only one point \textup{(see the construction of
\(X/X_1\) given in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2}{1.2})}.\footnote{Translator's note: \(X\) then lies over a
single point of \(\operatorname{Spf}k\), so that one may also replace \(k\) by the corresponding local
component and suppose \(k\) local, as in 1.4.3. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, notes this.}

In this case, let \(x\) be any point of \(X\), and let \(U\) be the
formal variety having \(x\) as its only point and the same local ring
as \(X\) at \(x\). As in Expos\'e V, \S~6, one then sees that the
equivalence relation induced by \((d_0,d_1)\) on \(U\) still satisfies
the hypotheses of the theorem and that it is enough to prove the
theorem for this latter equivalence relation \textup{(\(U\) is a
``quasi-section'')}.

Let us briefly recall the principle of the proof given in
Expos\'e V, \S~6. Put
\[
  V=d_0^{-1}(U)=U\times_{i,d_0}X_1,
\]
where \(i\) is the inclusion of \(U\) into \(X\), and let \(u\) and
\(v\) be the morphisms with source \(V\) induced respectively by
\(d_0\) and \(d_1\):
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIB:1.4.1:quasisection-span}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viib_15_95/diag_090}
\end{center}
It is clear that \(u\) and \(v\) are topologically flat and that
\(u\) is surjective; since \(X\) contains a single equivalence class,
\(v\) is surjective. If \((v_0,v_1)\) is the inverse image of the
equivalence pair \((d_0,d_1)\) under \(v\) \textup{(V, 3.a))},
it follows from V, 3.c) and 3.d) that \(X/X_1\) and the quotient of
\(U\) by the equivalence relation induced by \((d_0,d_1)\) both
identify with \(\operatorname{Coker}(v_0,v_1)\). The rest is easy
(V, \S~6).

This shows, on the one hand, that \(A\) is topologically free over
\(B\) ``with basis \(f\)'', and that a pseudobasis of \(A_1\) over \(A\)
(regarded as an \(A\)-module through \(i_1\); \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.2.1}{0.2.1}) is obtained by
taking the image under \(i_0\) of a pseudobasis of \(A\) over \(B\); it
follows that the morphism
\[
  A\widehat{\otimes}_B A\longrightarrow A_1
\]
with components \(i_1\) and \(i_0\) is an isomorphism:
\[
  A\widehat{\otimes}_B A
  \simeq
  A\widehat{\otimes}_B B\widehat{\otimes}_kV
  \simeq
  A\widehat{\otimes}_kV
  \simeq
  A_1.
\]

\subsubsection*{1.4.3}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.4.3}

\begin{samepage}
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 1.4.3.}
{\itshape Let \(k\) be a local pseudocompact ring, \(A\) a local
profinite \(k\)-algebra, \(A_1\) a topologically free \(A\)-module, and
\[
  i_0:M\longrightarrow A_1
\]
a morphism of pseudocompact \(k\)-modules such that the map
\[
  A\widehat{\otimes}_kM\longrightarrow A_1,
  \qquad
  a\widehat{\otimes}m
  \longmapsto
  a\cdot i_0(m),
\]
is surjective. There then exist a topologically free \(k\)-module
\(V\) and a morphism of pseudocompact \(k\)-modules
\[
  f:V\longrightarrow M
\]
such that the map
\[
  A\widehat{\otimes}_kV\longrightarrow A_1,
  \qquad
  a\widehat{\otimes}v
  \longmapsto
  a\cdot i_0\bigl(f(v)\bigr),
\]
is bijective.}
\end{samepage}

Since every pseudocompact \(k\)-module is a quotient of a
topologically free \(k\)-module, we may suppose, without loss of
generality, that \(M\) is topologically free over \(k\); let us therefore
take for \(M\) the direct product
\[
  M=\prod_{i\in I}M_i
\]
of a family of copies \(M_i\) of \(k\). In this case,
\(A\widehat{\otimes}_kM\) is none other than the product
\[
  \prod_{i\in I}
  A\widehat{\otimes}_kM_i,
\]
each factor of which is isomorphic to \(A\), hence is projective and
indecomposable. Since the map
\[
  a\widehat{\otimes}m\longmapsto a\cdot i_0(m)
\]
is surjective and \(A_1\) is projective, the kernel of this map is a
direct factor of \(A\widehat{\otimes}_kM\); it therefore follows from
the exchange theorem \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.4}{0.3.4})} that this kernel has as a
complement a partial product
\[
  \prod_{i\in J}A\widehat{\otimes}_kM_i,
\]
where \(J\) denotes a certain subset of \(I\).
