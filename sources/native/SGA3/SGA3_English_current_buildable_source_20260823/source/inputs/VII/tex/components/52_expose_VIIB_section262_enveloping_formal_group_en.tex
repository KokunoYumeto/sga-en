
\subsubsection*{2.6.2}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:2.6.2}

Conversely, every Lie algebra \(\mathbf L\) over \(\mathbf O_k\)
defines a \(k\)-group functor: let us denote indeed by \(U(\mathbf L)\)
the functor that assigns to every \(k\)-algebra of finite length \(C\)
the enveloping algebra \(U(\mathbf L(C))\) of the Lie algebra
\(\mathbf L(C)\) over \(C\). By VII\textsubscript{A}, \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:3.2.2}{3.2.2},
\(U(\mathbf L)\) is a bialgebra over \(\mathbf O_k\) and determines a
\(k\)-group functor \(\operatorname{Spf}^{*}U(\mathbf L)\)
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.2}{2.2})}, which we shall henceforth denote by
\(\mathscr G(\mathbf L)\): \(\mathscr G(\mathbf L)(C)\) is therefore the
group of the elements \(z\in U(\mathbf L(C))\) of augmentation \(1\) such
that
\[
  \Delta_{U(\mathbf L(C))}(z)=z\otimes z.
\]

\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:2.6.2:prop}%
\emph{When \(\mathbf L\) is a Lie algebra flat over \(\mathbf O_k\)}, the
bialgebra \(U(\mathbf L)\) is flat over \(\mathbf O_k\) by the
Poincaré--Birkhoff--Witt theorem \textup{(}Bourbaki, \emph{Alg\`ebres de
Lie}, I, 2.7\textup{)};\footnote{Translator's note: in more detail, \(\mathbf L\) flat over \(\mathbf O_k\)
means that \(\mathbf L(D)=\mathbf L(C)\otimes_CD\) for every morphism \(C\to D\) of \(\operatorname{Alf}/k\),
and that \(\mathbf L(C')\) is free over each local component \(C'\) of \(C\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2.1}{1.2.1}). As
the enveloping algebra commutes with base change, \(U(\mathbf L(D))=U(\mathbf L(C))\otimes_CD\); and
\(U(\mathbf L(C'))\) is free over \(C'\) by the Poincaré--Birkhoff--Witt theorem. The Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, writes these steps out.} consequently, \emph{\(\mathscr G(\mathbf L)\) is a
formal group topologically flat over \(k\) which has \(U(\mathbf L)\) for
\(\mathbf O_k\)-bialgebra}. \emph{This group is radicial}: indeed, we
reduce directly to the case where \(k\) is Artinian
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.6}{2.6})}. Put then \(U=U(L)\) and let \(U^+\) be the
two-sided ideal of \(U\) generated by the image of \(L\). Put further
\[
\begin{aligned}
  U_0&=k\cdot1_U,\\
  &\;\;\cdots\\
  U_n&=\{u\in U\mid\Delta_U(u)-u\otimes1\in U_{n-1}\otimes U^+\}
\end{aligned}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
U_n^+=U_n\cap U^+.
\]
By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.6}{1.3.6}, it suffices to show that \(U\) is the union of the
\(U_n\). Now, if we identify \(L\) with its canonical image in \(U\),
\(L\) is evidently contained in \(U_1\). If \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) are
elements of \(L\), we therefore have
\[
  \Delta_U(x_1\cdots x_n)
  =
  (x_1\otimes1+1\otimes x_1)\cdots
  (x_n\otimes1+1\otimes x_n),
\]
which shows, by induction on \(n\), that the product \(x_1\cdots x_n\)
belongs to \(U_n\), hence that
\[
  U=\bigcup_n U_n.
\]

\par\medskip\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIB:2.6.2:prop:item:iii}%
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation). Let \(\mathbf L\) be flat over
\(\mathbf O_k\). For every object \(C\) of \(\operatorname{Alf}/k\), the
bialgebra of the \(C\)-formal group \(\mathscr G(\mathbf L)\widehat\otimes_kC\)
is \(U(\mathbf L(C))\); by 2.6~c), \(\operatorname{Lie}(\mathscr G(\mathbf L))(C)\)
is therefore the set \(\operatorname{Prim}U(\mathbf L(C))\) of the primitive
elements \(x\) of \(U(\mathbf L(C))\), those with
\(\Delta(x)=x\otimes1+1\otimes x\) \textup{(}hence \(\varepsilon(x)=0\)\textup{)}. In the
language of 2.6~a): over \(D=C[d]/(d^2)\) one has
\(U(\mathbf L(D))=U(\mathbf L(C))\oplus U(\mathbf L(C))\,d\), and an element
\(z=1+xd\) satisfies \(\varepsilon(z)=1\) and \(\Delta(z)=z\otimes z\) if and
only if \(x\) is primitive. As the image of \(\mathbf L(C)\) consists of
primitive elements, one obtains a morphism of \(\mathbf O_k\)-Lie algebras
\[
  \tau_{\mathbf L}:\mathbf L\longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Lie}\bigl(\mathscr G(\mathbf L)\bigr),
\]
induced by the canonical map of \(\mathbf L\) into \(U(\mathbf L)\); it is
through \(\tau_{\mathbf L}\) that this canonical map is composed with
\(\operatorname{Lie}h\) in 2.6.3. The re-edition states this as a third
assertion of a proposition grouping the statements of 2.6.2, and uses it
in 2.6.3 and 3.3.2; when \(k\) contains \(\mathbb Q\), \(\tau_{\mathbf L}\)
is an isomorphism (see the Addition after 3.2).
