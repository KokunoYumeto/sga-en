
\section*{4. ``Frobeniuseries''}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:4}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{4. ``Frobeniuseries''}

Fix a prime number \(p\), and let
\((\mathbf{Sch}/\mathbb F_p)\) be the category of schemes of
characteristic \(p\), that is, schemes over the prime field
\(\mathbb F_p\). Following the general conventions of this seminar,
we identify \((\mathbf{Sch}/\mathbb F_p)\) with a subcategory of
\((\mathbf{Sch}/\mathbb F_p)^\wedge\) by means of the functor \(h\)
of \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:1-1}{Expos\'e I, 1.1}. We likewise use the isomorphism
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}(h_X,F)\xrightarrow{\sim}F(X)
\]
defined there to identify these sets whenever \(X\) is an
\(\mathbb F_p\)-scheme and \(F\) is an object of
\((\mathbf{Sch}/\mathbb F_p)^\wedge\). If \(T\) is an
\(\mathbb F_p\)-scheme, a \(T\)-functor is a morphism \(q:F\to T\) of
\((\mathbf{Sch}/\mathbb F_p)^\wedge\) with target \(T\); for every
\(T\)-scheme \(r:X\to T\), the set of morphisms \(s:X\to F\) such that
\(q\circ s=r\) will then be denoted
\[
  q(r),\qquad q(X/T),\qquad F(r),\qquad\text{or}\qquad F(X/T)
\]
(or even \(F(X)\) when no confusion with \(\operatorname{Hom}(h_X,F)\) is
possible).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{4.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:4.1}
For every scheme \(S\) of characteristic \(p\), let
\(\operatorname{fr}(S)\), or simply \(\operatorname{fr}\), denote the
endomorphism of \(S\) which is the identity on the underlying
topological space and which sends a section \(x\) of \(\mathcal O_S\)
over an open subset to \(x^p\).\footnote{Translator's note: as the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, points out,
\(\operatorname{fr}(S)\) is nowadays called the \emph{absolute} Frobenius
morphism of \(S\), as opposed to the \emph{relative} Frobenius morphism
\(\operatorname{Fr}(X/S)\) introduced below.}

The assignment
\[
  \operatorname{fr}:S\longmapsto\operatorname{fr}(S)
\]
is an endomorphism of the identity functor of
\((\mathbf{Sch}/\mathbb F_p)\).
This has the following consequences. If \(E\) is an
\(\mathbb F_p\)-functor, the assignment which sends every
\(\mathbb F_p\)-scheme \(S\) to the endomorphism
\(E(\operatorname{fr}(S))\) of \(E(S)\) is a functorial endomorphism
of \(E\), denoted \(\operatorname{fr}(E)\) or simply
\(\operatorname{fr}\). This notation is compatible with the
identification of \((\mathbf{Sch}/\mathbb F_p)\) with a subcategory
of \((\mathbf{Sch}/\mathbb F_p)^\wedge\). Moreover,
\(E\mapsto\operatorname{fr}(E)\) is itself an endomorphism of the
identity functor of \((\mathbf{Sch}/\mathbb F_p)^\wedge\), again
denoted \(\operatorname{fr}\).

For every \(\mathbb F_p\)-scheme \(S\) and every \(S\)-functor
\(q:X\to S\), let \(X^{(p/S)}\), or \(X^{(p)}\), denote the inverse
image \(S\times_{\operatorname{fr},q}X\) of \(X\) under the base change
\(\operatorname{fr}(S)\). The commutative square
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:4.1-absolute-frobenius-square}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_035_absolute_frobenius_square}
\end{center}
induces an \(S\)-morphism \(\operatorname{Fr}(X/S)\), or \(\operatorname{Fr}\),
from \(X\) to \(X^{(p/S)}\) such that
\[
  \operatorname{fr}(X)
  =
  \operatorname{pr}_2\circ\operatorname{Fr}(X/S).
\]
We call \(\operatorname{Fr}(X/S)\) the \emph{Frobenius morphism of
\(X\) relative to \(S\)}. Plainly the assignment
\[
  \operatorname{Fr}:X\longmapsto\operatorname{Fr}(X/S)
\]
is a functorial homomorphism.

By the definitions, for every \(S\)-scheme \(r:T\to S\), the map
\(\operatorname{Fr}(X/S)(r)\) can therefore be characterized by the
commutativity of the triangle\footnote{Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, details
this; in our words: the horizontal bijection comes from the definition
of \(X^{(p/S)}\) as a fibre product, an \(S\)-morphism
\(T\to X^{(p/S)}\) being the same as a morphism \(T\to X\) above
\(\operatorname{fr}(S)\circ r\), through \(\operatorname{pr}_2\); and
\(\operatorname{fr}(S)\circ r=r\circ\operatorname{fr}(T)\) because
\(\operatorname{fr}\) is an endomorphism of the identity functor. The
triangle commutes since, for \(\varphi\in X(r)\),
\(\operatorname{pr}_2\circ\operatorname{Fr}(X/S)\circ\varphi
=\operatorname{fr}(X)\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ\operatorname{fr}(T)\).}
\begin{center}
\((\dagger)\qquad\)%
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
X^{(p/S)}(r)
  \arrow[r,"\sim"]
&
X(\operatorname{fr}(S)\circ r)=X(r\circ\operatorname{fr}(T))
\\
X(r)
  \arrow[u,"\operatorname{Fr}(X/S)(r)"]
  \arrow[ur,"X(\operatorname{fr}(T))"']
&
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}

For example, if \(X\) is the subscheme of \(S\) defined by a
quasi-coherent ideal \(\mathcal I\), then \(X^{(p)}\) is the subscheme
defined by the ideal \(\mathcal I^{\{p\}}\) generated by the \(p\)-th
powers of local sections of \(\mathcal I\). Moreover,
\(\operatorname{Fr}(X/S)\) is the canonical immersion
\[
  \operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal O_S/\mathcal I)
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal O_S/\mathcal I^{\{p\}}).
\]
\footnote{The French source prints
\(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal O_X/\mathcal I)\) for the source of
this immersion. Since \(X\subset S\) is defined by \(\mathcal I\), the
intended source is
\(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal O_S/\mathcal I)=X\), as used here.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{4.1.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:4.1.1}
Consider now a base change \(t:T\to S\). Since the square
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
T
  \arrow[r,"\operatorname{fr}(T)"]
  \arrow[d,"t"']
&
T
  \arrow[d,"t"]
\\
S
  \arrow[r,"\operatorname{fr}(S)"]
&
S
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
is commutative, the inverse image of \(X\times_{q,t}T\) under
\(\operatorname{fr}(T)\) identifies with the inverse image of
\(S\times_{\operatorname{fr},q}X\) under \(t\); in other words, there is
a canonical isomorphism from \(X_T^{(p/T)}\) onto
\(\bigl(X^{(p/S)}\bigr)_T\). Clearly, under this identification,
\(\operatorname{Fr}(X_T/T)\) identifies with the inverse image
\(\operatorname{Fr}(X/S)_T\) of \(\operatorname{Fr}(X/S)\).

In particular, if \(S\) is the spectrum of the prime field
\(\mathbb F_p\), then \(X^{(p/S)}\) is equal to \(X\) and
\(\operatorname{Fr}(X/S)\) to \(\operatorname{fr}(X)\). Consequently,
\(X_T^{(p/T)}\) identifies with \(X_T\), and
\(\operatorname{Fr}(X_T/T)\) with \(\operatorname{fr}(X)_T\). Let, for
example, \(E\) be a set and \(E_T\) the constant \(T\)-scheme of type
\(E\); then
\[
  E_T^{(p/T)}\simeq E_T,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{Fr}(E_T/T)\simeq\operatorname{id}_{E_T}.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{4.1.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:4.1.2}
The functor \(X\mapsto X^{(p/S)}\) plainly commutes with products; it
therefore sends an \(S\)-group \(G\) to an \(S\)-group \(G^{(p/S)}\);
moreover, since \(\operatorname{Fr}\) is a functorial homomorphism,
\(\operatorname{Fr}(G/S)\) is a homomorphism of \(S\)-groups. We denote
by \(\operatorname{Fr}G\) the kernel of this homomorphism: if
\(r:T\to S\) is a scheme over \(S\), it follows from \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:4.1}{4.1} that the
value of \(\operatorname{Fr}G\) at \(r\) is the kernel of the
homomorphism
\[
  G(\operatorname{fr}(T)):
  G(r)\longrightarrow G(r\circ\operatorname{fr}(T)).
\]
For example, when \(T\) is the scheme \(I_R\) of dual numbers over an
\(S\)-scheme \(R\), \(\operatorname{fr}(I_R)\) factors as follows:
\[
  I_R\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{can.}\ }R
  \xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{fr}(R)\ }R
  \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{can.}\ }I_R.
\]
This shows that \((\operatorname{Fr}G)(I_R)\) contains the kernel of the
morphism \(G(I_R)\to G(R)\), and that
\[
  \operatorname{Lie}(G/S)
  =
  \operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{Fr}G/S).
\]
