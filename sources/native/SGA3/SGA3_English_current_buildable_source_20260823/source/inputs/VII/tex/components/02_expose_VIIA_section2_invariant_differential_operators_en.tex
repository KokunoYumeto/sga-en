
\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIA:nde:2}\section*{2. Invariant Differential Operators on Group Schemes}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:2}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{2. Invariant Differential Operators on Group Schemes}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{2.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:2.1}
Let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group scheme. We denote by \(\varepsilon\), or
\(\varepsilon_G:S\to G\), the unit section of \(G\), and by \(U(G)\) the
\(\Gamma(\mathcal O_S)\)-module of \(S\)-deviations of
\(\varepsilon_G\) (or \(S\)-deviations of the origin)
(cf.~\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.1}{1.1}). If \(d\) and \(e\) are two elements of \(U(G)\), then
\(d\times e\) is an \(S\)-deviation of
\[
  \varepsilon\times\varepsilon:
  S\simeq S\times S\longrightarrow G\times G.
\]
The image of \(d\times e\) under the multiplication morphism
\(m:G\times G\to G\) (cf.~\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.2}{1.2}) will be called the \emph{product} of
\(d\) and \(e\) and denoted \(d\cdot e\). The
\(\Gamma(\mathcal O_S)\)-module \(U(G)\) is thereby made into an
associative \(\Gamma(\mathcal O_S)\)-algebra with unit element
\(\varepsilon_G\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.1}{1.1}). We call \(U(G)\) the \emph{infinitesimal
algebra} of \(G\).\footnote{Translator's note: \(U(G)\) is now usually
called the algebra of distributions of \(G\) (at the origin); the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, refers to
M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel, \textit{Groupes alg\'ebriques}, tome~I,
Masson and North-Holland, 1970, II, \S4, 6.1, and to J.~C.~Jantzen,
\textit{Representations of Algebraic Groups}, second edition, American
Mathematical Society, 2003, I, 7.7.} As \(T\) ranges over schemes above \(S\), the
infinitesimal algebra \(U(G_T)\) of the \(T\)-group \(G\times T\)
plainly varies contravariantly with \(T\); thus one may speak of the
\emph{infinitesimal-algebra functor}. In particular, as \(T\) ranges
over the open subsets of \(S\), the functor \(T\mapsto U(G_T)\) is a
sheaf of algebras, which we denote by \(\mathcal U(G)\) and call the
\emph{sheaf of infinitesimal algebras of \(G\)}.\footnote{Translator's
note: as the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A},
points out, this follows from the local character of deviations (our
last note to 1.1): for \(T\) open in \(S\), \(U(G_T)\) is the module of
\(S\)-deviations of the restriction of \(\varepsilon\) to \(T\).}

The algebra \(U(G)\) is also covariantly functorial in \(G\): if
\(u:G\to H\) is a homomorphism of \(S\)-groups and \(d\) is an
\(S\)-deviation of \(\varepsilon_G\), its image under \(u\) is an
element
\[
  U(u)(d)=u d
\]
of \(U(H)\). The map \(U(u):U(G)\to U(H)\) so defined is plainly a
homomorphism of \(\Gamma(\mathcal O_S)\)-algebras. Likewise one obtains
a homomorphism
\(\mathcal U(u):\mathcal U(G)\to\mathcal U(H)\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{2.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:2.2}
Now let \(d\) be an \(S\)-deviation of the origin of \(G\), and let
\(d\times G\) be the \(S\)-deviation of
\(\varepsilon\times G:G\simeq S\times G\to G\times G\) obtained from
\(d\) by base change. The image of \(d\times G\) under the
multiplication morphism \(m:G\times G\to G\) is a differential operator
\(d^G\) of \(G\) over \(S\). Moreover, the map \(d\mapsto d^G\) is
plainly \(\Gamma(\mathcal O_S)\)-linear, and the ``commutative''
diagram
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:2.2-right-translation-associativity}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_006_right_translation_associativity}
\end{center}
shows that \((d\cdot e)^G=d^G\cdot e^G\): the commutativity of the two
lower triangles follows from the definition of \(d^G\) and \(e^G\); on
the other hand, the composite \(S\)-deviation of \(e\times G\),
\(d\times G\times G\) and \(m\times G\) coincides with
\((d\cdot e)\times G\), so that its image under \(m\) is equal to
\((d\cdot e)^G\). We thus obtain a homomorphism, called \emph{right
translation},\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, remarks that ``left action''
would be a better name. If \(d\) is an \(S\)-derivation of the origin,
given by 1.2.1 by a morphism \(x:I_S\to G\) with \(xs=\varepsilon_G\)
(notation of 1.5), then \(d^G\) sends a local section \(\varphi\) of
\(\mathcal O_G\) to the coefficient of \(t\) in the function
\(g\mapsto\varphi(x\cdot g)\): it is the infinitesimal left
translation by \(x\), and it commutes with right translations (2.3).} from the \(\Gamma(\mathcal O_S)\)-algebra \(U(G)\) to
\(\operatorname{Dif}_{G/S}\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, puts
\(G\times d\times G\) in place of \(d\times G\times G\) in this diagram
and concludes that \((e\cdot d)^G=d^G\cdot e^G\), right translation
being an antihomomorphism (and the map of 2.4 an anti-isomorphism).
That reading multiplies operators as maps. The product of
\(\operatorname{Dif}_{G/S}\) is, however, the composition of 1.2
(1.4): \(d^G\cdot e^G\) is ``\(d^G\) followed by \(e^G\)'', the map
\(e^G\circ d^G\); and for it the 1970 statement holds. In the affine
case, with comultiplication \(f\mapsto\sum f_{(1)}\otimes f_{(2)}\) and
deviations of the origin viewed as linear forms, one has
\(d\cdot e=(d\otimes e)\circ m^*\) and
\(d^G(f)=\sum d(f_{(1)})f_{(2)}\), and coassociativity gives
\(e^G(d^G(f))=(d\cdot e)^G(f)\). The diagram commutes as printed: both
routes from the left-hand \(G\times G\) to the right-hand one send
\((a,b)\) to \((\varepsilon,ab)\), whereas \(G\times\varepsilon\times G\)
followed by \(G\times m\) is the identity.} If \(\mathcal{Dif}_{G/S}\) denotes the
sheaf \(U\mapsto\operatorname{Dif}_{G\times U/U}\) on \(S\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.4}{1.4}), one likewise defines a ``right translation'' from the sheaf
\(\mathcal U(G)\) to \(\mathcal{Dif}_{G/S}\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{2.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:2.3}
We now characterize the differential operators on \(G/S\) of the
form \(d^G\). Let \(g:S\to G\) be a section of the structural
morphism and let \(g_G\) denote right translation by \(g\), namely
\[
  g_G:
  G\simeq G\times S
  \xrightarrow{G\times g}G\times G
  \xrightarrow{m}G.
\]
For every differential operator \(D\) on \(G/S\), we then denote by
\(D^g\) the operator \(g_G^{-1}Dg_G\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.2}{1.2}).
We call \(D\) \emph{right-invariant} if for every base change
\(t:T\to S\) and every section \(g:T\to G\times T\), one has
\[
  (D_T)^g=D_T.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Lemma.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:2.3-invariance-lemma}
\textit{For every differential operator \(D\) on \(G/S\), the
following assertions are equivalent:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textit{(i)}] \textit{\(D\) is right-invariant.}
\item[\textit{(ii)}] \textit{If \(m\) is the multiplication morphism of
\(G\), then}
\[
  Dm=m(D\times G).
\]
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\((ii)\Rightarrow(i)\).
Since condition (ii) is stable under base change, it suffices to show
that (ii) implies the equality \(D^g=D\) for every section
\(g:S\to G\). This follows from the commutative diagram
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:2.3-ii-implies-i}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_007_invariance_implication_square}
\end{center}
where \(h\) is the morphism
\[
  G\simeq G\times S\xrightarrow{G\times g}G\times G,
\]
and \(m\circ h\) is right translation by \(g\).

\noindent\((i)\Rightarrow(ii)\).
Take for \(t:T\to S\) the structural morphism \(p:G\to S\), and for
the section \(g:T\to G\times T\) the diagonal morphism
\(\Delta:G\to G\times G\). Right translation \(\Delta_{G\times G}\) of
\(G\times G\) by \(\Delta\) is then the morphism from \(G\times G\) to
\(G\times G\) with components \(m\) and \(\operatorname{pr}_2\). The
equality \((D_G)^\Delta=D_G\) is then equivalent to the commutativity
of the first square of the following diagram:
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:2.3-i-implies-ii}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_008_delta_translation_square}
\end{center}
Equality (ii) therefore follows from
\(m=\operatorname{pr}_1\circ\Delta_{G\times G}\).

Consider, for example, an element \(d\) of the infinitesimal algebra
\(U(G)\). The two squares of the diagram
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:2.3-origin-deviation-invariant}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_009_invariant_operator_rectangle}
\end{center}
are then commutative. Since
\[
  m\circ(d\times G)=d^G
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  (m\times G)\circ(d\times G\times G)=d^G\times G,
\]
one also has
\[
  d^G\circ m=m\circ(d^G\times G).
\]
Thus, for every \(S\)-deviation \(d\) of the origin, \(d^G\) is a
right-invariant differential operator.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Theorem 2.4.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:2.4}
\textit{The map \(d\mapsto d^G\) is an isomorphism of the infinitesimal
algebra \(U(G)\) onto the subalgebra \(\operatorname{Dif}^{G}_{G/S}\) of
\(\operatorname{Dif}_{G/S}\) formed by the right-invariant differential
operators.}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, writes ``anti-isomorphism'' here,
for the reason explained in the note to 2.2, and adds an assertion (ii)
on left-invariant operators. In our formulation, with the product of
1.4: the image \({}^Gd\) of \(G\times d\) under \(m\) satisfies
\({}^Gd\,m=m(G\times{}^Gd)\), and \(d\mapsto{}^Gd\) is an
anti-isomorphism of \(U(G)\) onto the subalgebra of
\(\operatorname{Dif}_{G/S}\) formed by the operators \(D\) with
\(Dm=m(G\times D)\), the left-invariant ones (an isomorphism if
operators are multiplied as maps). This is the theorem applied to the
group \(G\) with the opposite multiplication \((x,y)\mapsto yx\): its
infinitesimal algebra is \(U(G)\) with the opposite product, its right
translation is \(d\mapsto{}^Gd\), and its right-invariant operators are
the left-invariant operators of \(G\).}

Let \(D\) be an arbitrary differential operator on \(G/S\), and let
\(D_0\) denote its \emph{value at the origin}, that is, the composite
deviation
\[
  S\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}G
   \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{D}_{\operatorname{id}_G}G.
\]
The right-invariant differential operator \((D_0)^G\) is obtained by
the composite
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:2.4-D0-composite}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_010_D0_composition_sequence}
\end{center}
If \(D\) is right-invariant, then \(Dm=m(D\times G)\), and hence
\[
  D=Dm(\varepsilon\times G)
   =m(D\times G)(\varepsilon\times G)
   =(D_0)^G.
\]
Thus \(d\mapsto d^G\) is surjective.

Conversely, for every \(S\)-deviation \(d\) of the origin one has the
commutative square
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:2.4-origin-value-square}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_011_origin_value_square}
\end{center}
from which
\[
  d=m(G\times\varepsilon)d
   =m(d\times G)\varepsilon
   =(d^G)_0.
\]
\textit{A fortiori}, \(d\mapsto d^G\) is injective.

As \(S\) varies, \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:2.4}{Theorem~2.4} plainly implies that right translation
\[
  \mathcal U(G)\longrightarrow\mathcal{Dif}_{G/S}
\]
is an isomorphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-algebras from \(\mathcal U(G)\)
onto the subalgebra
\[
  \mathcal{Dif}^{G}_{G/S}:U\longmapsto
  \operatorname{Dif}^{G_U}_{G_U/U}.
\]

\[
  p^*(\mathfrak p^n_{G/S})\longrightarrow\mathcal O_G,
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{2.5.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:2.5}
Let \(\gamma:G\to\operatorname{Aut}G\)\footnote{Translator's note:
here, as the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A},
specifies, \(\operatorname{Lie}G\) and \(\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{Aut}G)\)
denote, in the notation of Expos\'e~II, the
\(\Gamma(\mathcal O_S)\)-Lie algebras
\(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)(S)\) and
\(\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{Aut}_S(G)/S)(S)\)
(II~3.14 and 4.11).} be the homomorphism of group
functors which associates with an \(S\)-morphism \(g:T\to G\) the
\emph{left} translation of \(G_T\) by \(g\), that is, the composite
morphism
\[
  G_T\simeq T\times_TG_T\xrightarrow{\;g\times G_T\;}G_T\times_TG_T
  \xrightarrow{\;m_T\;}G_T.
\]
This homomorphism \(\gamma\) defines the diagram of sets
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=huge,row sep=large]
\operatorname{Lie}G
  \arrow[r,"\operatorname{Lie}\gamma"]
&
\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{Aut}G)
  \arrow[d,"\beta"]
\\
U(G)
  \arrow[r,"\delta"]
&
\operatorname{Dif}_{G/S}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
where \(\beta\) denotes the map \(u\mapsto D_u\) of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.5}{1.5}, and \(\delta\)
the right translation defined in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:2.2}{2.2}. If \(x\) is an element of
\(\operatorname{Lie}G\), that is, a morphism from \(I_S\) to \(G\) such
that \(xs=\varepsilon_G\) with the notation of \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.5}{1.5}, one has the
following commutative square, which determines the image of \(x\) under
\(\operatorname{Lie}\gamma\):
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=huge,row sep=large]
I_S\times G
  \arrow[r,"(\operatorname{Lie}\gamma)(x)"]
  \arrow[d,"x\times G"']
&
I_S\times G
  \arrow[d,"\operatorname{pr}_2"]
\\
G\times G
  \arrow[r,"m"]
&
G\,.
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:1.5}{1.5}, the image of \((\operatorname{Lie}\gamma)(x)\) under \(\beta\) is
the composite deviation
\begin{center}
{\small
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=normal]
G\simeq S\times G
  \arrow[r,shift left=.55ex,"\sigma\times G"]
  \arrow[r,shift right=.55ex,"s\times G"']
&
I_S\times G
  \arrow[r,"x\times G"]
&
G\times G
  \arrow[r,"m"]
&
G\,.
\end{tikzcd}}
\end{center}
By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:2.2}{2.2}, this composite deviation is none other than
\((x\sigma)^G\). Denoting by \(\alpha\) the map \(x\mapsto x\sigma\)
from \(\operatorname{Lie}G\) to \(U(G)\), we therefore have
\[
  \beta(\operatorname{Lie}\gamma)=\delta\alpha.
\]
In particular, \(\alpha\) is an injective homomorphism from
\(\operatorname{Lie}G\) to the Lie algebra underlying the infinitesimal
algebra \(U(G)\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, reorders this paragraph, using
right translations and left-invariant operators, and adds two remarks,
in our formulation: (a) by 1.2.1, \(\alpha\) maps \(\operatorname{Lie}G\)
bijectively onto the \(S\)-derivations of the origin, so \(\alpha\) is an
isomorphism of Lie algebras onto them; (b) as \(\alpha\) is a Lie
homomorphism into an associative algebra, it factors through the
enveloping algebra of \(\operatorname{Lie}G\), and its injectivity shows
that the canonical map from \(\operatorname{Lie}G\) to its enveloping
algebra is injective, whereas over a general ring there are Lie algebras
for which it is not (N.~Bourbaki, \textit{Groupes et alg\`ebres de Lie},
chapter~I, \S2, exercise~9).}
