
\section*{1. Formal Varieties over a Pseudocompact Ring}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{1. Formal Varieties over a Pseudocompact Ring}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.1}
To every pseudocompact ring \(A\) one may associate a formal scheme
(EGA~I, 10.4.2) as follows. Its underlying topological space is the
set \(\Upsilon(A)\) of closed maximal ideals of \(A\), endowed with the
discrete topology. For every subset
\(E\subset\Upsilon(A)\), the space of sections of the structure sheaf
over \(E\) is the Cartesian product
\[
  \prod_{\mathfrak m\in E}A_{\mathfrak m}.
\]
The formal scheme so obtained is denoted by \(\operatorname{Spf}(A)\),
the \emph{formal spectrum} of \(A\).

If \(A\) and \(B\) are two pseudocompact rings, a \emph{morphism} from
\(\operatorname{Spf}(B)\) to \(\operatorname{Spf}(A)\) consists of a
map
\[
  f:\Upsilon(B)\longrightarrow\Upsilon(A)
\]
and a family of homomorphisms
\[
  f_y^\natural:A_{f(y)}\longrightarrow B_y
  \qquad(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.1.3}{0.1.3};\ y\in\Upsilon(B)).
\]
Such a morphism induces a homomorphism from \(A\) (the product of the
\(A_x\), \(x\in\Upsilon(A)\)) to \(B\) (the product of the \(B_y\)), and
the converse is true: if \(\phi:A\to B\) is a continuous homomorphism of
algebras, the inverse image \(\phi^{-1}(\mathfrak n)\) of a closed (hence
open) maximal ideal \(\mathfrak n\) of \(B\) is an open prime ideal of
\(A\); it is therefore maximal in \(A\), and one may put
\(\mathfrak m=\phi^{-1}(\mathfrak n)=(\operatorname{Spf}\phi)(\mathfrak n)\);\footnote{Translator's note:
the 1970 text has \((\operatorname{Spf})(\mathfrak n)\).} the map \(\phi\)
then induces a homomorphism from \(A_{\mathfrak m}\) to
\(B_{\mathfrak n}\).

One sees therefore that the contravariant functor
\(A\mapsto\operatorname{Spf}(A)\) is fully faithful. Although we shall
speak here only of formal schemes of the form \(\operatorname{Spf}(A)\),
we shall use the language of formal schemes rather than that of
pseudocompact rings, so as to ground our assertions in geometric
intuition.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.2}
Let \(k\) be a pseudocompact ring. A \emph{formal variety over
\(k\)} is a formal scheme \(X\) over \(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\) which is
isomorphic to a formal \(k\)-scheme \(\operatorname{Spf}(A)\) for some
profinite \(k\)-algebra \(A\). The algebra \(A\) is then isomorphic to
the \emph{affine algebra} of \(X\), that is, the algebra of sections of
the structure sheaf \(\mathcal O_X\) of \(X\). By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.1}{1.1}, the functor
\(A\mapsto\operatorname{Spf}(A)\) is an anti-equivalence from
\(\operatorname{Alp}/k\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.4.1}{0.4.1}) onto the full subcategory
\(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\) of the category of formal schemes over
\(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\) whose objects are the formal \(k\)-varieties.

The category \(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\) has projective and direct
limits.\footnote{Translator's note: by 1.1, every formal \(k\)-variety \(X\) is the direct sum in
\(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\) of the one-point formal varieties \(\operatorname{Spf}(\mathcal O_{X,x})\),
\(x\in X\). A filtered projective limit in \(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\) with surjective transition morphisms
may be empty, as the example at the end of 0.4.2 shows. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, makes these remarks.} For example, let \(Y\) and \(Z\) be formal
\(k\)-varieties over the same formal \(k\)-variety \(X\), and let
\(A,B,C\) be the affine algebras of \(X,Y,Z\). By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.4.1}{0.4.1}, the
affine algebra of the fiber product \(Y\times_X Z\) identifies with
\(B\widehat{\otimes}_A C\),\footnote{Translator's note: so \(Y\times_XZ\) is the direct sum, over
\(x\in X\), of the formal varieties \(\operatorname{Spf}(B_x\widehat\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}C_x)\),
where \(B_x\) (respectively \(C_x\)) is the product of the \(\mathcal O_{Y,y}\) for \(y\) above \(x\)
(respectively of the \(\mathcal O_{Z,z}\) for \(z\) above \(x\)), by 0.3.6. The Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, notes this.} so that the inclusion of
\(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\) into the category of all formal \(k\)-schemes
commutes with finite projective limits (cf.\ EGA~I, 10.7). Likewise,
the direct limits of formal \(k\)-varieties correspond to the projective
limits of their affine algebras:
\phantomsection\label{sga3:VIIB:1.2.C}%
let, for example, \(d,e:X\rightrightarrows Y\) be a double arrow in
\(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\); the affine algebra of
\(\operatorname{Coker}(d,e)\) is isomorphic to the kernel of the
homomorphisms induced on the affine algebras of \(X\) and \(Y\); but
one can also give the following construction of
\(\operatorname{Coker}(d,e)\): the set underlying
\(\operatorname{Coker}(d,e)\) is the cokernel of the underlying sets; if
\(p\) is the canonical projection of the set underlying \(Y\) onto the
cokernel and \(z\) belongs to the cokernel, the local algebra of
\(\operatorname{Coker}(d,e)\) at \(z\) is the kernel of the double
arrow

  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIB:1.2.C-cokernel-double-arrow}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viib_15_95/diag_084}
\end{center}
where we have put
\[
  q=p\circ d=p\circ e,
\]
and where \(d^\natural\) and \(e^\natural\) are induced by the
homomorphisms \(d_x^\natural\) and \(e_x^\natural\) (notation of
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.1}{1.1}).

\phantomsection\label{sga3:VIIB:1.2.D}%
Finally, if \(\phi:k\to\ell\) is a homomorphism of pseudocompact
rings and \(X\) is a formal \(k\)-variety with affine algebra
\(A\), the formal scheme
\[
  X\times_{\operatorname{Spf}(k)}\operatorname{Spf}(\ell),
\]
obtained by base change, is a formal \(\ell\)-variety, which we shall
also denote by
\[
  X\widehat{\otimes}_k\ell,
\]
and whose affine algebra is the completed tensor product
\(A\widehat{\otimes}_k\ell\) (cf.\ 0.5 and EGA~I, 10).

Since every formal variety over \(k\) decomposes into formal
varieties over the local components of \(k\), \emph{in certain proofs
we shall assume that \(k\) is a local pseudocompact ring}.

We now give some examples, while fixing our terminology:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.2.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.2.1}
A \(k\)-\emph{functor} is, by definition, a covariant functor from
\(\operatorname{Alf}/k\) to \((\mathbf{Sets})\). By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.4.2}{0.4.2}, we may
identify \(\operatorname{Vaf}/k\) with a full subcategory of
\(\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Alf}/k,(\mathbf{Sets}))\) by
associating with every formal \(k\)-variety \(X\) the functor
\(C\mapsto X(C)\) (\(C\) ranges over the objects of
\(\operatorname{Alf}/k\), and \(X(C)\) denotes the set
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Vaf}/k}(\operatorname{Spf}C,X)\)).

We shall later encounter functors \(\mathbf F\) which associate
to every object \(C\) of \(\operatorname{Alf}/k\) a \(C\)-module
\(\mathbf F(C)\), and to every morphism
\(\phi:C\to D\) in \(\operatorname{Alf}/k\) a \(k\)-linear map
\[
  \mathbf F(\phi):\mathbf F(C)\longrightarrow\mathbf F(D)
\]
such that, for \(x\in\mathbf F(C)\) and \(\lambda\in C\),
\[
  \mathbf F(\phi)(\lambda x)
  =
  \phi(\lambda)\mathbf F(\phi)(x).
\]
By \SGARefLink{sga3:i:definition:3-1}{Expos\'e I, 3.1}, such an \(\mathbf F\) is endowed with the structure
of an \(\mathbf O_k\)-module, where \(\mathbf O_k\) denotes the
\(k\)-functor in rings which assigns to every object \(C\) of
\(\operatorname{Alf}/k\) its underlying ring. An
\(\mathbf O_k\)-module \(\mathbf F\) will be called \emph{flat} if
\(\mathbf F(C)\) is flat over \(C\) for every object \(C\) of
\(\operatorname{Alf}/k\), and if every morphism \(\phi:C\to D\) of
\(\operatorname{Alf}/k\) induces a bijection from
\(D\otimes_C\mathbf F(C)\) onto \(\mathbf F(D)\).

For example, if \(M\) is a module over \(k\), we denote by
\(\mathbf W(M)\) the \(\mathbf O_k\)-module
\[
  C\longmapsto C\otimes_kM;
\]
then \(\mathbf W(M)\) is flat when \(M\) is flat over \(k\). Moreover,
the functor \(M\mapsto\mathbf W(M)\) has as right adjoint the functor
which associates with every \(\mathbf O_k\)-module \(\mathbf F\) the
projective limit
\[
  \varprojlim_{\mathfrak l}\mathbf F(k/\mathfrak l),
\]
where \(\mathfrak l\) ranges over the open ideals of \(k\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1.2.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:1.2.2}
In what follows, an \(\mathbf O_k\)-module will always be denoted by a
boldface letter such as \(\mathbf F\). When \(k\) is Artinian, we shall
simply write \(F\) instead of \(\mathbf F(k)\). In that case, it is
clear that the functor
\[
  \mathbf F\longmapsto F
\]
induces an equivalence from the category of flat
\(\mathbf O_k\)-modules onto the category of flat \(k\)-modules! The
purpose of this terminology is solely to allow us to reason ``as if
\(k\) were always Artinian.''

In accordance with Expos\'e I, \S~\SGARefLink{sga3:i:definition:3-1}{3.1}, we shall use analogous
conventions for other algebraic structures. Thus an
\(\mathbf O_k\)-algebra, respectively an \(\mathbf O_k\)-coalgebra,
an \(\mathbf O_k\)-Lie algebra, or a \(p\)-Lie algebra over
\(\mathbf O_k\), consists of an \(\mathbf O_k\)-module \(\mathbf M\)
together with, for every object \(C\) of \(\operatorname{Alf}/k\), a
structure of \(C\)-algebra, respectively \(C\)-coalgebra, \(C\)-Lie
algebra, or \(p\)-Lie algebra over \(C\), on \(\mathbf M(C)\). We further require that
for every morphism \(\phi:C\to D\) in \(\operatorname{Alf}/k\), the
map
\[
  D\otimes_C\mathbf M(C)\longrightarrow\mathbf M(D)
\]
induced by \(\mathbf M(\phi)\) be a homomorphism of \(D\)-algebras,
respectively of \(D\)-coalgebras, and so on.

Finally, if \(\mathbf F\) and \(\mathbf G\) are two
\(\mathbf O_k\)-modules, then \(\mathbf F\otimes_k\mathbf G\) denotes
the \(\mathbf O_k\)-module
\[
  C\longmapsto\mathbf F(C)\otimes_C\mathbf G(C).
\]
