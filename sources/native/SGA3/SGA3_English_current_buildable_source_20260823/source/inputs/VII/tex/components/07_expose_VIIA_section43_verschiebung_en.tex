
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{4.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:4.3}

Finally, let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group scheme in abelian groups. The
composite morphism
\[
  \mu(G):
  U^p(G)\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow G_S^p
  \longrightarrow G,
\]
defined by multiplication, factors through \(V^p(G)\), so that we
have the following commutative diagram:
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:4.3-verschiebung-master-diagram}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_046}
\end{center}

When \(G\) is flat over \(S\), \(\varphi(G)\) is an isomorphism,
and we may define the morphism called the \emph{Verschiebung}
\[
  \operatorname{Ver}(G/S):
  G^{(p/S)}
  \longrightarrow
  G
\]
by
\[
  \operatorname{Ver}(G/S)
  =
  \nu(G)\circ i(G)\circ\varphi(G)^{-1}.
\]
As \(G\) ranges over the flat commutative \(S\)-group schemes, the
assignment
\[
  \operatorname{Ver}:G\longmapsto\operatorname{Ver}(G/S)
\]
is plainly a functorial homomorphism. Consequently,
\(\operatorname{Ver}(G/S)\) is a group homomorphism.

For every \(S\)-scheme \(T\), the composite
\[
  G(T)
  \xrightarrow{\delta(G)(T)}
  U^p(G)(T)
  \xrightarrow{\mu(G)(T)}
  G(T)
\]
sends \(x\in G(T)\) to \(p\cdot x\). We may therefore write
\(p\cdot\operatorname{id}_G\) in place of
\(\mu(G)\circ\delta(G)\), obtaining the classical formula
\[
  \tag{*}\label{sga3:vii:paragraph:4-3:equation:star}
  \operatorname{Ver}(G/S)\circ\operatorname{Fr}(G/S)
  =
  p\cdot\operatorname{id}_G.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{4.3.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:4.3.1}
For example, when \(G\) is a constant \(S\)-scheme in abelian groups,
we know that \(\operatorname{Fr}(G/S)\) identifies with the identity
morphism of \(G\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:4.1.1}{4.1.1}). Hence \(\operatorname{Ver}(G/S)=p\).

When \(G\) is the diagonalizable \(S\)-group of type \(M\),
\(\operatorname{Fr}(G/S)\) is equal to \(p\) by \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:4.1.4}{4.1.4}~a);\footnote{Translator's
note: the 1970 text refers to 4.1.2; the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, corrects the reference to 4.1.4~a), where
\(\operatorname{Fr}(G/S)\) is computed for diagonalizable groups.} one sees
easily that \(\operatorname{Ver}(G/S)\) is the identity morphism of
\(G\).

When \(\mathcal E\) is a flat \(\mathcal O_S\)-module and \(G\) is the
\(S\)-group \(\mathcal V(\mathcal E)\), the morphism
\(\operatorname{Ver}(G/S)\) is zero, as is
\(p\cdot\operatorname{id}_G\). We shall see in Expos\'e
VII\textsubscript{B} that a commutative algebraic group \(G\) over a
field \(k\) is ``unipotent'' if and only if the composite homomorphism
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:4.3.1-iterated-verschiebung-chain}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_047}
\end{center}
is zero for some \(n\) (we have set
\(G^{(p^n)}=\bigl(G^{(p^{n-1})}\bigr)^{(p)}\)).\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A},
observes that this criterion is not stated explicitly in
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, and refers to M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel,
\textit{Groupes alg\'ebriques}, tome~I, Masson and North-Holland, 1970,
IV, \S3, 4.11.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{4.3.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:4.3.2}

Since
\(\operatorname{Ver}:G\mapsto\operatorname{Ver}(G/S)\) is a
functorial homomorphism on the category of flat commutative
\(S\)-group schemes, the square
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:4.3.2-frobenius-verschiebung-square}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_048}
\end{center}
is commutative (where \(\operatorname{Fr}(G/S)^{(p)}\) denotes the
inverse image of \(\operatorname{Fr}(G/S)\) under the base change
\(\operatorname{fr}(S)\)). As it follows directly from the definitions
that \(\operatorname{Fr}(G/S)^{(p)}\) is equal to
\(\operatorname{Fr}(G^{(p)}/S)\), we also have
\[
  \tag{**}\label{sga3:vii:paragraph:4-3-2:equation:double-star}
  \operatorname{Fr}(G/S)\circ\operatorname{Ver}(G/S)
  =
  \operatorname{Ver}(G^{(p)}/S)
    \circ\operatorname{Fr}(G^{(p)}/S)
  =
  p\cdot\operatorname{id}_{G^{(p)}}.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{4.3.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:4.3.3}

Suppose finally that \(G\) is a commutative \(S\)-group which is
finite and locally free; let \(\mathcal A\) be the affine
\(\mathcal O_S\)-algebra of \(G\), and let \(\pi\) be the endomorphism
of the sheaf of rings \(\mathcal O_S\) which sends a section \(x\) of
\(\mathcal O_S\) to \(x^p\). One then has a commutative diagram
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:4.3.3-cartier-duality-algebra-diagram}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_05_14/diagram_049}
\end{center}
where the various letters have the following meanings:
\(m^p(\mathcal A)\) is induced by the multiplication of \(\mathcal A\);
the morphism \(\Delta^p(\mathcal A)\) is associated with the morphism
\(\mu(G)\) considered above (it is therefore defined by the diagonal
morphism of the coalgebra \(\mathcal A\)); we denote by
\(\Sigma^p\mathcal A\) the subalgebra of
\(\bigotimes_{\mathcal O_S}^p\mathcal A\) formed by the sections
invariant under the action of the symmetric group, and by
\(i(\mathcal A)\) the inclusion of \(\Sigma^p\mathcal A\) in the tensor
product; likewise, \(S^p(\mathcal A)\) is the component of degree \(p\)
of the symmetric algebra of \(\mathcal A\), and \(q(\mathcal A)\) is the
canonical projection. Since \(\Sigma^p\mathcal A\) is the affine algebra
of \(V^p(G)\) with the notation above, the composite morphism
\(i(G)\circ\varphi(G)^{-1}\) induces a homomorphism of algebras
\(r(\mathcal A)\); this homomorphism vanishes on the sections of the
form
\[
  \sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_p}
    a_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{\sigma(p)}
\]
and sends \(a\otimes\cdots\otimes a\) to \(a\otimes_\pi1\). Likewise,
\(j(\mathcal A)\) is the morphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules
\(a\otimes_\pi1\mapsto q(a\otimes\cdots\otimes a)\). The composite
\(r(\mathcal A)\circ a(\mathcal A)\) is therefore associated with the
Verschiebung morphism \(\operatorname{Ver}(G/S)\), while
\(b(\mathcal A)\circ j(\mathcal A)\) is associated with the Frobenius
morphism \(\operatorname{Fr}(G/S)\) (\(a(\mathcal A)\) and
\(b(\mathcal A)\) are such that
\(i(\mathcal A)\circ a(\mathcal A)=\Delta^p(\mathcal A)\) and
\(b(\mathcal A)\circ q(\mathcal A)=m^p(\mathcal A)\)).

The commutative diagram \((\mathcal A)\) above is self-dual. Indeed,
let \(D\) be the functor which associates with every
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(\mathcal M\) the dual \(\mathcal O_S\)-module
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal M,\mathcal O_S)\); it is
clear that the image of \((\mathcal A)\) under the functor \(D\) is none
other than the diagram \((D\mathcal A)\), the morphisms
\(Dr(\mathcal A)\), \(Da(\mathcal A)\), \(Dj(\mathcal A)\) and
\(Db(\mathcal A)\) identifying respectively with \(j(D\mathcal A)\),
\(b(D\mathcal A)\), \(r(D\mathcal A)\) and \(a(D\mathcal A)\). One thus
sees that Cartier duality (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:3.3.1}{3.3.1}) interchanges the Frobenius morphism
and the Verschiebung.
