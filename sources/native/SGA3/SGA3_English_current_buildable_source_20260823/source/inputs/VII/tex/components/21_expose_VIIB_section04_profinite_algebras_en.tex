
\section*{0.4. Profinite Algebras}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.4}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.4}{0.4}. Profinite Algebras}

Let \(k\) be a pseudocompact ring. A topological \(k\)-algebra \(A\)
(with unit element and commutative) is called \emph{profinite} if the
underlying topological \(k\)-module is pseudocompact. If this is the case
and \(\mathfrak l\) is an open \(k\)-submodule of \(A\), the composite
map
\[
  \varphi:
  A\times A
  \xrightarrow{\mathrm{mult}}A
  \longrightarrow A/\mathfrak l
\]
is continuous. \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.1}{Lemma 0.3.1} therefore gives an open \(k\)-submodule
\(\mathfrak n\) of \(A\) such that
\(\varphi(A\times\mathfrak n)=0\). Thus \(\mathfrak l\) contains the
open ideal \(A\mathfrak n\), and \(A\) is a pseudocompact ring.

Let us consider likewise a topological \(A\)-module \(M\) whose
underlying \(k\)-module is pseudocompact. If \(M'\) is an open
\(k\)-submodule of \(M\), \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.1}{Lemma 0.3.1} applied to the map
\[
  A\times M
  \xrightarrow{\mathrm{mult}}M
  \longrightarrow M/M'
\]
shows that \(M'\) contains an open \(A\)-submodule, so that \(M\) is also
a pseudocompact \(A\)-module.\footnote{Translator's note: conversely, every pseudocompact \(A\)-module
\(M\) is pseudocompact over \(k\): a pseudocompact \(A\)-module of finite length is the quotient of some
\(A^n\) by an open submodule, hence a pseudocompact \(k\)-module of finite length, and \(M\) is the
projective limit of such modules. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, adds
this as a lemma.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.4.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.4.1}
If \(A\) and \(B\) are profinite \(k\)-algebras, a \emph{morphism}
from \(A\) to \(B\) is, by definition, a continuous homomorphism of
\(k\)-algebras. The category of profinite \(k\)-algebras plainly has
projective limits: the algebra underlying a projective limit is the
projective limit of the underlying algebras, and the topology is the
projective-limit topology. Likewise, there are finite direct limits: for
example, if
\[
  f:A\longrightarrow B,
  \qquad
  g:A\longrightarrow C
\]
are morphisms of profinite \(k\)-algebras, the amalgamated sum of
\(B\) and \(C\) over \(A\) has
\[
  B\widehat{\otimes}_A C
\]
as its underlying topological \(A\)-module (by \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.4}{0.4}, \(f\) and \(g\)
make \(B\) and \(C\) pseudocompact \(A\)-modules); the multiplication of
\(B\widehat{\otimes}_AC\) is plainly such that
\[
  (b\widehat{\otimes}c)
  (b'\widehat{\otimes}c')
  =
  bb'\widehat{\otimes}cc'
\]
for \(b,b'\in B\) and \(c,c'\in C\).

From now on we denote by \(\operatorname{Alp}/k\) the category of
profinite \(k\)-algebras.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.4.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.4.2}
A profinite \(k\)-algebra \(C\) is said to be \emph{of finite length} if
its underlying \(k\)-module has finite length (and is therefore discrete);
we denote by \(\operatorname{Alf}/k\) the full subcategory of
\(\operatorname{Alp}/k\) formed by the \(k\)-algebras of finite
length,\footnote{Translator's note: \(k\) itself, as a profinite \(k\)-algebra, belongs to
\(\operatorname{Alf}/k\) only when \(k\) is Artinian; the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, points this out.} and, for every profinite algebra \(A\), by \(h_A\)
the functor
\[
  h_A:\operatorname{Alf}/k\longrightarrow(\mathbf{Sets}),
  \qquad
  C\longmapsto
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Alp}/k}(A,C).
\]
It is clear that \(h_A\) is a left exact functor; moreover, when
\(\mathfrak l\) ranges over the open ideals of \(A\), the canonical
projections of \(A\) onto \(A/\mathfrak l\) induce an isomorphism
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Alp}/k}(A,C)
  \xleftarrow{\ \sim\ }
  \varinjlim_{\mathfrak l}
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Alf}/k}(A/\mathfrak l,C)
\]
whenever \(C\) has finite length. This means that \(h_A\) is the direct
limit of the representable functors \(h_{A/\mathfrak l}\); if \(B\) is
another profinite \(k\)-algebra, there are therefore isomorphisms
\begin{align*}
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Alp}/k}(B,A)
  &\simeq
  \varprojlim_{\mathfrak l}
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Alp}/k}(B,A/\mathfrak l)
  \simeq
  \varprojlim_{\mathfrak l}h_B(A/\mathfrak l)
  \\
  &\simeq
  \varprojlim_{\mathfrak l}
  \operatorname{Hom}(h_{A/\mathfrak l},h_B)
  \simeq
  \operatorname{Hom}(h_A,h_B),
\end{align*}
which show that the contravariant functor \(A\mapsto h_A\) is fully
faithful.

In fact, \(A\mapsto h_A\) is an anti-equivalence from
\(\operatorname{Alp}/k\) onto the category of left exact functors from
\(\operatorname{Alf}/k\) to \((\mathbf{Sets})\). Indeed, by what precedes,
it is enough to show that every left exact functor \(F\) is isomorphic
to a functor of the form \(h_A\); for this, the algebra \(A\) may be
constructed as follows (TDTE~II, \S~3). Because \(F\) is left exact,
for every finite-length \(k\)-algebra \(C\) and every \(\xi\in F(C)\),
there is a smallest subalgebra \(C'\) of \(C\) such that \(\xi\) belongs
to the image of \(F(C')\) in \(F(C)\); if \(C'=C\), the pair
\((C,\xi)\) is called \emph{minimal}. The minimal pairs form a category
when a morphism from \((C,\xi)\) to \((D,\eta)\) is defined to be a
homomorphism \(\phi:C\to D\) such that
\[
  (F\phi)(\xi)=\eta;
\]
it is clear that such a \(\phi\) is surjective, and that the category of
minimal pairs is ``left filtered.'' Moreover, since one may plainly
restrict to the pairs \((C,\xi)\) for which \(C\) belongs to a set
containing finite-length \(k\)-algebras of every type,\footnote{Translator's note: the underlying
\(k\)-module of a \(k\)-algebra of finite length \(n\) is a quotient of \(k^n\) by an open submodule,
and each such quotient carries only a set of profinite \(k\)-algebra structures; this gives the set in
question. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, makes this explicit.} the
functor \((C,\xi)\mapsto C\), from the category of minimal pairs to the
category of profinite \(k\)-algebras, has a projective limit; one takes
\(A\) to be this projective limit.

The existence of an anti-equivalence from \(\operatorname{Alp}/k\) onto
the category of left exact functors from \(\operatorname{Alf}/k\) to
\((\mathbf{Sets})\) implies, for example, that \(\operatorname{Alp}/k\)
has infinite direct limits: indeed, the category of left exact functors
has projective limits, which are given by the formula
\[
  (\varprojlim_iF_i)(C)=\varprojlim_iF_i(C),
\]
when \(C\) is a \(k\)-algebra of finite length and \((F_i)\) a
projective system of functors.\footnote{Translator's note: concretely, if \((A_i)\) is an inductive system
in \(\operatorname{Alp}/k\) and \(B\) the inductive limit of the underlying \(k\)-algebras, its
inductive limit in \(\operatorname{Alp}/k\) is the separated completion of \(B\) for the topology
defined by the ideals \(I\) of \(B\) such that \(B/I\) has finite length over \(k\) and the inverse
image of \(I\) in each \(A_i\) is open. For example, if \(k\) is a field and \(K\) an algebraic extension
of \(k\) of infinite degree, the inductive limit in \(\operatorname{Alp}/k\) of the finite subextensions
of \(K\) is \(0\): a nonzero \(k\)-algebra of finite length receives no homomorphism from the field
\(K\). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, gives this description and this
example.}
