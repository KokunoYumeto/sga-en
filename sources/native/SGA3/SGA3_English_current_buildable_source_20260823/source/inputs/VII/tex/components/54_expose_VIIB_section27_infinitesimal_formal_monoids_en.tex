
\subsection*{2.7}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:2.7}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.7:prop}{2.7}. Infinitesimal formal monoids}

We conclude these generalities with a statement that goes back to Lie
and that will be used in paragraph~\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5}{5}. A \emph{formal monoid with unit
element} over \(k\) is, by definition, a pair \((M,m)\) consisting of a
formal variety \(M\) and a morphism
\[
  m:M\times M\longrightarrow M
\]
such that \(m(C)\) makes \(M(C)\) a monoid with unit element for every
object \(C\) of \(\operatorname{Alf}/k\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.4.2}{0.4.2})}. In particular, the
unit element of \(M(k)\) defines a section \(\varepsilon_M\) of the
canonical projection of \(M\) onto \(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\). We shall say
that the formal monoid \(M\) is \emph{infinitesimal} if \(\varepsilon_M\)
induces a bijection of the underlying sets.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:2.7:prop}
{\itshape Every topologically flat infinitesimal formal
\(k\)-monoid \(M\) is a formal group.}

\textit{Proof.}
We must show that \(M(C)\) is a group for every object \(C\) of
\(\operatorname{Alf}/k\). We therefore reduce immediately to the
case in which \(k\) is Artinian. Let
\[
  U=\mathbf H(M)
\]
be the coalgebra of \(M\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.5}{1.3.5})}; the structural morphism \(m\)
induces a homomorphism of coalgebras \(m_U\) of \(U\otimes U\) into \(U\),
which makes \(U\) an associative algebra over \(k\); this algebra has for
unit element the image of the unit element of \(k\) under the map of \(k\)
into \(U\) that is induced by the unit section \(\varepsilon_M\) of \(M\).
Likewise, the canonical projection of \(M\) onto \(\operatorname{Spf}(k)\)
induces a homomorphism \(\varepsilon_U\) of \(U\) into \(k\); we shall
denote by \(U^+\) the kernel of \(\varepsilon_U\).

We must show that there exists an antipode, that is, a homomorphism of
coalgebras \(c_U:U\to U\) such that the composite
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:2.7:antipode-left-identity}
\[
  U\xrightarrow{\ \Delta_U\ }U\otimes_kU
  \xrightarrow{\ c_U\otimes U\ }U\otimes_kU
  \xrightarrow{\ m_U\ }U
\]
maps \(u\in U\) to \(\varepsilon_U(u)\cdot1_U\). Since \(M\) is
infinitesimal, \(U^+\) is the union of the submodules \(U_n^+\) defined as
in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.6.2}{2.6.2}; one then shows easily, by induction on \(n\), that there
exists one and only one linear map \(c_n:U_n^+\to U_n^+\) such that the
composite
\[
  U_n^+\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{incl.}\ }U
  \xrightarrow{\ \Delta_U\ }U\otimes U
  \xrightarrow{\ c_U\otimes U\ }U\otimes U
  \xrightarrow{\ m_U\ }U
\]
is zero. In particular, \(c_{n+1}\) extends \(c_n\), and one may put
\[
  c_U(x)=\varepsilon_U(x)\cdot1_U+c_n\bigl(x-\varepsilon_U(x)\cdot1_U\bigr),
\]
where \(n\) is large enough for \(U_n^+\) to contain
\(x-\varepsilon_U(x)\cdot1_U\).

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation). (a) By the equivalence of
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.5}{1.3.5}, a topologically flat formal monoid over \(k\) is the same as a
monoid in the category of flat \(\mathbf O_k\)-coalgebras, that is, a flat
cocommutative \(\mathbf O_k\)-bialgebra, with unit and counit but not
necessarily with an antipode; by \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.6}{1.3.6}, \(M\) is infinitesimal if and
only if this bialgebra is the union of the submodules \(U_n\), that is,
connected. For \(k\) Artinian, the proposition thus says that a connected
cocommutative bialgebra flat over \(k\) has an antipode; cocommutativity
is not used in (b).

(b) \emph{Translator's note on the proof.} The 1970 text constructs
\(c_U\) from the identity \(m_U\circ(c_U\otimes U)\circ\Delta_U=\eta\) only, where
\(\eta(u)=\varepsilon_U(u)\cdot1_U\), and calls it a homomorphism of
coalgebras without proof; one needs, for instance, a two-sided inverse.
Here is a complete argument. On \(\operatorname{End}_k(U)\), the product
\(f*g=m_U\circ(f\otimes g)\circ\Delta_U\) is associative, with unit
\(\eta\); put
\(\varphi=\operatorname{id}_U-\eta\), so that \(\varphi(1_U)=0\). If
\(u\in U_n\), write \(\Delta_U(u)=u\otimes1+\sum_iy_i\otimes z_i\) with
\(y_i\in U_{n-1}\) and \(z_i\in U^+\); then
\(\varphi^{*(n+1)}(u)=\varphi^{*n}(u)\,\varphi(1_U)+\sum_i\varphi^{*n}(y_i)\,\varphi(z_i)\),
and induction on \(n\) gives \(\varphi^{*(n+1)}(U_n)=0\). As
\(U=\bigcup_nU_n\), the sum \(c=\sum_{j\geq0}(-\varphi)^{*j}\) is finite at
each point of \(U\), and \(c*(\eta+\varphi)=(\eta+\varphi)*c=\eta\): \(c\) is
the two-sided inverse of \(\operatorname{id}_U=\eta+\varphi\), so it is the
\(c_U\) of the text. For \(C\) in \(\operatorname{Alf}/k\), \(c\otimes C\) is in
the same way the inverse of the identity of \(U\otimes_kC\), and \(M(C)\) is
the monoid, for the product \(m_U\otimes C\), of the \(z\in U\otimes_kC\) with
\(\varepsilon(z)=1\) and \(\Delta(z)=z\otimes z\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.5}{1.3.5})}. For such
\(z\), \(c(z)z=zc(z)=1\), and \(z^{-1}=c(z)\) again has augmentation \(1\) and
satisfies \(\Delta(z^{-1})=z^{-1}\otimes z^{-1}\), \(\Delta\) being a
homomorphism of algebras. So \(M(C)\) is a group. The re-edition proves the
two-sided property after writing \(c\) inductively, \(c(x)=-\sum_ic(y_i)z_i\)
for \(x\in U_n^+\).
