
\subsubsection*{2.6.3}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:2.6.3}

If \(\mathbf L\) is a Lie algebra flat over \(\mathbf O_k\), the formal
group \(\mathscr G(\mathbf L)\) can be characterized by a universal
property: let us consider indeed a homomorphism \(h\) of
\(\mathscr G(\mathbf L)\) into a formal group \(G\); such a homomorphism
induces a homomorphism \(\operatorname{Lie}h\) of the Lie algebra of
\(\mathscr G(\mathbf L)\) into that of \(G\). Composing
\(\operatorname{Lie}h\) with the canonical monomorphism of \(\mathbf L\)
into \(U(\mathbf L)\),\footnote{Translator's note: the image of \(\mathbf L\) in \(U(\mathbf L)\)
consists of primitive elements, that is, it lies in \(\operatorname{Lie}(\mathscr G(\mathbf L))\) (see the
Addition after 2.6.2); the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, composes
\(\operatorname{Lie}h\) with the resulting morphism \(\tau_{\mathbf L}\colon\mathbf L\to
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathscr G(\mathbf L))\).} one obtains a homomorphism
\(h':\mathbf L\to\operatorname{Lie}(G)\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:2.6.3:prop}
{\itshape If \(G\) is a formal \(k\)-group and \(\mathbf L\) a Lie algebra
flat over \(\mathbf O_k\), the map \(h\mapsto h'\) defined above is a
bijection
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{k\text{-}\mathrm{Gr}.}
    \bigl(\mathscr G(\mathbf L),G\bigr)
  \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
  \operatorname{Hom}\bigl(\mathbf L,\operatorname{Lie}(G)\bigr)
\]
from the set of homomorphisms of formal \(k\)-groups from
\(\mathscr G(\mathbf L)\) to \(G\) onto that of the morphisms of
\(\mathbf O_k\)-Lie algebras from \(\mathbf L\) to \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\).}

Indeed, we reduce at once to the case where \(k\) is Artinian. In this
case, \(\operatorname{Hom}_{k\text{-}\mathrm{Gr}.}(\mathscr G(\mathbf L),G)\) is in
canonical one-to-one correspondence \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.3.1}{2.3.1})} with the set of the
homomorphisms of unital algebras \(g:U(L)\to\mathbf H(G)\) such that the
following diagram is commutative:
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIB:2.6.3:universal-property-diagram}
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=1.2em,column sep=4.2em]
U(L) \arrow[r,"g"] \arrow[dd,"\Delta_{U(L)}"']
  & \mathbf H(G) \arrow[dr,"\delta_G"] & {} \\
{} & {} & \mathbf H(G\times G) \\
U(L)\otimes U(L) \arrow[r,"g\otimes g"]
  & \mathbf H(G)\otimes\mathbf H(G)
      \arrow[ur,"\varphi_G"']
  & {.}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
Now \(g\) is defined by its restriction to \(L\), which is a homomorphism
of Lie algebras from \(L\) to the Lie algebra underlying
\(\mathbf H(G)\). The commutativity of the diagram therefore simply means
that \(g\) maps \(L\) into the part \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\) of
\(\mathbf H(G)\) formed by the \(x\) such that
\(\delta_G(x)=\varphi_G(x\otimes1+1\otimes x)\).
