
\subsubsection*{5.1.4}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:5.1.4}

In the case that interests us, one has
\[
  (A_n)=\operatorname{Gr}_I(A)
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  A_0=A/I.
\]
Moreover, it is clear that the algebra \((A_n)\) is generated by
\(A_0\) and \(A_1\), that is, for \(n\geq1\), the map
\[
  A_1\widehat\otimes_{A_0}A_1\widehat\otimes_{A_0}\cdots
  \widehat\otimes_{A_0}A_1
  \longrightarrow A_n
\]
defined by the multiplication is surjective. Since
\[
  A_1\simeq A_0\widehat\otimes_kA'_1
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  A_n\simeq A_0\widehat\otimes_kA'_n,
\]
hence also\footnote{Translator's note: on the left of the following display, the 1970 text prints
\(A_1\widehat\otimes_{A_0}A\widehat\otimes_{A_0}\cdots\widehat\otimes_{A_0}A\).}
\[
  A_1\widehat\otimes_{A_0}A_1\widehat\otimes_{A_0}\cdots
  \widehat\otimes_{A_0}A_1
  \simeq
  A_0\widehat\otimes_k
  \bigl(A'_1\widehat\otimes_k\cdots\widehat\otimes_kA'_1\bigr),
\]
one sees that the map
\[
  A'_1\widehat\otimes_k\cdots\widehat\otimes_kA'_1
  \longrightarrow A'_n,
\]
which is induced by the multiplication of \((A'_n)\), is surjective;
in other words, \((A'_n)\) is generated by its terms of degree \(1\).
