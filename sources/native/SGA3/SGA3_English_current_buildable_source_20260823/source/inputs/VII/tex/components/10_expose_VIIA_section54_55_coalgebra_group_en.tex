
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{5.4.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:5.4}

The universal property of \(U_p(\mathfrak g)\) implies that
\(U_p(\mathfrak g)\) is functorial in \(\mathfrak g\). Every
homomorphism of restricted \(p\)-Lie algebras
\[
  \varphi:\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\mathfrak h
\]
induces a unique homomorphism of unital algebras \(U_p(\varphi)\)
such that
\[
  j_{\mathfrak h}\circ\varphi
  =
  U_p(\varphi)\circ j_{\mathfrak g}.
\]
Here are some examples.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{a)}
If \(\mathfrak h=0\), then \(U_p(\mathfrak h)\) identifies with the
base ring, and \(U_p(\varphi)\) is an algebra homomorphism
\[
  \varepsilon_{\mathfrak g}:
  U_p(\mathfrak g)\longrightarrow R
\]
called the \emph{augmentation}.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{b)}
Let now \(\mathfrak h=\mathfrak g^\circ\), the Lie algebra opposite
to \(\mathfrak g\). Thus \(\mathfrak g^\circ\) has the same
underlying module and the same symbolic \(p\)-th power as
\(\mathfrak g\), while its bracket is the negative of the bracket
of \(\mathfrak g\). We may identify
\(U_p(\mathfrak g^\circ)\) with the algebra opposite to
\(U_p(\mathfrak g)\). Moreover, the isomorphism
\[
  x\longmapsto-x
\]
from \(\mathfrak g\) onto \(\mathfrak g^\circ\) induces an
isomorphism
\[
  c_{\mathfrak g}:
  U_p(\mathfrak g)
  \longrightarrow
  U_p(\mathfrak g^\circ)
  \simeq
  U_p(\mathfrak g)^\circ.
\]
We call \(c_{\mathfrak g}\) the \emph{antipode} of
\(U_p(\mathfrak g)\).

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{c)}
Finally, let \(\mathfrak f\) and \(\mathfrak g\) be two restricted
\(p\)-Lie algebras, and let
\(\mathfrak h=\mathfrak f\times\mathfrak g\) be their product.
Its underlying \(R\)-module is the direct product, and
\[
  [(x,y),(x',y')]
  =
  ([x,x'],[y,y']),
  \qquad
  (x,y)^{(p)}
  =
  (x^{(p)},y^{(p)}).
\]
If
\[
  h_1:\mathfrak f\to\mathfrak k,
  \qquad
  h_2:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak k
\]
are restricted \(p\)-Lie algebra homomorphisms satisfying
\[
  [h_1(x),h_2(y)]=0
  \quad
  (x\in\mathfrak f,\ y\in\mathfrak g),
\]
then
\[
  h_1+h_2:(x,y)\longmapsto h_1(x)+h_2(y)
\]
is a restricted \(p\)-Lie algebra homomorphism. Conversely, every
homomorphism \(\mathfrak f\times\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak k\) is of
this form. This characterizes \(\mathfrak f\times\mathfrak g\) by
a universal property.

For example, the maps
\[
  h_1:x\longmapsto j_{\mathfrak f}(x)\otimes1,
  \qquad
  h_2:y\longmapsto1\otimes j_{\mathfrak g}(y)
\]
induce a homomorphism \(h_1+h_2\) from
\(\mathfrak f\times\mathfrak g\) to the restricted \(p\)-Lie
algebra underlying
\[
  U_p(\mathfrak f)\otimes U_p(\mathfrak g).
\]
The universal properties of \(\mathfrak f\times\mathfrak g\) and
of the restricted enveloping algebras show that this homomorphism
extends to an isomorphism
\[
  \varphi:
  U_p(\mathfrak f\times\mathfrak g)
  \xrightarrow{\sim}
  U_p(\mathfrak f)\otimes U_p(\mathfrak g).
\]

If \(\mathfrak f=\mathfrak g\), the diagonal map
\[
  \delta:x\longmapsto(x,x)
\]
from \(\mathfrak g\) to
\(\mathfrak g\times\mathfrak g\) induces a homomorphism from
\(U_p(\mathfrak g)\) to
\(U_p(\mathfrak g\times\mathfrak g)\). We denote by
\[
  \Delta_{\mathfrak g}:
  U_p(\mathfrak g)
  \longrightarrow
  U_p(\mathfrak g)\otimes U_p(\mathfrak g)
\]
the composite of this homomorphism with \(\varphi\).\footnote{Translator's
note: as the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, points out,
\(\Delta_{\mathfrak g}(x)=x\otimes1+1\otimes x\) for \(x\in\mathfrak g\)
(identified with its image in \(U_p(\mathfrak g)\)), since \(\varphi\)
sends \((x,0)\) to \(x\otimes1\) and \((0,x)\) to \(1\otimes x\); as
these elements generate \(U_p(\mathfrak g)\) and \(\Delta_{\mathfrak g}\)
is multiplicative, \(\Delta_{\mathfrak g}\) is cocommutative, as condition
(i) of 3.1 requires.}

It is then easy to see that \(\Delta_{\mathfrak g}\), together
with the multiplication of \(U_p(\mathfrak g)\), makes
\(U_p(\mathfrak g)\) an \(R\)-coalgebra in groups (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:3.2}{3.2}), with
augmentation \(\varepsilon_{\mathfrak g}\) and antipode
\(c_{\mathfrak g}\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{5.4.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:5.4.1}
Likewise, let \(S\) be a scheme of characteristic \(p\) and
\(\mathcal G\) an \(\mathcal O_S\)-restricted \(p\)-Lie algebra. As \(V\)
ranges over the open subsets of \(S\), the structures of coalgebras in
groups defined above on the sets \(U_p(\Gamma(V,\mathcal G))\) induce on
the restricted enveloping algebra \(\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G)\) a
structure of \(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebra in groups. By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.3.1}{5.3.1}, the
corresponding \(S\)-functor in groups
\(\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G)\) associates with every
\(S\)-scheme \(T\) the set of sections \(x\) of
\(\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal O_T)\) such
that\footnote{Translator's note: this description combines the
definition of \(\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\) in 3.1.2 with the
identification of
\(\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal O_T)\) with
\(\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal O_T\) in
5.3.4.}
\[
  \varepsilon(x)=1
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \Delta x=x\otimes x.
\]
Here \(\varepsilon\) denotes the augmentation of
\(\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal O_T)\), \(\Delta\)
the diagonal morphism, and \(x\otimes x\) the canonical image of
\((x,x)\) in
\(\Gamma\bigl(T,\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G_T)\otimes_{\mathcal O_T}
\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G_T)\bigr)\).

When \(\mathcal G\) is locally free of finite type as an
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module, \(\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G)\)
is representable by a finite, locally free \(S\)-scheme (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:5.3.3}{5.3.3} and
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:3.1.2}{3.1.2}).

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, 5.5; our formulation). The re-edition
replaces 5.4.1 by a paragraph 5.5 which gathers, in a slightly more
general form, results that the 1970 text proves in 7.3 and 7.4 below.
(a) Let \(\mathcal U\) be an \(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebra in groups and
\(G=\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U\). For every \(S\)-scheme \(T\),
\((\operatorname{Lie}G)(T)\) is the module of primitive elements of
\(\Gamma(T,\mathcal U_T)\) (3.2.3); it is stable under \(x\mapsto x^p\),
since in characteristic \(p\)
\(\Delta(x^p)=(x\otimes1+1\otimes x)^p=x^p\otimes1+1\otimes x^p\), the two
terms commuting; with this operation it is a restricted \(p\)-Lie
subalgebra of \(\Gamma(T,\mathcal U_T)_{\mathrm{Lie}}\) (5.2). (b) The
argument of Lemma 7.3 applies to every quasi-coherent
\(\mathcal O_S\)-restricted \(p\)-Lie algebra \(\mathcal G\) which is
locally free, of finite type or not: \(j_{\mathcal G}\) identifies
\(\mathcal W(\mathcal G)\) with the primitive elements of
\(\mathcal W(\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G))\), that is, with
\(\operatorname{Lie}\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G)\), as
restricted \(p\)-Lie algebras, since \(\mathcal U_p\) commutes with base
change (5.3.4). (c) When \(\mathcal G\) is finite and locally free, the
augmentation ideal \(\mathcal I\) of the affine algebra of
\(\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U_p(\mathcal G)\) satisfies
\(\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}
(\mathcal G,\mathcal O_S)\), as shown in 7.4.1.
