\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation). \emph{Unipotent group
schemes over a field.} Let \(k\) be a field and \(G\) an affine \(k\)-group
scheme. One calls \(G\) \emph{unipotent} if every simple
\(\mathcal O(G)\)-comodule is trivial, that is, of dimension \(1\) with
coaction \(v\mapsto v\otimes1\); the re-edition refers for this to
M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel, \emph{Groupes alg\'ebriques}, I, Masson and
North-Holland, 1970, IV, \S~2, 2.5, where it is shown to agree, for \(G\)
of finite type, with the definition of Expos\'e~XVII, \S~1 (a composition
series whose quotients are isomorphic to subgroups of \(\mathbb G_{a,k}\)).
Let \(A=\mathcal O(G)^*\) be the dual of the coalgebra \(\mathcal O(G)\): a
profinite \(k\)-algebra, not necessarily commutative, whose product is dual
to the comultiplication and whose unit is the counit; the two-sided ideal
\(I=\{f\in A\mid f(1)=0\}\) satisfies \(A=k\cdot1_A\oplus I\). Every
finite-dimensional subspace of \(\mathcal O(G)\) lies in a
finite-dimensional subcoalgebra \(C\), and the two-sided ideals
\(C^\perp\), of finite codimension, form a basis of neighbourhoods of \(0\)
in \(A\). So an \(\mathcal O(G)\)-comodule \((V,\rho)\) is the same as a
discrete \(A\)-module on which \(A\) acts continuously, by
\(f\cdot v=(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes f)(\rho(v))\). A simple one is
finite-dimensional, and is a faithful simple module over the
finite-dimensional algebra \(A/\operatorname{Ann}(V)\), which is therefore
simple, \(\operatorname{Ann}(V)\) being open. Conversely, if \(P\) is an
open prime two-sided ideal of \(A\) (the product of two two-sided ideals
not contained in \(P\) is not contained in \(P\)), then \(A/P\) is a
finite-dimensional prime, hence simple, algebra, and has up to isomorphism
one simple module, whose annihilator is \(P\). Thus
\(V\mapsto\operatorname{Ann}(V)\) is a bijection from the isomorphism classes
of simple comodules onto the open prime ideals of \(A\); the trivial
comodule, on which \(f\) acts by \(f(1)\), corresponds to \(I\).
Consequently:

(i) \emph{\(G\) is unipotent if and only if \(I\) is the only open prime
ideal of \(A\)}, or again, as the open prime ideals are the orthogonals of
the simple subcoalgebras, if and only if \(k\cdot1\) is the only simple
subcoalgebra of \(\mathcal O(G)\).

(ii) \emph{If \(G\) is commutative, so that \(\mathcal O(G)=\mathbf H(D(G))\),
where \(D(G)=\operatorname{Spf}(A)\) is the dual formal group
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.2.2}{2.2.2})}, then \(G\) is unipotent if and only if \(D(G)\) is
infinitesimal.} Indeed, \(A\) is then commutative, its open prime ideals are
the points of \(D(G)\), and \(I\) is its unit point. Thus, for affine
commutative \(k\)-groups, ``unipotent'' in the sense of 2.5.2 agrees with
the usual notion.
