
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:0.2}
Let \(A\) be a commutative pseudocompact ring. A
\emph{pseudocompact \(A\)-module} \(M\) is a separated, complete, unital
topological \(A\)-module having a basis of neighborhoods of \(0\)
consisting of submodules \(M'\) for which \(M/M'\) has finite length
over \(A\). If \(M\) and \(N\) are pseudocompact \(A\)-modules, a
\emph{morphism} from \(M\) to \(N\) is, by definition, a continuous
\(A\)-linear map. The pseudocompact \(A\)-modules form an abelian
category, which has as cogenerators the pseudocompact \(A\)-modules of
finite length (whose topology is therefore discrete). If \((M_i)\) is a
projective system of pseudocompact modules, the projective limit of the
\(M_i\) has as underlying module the projective limit of the underlying
modules, and as topology the projective-limit topology. Moreover, the
projective-limit functor is exact when the indices form a filtered
ordered set (Axiom AB5\(^*\)). Likewise, if \(f\) is a morphism of
pseudocompact \(A\)-modules, \(\operatorname{Coker}f\) has as underlying
module the cokernel of the underlying modules, and the topology of
\(\operatorname{Coker}f\) is the quotient of that of the target of \(f\)
(cf.\ [CA], IV, \S~3).

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
\emph{Some details on the category \(\operatorname{PC}(A)\) of pseudocompact \(A\)-modules} (cf.\ [CA],
IV, \S~3).

(a) Let \((s_i)\colon(M_i)\to(N_i)\) be a morphism of projective systems of modules over a ring,
indexed by a filtered ordered set, such that every \(s_i\) is surjective with Artinian kernel. Then
\(\varprojlim_is_i\) is surjective ([CA], IV, \S~3, Lemma~1; for a proof, N.~Bourbaki, \emph{Th\'eorie
des ensembles}, Chap.~III, \S~7, no.~4, Th.~1 and Example~2).

(b) If \(K\) is a closed submodule of a pseudocompact module \(M\), then \(M/K\), with the quotient
topology, is pseudocompact. Indeed, the submodules \((K+L)/K\), \(L\) open in \(M\), have finite
colength, and their intersection is \(0\) because \(K=\bigcap_L(K+L)\) is closed; so the canonical map
\(M/K\to\varprojlim_LM/(K+L)\) is injective. It is surjective by (a), applied to the maps
\(M/L\to M/(K+L)\), whose kernels have finite length, since \(M=\varprojlim_LM/L\).

\phantomsection\label{sga3:VIIB:0.2.D}%
(c) Let \((M_i)\) be a filtered decreasing family of closed submodules of a pseudocompact module \(M\).
Then \(M\to\varprojlim_iM/M_i\) is surjective, with kernel \(\bigcap_iM_i\). Indeed, for \(L\) open in
\(M\), the submodules \(M_i+L\) have a smallest element, \(M/L\) having finite length; so
\(M/L\to\varprojlim_iM/(M_i+L)\) is surjective with kernel of finite length, and by (a) and (b) the
module \(M=\varprojlim_LM/L\) maps onto \(\varprojlim_L\varprojlim_iM/(M_i+L)=\varprojlim_iM/M_i\).
Consequently, for every closed submodule \(N\) of \(M\),
\[
  N+\bigcap_iM_i=\bigcap_i(N+M_i):
\]
apply the snake lemma to the morphism from the exact sequence \(0\to N\to M\to M/N\to0\) to the exact
sequence \(0\to\varprojlim_iN/(N\cap M_i)\to\varprojlim_iM/M_i\to\varprojlim_iM/(N+M_i)\), whose first
two components are surjective by what precedes, \(N\) being pseudocompact.

\phantomsection\label{sga3:vii:proposition:0-2-b}%
(d) Let \(f\colon M\to N\) be a morphism of pseudocompact modules, with kernel \(K\). The continuous
bijection \(M/K\to f(M)\) is a homeomorphism when \(f(M)\) carries the topology induced by \(N\); hence
\(f(M)\) is complete, so closed in \(N\), and \(N/f(M)\) is pseudocompact by (b). In particular a
bijective morphism of pseudocompact modules is an isomorphism. To see this, identify \(M/K\), which is
pseudocompact by (b), with \(f(M)\), and let \(P\) be an open submodule of \(M/K\). The traces \(N'_i\)
on \(f(M)\) of the open submodules \(N_i\) of \(N\) are closed submodules of \(M/K\) (they are open, hence
closed, for the induced topology, which is coarser), with intersection \(0\). As \((M/K)/P\) has finite length, the submodules \(N'_i+P\) have a smallest element
\(N'_{i_0}+P\), and by (c) \(N'_{i_0}+P=\bigcap_i(N'_i+P)=P\). So \(P\) contains \(N'_{i_0}\) and is
open for the induced topology.

(e) A product of exact sequences of pseudocompact modules is exact, kernels and cokernels having as
underlying modules those of the underlying homomorphisms. For the axiom AB5\(^*\), see A.~Grothendieck,
\emph{Sur quelques points d'alg\`ebre homologique}, T\^ohoku Math.\ J.\ \textbf{9} (1957), 1.5.

(f) The modules of finite length even form a \emph{set} of cogenerators, up to isomorphism: \(M\) embeds
in \(\prod_LM/L\), and a module of finite length \(n\) is the quotient of \(A^n\) by an open
submodule.
