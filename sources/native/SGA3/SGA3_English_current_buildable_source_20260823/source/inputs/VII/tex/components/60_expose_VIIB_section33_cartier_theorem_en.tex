
\subsection*{3.3}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:3.3}

If \(G\) is a formal \(k\)-group with affine algebra \(A\), recall that
we denote by \(I_A\) the augmentation ideal of \(A\) and by
\(\omega_{G/k}\) the pseudocompact \(k\)-module
\[
  I_A/\overline{I_A^2}
  \simeq
  I_A\widehat\otimes_A(A/I_A).
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Theorem 3.3 \textup{(Cartier)}.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:3.3:cartier-theorem}
{\itshape Let \(k\) be a local pseudocompact ring containing the field
\(\mathbb Q\),\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, states the theorem without assuming \(k\) local: the proof of
(iv)~\(\Rightarrow\)~(ii) below begins by reducing to that case, and the other implications do not use the
hypothesis. It adds that in (i) one then has \(\mathbf L\simeq\operatorname{Lie}(G)\), since
\(\mathbf L\to\operatorname{Lie}(\mathscr G(\mathbf L))\) is an isomorphism (Addition after 3.2), and that in (ii)
one has \(\omega\simeq\omega_{G/k}\), as the proof of (ii)~\(\Rightarrow\)~(iii) shows.} and \(G\) a formal group over \(k\). The following
assertions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIB:3.3:cartier-theorem:item:i}(i)}] There exists a flat \(\mathbf O_k\)-Lie algebra
\(\mathbf L\) such that \(G\) is isomorphic to \(\mathscr G(\mathbf L)\).

\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIB:3.3:cartier-theorem:item:ii}(ii)}] There exists a projective pseudocompact
\(k\)-module \(\omega\) such that the formal variety underlying \(G\) is
isomorphic to the variety \(\mathbf V_k^{f,0}(\omega)\) with affine algebra
\(k[[\omega]]\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2.5}{1.2.5})}.

\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIB:3.3:cartier-theorem:item:iii}(iii)}] \(G\) is infinitesimal and \(\omega_{G/k}\) is a
projective pseudocompact \(k\)-module.

\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:VIIB:3.3:cartier-theorem:item:iv}(iv)}] \(G\) is infinitesimal and topologically flat
over \(k\).
\end{enumerate}}

\noindent\textup{(i)}~\(\Rightarrow\)~\textup{(ii)}: Let \(\omega\) be the pseudocompact
\(k\)-module dual to \(\mathbf L\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2.3}{1.2.3})}. For every profinite
\(k\)-algebra of finite length \(C\), we must exhibit an isomorphism of
\(\mathbf V_k^{f,0}(\omega)(C)\) onto \(\mathscr G(\mathbf L)(C)\) that is
functorial in \(C\). By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2.5}{1.2.5}, \(\mathbf V_k^{f,0}(\omega)(C)\) is identified
with the set \(\operatorname{Hom}_c(\omega,\mathfrak r(C))\) of the continuous
\(k\)-linear maps from \(\omega\) to the radical of \(C\). This set is
naturally isomorphic to the set
\(\operatorname{Hom}_c(\omega\widehat\otimes_kC,\mathfrak r(C))\) of the continuous
\(C\)-linear maps from \(\omega\widehat\otimes_kC\) to \(\mathfrak r(C)\);
finally, since \(\omega\widehat\otimes_kC\) is a projective pseudocompact
\(C\)-module, the canonical map
\[
  \bigl(\omega\widehat\otimes_kC\bigr)^\dagger
  \otimes_C\mathfrak r(C)
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Hom}_c\bigl(
    \omega\widehat\otimes_kC,\mathfrak r(C)
  \bigr)
\]
is bijective. Since \((\omega\widehat\otimes_kC)^\dagger\) is identified with
\(\mathbf L(C)\), we see finally that \(\mathbf V_k^{f,0}(\omega)(C)\) is
canonically isomorphic to \(\mathbf L(C)\otimes_C\mathfrak r(C)\).\footnote{Translator's note: the map
is bijective because \(\omega\widehat\otimes_kC\) is a direct factor of a product \(C^\Lambda\), and every continuous
linear map from \(C^\Lambda\) to the discrete module \(\mathfrak r(C)\) factors through a finite subproduct;
and \((\omega\widehat\otimes_kC)^\dagger=\mathbf V_k^f(\omega)(C)\) is \(\mathbf L(C)\) by the duality of 1.2.3,
\(\mathbf L\) being flat. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, refers for these two
points to its \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.6.A}{0.3.6.A} and \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2.3.E}{1.2.3.E}.} The
implication \textup{(i)}~\(\Rightarrow\)~\textup{(ii)} therefore follows from
Proposition \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:3.2:prop}{3.2}.

\medskip
\noindent\textup{(ii)}~\(\Rightarrow\)~\textup{(iii)}: Let \(h\) be an isomorphism of
\(k[[\omega]]\) onto the affine algebra \(A\) of \(G\). Composing \(h\) with the
augmentation \(\varepsilon_A\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.1}{2.1})}, we obtain a homomorphism
\(\varepsilon_Ah:k[[\omega]]\to k\), which is determined by the restriction
\(\lambda\) of \(\varepsilon_Ah\) to \(\omega\); this restriction maps
\(\omega\) into the radical \(\mathfrak r\) of \(k\), and the map
\(x\mapsto x-\lambda(x)\) from \(\omega\) to the radical of \(k[[\omega]]\)
extends to an endomorphism \(\ell_\lambda\) of \(k[[\omega]]\). The equalities
\[
  \ell_\lambda\circ\ell_{-\lambda}
  =
  \ell_{-\lambda}\circ\ell_\lambda
  =
  \operatorname{id}_{k[[\omega]]}
\]
show that \(\ell_\lambda\) is an automorphism of \(k[[\omega]]\).
Consequently, \(h\circ\ell_\lambda\) is an isomorphism of \(k[[\omega]]\)
onto \(A\), and \(\varepsilon_Ah\ell_\lambda\) maps \(\omega\) to \(0\).
Replacing \(h\) by \(h\ell_\lambda\), we may therefore suppose that
\(\varepsilon_Ah\) vanishes on the closed ideal \(I\) of \(k[[\omega]]\)
generated by \(\omega\): in this case, \(h\) induces a bijection of
\(I/\overline{I^2}\) onto \(I_A/\overline{I_A^2}\); since
\(I/\overline{I^2}\) is identified with \(\omega\), we see that
\(\omega_{G/k}\) is projective. It is clear, on the other hand, that
\(\mathbf V_k^{f,0}(\omega)\) is infinitesimal, as is \(G\).

\medskip
\noindent\textup{(iii)}~\(\Rightarrow\)~\textup{(i)}: Let indeed \(\mathbf L\) be the
\(\mathbf O_k\)-Lie algebra of \(G\); this algebra has for underlying
\(\mathbf O_k\)-module the dual of \(\omega_{G/k}\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.1}{2.1})}; consequently,
\(\mathbf L\) is flat over \(\mathbf O_k\). If we identify
\(k[[\omega_{G/k}]]\) with the affine algebra of the formal group
\(\mathscr G(\mathbf L)\) \textup{(}this is possible by
\textup{(i)}~\(\Rightarrow\)~\textup{(ii)}\textup{)}, the identity morphism of
\(\mathbf L\) is associated canonically with a homomorphism of
\(\mathscr G(\mathbf L)\) into \(G\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.6.3:prop}{2.6.3})}, hence with a
continuous homomorphism of algebras
\[
  a:A\longrightarrow k[[\omega_{G/k}]].
\]
We must show that \(a\), which by definition induces an isomorphism of
\(\omega_{G/k}\) onto \(\omega_{\mathscr G(\mathbf L)/k}\),\footnote{Translator's note: indeed, the
homomorphism \(h\colon\mathscr G(\mathbf L)\to G\) associated with \(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbf L}\) satisfies
\(\operatorname{Lie}(h)\circ\tau_{\mathbf L}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbf L}\), and \(\tau_{\mathbf L}\) is an
isomorphism (Addition after 3.2); so \(\operatorname{Lie}(h)\) is an isomorphism, and so is the map
\(\omega_{G/k}\to\omega_{\mathscr G(\mathbf L)/k}\) induced by \(a\), whose dual it is (2.6, 1.2.3). The
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, obtains this from its additions 2.6.2~(iii) and
3.2~(ii).} is an isomorphism.
For this, consider a section \(\tau\) of the canonical projection of
\(I_A\) onto \(\omega_{G/k}\). By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2.5}{1.2.5}, such a section defines a
continuous homomorphism of algebras
\[
  b:k[[\omega_{G/k}]]\longrightarrow A.
\]
Let \(I\) be the closed ideal of \(k[[\omega_{G/k}]]\) generated by
\(\omega_{G/k}\), and filter \(k[[\omega_{G/k}]]\) \textup{(}resp.\ \(A\)\textup{)}
by the closures of the ideals \(I^n\) \textup{(}resp.\ \(I_A^n\)\textup{)}. The
composite \(a\tau\) is evidently a section of the canonical projection of
\(I\) onto \(\omega=I/\overline{I^2}\). Consequently, \(ab\) induces the
identity on \(I/\overline{I^2}\), hence also on the graded algebra associated
with \(k[[\omega_{G/k}]]\). It follows that \(ab\) is an isomorphism
\textup{([CA], V, \S~7, Lemma~1)}. Moreover, \(b\) induces an isomorphism of
\(I/\overline{I^2}\) onto \(I_A/\overline{I_A^2}\), hence a surjection of the
graded algebras associated with \(k[[\omega_{G/k}]]\) and \(A\)
\textup{(}as \(G\) is radicial, \(I_A\) is contained in the radical of \(A\), so
that the intersection of the \(\overline{I_A^n}\) is zero\textup{)}.\footnote{Translator's note: the
same lemma of [CA] then shows that \(b\) is surjective, the filtration of \(A\) by the \(\overline{I_A^n}\) being
separated. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, writes this step out, and compares
the lemma with \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.1.5}{5.1.5} below and with N.~Bourbaki, \emph{Alg\`ebre commutative},
Chap.~III, \S~2, no.~8, Th.~1 and its corollaries.} Since \(ab\)
is an isomorphism and \(b\) a surjection, \(b\) and \(a\) are
isomorphisms.

\medskip
Let us note finally that it is clear that \textup{(i)} or \textup{(ii)} implies
\textup{(iv)}, so that it remains to prove the implication
\textup{(iv)}~\(\Rightarrow\)~\textup{(ii)}: for this, we may suppose \(k\) local,
with residue field \(k_0\). Put then
\[
  G_0=G\widehat\otimes_kk_0,
  \qquad
  \omega=\omega_{G/k},
  \qquad
  \omega_0=\omega\widehat\otimes_kk_0,\ \ldots
\]
Since \(\omega_0\) is topologically free over \(k_0\), there exist a
topologically free \(k\)-module \(\omega'\) and a continuous \(k\)-linear
map \(f:\omega'\to\omega\) such that \(f_0=f\widehat\otimes_kk_0\) is
invertible \textup{(}\(f\) is the projective envelope of \(\omega\)\textup{)}. Since
\(\omega'\) is a projective pseudocompact \(k\)-module, \(f\) is the
composite of a continuous \(k\)-linear map \(g:\omega'\to I_A\) and of the
canonical projection of \(I_A\) onto \(\omega\). In turn, \(g\) induces a
homomorphism of topological algebras \(g:k[[\omega']]\to A\)
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.2.5}{1.2.5})}. Since \(\omega_0\) is identified with
\(\omega_{G_0/k_0}\) and \(k_0[[\omega_0]]\) with
\(k[[\omega']]\widehat\otimes_kk_0\),\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text writes
\(k[[\omega]]\) for \(k[[\omega']]\) in this paragraph, and ``topologically free over \(k\)'' for \(\omega_0\).
The module \(\omega\) is not known to be projective in advance, which is why \(\omega'\) is introduced, and
\(g\) is defined on \(\omega'\); the identification of \(k_0[[\omega_0]]\) with
\(k[[\omega']]\widehat\otimes_kk_0\) is made through \(f_0\). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, makes these corrections, obtaining \(f\) by lifting to \(\omega\) a pseudobasis
of the \(k_0\)-module \(\omega_0\), and adds that \(g\), once invertible, induces an isomorphism of \(\omega'\)
onto \(\omega\), so that \(\omega\) is projective after all (compare Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A},
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:7.4}{7.4}).} \(g_0=g\widehat\otimes_kk_0\) is invertible
because \textup{(iii)} implies \textup{(ii)} and the hypotheses \textup{(iii)} are
satisfied for \(k_0\) and \(G_0\).\footnote{Translator's note: more precisely, it is the proof of
(iii)~\(\Rightarrow\)~(i) that applies: there the morphism \(b\) defined by an arbitrary section of the
projection of \(I_A\) onto \(\omega_{G/k}\) was shown to be an isomorphism, and here, for \(k_0\) and
\(G_0\), \(g_0\) is the composite of the morphism defined by the section \(g_0\circ f_0^{-1}\) with the
isomorphism \(k_0[[\omega'_0]]\to k_0[[\omega_0]]\) induced by \(f_0\). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, refers to that proof.} Since, on the other hand, \(k[[\omega]]\)
and \(A\) are projective pseudocompact \(k\)-modules, \(g\) is invertible by
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.3.4}{0.3.4}.
