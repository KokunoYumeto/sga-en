
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 2.3.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:2.3.1}
{\itshape Let \(K\) and \(G\) be two formal \(k\)-groups, \(K\) being
topologically flat over \(k\). There then exists a canonical bijection
from the set
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Grf}/k}(K,G)
\]
of homomorphisms of formal \(k\)-groups from \(K\) to \(G\) onto the set of homomorphisms of unital \(\mathbf O_k\)-algebras
\[
  h:\mathbf H(K)\longrightarrow\mathbf H(G)
\]
for which the diagram
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIB:2.3.1:compatibility-diagram}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viib_15_95/diag_102}
\end{center}
is commutative.}

Since \(K\) is topologically flat, \(\mathbf H(K)\) is endowed with a
bialgebra structure \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.2}{2.2})}, and \(\Delta_{\mathbf H(K)}\) is its
diagonal morphism; in other words,
\[
  \Delta_{\mathbf H(K)}=\phi_K^{-1}\circ\delta_K.
\]
When \(G\) is also topologically flat over \(k\), our proposition
follows from the equivalence of categories defined in \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.2.1}{2.2.1}.

In the general case, we may suppose \(k\) Artinian and argue on the
algebras \(\mathbf H(K)\) and \(\mathbf H(G)=\mathbf H(G)(k)\). Let then
\(B\) and \(A\) be the affine algebras of \(K\) and \(G\),
\(\operatorname{Hom}_c(A,B)\) the set of continuous \(k\)-linear maps
from \(A\) to \(B\), and \(\operatorname{Hom}_k(B^\dagger,A^\dagger)\)
the set of \(k\)-linear maps from \(B^\dagger\) to \(A^\dagger\). It is
known that the canonical map \(f\mapsto{}^tf\) from
\(\operatorname{Hom}_c(A,B)\) to
\(\operatorname{Hom}_k(B^\dagger,A^\dagger)\) is bijective when \(B\) is
a projective pseudocompact \(k\)-module.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VII\textsubscript{B}, refers here to its 0.3.6.A and writes out the
verification. The bijection is applied to maps \(A\widehat\otimes_kA\to B\) and
\(A\to B\widehat\otimes_kB\), \(B\widehat\otimes_kB\) being projective together
with \(B\); the transpose of \(f\widehat\otimes f\) corresponds to
\({}^tf\otimes{}^tf\) through the canonical maps \(\phi_K\) and \(\phi_G\) of
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.3}{2.3}, \(\phi_K\) being bijective since \(K\) is topologically flat;
the transpose of \(\Delta_A\) gives the multiplication of \(A^\dagger\), and
that of \(m_B\) is \(\delta_K=\phi_K\circ\Delta_{\mathbf H(K)}\).} As this map is moreover
functorial in \(A\) and \(B\), one sees easily that \(f\) is compatible
with the diagonal morphisms of \(A\) and \(B\) if and only if \({}^tf\)
is compatible with the multiplication of \(A^\dagger\) and that of
\(B^\dagger\). Likewise, \(f\) is compatible with the multiplication of
\(B\) and that of \(A\) if and only if the diagram
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=1.25em,column sep=3.5em]
B^\dagger\otimes_kB^\dagger
  \arrow[r,"{}^tf\otimes{}^tf"]
  & A^\dagger\otimes_kA^\dagger
      \arrow[dr,"\mathrm{can.}"] & {} \\
{} & {} & (A\widehat\otimes_kA)^\dagger \\
B^\dagger \arrow[uu,"\Delta_{\mathbf H(K)}"]
  \arrow[r,"{}^tf"]
  & A^\dagger \arrow[ur,"{}^tm_A"']
  & {}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
is commutative \textup{(\(m_A\) denotes the linear map
\(a\widehat\otimes a'\mapsto a\cdot a'\) from \(A\widehat\otimes_kA\) to
\(A\))}.

Finally, if \(f\) is a homomorphism of unital algebras and is compatible
with the diagonal morphisms, then \(\varepsilon_B\circ f=\varepsilon_A\),
so that \({}^tf\) sends the unit element of \(B^\dagger\) to that of
\(A^\dagger\). Conversely, if \({}^tf\) is a homomorphism of unital
algebras that makes the above diagram commutative, then \(f\) is
compatible with the multiplications and the diagonal morphisms, and
\(\varepsilon_B\circ f=\varepsilon_A\). One deduces the equalities
\[
  \varepsilon_Bf(1)=1,
  \qquad
  f(1)\cdot f(1)=f(1),
  \qquad
  \Delta_Bf(1)=f(1)\widehat\otimes f(1).
\]
If \(c_B\) is the antipode of \(B\), it follows from \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.1}{2.1}~\textup{\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:2.1:item:iii}{(iii)}}
that \(f(1)\) has the inverse \(c_Bf(1)\) in \(B\), so that \(f(1)\) is
the unit element of \(B\).
