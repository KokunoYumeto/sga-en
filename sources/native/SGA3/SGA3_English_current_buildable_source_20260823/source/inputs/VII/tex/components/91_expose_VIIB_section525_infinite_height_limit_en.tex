
\subsubsection*{5.2.5}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIB:5.2.5}

\emph{It remains to consider the case where \(B\) has infinite height},
and where the canonical projection of \(I\) onto \(\omega\) has a
section compatible with the filtrations of \(\omega\) and \(I\). It
suffices to show that, for every \(r\), the map
\[
  \varphi_r:B'_r\longrightarrow B_r
\]
induced by \(\varphi\) is invertible.\footnote{Translator's note: as the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, points out, this suffices because \(B\) is the projective limit of the \(B_r\)
and \(B'\) that of the \(B'_r\), the ideals \(((x^{p^r}))\) being contained in the closures of the powers
\(\overline{I^{p^r}}\), which tend to \(0\) (0.1.2). Proposition 5.1 applies below as in 5.2.3, with
\(p^r\)-th powers in place of \(p\)-th powers; the re-edition obtains \(B_r\) instead as the affine algebra of
the homogeneous space of the inverse image of \(H^{(p^r)}\) under the iterated Frobenius morphism
\(G\to G^{(p^r)}\).} Now \(B\) is a direct factor of
the pseudocompact \(B\)-module \(A\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:1.3.2}{1.3.2})}. If one puts
\[
  L=\bigl((x^{p^r})\bigr)_{x\in\mathfrak r(B)},
\]
one can therefore identify \(B/\overline{BL}\) with a closed subalgebra
of \(A/\overline{AL}\); Proposition \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.1:proposition}{5.1} shows that
\(A/\overline{AL}\) is the affine algebra of a formal subgroup \(G_r\)
of \(G\) and that \(B/\overline{BL}\) is the affine algebra of a
homogeneous space of \(G_r\).

Put
\[
  M=I/\overline{BL};
\]
the canonical projection of \(B\) onto \(B_r\) obviously induces an
isomorphism of \(\omega=I/\overline{I^2}\) onto \(M/\overline{M^2}\),
which allows us to identify these two spaces. Let then
\(\omega^r_n\) be the image in \(\omega\) of the set
\[
  M_n=\{\,x\in M\mid x^{p^n}=0\,\},
\]
and let \(I^r_n\) be the inverse image of \(M_n\) in \(I\). The exact
sequence
\[
  0\longrightarrow I^r_n\cap\overline{I^2}
  \longrightarrow I^r_n
  \longrightarrow\omega^r_n
  \longrightarrow0
\]
and the equality \(I_n=\bigcap_rI^r_n\) show, by passage to the
projective limit, that \(\omega_n\) is the intersection of the
\(\omega^r_n\) \textup{(AB5\(^*\), \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:0.2}{0.2})}. Now, since Theorem 5.2 is already
proved for algebras of finite height, and hence for the \(B_r\), one
has \(\omega^r_n=\omega^{r+1}_n\) if \(n<r\) and \(\omega^r_n=\omega\) if
\(n\geq r\). It follows that \(\omega^r_n=\omega_n\) if \(n<r\) and
\(\omega^r_n=\omega\) if \(n\geq r\).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text writes
\(\omega^r=\omega\) for \(\omega^r_n=\omega\) in the last formula. The equality
\(\omega^r_n=\omega^{r+1}_n\) for \(n<r\) is seen as follows (the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}, adds a remark for it after 5.2.4): by the case of finite height, \(B_{r+1}\) is
isomorphic to a completed tensor product of algebras \(k[[S_i]]/((x^{p^i}))_{x\in S_i}\), \(1\leq i\leq
r+1\), the \(S_1\times\cdots\times S_n\) being the \(\omega^{r+1}_n\); \(B_r\) is its quotient by the closed
ideal generated by the \(x^{p^r}\), \(x\in S_{r+1}\), that is, the same tensor product with \(x^{p^r}\) in
place of \(x^{p^{r+1}}\) for \(S_{r+1}\); and in such a tensor product the image in \(\omega\) of the elements
\(y\) with \(y^{p^n}=0\), \(n<r\), is \(S_1\times\cdots\times S_n\), as one sees on the terms of degree
\(p^n\).}

In other words, the vector space \(\omega\) filtered by the subspaces
\(\omega^r_n\) is none other than \({}^r\omega\)
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.2}{5.2})}. A fortiori, the map \(\sigma^r\), composite of
\(\sigma:\omega\to I\) and of the canonical projection of \(I\) onto
\(M\), is compatible with the filtrations \((\omega^r_n)\) and
\((M_n)\) of \(\omega\) and \(M\). Since
\[
  k[[{}^r\omega]]
  \big/
  \bigl((x^{p^n})\bigr)_{x\in{}^r\omega_n}
\]
is none other than \(B'_r\), and \(\varphi_r:B'_r\to B_r\) is the
homomorphism induced by \(\sigma^r\), \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.2.4}{5.2.4} shows that \(\varphi_r\) is an
isomorphism, q.e.d.
