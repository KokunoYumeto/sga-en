
\section*{3. Coalgebras and Cartier Duality}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:3}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{3. Coalgebras and Cartier Duality}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{3.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:3.1}
Let \(S\) be a scheme (or, more generally, a ringed space). An
\(\mathcal O_S\)-\emph{coalgebra}
is a pair \((\mathcal U,\Delta_{\mathcal U})\) consisting of an
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(\mathcal U\) and a morphism of
\(\mathcal O_S\)-modules
\[
  \Delta_{\mathcal U}:\mathcal U\longrightarrow
  \mathcal U\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal U,
\]
called the \emph{diagonal morphism}, such
that:

\smallskip
\noindent
(i) \(\sigma\circ\Delta_{\mathcal U}=\Delta_{\mathcal U}\), where
\(\sigma(a\otimes b)=b\otimes a\).\footnote{Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, recalls that
the word ``cogebra'' is also used (N.~Bourbaki, \textit{Alg\`ebre},
chapter~III, \S11), and stresses that condition (i), cocommutativity, is
part of the definition here and in Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B}: it is
needed for the tensor product to be the product of two coalgebras (see
below), and hence for the notion of a coalgebra in groups (3.2).}

\smallskip
\noindent
(ii) The square
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:3.1-coassociativity-square}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_020_coassociativity_square}
\end{center}
is commutative.

\smallskip
\noindent
(iii) There exists a morphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules
\[
  \varepsilon_{\mathcal U}:\mathcal U\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S,
\]
called the \emph{augmentation}, such that the two composites
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:3.1-augmentation-sequences}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_021_augmentation_sequences}
\end{center}
are the identity morphism of \(\mathcal U\).

If \(\varepsilon_{\mathcal U}\) and
\(\varepsilon'_{\mathcal U}\) are two augmentations, then
\[
  \varepsilon_{\mathcal U}
  =(\varepsilon_{\mathcal U}\otimes\varepsilon'_{\mathcal U})
    \circ\Delta_{\mathcal U}
  =\varepsilon'_{\mathcal U};
\]
the augmentation is therefore uniquely determined by~(iii).

If \((\mathcal U,\Delta_{\mathcal U})\) and
\((\mathcal V,\Delta_{\mathcal V})\) are two
\(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebras, a \emph{morphism} from the first to the
second is a morphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules
\(f:\mathcal U\to\mathcal V\) for which the diagrams
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:3.1-coalgebra-morphism-diagrams}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_022_coalgebra_morphism_diagrams}
\end{center}
are commutative. Morphisms of coalgebras compose like morphisms of
\(\mathcal O_S\)-modules, so we may speak of the
\emph{category of \(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebras}.

This category has finite products: the final object is the
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(\mathcal O_S\), the diagonal morphism being the
identity; the product of two coalgebras
\((\mathcal U,\Delta_{\mathcal U})\) and
\((\mathcal V,\Delta_{\mathcal V})\) is the tensor product
\(\mathcal U\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal V\), the diagonal morphism
being the composite
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:3.1.0-product-comultiplication}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_023_product_comultiplication_sequence}
\end{center}
where \(\sigma(a\otimes b)=b\otimes a\). The canonical projections
of \(\mathcal U\otimes\mathcal V\) onto the factors
\(\mathcal U\) and \(\mathcal V\) are
\(\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal U}\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal V}\)
and
\(\varepsilon_{\mathcal U}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal V}\),
respectively.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, adds a verification, which we give
in our words. If \(f:\mathcal E\to\mathcal U\) and
\(g:\mathcal E\to\mathcal V\) are morphisms of coalgebras and
\(\varphi:\mathcal E\to\mathcal U\otimes\mathcal V\) is a morphism of
coalgebras with \((\mathrm{id}\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal V})\varphi=f\)
and \((\varepsilon_{\mathcal U}\otimes\mathrm{id})\varphi=g\), applying
\(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varepsilon_{\mathcal V}\otimes
\varepsilon_{\mathcal U}\otimes\mathrm{id}\) to
\((\varphi\otimes\varphi)\Delta_{\mathcal E}=
\Delta_{\mathcal U\otimes\mathcal V}\varphi\) gives
\(\varphi=(f\otimes g)\Delta_{\mathcal E}\). Conversely
\((f\otimes g)\Delta_{\mathcal E}\) is a morphism of coalgebras because
\(\mathcal E\) is cocommutative: the two sides of the required identity
differ by the exchange of the two middle factors of the iterated
diagonal of \(\mathcal E\). In particular, the morphism
\(\mathcal U\to\mathcal U\otimes\mathcal U\) defined by
\((\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal U},\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal U})\) is
\(\Delta_{\mathcal U}\).}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{3.1.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:3.1.1}
Let \(\mathcal A\) be a commutative \(\mathcal O_S\)-algebra which,
as an \(\mathcal O_S\)-module, is locally free and of finite type.
Put
\[
  \mathcal A^{*}
  =\mathcal Hom_{\mathcal O_S\text{-Mod.}}
    (\mathcal A,\mathcal O_S).
\]
The canonical morphism
\[
  \varphi:\mathcal A^{*}\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal A^{*}
  \longrightarrow
  (\mathcal A\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal A)^{*}
\]
is invertible. If
\(m:\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal A\to\mathcal A\) is the morphism
defining the multiplication of \(\mathcal A\), composition gives a
diagonal morphism
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:3.1.1-dual-coalgebra-sequence}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_024_dual_coalgebra_sequence}
\end{center}
This diagonal morphism plainly makes \(\mathcal A^{*}\) into an
\(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebra whose augmentation is the transpose of
the morphism \(\mathcal O_S\to\mathcal A\) defined by the unit
section of \(\mathcal A\). Moreover, it is clear that the functor
\(\mathcal A\mapsto\mathcal A^{*}\) is an anti-equivalence from the
category of \(\mathcal O_S\)-algebras which are locally free and of finite
type as \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules onto the category of
\(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebras which are locally free and of finite type as
\(\mathcal O_S\)-modules.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{3.1.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:3.1.2}
To every \(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebra \(\mathcal U\) there is
canonically associated an \(S\)-functor
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:3.1.2-spec-star-functor}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_025_spec_star_functor_sequence}
\end{center}
Indeed, for every \(S\)-scheme \(q:T\to S\),
\[
  q^{*}(\mathcal U\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal U)
  \simeq
  q^{*}(\mathcal U)\otimes_{\mathcal O_T}q^{*}(\mathcal U),
\]
so \(q^{*}(\Delta_{\mathcal U})\) makes
\(\mathcal U_T=q^{*}(\mathcal U)\) into an
\(\mathcal O_T\)-coalgebra. Thus, by definition and with an obvious
abuse of notation,
\[
  (\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U)(T)
  =
  \left\{
    x\in\Gamma(T,\mathcal U_T)
    \ \middle|\
    \varepsilon_{\mathcal U_T}(x)=1,\quad
    \Delta_{\mathcal U_T}(x)=x\otimes x
  \right\}.
\]

Sections \(x\) of \(\mathcal U_T\) plainly correspond to morphisms
of \(\mathcal O_T\)-modules
\(\xi:\mathcal O_T\to\mathcal U_T\). The conditions
\(\varepsilon(x)=1\) and \(\Delta(x)=x\otimes x\) say exactly that
\(\xi\) is a morphism of coalgebras. Hence also
\[
  (\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U)(T)
  =
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_T\text{-coalg.}}
    (\mathcal O_T,\mathcal U_T).
\]
In particular, if \(\mathcal A\) is a commutative
\(\mathcal O_S\)-algebra which is locally free of finite type as an
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module, there are isomorphisms
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  (\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal A^{*})(T)
  &=
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_T\text{-coalg.}}
    (\mathcal O_T,\mathcal A_T^{*})                                      \\
  &\simeq
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_T\text{-alg.}}
    (\mathcal A_T,\mathcal O_T)
  \simeq
  (\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A)(T)
  \end{aligned}
\]
and \(\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal A^{*}\simeq\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A\).\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A},
adds that \(\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U\) is a sheaf for the
Zariski topology, the two conditions on \(x\) being local on \(T\), and
even for the fpqc topology when \(\mathcal U\) is quasi-coherent: then
\(T\mapsto\Gamma(T,\mathcal U_T)\) and
\(T\mapsto\Gamma(T,(\mathcal U\otimes\mathcal U)_T)\) are fpqc sheaves by
faithfully flat descent, and the two conditions may be checked after a
faithfully flat quasi-compact base change.}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{3.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:3.2}
An \(\mathcal O_S\)-\emph{coalgebra in groups}, that is, a group in
the category of \(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebras, consists of an
\(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebra
\((\mathcal U,\Delta_{\mathcal U})\) and a morphism of
\(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebras
\[
  m_{\mathcal U}:\mathcal U\otimes\mathcal U\longrightarrow\mathcal U.
\]
Such a morphism is a morphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules making the
following diagrams commutative:
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:3.2-compatibility-iv-v}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_026_group_coalgebra_compatibility_iv_v}
\end{center}
The morphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebras \(m_{\mathcal U}\) must
moreover satisfy conditions \((\mathrm{ii})^{*}\),
\((\mathrm{iii})^{*}\) and~(vi) below:

\noindent
\((\mathrm{ii})^{*}\) The square
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:3.2-associativity-ii-star}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_027_group_coalgebra_associativity_ii_star}
\end{center}
is commutative.

\noindent
\((\mathrm{iii})^{*}\) There exists a morphism of
\(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebras
\(\eta_{\mathcal U}:\mathcal O_S\to\mathcal U\) such that the composites
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:3.2-unit-sequences-iii-star}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_028_group_coalgebra_unit_sequences_iii_star}
\end{center}
are the identity morphisms of \(\mathcal U\).

\smallskip
\noindent
(vi) There exists a morphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebras
\(c_{\mathcal U}:\mathcal U\to\mathcal U\) such that the composite
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:3.2-antipode-sequence-vi}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_029_group_coalgebra_antipode_sequence_vi}
\end{center}
is equal to \(\eta_{\mathcal U}\circ\varepsilon_{\mathcal U}\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{3.2.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:3.2.1}
The morphisms \(\eta_{\mathcal U}\) and \(c_{\mathcal U}\) of
\((\mathrm{iii})^{*}\) and~(vi) are plainly unique. Conditions
\((\mathrm{ii})^{*}\) and \((\mathrm{iii})^{*}\) simply say that
\(m_{\mathcal U}\) makes \(\mathcal U\) into an
\(\mathcal O_S\)-algebra whose unit section is the image under
\(\eta_{\mathcal U}\) of the unit section of \(\mathcal O_S\).
Condition~(iv) also says that the diagonal morphism
\(\Delta_{\mathcal U}\) is compatible with multiplication. Indeed,
\[
  \Delta_{\mathcal U}:\mathcal U\longrightarrow
  \mathcal U\otimes\mathcal U
\]
must be a homomorphism of coalgebras in groups, which also implies
the commutativity of the triangle
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:3.2.1-unit-triangle-v-star}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_030_group_coalgebra_unit_triangle_v_star}
\end{center}

On the other hand, as in every category, the antipode
\(c_{\mathcal U}\) is an isomorphism from \(\mathcal U\) onto the
opposite coalgebra in groups;
in particular, \(c_{\mathcal U}\) induces an algebra isomorphism
from \(\mathcal U\) onto the opposite algebra
\(\mathcal U^{\circ}\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{3.2.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:3.2.2}
Since the functor
\(\mathcal U\mapsto\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U\) commutes
with finite products, it carries a coalgebra in groups to a
group-valued \(S\)-functor. Indeed, for every \(S\)-scheme \(T\),
the elements
\[
  x\in\Gamma(T,\mathcal U_T)
\]
belonging to
\((\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U)(T)\) form a group under the
multiplication of the algebra \(\Gamma(T,\mathcal U_T)\), and the
inverse of \(x\) is \(c_{\mathcal U}(x)\).\footnote{Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, states
here explicitly what 3.3.1 below uses: if \(\mathcal U\) is finite and
locally free as an \(\mathcal O_S\)-module, the \(S\)-functor in groups
\(\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U\) is represented by the finite locally
free \(S\)-group \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal U^{*}\) (3.1.2 applied to
the algebra \(\mathcal A=\mathcal U^{*}\), whose dual is
\(\mathcal U\)).}

Let, for example, \(\mathcal L\) be an \(\mathcal O_S\)-Lie algebra and
\(\mathcal U(\mathcal L)\) its enveloping algebra, that is, the
sheaf on \(S\) associated with the presheaf assigning to every open
subset \(V\) the enveloping algebra
\[
  U\bigl(\Gamma(V,\mathcal L)\bigr)
\]
of the Lie algebra \(\Gamma(V,\mathcal L)\).

Every homomorphism from \(\mathcal L\) to the Lie algebra underlying
an \(\mathcal O_S\)-algebra factors uniquely through
the canonical morphism
\(\mathcal L\to\mathcal U(\mathcal L)\). Besides the functoriality
of \(\mathcal U(\mathcal L)\) in \(\mathcal L\), this universal
property also shows that the enveloping algebra of a product of Lie
algebras is identified with the tensor product of their enveloping
algebras.

In particular, the diagonal morphism
\(\delta:\mathcal L\to\mathcal L\times\mathcal L\) induces an
algebra homomorphism
\[
  \Delta:\mathcal U(\mathcal L)\longrightarrow
  \mathcal U(\mathcal L\times\mathcal L)
  \simeq
  \mathcal U(\mathcal L)\otimes\mathcal U(\mathcal L).
\]
The zero morphism \(\mathcal L\to0\) induces a homomorphism
\[
  \varepsilon:\mathcal U(\mathcal L)\longrightarrow
  \mathcal U(0)\simeq\mathcal O_S.
\]
The isomorphism \(x\mapsto-x\) from \(\mathcal L\) onto the opposite
Lie algebra \(\mathcal L^{\circ}\) induces an anti-isomorphism \(c\)
of the algebra \(\mathcal U(\mathcal L)\). One then checks readily
that the multiplication \(m\) of \(\mathcal U(\mathcal L)\) makes
\((\mathcal U(\mathcal L),\Delta)\) into an
\(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebra in groups with augmentation
\(\varepsilon\) and antipode \(c\).\footnote{Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, adds that
the \(S\)-functor in groups \(\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U(\mathcal L)\)
is not representable in general (for \(\mathcal L=\mathcal O_S\) over a
field of characteristic \(0\), its points over \(T\) are the
\(\exp(ax)\), \(a\) a nilpotent section of \(\mathcal O_T\), \(x\) a
generator of \(\mathcal L\)), whereas in characteristic \(p\) the
restricted enveloping algebra of a finite locally free \(p\)-Lie algebra
gives a finite locally free \(S\)-group (5.5 below).}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{3.2.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:3.2.3}
Let \(\mathcal U\) be an \(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebra in groups. One
checks easily that \(G=\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U\) is a good
\(S\)-group (\SGARefLink{sga3:ii:definition:4-6}{II~4.6}). Since
\[
  \Gamma(I_S,\mathcal U_{I_S})\simeq
  \Gamma(S,\mathcal U)\oplus d\,\Gamma(S,\mathcal U),
\]
an element \(u_0+du_1\) of \(\Gamma(I_S,\mathcal U_{I_S})\) belongs to
\((\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U)(I_S)\) if and only if
\[
  \Delta(u_0+du_1)=(u_0+du_1)\otimes(u_0+du_1)
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \varepsilon(u_0+du_1)=1,
\]
whence
\[
  \Delta u_0+d\,\Delta u_1
  =u_0\otimes u_0+(u_1\otimes u_0+u_0\otimes u_1)d
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \varepsilon(u_0)+d\,\varepsilon(u_1)=1,
\]
that is,
\[
  \Delta u_0=u_0\otimes u_0,\qquad
  \Delta u_1=u_1\otimes u_0+u_0\otimes u_1,\qquad
  \varepsilon(u_0)=1,\qquad
  \varepsilon(u_1)=0.
\]
In particular, \((\operatorname{Lie}G)(T)\) is the set of
\emph{primitive} elements of \(\Gamma(T,\mathcal U_T)\), that is, of the
elements \(u\) such that \(\Delta u=u\otimes1+1\otimes u\) (with the
obvious abuse of notation already pointed out).

The \(\Gamma(T,\mathcal O_T)\)-module structure of
\((\operatorname{Lie}G)(T)\) is plainly induced by that of
\(\Gamma(T,\mathcal U_T)\). On the other hand, consider two primitive
elements \(u\) and \(v\) of \((\operatorname{Lie}G)(S)\), and put
\[
  I=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_S[d]/(d^2),
  \qquad
  I'=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_S[d']/(d'^2).
\]
Since the composition law of \(G(I\times I')\) is induced by the
multiplication of the algebra \(\mathcal U_{I\times I'}\), one has
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  &(1+ud)(1+vd')(1+ud)^{-1}(1+vd')^{-1}\\
  &\qquad
    =(1+ud)(1+vd')(1-ud)(1-vd')\\
  &\qquad
    =1+(uv-vu)dd'.
  \end{aligned}
\]
\begin{quote}
\small
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:3.2.3-source-note-missing-prime}
\emph{Translator's note.} The French text (1970, and the re-edition) prints \(1-vd\), without a prime,
in the last factor of the middle expression. Since that factor is
the inverse of \(1+vd'\), and since the displayed result is
proportional to \(dd'\), this translation makes the required reading \(1-vd'\) explicit. Reinhold's English translation reproduces the printed missing prime.
\end{quote}
Hence the equality \([u,v]=uv-vu\), which proves that \(G\) is very good
(\SGARefLink{sga3:ii:definition:4-10}{II~4.10}).

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{A}, 3.2.3; our formulation). The verification
that \(G\) is good. Let \(T\) be an \(S\)-scheme and \(\mathcal M\) a free
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module with basis \(d_1,\ldots,d_r\). As
\(I_T(\mathcal M)=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal O_T\oplus\mathcal M_T)\) is
affine over \(T\), the sections of \(\mathcal U\) over
\(I_T(\mathcal M)\) are the sums \(u_0+\sum_iu_id_i\) with
\(u_0,u_i\in\Gamma(T,\mathcal U_T)\), and the computation above, made
with the \(r\) variables \(d_i\) (whose products vanish), shows that such
a sum lies in \(G(I_T(\mathcal M))\) if and only if \(u_0\in G(T)\) and
\(\varepsilon(u_i)=0\), \(\Delta u_i=u_i\otimes u_0+u_0\otimes u_i\) for
every \(i\). These conditions bear on each \(u_i\) separately; hence, for
a second free module \(\mathcal N\) of finite rank, the map from
\(G(I_T(\mathcal M\oplus\mathcal N))\) to the fibre product of
\(G(I_T(\mathcal M))\) and \(G(I_T(\mathcal N))\) over \(G(T)\) is
bijective, which is condition (E) of \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:definition:3-5}{II~3.5}. Over \(u_0=1\) the
conditions say that the \(u_i\) are primitive; the condition
\(\varepsilon(u)=0\) is automatic for a primitive \(u\), since applying
\(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varepsilon\) to \(\Delta u=u\otimes1+1\otimes u\)
gives \(u=u+\varepsilon(u)1\). Thus the primitive elements of
\(\Gamma(I_T(\mathcal M),\mathcal U)\) are the sums with primitive
components, so that the natural map
\((\operatorname{Lie}G)(T)\otimes_{\mathcal O(T)}\mathcal O(I_T(\mathcal M))
\to(\operatorname{Lie}G)(I_T(\mathcal M))\) is bijective, that is,
\(\operatorname{Lie}G\) is a good \(\mathcal O_S\)-module (II~4.4); so \(G\)
is good (II~4.6). The computation of the bracket above, made over an
arbitrary \(T\), then shows that \(\operatorname{Lie}G\) is isomorphic,
as an \(\mathcal O_S\)-Lie algebra, to the functor which assigns to \(T\)
the primitive elements of \(\Gamma(T,\mathcal U_T)\) with the commutator
\(uv-vu\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{3.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:3.3}
Suppose finally that \(\mathcal U\) is an
\(\mathcal O_S\)-\emph{coalgebra in commutative groups}, that is, that
the triangle
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIIA:3.3-commutative-triangle-i-star}
  \input{native_diagrams/exp7/viia_00_04/diag_032_commutative_group_coalgebra_triangle_i_star}
\end{center}
is commutative, or equivalently that \(m_{\mathcal U}\) makes
\(\mathcal U\) into a commutative \(\mathcal O_S\)-algebra.
Conditions (i), (ii), (iii), (iv), (v), (vi),
\((\mathrm{i})^{*}\), \((\mathrm{ii})^{*}\),
\((\mathrm{iii})^{*}\) and \((\mathrm{v})^{*}\) then also say that
\(\mathcal U\) is a cogroup in the category of commutative
\(\mathcal O_S\)-algebras. In particular, if in addition \(\mathcal U\) is
a quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_S\)-module, \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal U\)
is a commutative \(S\)-group scheme.

A homomorphism of \(S\)-groups from \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal U\) to
\(\mathbb G_{m,S}\) (I~4.3.2) is then induced by a homomorphism of unital
\(\mathcal O_S\)-algebras
\[
  \varphi:\mathcal O_S[T,T^{-1}]\longrightarrow\mathcal U
\]
such that \((\varphi\otimes\varphi)\circ\Delta'=\Delta_{\mathcal U}\circ\varphi\)
(the diagonal morphism \(\Delta'\) of \(\mathcal O_S[T,T^{-1}]\) sends
\(T\) to \(T\otimes T\)). Such a homomorphism \(\varphi\) is determined by
the image \(\varphi(T)\), which must be an invertible element \(x\) of
\(\mathcal U\) such that \(\Delta_{\mathcal U}x=x\otimes x\); as
\(\varphi\) then necessarily commutes with the augmentations, one has
\(\varepsilon_{\mathcal U}x=1\) (the augmentation of
\(\mathcal O_S[T,T^{-1}]\) sends \(T\) to \(1\)). Therefore
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}
  (\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal U,\mathbb G_{m,S})
  \simeq
  (\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U)(S).
\]
Since this formula remains valid after any base change, we finally have
\[
  \operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U
  =
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}
  (\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal U,\mathbb G_{m,S})
\]
for every quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_S\)-coalgebra in commutative groups
\(\mathcal U\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{3.3.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIIA:3.3.1}
If one assumes moreover that \(\mathcal U\) is a locally free
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module of finite type, \(\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U\)
is also representable, and one has (cf.~\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:3.1.2}{3.1.2})
\[
  \operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal U\simeq\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal U^{*}.
\]
The functor
\(\mathcal U\mapsto\mathcal U^{*}
=\mathcal Hom_{\mathcal O_S\text{-Mod.}}(\mathcal U,\mathcal O_S)\)
therefore induces a duality\textsuperscript{\(\ast\)} of the category of
commutative \(S\)-group schemes which are finite and locally free over
\(S\) (this is \emph{Cartier duality}). By \SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:3.3}{3.3}, this duality associates
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(G,\mathbb G_{m,S})\) with an
\(S\)-group \(G\).

\begingroup
\renewcommand{\thefootnote}{\fnsymbol{footnote}}
\footnotetext[1]{A duality of a category \(\mathscr C\) is a pair
\((D,\varphi)\) consisting of a contravariant functor
\(D:\mathscr C\to\mathscr C\) and a natural isomorphism
\(\varphi:\operatorname{Id}_{\mathscr C}\to DD\), such that the
isomorphisms
