\textbf{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:paragraph:3-1}3.1.}---
Let \(S\) be a scheme, and let \(T\) be an \(S\)-torus acting linearly
(Exposé I) on a finite locally free \(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(F\).
For every character of \(T\), let
\[
  \alpha\in
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr.}}(T,\mathbb G_{m,S});
\]
we define a subfunctor of \(\mathbb W(F)\) by
\[
  \mathbb W(F)^\alpha(S')
  =
  \left\{
    x\in\mathbb W(F)(S')
    \ \middle|\
    t\cdot x=\alpha(t)x
    \text{ for every }t\in T(S''),\ S''\to S'
  \right\}.
\]
Then
\(\mathbb W(F)^\alpha=\mathbb W(F^\alpha)\), where \(F^\alpha\) is a
submodule of \(F\), locally a direct summand of \(F\), and hence also
locally free.
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xix:paragraph:3:1}

Indeed, the assertion is local for the fpqc topology, and we may
suppose that \(T=D_S(M)\), where \(M\) is a finite-type (free) abelian
group.  Then \(\alpha\) is identified with a locally constant function
from \(S\) to \(M\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:viii:corollary:1:3}{Exposé VIII, 1.3}), and, after restricting \(S\) if
necessary, we may suppose that this function is constant.  We are then
reduced to \SGARefLink{sga3:i:proposition:4-7-3}{Exposé I, 4.7.3}.

\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:definition:3-2}\textbf{\textit{\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:definition:3:2}{Definition 3.2}.}}---
\textit{Let \(S\) be a scheme, let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group scheme
smooth and of finite presentation over \(S\), with connected fibers,\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, omits
the hypothesis of finite presentation, here and in 4.7: it follows from the
others, since a group scheme smooth over \(S\) with connected fibres is of
finite presentation over \(S\) (Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, \SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-5.5}{5.5}).} and
let \(T\) be a torus of \(G\).  A character \(\alpha\) of \(T\) is called a
\emph{root} of \(G\) with respect to \(T\) if the following equivalent
conditions are satisfied:}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xix:definition:3:2}

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textit{(i)}]
\textit{For every \(s\in S\), \(\alpha_s\) is a root of \(G_s\) with
respect to \(T_s\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-10}{1.10}).}

\item[\textit{(ii)}]
\textit{\(\alpha\) is nontrivial on every fiber, and
\(\mathfrak g^\alpha(s)\ne0\) for every \(s\in S\).}
\end{enumerate}
(We write \(\mathfrak g=\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\), and let \(T\) act on
\(\mathfrak g\) through the adjoint representation of \(G\).)

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\textit{\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:3:3}{Lemma 3.3}.}}---
\textit{Let \(S\) be a scheme, let \(T\) be an \(S\)-torus, and let
\(\alpha\) be a character of \(T\).  The following conditions are
equivalent:}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xix:lemma:3:3}

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textit{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:lemma:3:3:item:i}(i)}]
\textit{\(\alpha\) is nontrivial on every fiber; that is, for every
\(s\in S\), \(\alpha_s\) is distinct from the unit character of
\(T_s\).}

\item[\textit{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:lemma:3:3:item:ii}(ii)}]
\textit{For every \(S'\to S\) with \(S'\ne\varnothing\),
\(\alpha_{S'}\) is distinct from the unit character of \(T_{S'}\).}

\item[\textit{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:lemma:3:3:item:iii}(iii)}]
\textit{The morphism \(\alpha\) is faithfully flat.}
\end{enumerate}

We have \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:3:3:item:ii}{(ii)}\(\Leftrightarrow\)\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:3:3:item:i}{(i)} by \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:5:2}{Exposé IX, 5.2}, and
\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:3:3:item:iii}{(iii)}\(\Rightarrow\)\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:3:3:item:i}{(i)}.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, proves the
equivalence of (i) and (ii) without Exposé~IX, 5.2. In our formulation: (ii)
gives (i) by taking for \(S'\) the spectrum of an algebraically closed
extension of \(\kappa(s)\); conversely, if \(s'\in S'\) lies over \(s\), the
character at \(s'\) is obtained from the character at \(s\) by a field
extension, and a character of a torus over a field that becomes trivial after
a field extension is trivial. It also observes that, by Exposé~IX,
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:5:3}{5.3}, when \(S\) is connected these conditions hold as soon as \(\alpha\)
is nontrivial on the fibre at a single point of \(S\).}  To prove that \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:3:3:item:i}{(i)} implies \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:3:3:item:iii}{(iii)}, we
reduce to the case in which \(T\) is diagonalizable and conclude by
\SGARefLink{sga3:viii:corollary:3:2:item:a:01}{Exposé VIII, 3.2(a)}.

\textbf{3.4.}---
Suppose, under the conditions of \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:definition:3-2}{3.2}, that \(G\) is reductive and that
\(T\) is a maximal torus of \(G\).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text does not require \(T\) to be
maximal here; the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, adds this
hypothesis, which \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:1-12}{1.12} needs. Without it the rank of \(\mathfrak g^\alpha\) can
exceed~\(1\): for the torus \(T\) of the matrices \(\operatorname{diag}(t,t,1)\)
in \(\mathrm{GL}_3\), the character \(t\) has the root space spanned by
\(E_{13}\) and \(E_{23}\).}  Then \(\mathfrak g^\alpha\) is locally free of
rank one (\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:1-12}{1.12}).  Moreover, if \(\alpha\) is a root of \(G\) with
respect to \(T\), so is \(-\alpha\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-10}{1.10}).  In particular, if \(G\) has
semisimple rank one, we have by \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:1-11}{1.11}:
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xix:paragraph:3:4}
