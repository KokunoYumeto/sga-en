\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:lemma:2-3}\textbf{\textit{\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:2:3}{Lemma 2.3}.}}---
\textit{Let \(S\) be a scheme, let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group scheme that
is smooth and affine over \(S\), with connected fibers, and let \(T\)
be a torus of \(G\).  The set of points \(s\in S\) for which
\(G_{\bar s}\) is reductive, of semisimple rank \(1\), with maximal
torus \(T_{\bar s}\), is an open subset \(U\) of \(S\).}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xix:lemma:2:3}

\SGAProof{Proof}
Since \(G\) and \(T\) are flat over \(S\), the function
\[
  s
  \longmapsto
  \dim(G_{\bar s}/\bar s)-\dim(T_{\bar s}/\bar s)
\]
is locally constant.  Let \(U_1\) be the open subset of \(S\) on which
this function has the value \(2\).

Consider now the Weyl group \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:6-3}{6.3})}
\[
  W_G(T)
  =
  \underline{\operatorname{Norm}}_{G}(T)/
  \underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(T).
\]
The function
\[
  s\longmapsto
  \text{the number of points of \(W_G(T)_{\bar s}\)}
\]
is lower semicontinuous \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:6-3}{6.3}~(iii))}.\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text refers here to
6.3~(ii); the semicontinuity is assertion~(iii) of 6.3.}  Let \(U_2\) be the set of points
of \(S\) at which this function is greater than \(1\); it is open.  It
follows from \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:1-11}{1.11} that the set of \(s\) for which \(G_{\bar s}\) is
reductive, of semisimple rank \(1\), with maximal torus \(T_{\bar s}\), is
\[
  U=U_1\cap U_2.
\]

\par\medskip\noindent
\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:remark:2-4}\textbf{\textit{\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:remark:2:4}{Remark 2.4}.}}---
\textit{The group \(W_G(T)_U\) is isomorphic to
\((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)_U\).}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xix:remark:2:4}

It is indeed étale and finite over \(U\)
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:6-3}{6.3}~(iii))}; since the automorphism functor of
\((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)_U\) is the trivial group, it is enough to
verify the assertion locally, and it is immediate whenever the algebra
defining \(W_G(T)_U\) is a free \(\mathcal O_U\)-module.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, details
both steps. In our formulation: for \(s\in U\), \(W_G(T)_{\bar s}\) has exactly
two points by \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:1:11}{1.11}~(v); so the number of geometric points is constant on
\(U\), and \(W_G(T)_U\) is étale and finite over \(U\) by 6.3 (compare SGA~1,
Exposé~I, 10.9). Over a small enough affine open \(\operatorname{Spec}(R)\) of
\(U\), its algebra is then \(A=R\oplus Rx\), free of rank~\(2\), with \(x\) a
generator of the augmentation ideal, so that \(x^2=ax\) for some \(a\in R\). As
\(A=R[x]/(x^2-ax)\) is étale over \(R\), its discriminant \(a^2\) is
invertible; then \(a^{-1}x\) is an idempotent, \(A\simeq R\times R\), and the
factor where \(a^{-1}x=1\) is a section of order~\(2\), which gives
\((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)_U\simeq W_G(T)_U\). Compare J.~Tate and F.~Oort,
\textit{Group schemes of prime order}, Ann.\ Sci.\ École Norm.\ Sup.\ (4)
\textbf{3} (1970), 1--21.}
