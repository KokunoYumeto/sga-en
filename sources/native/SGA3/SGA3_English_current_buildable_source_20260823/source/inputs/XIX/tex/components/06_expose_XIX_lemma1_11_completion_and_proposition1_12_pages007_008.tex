Let us finally prove (i)\(\Rightarrow\)(v).  If \(Q\) is the radical of
\(G\), then
\(\dim(T)-\dim(Q)=1\), and \(Q\) is central in \(G\).  Hence
\(G=\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(Q)\), which proves that \(Q\)
is a singular torus.  By \emph{Bible}, 11-07, Theorem 2,
\[
  W_G(T)=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)_k,
\]
and, by \emph{Bible}, 12-01, Lemma 1,
\(\dim(G)-\dim(T)=2\).  Thus \(G\) has at most two roots relative to
\(T\), while it has at least two, opposite to one another, by \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-10}{1.10}.

\par\medskip\noindent
\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:proposition:1-12}\textbf{\textit{\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:1:12}{Proposition 1.12}.}}---
\textit{Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field, \(G\) a smooth
connected \(k\)-group, \(T\) a torus of \(G\), and \(R\) the set of
roots of \(G\) relative to \(T\).  Write
\[
  \mathfrak g
  =
  \mathfrak g^{0}
  \oplus
  \bigoplus_{\alpha\in R}\mathfrak g^{\alpha},
  \qquad
  \mathfrak g^{\alpha}\ne0,
\]
for the decomposition of \(\mathfrak g\) under
\(\operatorname{Ad}(T)\).  For every \(\alpha\in R\), let \(T_{\alpha}\) be the maximal torus of
\(\operatorname{Ker}(\alpha)\), and put
\[
  Z_{\alpha}=\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(T_{\alpha}).
\]
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:proposition:1:12:item:i}(i)] \(G\) is affine and reductive, and \(T\) is maximal.
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:proposition:1:12:item:ii}(ii)] \(\mathfrak g^{0}=\mathfrak t\), and every \(Z_{\alpha}\),
  for \(\alpha\in R\), is reductive.
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:proposition:1:12:item:iii}(iii)] \(\mathfrak g^{0}=\mathfrak t\); every
  \(\mathfrak g^{\alpha}\), for \(\alpha\in R\), has dimension \(1\);
  if \(\alpha,\beta\in R\) and \(\beta=q\alpha\) for some
  \(q\in\mathbb Q\), then \(q=\pm1\); and, for every \(\alpha\in R\),
  there exists \(w_{\alpha}\in G(k)\) which normalizes \(T\),
  centralizes \(T_{\alpha}\), but does not centralize \(T\).
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, under these conditions every \(Z_{\alpha}\) has semisimple
rank \(1\), and
\[
  \operatorname{Lie}(Z_{\alpha})
  =
  \mathfrak t\oplus
  \mathfrak g^{\alpha}\oplus
  \mathfrak g^{-\alpha}.
\]}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xix:proposition:1:12}

\SGAProof{Proof}
If \(G\) is affine and reductive and \(T\) is maximal, every
\(Z_{\alpha}\) is affine and reductive (\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:definition:1-6-1}{1.6}), with maximal torus \(T\);
moreover, \(T\) is its own centralizer (\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:definition:1-6-1}{1.6}), so
\(\mathfrak g^{0}=\mathfrak t\), which proves
\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:1:12:item:i}{(i)}\(\Rightarrow\)\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:1:12:item:ii}{(ii)}.

On the other hand, \(\mathfrak g^{0}=\mathfrak t\) implies that \(T\) is
maximal and \(G\) affine (\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-10}{1.10}), so that every \(Z_{\alpha}\) is affine
(\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-3}{1.3}).  In any case,
\SGARefLink{sga3:ii:lemma:5-2-2}{Exposé II, 5.2.2} gives
\[
  \operatorname{Lie}(Z_{\alpha})
  =
  \mathfrak g^{T_{\alpha}}
  =
  \mathfrak g^{0}
  \oplus
  \bigoplus_{\beta\in R\cap\mathbb Q\alpha}
    \mathfrak g^{\beta}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
  \operatorname{Lie}(Z_{\alpha})
  \supset
  \mathfrak t\oplus
  \mathfrak g^{\alpha}\oplus
  \mathfrak g^{-\alpha},
\]
so \(Z_{\alpha}\ne T\); by \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:1-11}{1.11},\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, makes
explicit how 1.11 is applied: to the group \(Z_{\alpha}\), which is smooth,
affine and connected (\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-3}{1.3}), and its torus \(T\), in which the maximal
torus \(T_{\alpha}\) of the kernel of the nontrivial character \(\alpha\) is a
subtorus of codimension~\(1\), central in its centralizer \(Z_{\alpha}\).} this gives at once the equivalence of
\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:1:12:item:ii}{(ii)} and \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:1:12:item:iii}{(iii)}, together with the additional assertions.

It remains to prove \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:1:12:item:ii}{(ii)}\(\Rightarrow\)\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:1:12:item:i}{(i)}.  We already know that \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:1:12:item:ii}{(ii)}
implies that \(G\) is affine.  Let \(U\) be
the unipotent radical of \(G\).  It is invariant in \(G\), and its Lie
algebra is invariant under \(\operatorname{Ad}(T)\); hence
\[
  \operatorname{Lie}(U)
  =
  \bigl(\operatorname{Lie}(U)\cap\mathfrak g^{0}\bigr)
  \oplus
  \bigoplus_{\alpha\in R}
    \bigl(\operatorname{Lie}(U)\cap\mathfrak g^{\alpha}\bigr).
\]
By \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-3}{1.3},
\[
\begin{aligned}
  U\cap T
    &=
    U\cap\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(T)
     =
    \operatorname{rad}^{u}
      \bigl(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(T)\bigr)
     =
    \operatorname{rad}^{u}(T)
     =
    \{e\},\\
  U\cap Z_{\alpha}
    &=
    U\cap\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(T_{\alpha})
     =
    \operatorname{rad}^{u}
      \bigl(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(T_{\alpha})\bigr)
     =
    \operatorname{rad}^{u}(Z_{\alpha})
     =
    \{e\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
It follows that\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, recalls the fact used
here: for subgroup schemes \(U\) and \(L\) of \(G\),
\(\operatorname{Lie}(U\cap L)=\operatorname{Lie}(U)\cap\operatorname{Lie}(L)\).
Indeed \(U\cap L=U\times_GL\), and the Lie algebra, the kernel of the reduction
map on points with values in the dual numbers, commutes with fibre products.}
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{Lie}(U)\cap\mathfrak g^{0}
    &=
    \operatorname{Lie}(U)\cap\mathfrak t
     =
    \operatorname{Lie}(U\cap T)
     =
    0,\\
  \operatorname{Lie}(U)\cap\mathfrak g^{\alpha}
    &\subset
    \operatorname{Lie}(U)\cap\operatorname{Lie}(Z_{\alpha})
     =
    \operatorname{Lie}(U\cap Z_{\alpha})
     =
    0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{Lie}(U)=0\) and \(U=\{e\}\).

\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:corollary:1-13}\textbf{\textit{\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:corollary:1:13}{Corollary 1.13}.}}---
\textit{Let \(k\), \(G\), \(T\), \(R\), and
\[
  \mathfrak g
  =
  \mathfrak g^{0}
  \oplus
  \bigoplus_{\alpha\in R}\mathfrak g^{\alpha}
\]
be as in \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:1-12}{Proposition 1.12}.  For every \(\alpha\in R\), let
\(T_{\alpha}\) be the maximal torus of \(\operatorname{Ker}(\alpha)\)
and put
\(Z_{\alpha}=\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(T_{\alpha})\).
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \(G\) is affine and semisimple, and \(T\) is maximal.
\item[(ii)] \(\mathfrak g^{0}=\mathfrak t\), every \(Z_{\alpha}\) is
  reductive, and
  \(\bigcap_{\alpha\in R}\operatorname{Ker}(\alpha)\) is finite.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, adds a
proof. In our formulation: by \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:1-12}{1.12}, each of the two conditions implies
that \(G\) is affine and reductive, that \(T\) is maximal and that
\(\mathfrak g^{0}=\mathfrak t\). Then the center of \(G\) is
\(\bigcap_{\alpha\in R}\operatorname{Ker}(\alpha)\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-10}{1.10}), and
\(\operatorname{rad}(G)\) is its maximal torus (\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:definition:1-6-1}{1.6}), which is trivial if
and only if this diagonalizable group is finite; that is, \(G\) is semisimple
(\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-8}{1.8}) if and only if
\(\bigcap_{\alpha\in R}\operatorname{Ker}(\alpha)\) is finite.}
\end{enumerate}}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xix:corollary:1:13}
