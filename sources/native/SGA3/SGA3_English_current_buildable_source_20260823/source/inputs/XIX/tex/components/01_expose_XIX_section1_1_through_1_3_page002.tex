
All the results of the \emph{Bible} (\emph{loc.\ cit.}) concerning semisimple
groups hold more generally for reductive groups (defined below), and their
proofs are identical after the following harmless modifications:
\begin{itemize}
\item 9-06, Definition 3 (!), see below;
\item 12-09, Theorem 2(e): omit ``finite'';
\item 13-08, Theorem 2: replace ``rank'' by ``semisimple rank'';
\item 13-12, Corollary 2 to Theorem 3: replace ``rank'' by
  ``reductive rank''.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-2}\textbf{1.2.}\quad
Let \(G\) be a smooth affine connected \(k\)-group.  The \emph{radical} of
\(G\) (\emph{Bible}, 9-06, Proposition 2)
is the reduced subgroup associated with the identity component of the
intersection of the Borel subgroups of \(G\); it is also the largest smooth
connected solvable normal subgroup of \(G\).  We denote it by
\(\operatorname{rad}(G)\).

The \emph{unipotent radical} of \(G\) is the unipotent part of the radical of
\(G\); it is also the largest smooth connected unipotent normal subgroup of
\(G\).  We denote it by \(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\).

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-3}\textbf{1.3.}\quad
If \(G\) is a smooth affine connected \(k\)-group and \(Q\) is a torus
of \(G\), then the centralizer
\(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(Q)\) of \(Q\) in \(G\) is a closed
subgroup of \(G\),\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, cites
\SGARefLink{sga3:viii:corollary:6:7}{Exposé~VIII, 6.7}, for the representability of this centralizer by a
closed subgroup.} smooth (\SGARefLink{sga3:xi:corollary:2:4}{Exposé XI, 2.4}), and connected
(\emph{Bible}, 6-14, Theorem 6).

We have the fundamental relation
\[
  \operatorname{rad}^{u}\!\bigl(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(Q)\bigr)
  =
  \operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\cap
  \underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(Q).
\]

Indeed, by the \emph{Bible}, 12-09, Corollary to Theorem 1,
\[
  \operatorname{rad}^{u}\!\bigl(
    \underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(Q)\bigr)(k)
  \subset
  \operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)(k).
\]
To prove the preceding equality, it is therefore enough to see that
\(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\cap\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(Q)\)
is smooth and connected.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, spells out why this is
enough. In our formulation: \(U=\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\cap
\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(Q)\) is a normal unipotent subgroup of
\(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(Q)\); if it is smooth and connected, it
is contained in the largest such subgroup,
\(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(Q))\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-2}{1.2}). Conversely,
\(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(Q))\) is reduced
and, by the inclusion of \(k\)-points above, its \(k\)-points lie in the
closed subgroup \(U\); hence it is contained in \(U\).}  If we let \(Q\) act on
\(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\) by inner automorphisms, the preceding group
is none other than the scheme of invariants of this action.  Now:
