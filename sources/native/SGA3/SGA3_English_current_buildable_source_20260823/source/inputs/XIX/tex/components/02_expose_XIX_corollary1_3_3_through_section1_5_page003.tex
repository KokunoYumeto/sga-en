

\par\medskip\noindent
\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:lemma:1-4}\textbf{\textit{\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:1-4}{Lemma 1.4}.}}---
\textit{Let \(S\) be a scheme, \(Q\) an \(S\)-torus, and \(H\) an \(S\)-group
scheme on which \(Q\) acts.  Suppose \(H\) separated over \(S\), so that the
functor of invariants \(H^{Q}\) is representable
\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:viii:examples:6:5:item:d:01}{Exposé VIII, 6.5(d)})}.  If \(H\) is smooth over \(S\)
\textup{(resp. smooth, affine, and with connected fibers over \(S\))}, then
so is \(H^{Q}\).}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xix:lemma:1:4}

\SGAProof{Proof}
Indeed, consider the semidirect product \(G=H\cdot Q\).  It is smooth over
\(S\) \textup{(resp. smooth, affine, and with connected fibers over \(S\))}.
Obviously
\[
  \underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(Q)=H^{Q}\cdot Q,
\]
and this group is smooth over \(S\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:xi:corollary:2:4}{Exposé XI, 2.4}) \textup{(resp. smooth
and affine over \(S\), with connected fibers
(\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-3}{1.3}))}.  Since \(Q\) is smooth, affine, and has connected
fibers over \(S\), we are done.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, states the
lemma in two parts and details the proof. In our formulation: since \(H\) is
separated over \(S\), \(H^{Q}\) is represented by a closed subscheme of \(H\),
affine over \(S\) when \(H\) is. As \(Q\) acts trivially on \(H^{Q}\), the
product \(H^{Q}\times_SQ\to\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_G(Q)\) is an
isomorphism of \(S\)-schemes, and the unit section of \(Q\) gives a closed
immersion of finite presentation \(H^{Q}\to H^{Q}\times_SQ\), with the first
projection as retraction. Hence, if \(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_G(Q)\)
is smooth over \(S\), so is \(H^{Q}\): it is locally of finite presentation
over \(S\), and formally smooth as a retract of a formally smooth
\(S\)-scheme. Finally, \((H^{Q})_{\bar s}\) is the image under the first
projection of \(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G_{\bar s}}(Q_{\bar s})\),
which is connected by \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-3}{1.3}; so it is connected.}
\hfill\(\square\)

Let us record one special case of the preceding result: if \(G\) is a smooth
affine connected \(k\)-group and \(T\) is a maximal torus of \(G\), then
\[
  \underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(T)
  =
  T\cdot\bigl(
    \underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(T)\cap
    \operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\bigr).
\]
Indeed (\emph{Bible}, 6-14, Theorem 6),
\(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(T)\) is solvable and hence is the
semidirect product of its maximal torus \(T\) and its unipotent radical.

By the density theorem (\emph{Bible}, 6-13, Theorem 5), it follows that, if
\(G\) is a smooth affine connected \(k\)-group, the union of the
\(T\cdot\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\), as \(T\) ranges over the maximal tori of
\(G\), is dense in \(G\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, records the
stronger form given by the density theorem: the union of the centralizers
\(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_G(T)\) of the maximal tori \(T\), and hence,
by the preceding relation, the union of the
\(T\cdot\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\), contains a dense open subset of \(G\).}

\par\medskip\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-5}\textbf{1.5.}\quad
Recall that the \emph{reductive rank} of a smooth affine \(k\)-group \(G\) is
the common dimension of its maximal tori.  We denote it by
\(\operatorname{rgred}(G/k)\) or \(\operatorname{rgred}(G)\).  The equality
\(\operatorname{rgred}(G/k)=0\) holds if and only if \(G\) is unipotent
(that is, \(G=\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\)), by the \emph{Bible}, 6-10,
Corollary 1 to Theorem 4.

If \(H\) is a normal subgroup of the smooth affine \(k\)-group \(G\), then the
quotient \(G/H\) is affine and smooth (Exposé VI).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, gives the precise
references: Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, \SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIB-11.17}{11.17}, for the affineness of \(G/H\),
and Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, \SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIA-3.3.2:item:iii}{3.3.2(iii)}, for its smoothness.}  Moreover (\emph{Bible}, 7-05,
Theorem 3(a) and (c)),
\[
  \operatorname{rgred}(G)
  =
  \operatorname{rgred}(G/H)+\operatorname{rgred}(H).
\]
