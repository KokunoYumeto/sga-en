\SGAProof{Proof}
By 5.6 and SGA 1, II 4.10, it is enough to prove that the
differentials of the functions
\[
\begin{gathered}
  a(d-1)-ec,\qquad
  b(d+1)-ec',\qquad
  b'(d+1)-e'c,\\
  e-2t(bc-ac'),\qquad
  e'-2t(b'c'-a'c),\qquad
  aa'-bb'-d
\end{gathered}
\]
at the points of the identity section of \(G\) are linearly
independent.  If the differential of a lower-case letter is denoted
by the corresponding capital letter, these differentials are
\[
  D,\qquad
  2B,\qquad
  2B',\qquad
  E+2tC',\qquad
  E'+2tC,\qquad
  A+A'-D,
\]
and they are indeed linearly independent modulo every
\((t-\lambda)\), \(\lambda\in k\).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has ``SGA~II~4.10'', that is,
SGA~1, Exposé~II, 4.10; it gives the last differential as \(A+A'+B\), and has
``modulo every \((t-a)\), \(a\in A\)''. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~XIX, corrects the last two points: at the unit section (\(a=a'=d=1\),
the other coordinates zero) the differential of \(aa'-bb'-d\) is \(A+A'-D\),
and the closed points of \(S\) are the ideals \((t-\lambda)\),
\(\lambda\in k\).}

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{\textit{\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:5:8}{Lemma 5.8}.}}---
\textit{For \(s\in S\), \(s\ne s_0\), the fiber \(G_s\) is connected
and semisimple.}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xix:lemma:5:8}

\SGAProof{Proof}
Indeed, \(\mathfrak g(s)\) is isomorphic to the Lie algebra of
\(\mathrm{PGL}_{2,\kappa(s)}\), we have \(G_s=(G_1)_s\), smooth over
\(\kappa(s)\), and it is known that the automorphism group of the Lie algebra
of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\) over a field of characteristic \(0\) is
\(\mathrm{PGL}_2\) itself.

\textbf{\textit{\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:5:9}{Lemma 5.9}.}}---
\textit{The fiber \(G_{s_0}\) is solvable and has two connected
components, of the following form:}
\[
\begin{aligned}
  G^0_{s_0}
  &=
  \left\{
  \begin{pmatrix}
    a&0&0\\
    0&a^{-1}&0\\
    c&c'&1
  \end{pmatrix}
  \right\},
  &
  G^-_{s_0}
  &=
  \left\{
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0&b&0\\
    b^{-1}&0&0\\
    c&c'&-1
  \end{pmatrix}
  \right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xix:lemma:5:9}

\SGAProof{Proof}
We have \(e=e'=0\), since \(t=0\) at \(s_0\).  The equations
\textup{(1)} to \textup{(5)} and \textup{(D)} can then be solved immediately.

\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:lemma:5-10}\textbf{\textit{\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:5:10}{Lemma 5.10}.}}---
\textit{The matrix}
\[
  w=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0&1&0\\
    1&0&0\\
    0&0&-1
  \end{pmatrix}
\]
\textit{is a section of \(G\) over \(S\), such that
\(w(s_0)\in G^-_{s_0}\).}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xix:lemma:5:10}

Let us now prove \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:5-5}{Proposition 5.5}.  We know that, if \(G^0\) denotes the union of the identity
components of the fibers of \(G\) (that is, the complement of
\(G^-_{s_0}\)), then \(G^0\) is an open subgroup of \(G\), smooth over
\(S\), by \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:5-7}{5.7}.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, adds the reference for
this step: Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, \SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIB-3.10}{3.10}, by which a group scheme smooth over
\(S\) along the unit section has its identity component \(G^0\) representable
by an open subscheme smooth over \(S\).}  Since, by translation, \(G\) is plainly smooth at the
points of \(w(S)\), \(G\) is smooth over \(S\).
