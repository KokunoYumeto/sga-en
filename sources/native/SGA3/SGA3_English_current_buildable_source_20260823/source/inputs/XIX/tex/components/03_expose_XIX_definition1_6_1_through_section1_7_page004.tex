\par\medskip\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:definition:1-6-1}\textbf{1.6.}\quad
A smooth affine connected \(k\)-group \(G\) is said to be \emph{reductive}
if \(\operatorname{rad}(G)\) is a torus, that is, if
\(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)=\{e\}\).  If \(G\) is a reductive \(k\)-group
and \(Q\) is a torus of \(G\), then
\(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(Q)\) is reductive
(\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-3}{1.3}).  In particular, if \(T\) is a maximal torus of \(G\),
then \(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_{G}(T)=T\).  It follows that the
center of \(G\) is contained in every maximal torus, and is therefore
diagonalizable.  The radical of \(G\) is then the unique maximal torus of
\(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}(G)\): indeed, the latter is a smooth
normal solvable connected subgroup, and hence is contained in
\(\operatorname{rad}(G)\); conversely, \(\operatorname{rad}(G)\) is a torus
normal in \(G\), and hence central (\emph{Bible}, 4-07, Corollary to
Proposition 2).
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xix:definition:1:6:1}

If \(G\) is reductive and \(\dim(G)\ne\operatorname{rgred}(G)\), then
\(\dim(G)-\operatorname{rgred}(G)\geq 2\).  Indeed, this difference is
always even (cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-10}{1.10} below).
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xix:remark:1:6:3}

\par\medskip\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xix:paragraph:1-7}\textbf{1.7.}\quad
Let \(G\) be a smooth affine connected \(k\)-group, and let \(H\) be a
smooth connected normal subgroup.  Then
\[
  \operatorname{rad}(H)
  =
  \bigl(\operatorname{rad}(G)\cap H\bigr)_{\mathrm{red}}^{0}
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \operatorname{rad}^{u}(H)
  =
  \bigl(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\cap H\bigr)_{\mathrm{red}}^{0},
\]
as is seen immediately.  In particular, if \(G\) is reductive, then so is
\(H\).

Let \(f:G\longrightarrow G'\) be a surjective morphism of smooth affine
connected \(k\)-groups.  Then
\[
  f\bigl(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\bigr)
  =
  \operatorname{rad}^{u}(G').
\]
It is first clear that \(f\) maps \(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\) into
\(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G')\).  On introducing
\[
  H=
  \bigl(f^{-1}(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G'))\bigr)_{\mathrm{red}}^{0}
  \supset\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G),
\]
we have \(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(H)=\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\); thus we are
reduced to the case \(G=H\), that is, to the case in which \(G'\) is
unipotent.  As \(T\) runs over the maximal tori of \(G\), the union of the
groups \(T\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\) is dense in \(G\).  Hence the union of
the groups \(f(T)\,f\bigl(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\bigr)\) is dense in
\(G'\).  But \(f(T)\) consists of semisimple elements, so \(f(T)=\{e\}\),
since \(G'\) is unipotent.  It follows that
\(f\bigl(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\bigr)\) is dense in \(G'\), and therefore
\(f\bigl(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G)\bigr)=G'\) (cf.\ \emph{Bible}, 5-13,
Lemma~4).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, writes this step
out. In our formulation: the image \(f(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G))\) is a
constructible subgroup of \(G'\); being dense in the irreducible \(G'\), it
contains a dense open subset of \(G'\), hence is an open subgroup of \(G'\).
An open subgroup is also closed, and \(G'\) is connected, so
\(f(\operatorname{rad}^{u}(G))=G'\).}  In particular, every quotient of a reductive group is reductive.

\noindent\textit{N.B.}\quad Under the same hypotheses one can prove that
\[
  f\bigl(\operatorname{rad}(G)\bigr)=\operatorname{rad}(G').
\]
