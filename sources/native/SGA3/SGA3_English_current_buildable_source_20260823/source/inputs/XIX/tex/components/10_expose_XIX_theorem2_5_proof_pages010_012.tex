\SGAProof{Proof}
Since \(G\) is flat over \(S\) at \(e_G(s_0)\) and \(G_{\bar s_0}\) is
reduced, \(G\) is smooth over \(S\) at \(e_G(s_0)\).  Since \(G\) has
connected fibers, we may, after restricting \(S\), suppose that \(G\) is
smooth over \(S\) (VI\textsubscript{B}, \SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIB-3.10}{3.10}).  By
\SGARefLink{sga3:xi:theorem:5:8:item:a}{Exposé XI, 5.8(a)}, and \SGARefLink{sga3:x:corollary:4-5}{Exposé X, 4.5} (see also \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:6-1}{6.1}),
there exist an étale morphism \(S''\to S\), a point \(s''_0\) above
\(s_0\), and a split torus \(T\) of \(G_{S''}\), maximal at \(s''_0\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, details these
steps. In our formulation: \(G_{\bar s_0}\), being reduced, is smooth
(Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-1.3.1}{1.3.1}), so \(G\), flat over \(S\) at \(e_G(s_0)\), is
smooth over \(S\) there (EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 17.5.1); one restricts \(S\)
to the inverse image under \(e_G\) of an open set on which \(G\) is smooth,
and applies Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, 3.10. Exposé~XI, 5.8(a), lifts a torus
of the fibre at a point; to have one that is maximal in \(G_{\bar s_0}\), the
re-edition uses \SGARefLink{sga3:xi:theorem:4:1}{Exposé~XI, 4.1}: the subtori of \(G\) of rank
\(r_0=\operatorname{rgred}(G_{\bar s_0})\) are parametrized by an \(S\)-scheme
smooth over \(S\), whose fibre at \(s_0\) therefore has a point over a finite
separable extension of \(\kappa(s_0)\) (EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 17.15.10(iii));
such an extension is the residue field of a point above \(s_0\) of an étale
\(S\)-scheme.}
Since an étale morphism is open, and the assertions in
\textup{\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:theorem:2:5:item:i}{(i)}} are local for the étale topology, we may thus suppose
that \(G\) has a split torus \(T\), maximal at \(s_0\).\footnote{Translator's note: in this proof and below,
\emph{split} renders the 1970 text's \emph{trivial}, said of a torus
isomorphic to \(\mathbb G_{m,S}^{\,r}\) for some \(r\). The Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Exposé~XIX, inserts before Theorem~2.5 a paragraph of reminders: when
\(G\) is smooth over \(S\), \(\mathfrak g=\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\) is a finite
locally free \(\mathcal O_S\)-module, on which \(G\) acts through the adjoint
representation (Exposé~II); and a torus \(T\) of \(G\) is maximal if
\(T_{\bar s}\) is a maximal torus of \(G_{\bar s}\) for every \(s\in S\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:xii:1.3}{Exposé~XII, 1.3}).}  Write
\(T=D_S(M)\), and let
\[
  \mathfrak g=\bigoplus_{m\in M}\mathfrak g^{m}
\]
be the decomposition of
\(\mathfrak g=\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\) under
\(\operatorname{Ad}(T)\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:i:proposition:4-7-3}{Exposé I, 4.7.3}).

Let \(R\) be the set of \(m\in M\), \(m\ne0\), such that
\(\mathfrak g^{m}(s_0)\ne0\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XIX, notes that
\(R\) is finite, \(\mathfrak g(s_0)\) being finite-dimensional; so the
intersection \(U\) below is open.}  Since \(G_{\bar s_0}\) is reductive,
\(\mathfrak g^{0}(s_0)=\mathfrak t(s_0)\) (we write
\(\mathfrak t=\operatorname{Lie}(T/S)\)), and hence
\[
  \mathfrak g(s_0)
  =
  \mathfrak t(s_0)
  \oplus
  \bigoplus_{\alpha\in R}\mathfrak g^{\alpha}(s_0).
\]
Since the modules in question are locally free, after shrinking \(S\)
we may suppose that the \(\mathfrak g^{\alpha}\) are free and that
\[
  \mathfrak g
  =
  \mathfrak t
  \oplus
  \bigoplus_{\alpha\in R}\mathfrak g^{\alpha}.
\]

Let \(T_\alpha\) then be the maximal torus of the kernel of \(\alpha\)
(recall, cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:xii:1.12}{Exposé XII, 1.12}, that a group of multiplicative type has a
unique maximal torus; this also follows almost immediately by descent,
the diagonalizable case being evident).  Put
\[
  Z_\alpha=\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_G(T_\alpha);
\]
the group \(Z_\alpha\) satisfies the hypotheses of \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:2-3}{2.3}, and its fiber
\((Z_\alpha)_{\bar s_0}\) is reductive, of semisimple rank \(1\), with
maximal torus \(T_{\bar s_0}\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:1-12}{1.12}).  By \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:lemma:2-3}{2.3} there is therefore an open
subset \(U_\alpha\subset S\), containing \(s_0\), on which
\(Z_\alpha\) has reductive fibers.

Set
\[
  U=\bigcap_{\alpha\in R}U_\alpha.
\]
By \SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:1-12}{1.12}, \(G_{\bar s}\) is reductive, with maximal torus
\(T_{\bar s}\), for \(s\in U\).  For every \(s\in U\), the set of roots of
\(G_{\bar s}\) relative to \(T_{\bar s}\) is identified with \(R\); hence
\[
  \operatorname{rgred}(G_{\bar s})
  =
  \operatorname{rg}(M),
  \qquad
  \operatorname{rgss}(G_{\bar s})
  =
  \operatorname{rg}(R)
  \quad\text{(cf. 1.10).}
\]
This proves \textup{\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:theorem:2:5:item:i}{(i)}} and the first assertion of \textup{\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:theorem:2:5:item:ii}{(ii)}}.
It remains to prove that the Weyl group \(W_{G_U}(T_U)\) is finite over
\(U\), that is \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:xix:proposition:6-3}{6.3})}, ``that it has the same number of points in
every geometric fiber''.

For this it is enough to observe that, for \(s\in U\), the geometric
fiber of this group is determined by the configuration \(R\subset M\):
it is the constant group associated with the abstract Weyl group of
this root system.  In particular it is independent of the point \(s\); cf.\
\emph{Bible}, 11-07, Theorem 2 (see also Exposé XXII, no. 3).
\hfill\(\square\)
