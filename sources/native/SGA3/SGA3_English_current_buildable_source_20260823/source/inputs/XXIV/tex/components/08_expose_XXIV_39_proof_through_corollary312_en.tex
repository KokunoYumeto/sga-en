
Remarking now that, for an arbitrary reductive \(S\)-group \(G\), the
\(S\)-scheme \(\operatorname{Bor}(G)\) is smooth and projective
(\SGARefLink{sga3:xxii:cor:5.8.3}{Exp.~XXII, 5.8.3}), and therefore has sections after a finite
surjective étale extension of the base (EGA~IV, \S24),\footnote{Translator's note: this section of EGA~IV did not appear. The Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXIV, gives an argument, indicated to its editors by O.~Gabber; see
the addition after 3.9.2.} one deduces:

\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxiv:3.9.2}
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Corollary 3.9.2.} ---
{\itshape Let \(S\) be a semilocal scheme and \(G\) a reductive
\(S\)-group. There is an étale, finite, and surjective morphism
\(S'\to S\) such that \(G_{S'}\) is quasi-splittable.}

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXIV; our formulation). The step quoted from EGA~IV, \S24, is the following
assertion, for which the re-edition gives an argument indicated to its editors by O.~Gabber:
\emph{if \(A\) is a semilocal ring, \(S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\), and \(X\to S\) is a smooth,
projective and surjective morphism, there is a finite étale surjective morphism \(S'\to S\) such
that \(X(S')\neq\varnothing\).} It applies to \(X=\operatorname{Bor}(G)\), which is surjective over
\(S\), a reductive group over an algebraically closed field having Borel subgroups. In our
formulation of the proof:

(a) \(S\) has finitely many connected components, each containing a closed point, and finite
étale covers of the components can be put together; so we may assume \(S\) connected. The relative
dimension of \(X\) over \(S\) is locally constant on \(X\); the part of \(X\) where it equals a given
\(d\) is open and closed, and its image in \(S\) is open (\(X\to S\) is flat and of finite
presentation) and closed (\(X\to S\) is proper). So we may assume that \(X\) has constant relative
dimension \(d\). As \(S\) is affine, \(X\) is a closed subscheme of a projective space
\(\mathbb P^n_S\).

(b) Let \(s_1,\dots,s_r\) be the closed points of \(S\), with residue fields \(k_i\). As \(X_{s_i}\) is
smooth of pure dimension \(d\), Bertini's theorem (J.-P.~Jouanolou, \emph{Théorèmes de Bertini et
applications}, Birkhäuser, 1983, I, 6.10) shows that the \(d\)-tuples of linear forms
\((\ell_1,\dots,\ell_d)\) on \(\mathbb P^n\) such that \(X_{s_i}\cap\{\ell_1=\dots=\ell_d=0\}\) is étale
over the base field contain a nonempty open subset of the affine space of such \(d\)-tuples over
\(k_i\), hence the complement of a hypersurface \(Q_i=0\), with \(Q_i\neq0\) of some degree \(m_i\). A
nonzero polynomial of degree \(m\) in \(N\) variables over a field with more than \(m\) elements
does not vanish at all the rational points (induction on \(N\)).

(c) Let \(m\geq1\) be larger than all \(m_i\). Since \(A\to\prod_ik_i\) is surjective, there is a monic
polynomial \(F\in A[t]\) of degree \(m\) whose reduction modulo the maximal ideal of \(s_i\) is
irreducible if \(k_i\) is finite (it is then separable) and has \(m\) distinct roots in \(k_i\) if \(k_i\)
is infinite. Then \(A'=A[t]/(F)\) is finite, étale and faithfully flat over \(A\), and semilocal; the
residue field of each of its closed points is either an extension of degree \(m\) of a finite
\(k_i\), with at least \(2^m>m_i\) elements, or an infinite \(k_i\).

(d) Let \(S'=\operatorname{Spec}(A')\). For each closed point \(s'\) of \(S'\), over \(s_i\), choose a
\(d\)-tuple of linear forms with coefficients in \(\kappa(s')\) at which \(Q_i\) does not vanish;
since \(A'\to\prod_{s'}\kappa(s')\) is surjective, these lift to linear forms
\(\ell_1,\dots,\ell_d\) with coefficients in \(A'\). Let \(Z\) be the closed subscheme
\(\ell_1=\dots=\ell_d=0\) of \(X_{S'}\). It is proper over \(S'\), and its closed fibres are étale and
nonempty (a closed subscheme of \(\mathbb P^n\) of pure dimension \(d\) meets every linear subspace of
codimension \(d\)). As \(Z\) is defined in \(X_{S'}\), which is smooth of relative dimension \(d\), by
\(d\) equations, which form a regular sequence in the local rings of the closed fibres at the points
of \(Z\), \(Z\) is flat, hence étale, over \(S'\) at these points (EGA~IV\textsubscript{3}, 11.3.8). The
points of \(Z\) at which \(Z\to S'\) is not étale form a closed subset, whose image in \(S'\) is closed
and contains no closed point; it is therefore empty. Thus \(Z\to S'\) is étale and proper, hence
finite, and it is surjective, its image being open and closed and containing every closed point.
So \(Z\to S\) is finite, étale and surjective, and the morphism \(Z\to X_{S'}\to X\) is a point of
\(X(Z)\).

\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxiv:3.9.3}
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Remark 3.9.3.} ---
Under the preceding hypotheses, let \(T\) be a maximal torus of
\(G\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:xiv:3.20}{Exp.~XIV, 3.20}). One may moreover assume that \(G_{S'}\)
is quasi-splittable relative to \(T_{S'}\): it suffices to apply the
preceding argument to the ``scheme of Borel subgroups containing
\(T\),'' which is finite and étale over \(S\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:xxii:corollary:5-5-5:item:ii}{Exp.~XXII, 5.5.5(ii)}).

\medskip\noindent\textbf{3.10.} ---
Let \(E\) and \(E'\) be two quasi-pinnings of the reductive
\(S\)-group \(G\). There is a unique inner automorphism of \(G\)
carrying \(E\) to \(E'\). Indeed, one reduces immediately to the
split case, where the assertion has already been proved (\SGARefLink{sga3:xxiv:1.5}{1.5}; it
suffices to observe that for an inner automorphism of \(G\), preserving
a pinning is equivalent to preserving the underlying quasi-pinning).
One concludes, as in \S1, that a


quasi-pinning of the reductive \(S\)-group \(G\) defines a splitting
\(h\) of the exact sequence:

\[
  \input{native_diagrams/exp24/001_split_autext_exact_sequence}
\]

the image of \(h\) being the subgroup of
\(\underline{\Aut}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(G)\) that preserves the
quasi-pinning.

Likewise, if \(G\) and \(G'\) are two quasi-pinned \(S\)-groups, one
defines naturally the subfunctor
\[
  \underline{\operatorname{Isom}}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr},\,q\text{-}\acute{e}p}
    (G,G')
\]
of
\(\underline{\operatorname{Isom}}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(G,G')\).
The projection from
\(\underline{\operatorname{Isom}}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(G,G')\)

\[
  \underline{\operatorname{Isom}}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr},\,q\text{-}\acute{e}p}
    (G,G')
  \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
  \underline{\operatorname{Isomext}}(G,G').

\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxiv:3.11}
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Theorem 3.11.} ---
{\itshape Let \(S\) be a scheme, let \(\mathscr R\) be a pinned reduced
root datum such that
\(\operatorname{\acute{E}p}_S(\mathscr R)\) exists
(cf.\ Exp.~XXV), and let \(E\) be its group of automorphisms.
Consider the following three categories:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xxiv:3.11:item:i}(i)] the category \(\mathsf{Rev}\) of principal Galois coverings
of \(S\) with group \(E\) (the morphisms are the isomorphisms);
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xxiv:3.11:item:ii}(ii)] the category \(\mathsf{Redext}\), whose objects are the
reductive \(S\)-groups of type \(\mathscr R\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:xxii:def:2.7}{Exp.~XXII, 2.7}), the
morphisms from \(G\) to \(G'\) being the elements of
\(\underline{\operatorname{Isomext}}(G,G')(S)\);
\item[(iii)] the category \(\mathsf{Q\acute{e}p}\) of quasi-pinned
reductive \(S\)-groups of type \(\mathscr R\) (the morphisms are the
isomorphisms preserving the quasi-pinnings).
\end{enumerate}
These three categories are equivalent. More precisely, there is a
diagram of functors, commutative up to
isomorphism:}

\[
  \input{native_diagrams/exp24/002_rev_qep_redext_triangle}
\]

We describe these three functors below, leaving to the reader the
verification that the diagram commutes.

\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxiv:3.11.1}
\medskip\noindent\textbf{3.11.1. The functor \(i\).} ---
This is the evident functor:
\[
  i(G)=G,
  \qquad
  i(f)=\text{the image of \(f\) under the morphism described in 3.10}.
\]

\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxiv:3.11.2}
\medskip\noindent\textbf{3.11.2. The functor
\(q\acute{e}p\).} ---
Let \(S'\) be a principal Galois covering of \(S\) with group \(E\).
The canonical homomorphism
\[
  h:E
  \longrightarrow
  \underline{\Aut}_{S'\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}
    \bigl(\operatorname{\acute{E}p}_{S'}(\mathscr R)\bigr)
  =A_S(\mathscr R)(S')
\]
defines an action of \(E\) on \(\operatorname{\acute{E}p}_{S'}(\mathscr R)\), an action which
respects the canonical quasi-pinning of
\(\operatorname{\acute{E}p}_S(\mathscr R)\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXIV, details this action. In
our formulation: for \(e\in E\), \(h(e)\) is the automorphism of the pinned group
\(\operatorname{\acute{E}p}_S(\mathscr R)\) defined by \(e\) \textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:xxiv:1.2}{1.2})},
extended to \(S'\); composing it with the automorphism of
\(\operatorname{\acute{E}p}_{S'}(\mathscr R)=\operatorname{\acute{E}p}_S(\mathscr R)\times_SS'\) given by
the action of \(e\) on \(S'\), one obtains an action of \(E\) compatible with its action on \(S'\),
which is the one used for the descent. It preserves the canonical Killing couple, and the
canonical quasi-pinning, which is \(X_\alpha\) on the copy of \(S'\) indexed by \(\alpha\) in
\(\operatorname{Dyn}=\Delta_{S'}\): \(E\) permutes these copies and these elements in the same way.}
Since \(S'\to S\) is a covering for the \textup{(fpqc)} topology, it
is an effective descent morphism for the fibered category of affine
morphisms, and we write
\[
  q\acute{e}p(S')
  =
  Q\text{-}\operatorname{\acute{E}p}_{S'/S}(\mathscr R)
\]
for the quasi-pinned \(S\)-group obtained by Galois
descent.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXIV, refers here to TDTE~I, p.~22, Example~1, and describes
\(q\acute{e}p(S')\) directly. In our formulation: it is the twist of
\(\operatorname{\acute{E}p}_S(\mathscr R)\) by the \(E\)-torsor \(S'\), that is, the quotient of
\(S'\times_S\operatorname{\acute{E}p}_S(\mathscr R)\) by \(E\) acting on both factors, on the second
through the automorphisms of the pinned group; the quotient exists because
\(\operatorname{\acute{E}p}_S(\mathscr R)\) is affine over \(S\). Its Dynkin scheme is the twist of the
constant Dynkin scheme \(\Delta_S\) by \(S'\), and the canonical Killing couple and quasi-pinning,
being stable under \(E\), descend to it.}

\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxiv:3.11.3}
\medskip\noindent\textbf{3.11.3. The functor
\(\operatorname{rev}\).} ---
Let \(G\) be a reductive \(S\)-group of type \(\mathscr R\). Write
\[
  \operatorname{rev}(G)
  =
  \underline{\operatorname{Isomext}}
    \bigl(\operatorname{\acute{E}p}_S(\mathscr R),G\bigr).
\]
This is a principal homogeneous bundle (\SGARefLink{sga3:xxiv:1.10}{1.10}) under \(E_S\),
that is, an object of \(\mathsf{Rev}\).

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXIV; our formulation). The re-edition adds a paragraph (3.11.4 there) sketching
the verification left to the reader. In our formulation:

(a) By the isomorphism at the end of \SGARefLink{sga3:xxiv:3.10}{3.10}, the functor \(i\) is fully
faithful.

(b) For reductive \(S\)-groups \(G_0\), \(G\), \(G'\), the composition of isomorphisms passes to the
quotients and gives a composition
\(\underline{\operatorname{Isomext}}(G_0,G)\times_S\underline{\operatorname{Isomext}}(G,G')\to
\underline{\operatorname{Isomext}}(G_0,G')\): for isomorphisms \(u\colon G_0\to G\),
\(v\colon G\to G'\) and sections \(g_0\) of \(G_0\), \(g\) of \(G\), one has
\(v\circ\operatorname{int}(g)\circ u\circ\operatorname{int}(g_0)
=v\circ u\circ\operatorname{int}(u^{-1}(g)g_0)\). This makes \(\operatorname{rev}\) a functor. It is
fully faithful: the bijectivity of
\(\underline{\operatorname{Isomext}}(G,G')(S)\to
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathsf{Rev}}(\operatorname{rev}(G),\operatorname{rev}(G'))\) can be checked
locally for the \textup{(fpqc)} topology, hence when \(G\) and \(G'\) are pinned, where it is evident.
In the same way, \(q\acute{e}p\) is fully faithful, by reduction to trivial torsors.

(c) For every object \(S'\) of \(\mathsf{Rev}\), the construction of 3.11.2 gives an
\(E\)-equivariant morphism
\(S'\to\underline{\operatorname{Isomext}}(\operatorname{\acute{E}p}_S(\mathscr R),q\acute{e}p(S'))
=\operatorname{rev}(i(q\acute{e}p(S')))\), which is an isomorphism, as one sees when \(S'\) is
trivial. Thus \(\operatorname{rev}\circ i\circ q\acute{e}p\) is isomorphic to the identity functor of
\(\mathsf{Rev}\); together with (a) and (b), this shows that the three functors are equivalences.
In particular, for every reductive \(S\)-group \(H\) of type \(\mathscr R\), the identity of
\(\operatorname{rev}(H)\) corresponds to a canonical element
\(u_0\in\underline{\operatorname{Isomext}}(q\acute{e}p(\operatorname{rev}(H)),H)(S)\); this is the
\(u\) of 3.12 below.

We develop one of the corollaries of \SGARefLink{sga3:xxiv:3.11}{3.11}.

\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxiv:3.12}
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Corollary 3.12.} ---
{\itshape For every reductive \(S\)-group \(G\), there is a quasi-pinned
\(S\)-group \(G_{q\text{-}\acute{e}p}\) and an ``outer isomorphism''}
\[
  u\in
  \underline{\operatorname{Isomext}}
    (G_{q\text{-}\acute{e}p},G)(S).
\]
{\itshape The pair \((G_{q\text{-}\acute{e}p},u)\) is unique up to a
unique isomorphism.}

Indeed, one may assume that \(G\) has constant type \(\mathscr R\),
and one takes
\[
  G_{q\text{-}\acute{e}p}
  =
  Q\text{-}\operatorname{\acute{E}p}_{\operatorname{rev}(G)/S}
    (\mathscr R).
\]

\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxiv:3.12.1}
\medskip\noindent\textbf{3.12.1.} ---
Observe that the datum of \(u\) allows one to define canonically the \(S\)-scheme
(\SGARefLink{sga3:xxiv:1.11}{1.11})
\[
  Q=
  \underline{\operatorname{Isomint}}
    (G_{q\text{-}\acute{e}p},G),
\]
which is a principal homogeneous bundle under
\(\operatorname{ad}(G_{q\text{-}\acute{e}p})\), and whose data are
``equivalent'' to those of the inner twisted form \(G\) of
\(G_{q\text{-}\acute{e}p}\). Moreover, \(Q\) is precisely the
``scheme of quasi-pinnings of \(G\),'' a definition that explains its
structure as a principal homogeneous bundle (on the left) under
\(\operatorname{ad}(G)\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:xxiv:3.10}{3.10}); compare \SGARefLink{sga3:xxiv:1.20}{1.20}.
