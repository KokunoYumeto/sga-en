\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:iv:lemma:4:4:5}\textbf{Lemma 4.4.5.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)]\SGARefTarget{sga3:iv:lemma:4:4:5:item:i:01} \textit{Suppose that, for every
    \(S\in\operatorname{Ob}\mathscr C\), the empty sieve of \(S\) is not a
    refinement of \(S\). Then the presheaf direct sum of a family of
    separated presheaves is separated.}\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text states (i) without the
hypothesis on the empty sieve; the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~IV,
adds it. It cannot be omitted: let \(\mathscr C\) have one object \(S\) and
one morphism, with the topology for which both sieves of \(S\), the empty one
and \(S\), are covering. A presheaf \(P\) is then separated if and only if
\(P(S)\) has at most one element, so \(h_S\) is a sheaf while
\(h_S\amalg h_S\) is not separated. The re-edition also remarks that the
presheaf direct sum of two sheaves need not be a sheaf; for instance, if
\(\mathscr C\) has an initial object \(\varnothing\) whose empty sieve is
covering (as for the Zariski topology on the category of schemes), every sheaf
\(F\) has \(F(\varnothing)\) reduced to one point, while
\((F\amalg G)(\varnothing)\) has two.}

  \item[(ii)]\SGARefTarget{sga3:iv:lemma:4:4:5:item:ii:01} \textit{Consider an equivalence relation in the category of
    presheaves}
    \[
      X\overset{u}{\underset{v}{\rightrightarrows}}Y,
    \]
    \textit{and let \(w:Y\to Z\) be the quotient. If
    \(u\times v:X\to Y\times Y\) is a monomorphism, if \(X\) is a sheaf and if
    \(Y\) is separated, then \(Z\) is separated.}

  \item[(iii)]\SGARefTarget{sga3:iv:lemma:4:4:5:item:iii:01} \textit{Consider an amalgamated sum in the category of
    presheaves, in which \(u\) and \(u'\) are monomorphisms:}
    \[
    \begin{array}{ccc}
      X & \xrightarrow{\ u\ } & Y\\
      {\scriptstyle u'}\big\downarrow && \big\downarrow\\
      Y' & \longrightarrow & V.
    \end{array}
    \]
    \textit{If \(Y\) and \(Y'\) are separated and \(X\) is a sheaf, then
    \(V\) is separated.}
\end{enumerate}

Assertion \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:lemma:4:4:5:item:i:01}{(i)} is proved at once, and \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:lemma:4:4:5:item:iii:01}{(iii)} without difficulty, by
noting that, for every \(S\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathscr C)\), the square formed
by \(X(S)\), \(Y(S)\), \(Y'(S)\) and \(V(S)\) is an amalgamated sum in the
category of sets. To prove \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:lemma:4:4:5:item:ii:01}{(ii)}, one must prove that two morphisms from an
\(S\) to \(Z\) that coincide on a refinement \(R\) of \(S\) are equal. Now every
morphism from a representable \(S\) to \(Z\) lifts, by the construction of
\(Z\), to a morphism from \(S\) to \(Y\). One then sees at once that it is
enough to prove the following: for every presheaf \(U\) and every pair of
morphisms \(f,g:U\to Y\) such that \(wf=wg\), there exists a unique morphism
\(h:U\to X\) with \(uh=f\) and \(vh=g\).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has ``avec \(uf=vg\)''.} For this it is enough to prove the
corresponding assertion for the composites of these morphisms with every
\(T\to U\), where \(T\) is representable; we may therefore suppose that
\(U=T\) is representable. But the diagram
\(X(T)\rightrightarrows Y(T)\to Z(T)\) is a cokernel in the category of sets,
\(X(T)\) being an equivalence relation in \(Y(T)\), which completes the proof.

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~IV; our
formulation).  Detailed proofs.
(i) Let \(R\) be a refinement of \(S\), and let \(x_1\in X_i(S)\) and
\(x_2\in X_j(S)\) be two elements of \(X(S)=\coprod_kX_k(S)\) with the same
image in \(\operatorname{Hom}(R,X)\). As \(R\) is not the empty sieve, it
contains a morphism \(T\to S\) with \(T\in\operatorname{Ob}\mathscr C\);
comparing the images in \(X(T)=\coprod_kX_k(T)\) gives \(i=j\). As \(X_i\) is
separated and \(\operatorname{Hom}(R,X_i)\to\operatorname{Hom}(R,X)\) is
injective, \(x_1=x_2\).
(iii) Since \(u\) and \(u'\) are monomorphisms, \(V(T)\) is, for every \(T\),
the union of \(Y(T)\) and \(Y'(T)\) glued along \(X(T)\); hence \(Y\to V\) and
\(Y'\to V\) are monomorphisms and \(Y\times_VY'=X\). Let \(R\) be a refinement
of \(S\) and \(v_1,v_2\in V(S)\) with the same image in
\(\operatorname{Hom}(R,V)\). If both come from \(Y(S)\), or both from
\(Y'(S)\), they are equal, \(Y\) and \(Y'\) being separated and
\(\operatorname{Hom}(R,Y)\to\operatorname{Hom}(R,V)\) and
\(\operatorname{Hom}(R,Y')\to\operatorname{Hom}(R,V)\) injective. If \(v_1\)
comes from \(y\in Y(S)\) and \(v_2\) from \(y'\in Y'(S)\), the restrictions of
\(y\) and \(y'\) to \(R\) define a morphism \(R\to Y\times_VY'=X\), which
extends to some \(x\in X(S)\), \(X\) being a sheaf; then \(u(x)\) and \(y\)
agree on \(R\), hence are equal, and likewise \(u'(x)=y'\), so \(v_1=v_2\).
(ii) Here \(Z(T)=Y(T)/X(T)\). Let \(z_1,z_2\in Z(S)\) agree on a refinement
\(R\) of \(S\), and lift them to \(y_1,y_2\in Y(S)\). For every \(T\to S\) in
\(R\), the restrictions of \(y_1\) and \(y_2\) to \(T\) are equivalent modulo
\(X(T)\); so the restriction of \((y_1,y_2)\in(Y\times Y)(S)\) to \(R\)
factors through the subfunctor \(X\) of \(Y\times Y\), and extends to an
element \((y'_1,y'_2)\) of \(X(S)\), \(X\) being a sheaf. As \(Y\) is
separated, \(y'_1=y_1\) and \(y'_2=y_2\); thus \((y_1,y_2)\in X(S)\) and
\(z_1=z_2\).
