\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:iv:lemma:4:4:6}\textbf{Lemma 4.4.6.}
\textit{A covering family is a universal effective epimorphic family.}

Since the notion of a covering family is stable under base change, it is
enough to show that every covering family is effective epimorphic. Let
\(\{F_i\to F\}\) be a covering family, and consider the presheaf \(G\), the
image of this family. There is a diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
 F_i \arrow[r] & G \arrow[r] \arrow[dr] & F\\
 & & a(G) \arrow[u,"\wr"']
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The family of morphisms of presheaves \(\{F_i\to G\}\) is effective
epimorphic, since this is checked argument by argument, and in the category
of sets an effective epimorphic family is simply a surjective family. As
\(G\to F\) is a monomorphism, the fiber products \(F_i\times_GF_j\) and
\(F_i\times_FF_j\) are the same. Moreover, for every sheaf \(H\),
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(G,H)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(F,H).
\]
It follows that the given family is indeed an effective epimorphic family of
morphisms of sheaves.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~IV, details
this proof. In our formulation: \(G\) is a refinement of \(F\), the family
being covering, and \(F\) is a sheaf, so \(a(G)\simeq F\) by 4.3.12; and if
morphisms of presheaves \(\varphi_i\colon F_i\to H\) agree on the
\(F_i\times_GF_j\), then for each \(S\) they define a map \(G(S)\to H(S)\)
through the surjection \(\coprod_iF_i(S)\to G(S)\), whence the effectivity of
\(\{F_i\to G\}\).}
