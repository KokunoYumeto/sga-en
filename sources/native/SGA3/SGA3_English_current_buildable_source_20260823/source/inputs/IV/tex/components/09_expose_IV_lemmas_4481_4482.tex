\SGARefTarget{sga3:iv:lemma:4:4:8:1}\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~IV;
our formulation).  The re-edition adds here two lemmas (its 4.4.8.1 and
4.4.8.2), which it uses in Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, \SGARefLink{par:sga3-VIB-8.1}{8.1} and \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-8.2}{8.2}.
(a) Let \(Q\subset P\) be presheaves of groups on \(\mathscr C\), with \(Q\)
normal in \(P\), \(P\) separated and \(Q\) a sheaf. Then the presheaf of
groups \(P/Q\colon S\mapsto P(S)/Q(S)\) is separated, so that
\(a(P/Q)=L(P/Q)\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:4:3:11:item:iv:01}{4.3.11(iv)}, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:theorem:4:3:14}{4.3.14}), that is,
\(\operatorname{Hom}(S,a(P/Q))=\varinjlim_{R\in J(S)}\operatorname{Hom}(R,P/Q)\).
Indeed, \(P/Q\) being a presheaf of groups, it is enough to show that an
element of \(P(S)/Q(S)\) whose image in \(\operatorname{Hom}(R,P/Q)\) is the
unit, for a refinement \(R\) of \(S\), is the unit. Let \(y\in P(S)\)
represent it: for every \(T\to S\) in \(R\), the restriction of \(y\) to \(T\)
lies in \(Q(T)\); so the restriction \(R\to P\) of \(y\) factors through
\(Q\), and extends to some \(q\in Q(S)\), \(Q\) being a sheaf. Then \(y\) and
\(q\) agree on \(R\), hence are equal, \(P\) being separated, and
\(y\in Q(S)\).
\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iv:lemma:4:4:8:2}(b) Let \(H\subset G\) be presheaves of groups on \(\mathscr C\). If
\(H\) is normal in \(G\), then \(LH\) is normal in \(LG\);
\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:iv:lemma:4:4:8:2:item:ii:01}if \(H\) is central in \(G\), then \(LH\) is central in \(LG\) (normality and
centrality being meant sectionwise, Expos\'e~I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:definition:2:3:6}{2.3.6}). Indeed,
\(LH\to LG\) is a monomorphism (see the note to the definition of the functor
\(L\)), and two sections \(h\) of \(LH\) and \(g\) of \(LG\) over \(S\) come,
\(J(S)\) being filtered, from elements \(h'\in\operatorname{Hom}(R,H)\) and
\(g'\in\operatorname{Hom}(R,G)\) for one refinement \(R\) of \(S\); as \(z_R\)
is a homomorphism of groups, \(ghg^{-1}=z_R(g'h'g'^{-1})\) with
\(g'h'g'^{-1}\in\operatorname{Hom}(R,H)\), and \(ghg^{-1}h^{-1}=1\) when
\(g'h'g'^{-1}h'^{-1}=1\).
