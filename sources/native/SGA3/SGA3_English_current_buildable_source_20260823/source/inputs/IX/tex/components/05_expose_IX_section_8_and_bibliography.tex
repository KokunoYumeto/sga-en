\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009),
Expos\'e~IX; our formulation). Let \(H\) be an affine group scheme over a
field \(k\), with affine algebra \(k[H]\) and comultiplication \(\Delta\).
A character of \(H\), that is, a homomorphism \(H\to\mathbf G_{m,k}\), is
the same thing as an invertible element \(f\) of \(k[H]\) such that
\(\Delta(f)=f\otimes f\); the characters form a group \(X(H)\) under
multiplication. Distinct characters are linearly independent: in a
relation \(\sum_{j=1}^rc_j\chi_j=0\) of minimal length, with distinct
\(\chi_j\) and nonzero \(c_j\), one has \(r\geq2\), the \(\chi_j\) being
invertible; applying \(\Delta\) and subtracting
\(\chi_1\otimes\sum_jc_j\chi_j=0\), one obtains
\(\sum_{j\geq2}c_j(\chi_j-\chi_1)\otimes\chi_j=0\), whence
\(\chi_j=\chi_1\) for \(j\geq2\), since \(\chi_2,\ldots,\chi_r\) are
linearly independent, which is absurd. Consequently \(H\) is
diagonalizable if and only if its characters span the \(k\)-vector space
\(k[H]\). Indeed, if they do, they form a basis of \(k[H]\), so that
\(k[H]\) is the algebra \(k(X(H))\) of the group \(X(H)\), with the
comultiplication \(\chi\mapsto\chi\otimes\chi\), and
\(H\simeq D_k(X(H))\); conversely, the algebra \(k(M)\) of \(D_k(M)\) is
spanned by the elements of \(M\), which are characters.
\par

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:proposition:8:1}\textbf{Proposition 8.1.} ---
\textit{Let \(G\) be a diagonalizable group scheme (respectively, a group
scheme of multiplicative type) over a field \(k\).\footnote{Translator's
note: the 1970 text has ``groupe alg\'ebrique'' (algebraic group) in place
of ``group scheme'', and does not name the field. As the proof below reduces
to the case of finite type by a passage to the limit, the statement is meant
for group schemes over a field that need not be of finite type; the
Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, states it so.} Then, for
every subgroup scheme \(H\) of \(G\), the schemes \(H\) and \(G/H\) are
diagonalizable (respectively, of multiplicative type).}

Definition \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:definition:1:1}{1.1} immediately reduces
the assertion to the case without the parenthetical qualification. An
easy passage to the limit, using VI\textsubscript{B} \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-11.13}{11.13} and \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:theorem:3:1}{VIII 3.1},
reduces us to the case in which \(G\) is of finite type over
\(k\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009),
Expos\'e~IX, asks for this passage to the limit to be spelled out, pointing
to the criterion of the Addition above, to the description of \(G/H\) as a
projective limit in the proof of VI\textsubscript{B}
\SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIB-11.17}{11.17}, and to the closedness of \(H\) in
\(G\). For \(H\) closed in \(G=D_k(M)\), one argument runs as follows. The
algebra \(k[H]\) is a quotient of \(k(M)\), the union of the \(k(M_i)\),
\(M_i\) ranging over the finitely generated subgroups of \(M\); the image of
\(k(M_i)\) is the algebra of a closed subgroup of \(D_k(M_i)\), so, by the
case of finite type, it is spanned by characters that are images of elements
of \(M_i\). Thus \(k[H]\) is spanned by the images of the elements of \(M\),
and \(H\simeq D_k(M/N)\), where \(N\) is the subgroup of the \(m\in M\) with
image \(1\); \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:theorem:3:1}{VIII 3.1} then gives
\(G/H\simeq D_k(N)\).}
By VI\textsubscript{B} \SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIB-11.16}{11.16}, one can find a finite
family of nonzero elements
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:eq:8:1:1}
\begin{equation}
  \tag{8.1.1}
  f_i=\sum_m a_{im}m,\qquad a_{im}\in k,
\end{equation}
of the affine ring \(k(M)\) of \(G=D_k(M)\), such that the points of
\(H\), with values in an arbitrary \(k\)-algebra \(k'\), are precisely
the points \(g\in G(k')\) for which
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:eq:8:1:2}
\begin{equation}
  \tag{8.1.2}
  \tau_g f_i=\lambda_i(g)f_i,
  \qquad \lambda_i(g)\in k',
\end{equation}
where \(\tau_g\) denotes translation by \(g\). But
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:eq:8:1:3}
\begin{equation}
  \tag{8.1.3}
  \tau_g f_i=\sum_m a_{im}\chi_m(g)m,
\end{equation}
where \(\chi_m:G\to\mathbf G_{m,k}\) is the character associated with
\(m\). Thus \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:eq:8:1:2}{(8.1.2)} is equivalent to
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:eq:8:1:4}
\begin{equation}
  \tag{8.1.4}
  \chi_m(g)=\chi_{m'}(g)
  \qquad\text{if }m,m'\in Z_i,
\end{equation}
where \(Z_i\) denotes the set of \(m\in M\) for which \(a_{im}\neq0\).
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:eq:8:1:4}{The same relation} may be written
\[
  \chi_{m'-m}(g)=1
  \qquad\text{if }m,m'\in Z_i.
\]

Let \(N\) be the subgroup of \(M\) generated by all the elements
\(m'-m\), where \(i\) varies and \(m,m'\in Z_i\). It follows from the
definition of \(D_k(M)\simeq G\) that \(H\) identifies with
\(D_k(M/N)\). Hence \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:theorem:3:1}{VIII 3.1} identifies \(G/H\) with \(D_k(N)\).
\hfill\(\square\)

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:proposition:8:2}\textbf{Proposition 8.2.} ---
\textit{Let \(G\) be an algebraic group over a field \(k\), an extension
of a group \(K\) of multiplicative type by a group \(H\) of multiplicative
type. If \(G\) is commutative or \(K\) is connected, then \(G\) is of
multiplicative type. If \(K\) and \(H\) are diagonalizable, \(K\) being a
torus, then \(G\) is diagonalizable.}

For the proof of the first assertion, the reader is referred to
\SGARefLink{sga3:xvii:proposition:7:1:1}{XVII 7.1.1}, of which 8.2 is a
special case; the case of 8.2 is treated in part
\SGARefLink{sga3:xvii:proposition:7:1:1:item:i}{(i)} of the proof of
\SGARefLink{sga3:xvii:proposition:7:1:1}{XVII 7.1.1}, which does not use
the results of the later expos\'es. Suppose now that \(K\) and \(H\) are
diagonalizable:
\[
  K\simeq D_k(M),
  \qquad
  H\simeq D_k(N).
\]
Then, by Definition \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:definition:1:1}{1.1} and the
exactness sorite \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:theorem:3:1}{VIII 3.1}, one sees
that the classification of the extensions \(G\) of \(K\) by \(H\) which,
over an algebraic closure of \(k\), become isomorphic to a given
diagonalizable extension \(G_0\) of \(K\) by \(H\)\footnote{Translator's note:
the 1970 text has ``de \(G\)'' here in place of ``of \(K\) by \(H\)''.} is
the same as that of
the torsors, for the fpqc topology, under the group
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(K,H)\), which is the constant
\(k\)-group with value \(L=\operatorname{Hom}(N,M)\). If \(K\) is a torus,
that is, if \(M\) is torsion-free, then so is \(L\), whence
\[
  H^1(k_{\mathrm{fpqc}},L_k)
  =\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(\operatorname{Gal}(\bar k/k),L\bigr)
  \simeq0,
\]
which means that every extension of \(K\) by \(H\) is already
diagonalizable.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo draft
re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, states the proposition for \(H\) and \(K\) of
finite type, as two assertions (i) and (ii), and details the proof of the
second. In our formulation: \(G\) is then of multiplicative type by the
first assertion, hence split by a finite separable extension of \(k\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:x:proposition:1:4}{X 1.4}), so that the \'etale topology
and a separable closure \(k_s\) of \(k\) suffice. The automorphisms of the
commutative extension \(G_0\) inducing the identity on \(H\) and on \(K\)
are the maps \(g\mapsto f(\bar g)\,g\), where \(\bar g\) is the image of
\(g\) in \(K\) and \(f:K\to H\) is a homomorphism; they form the group
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(K,H)\), constant by
\SGARefLink{sga3:viii:corollary:1:5}{VIII 1.5}. Finally
\(H^1(\operatorname{Gal}(k_s/k),L)\) is the group of continuous
homomorphisms \(\operatorname{Gal}(k_s/k)\to L\), which is zero when \(L\)
is torsion-free, a profinite group having only finite images in a discrete
group.}

\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:remark:8:3}\textbf{Remarks 8.3.} ---
\textit{As already noted in the proof of
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:proposition:8:2}{8.2}, the first assertion is stated
and proved over an arbitrary base in \SGARefLink{sga3:xvii:proposition:7:1:1}{XVII 7.1.1}. The second assertion
generalizes, with essentially the same proof, to the case of an integral
normal base scheme---or, more generally, a geometrically unibranch
one---by using \SGARefLink{sga3:x:lemma:5-13}{X 5.13} below.}

\textit{Finally, \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:proposition:8:1}{8.1} may also be
regarded as a corollary of the distinctly less trivial result \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:6:8}{6.8}, which
is likewise valid over an arbitrary base scheme.}\footnote{Translator's note:
as the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, points out, the
proof of 6.8 given above uses 8.1, to know that the fiber \(K_s\), a closed
subgroup of the diagonalizable group \(H_s\), is diagonalizable; deducing 8.1
from 6.8 therefore requires another proof of that step.}
