\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:definition:4:1}\textbf{Definition 4.1.}
\textit{Let \(X\) be a prescheme. A family
\((Z_i)_{i\in I}\) of subpreschemes of \(X\) is said to be
\emph{schematically dense} if, for every open subset \(U\) of \(X\) and
every closed subprescheme \(U_0\) of \(U\) containing all the
\(Z_i\cap U\), one has \(U_0=U\).}

\textit{A subprescheme \(Z\) of \(X\) is said to be
\emph{schematically dense in \(X\)} if the one-member family consisting
of \(Z\) is schematically dense.}

It is immediate that the definition is
equivalent to saying that, for every open subset \(U\) of \(X\), every
section \(f\) of \(\mathscr O_U\) that vanishes on each \(Z_i\cap U\)
is zero.
When \(X\) lies over a prescheme \(S\), this is also equivalent to the
following assertion: for every open subset \(U\) of \(X\), every
\(S\)-prescheme \(Y\) separated over \(S\), and every pair of morphisms
\(u,v:U\to Y\) that coincide on the \(Z_i\cap U\), one has \(u=v\).
Indeed, \(u=v\) is equivalent to \(U_0=U\), where \(U_0\) is the inverse
image of the diagonal in \(Y\times_S Y\) under the \(S\)-morphism
\(U\to Y\times_S Y\) defined by \((u,v)\).\footnote{Translator's note: the
1970 text has \(X\to Y\times_SY\) here. The equivalences of this paragraph
are proved in EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.10.1; there the results of 11.10 are derived
from those of 11.9 on separating families of homomorphisms. The
Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, refers to both.} The diagonal is a closed
subprescheme of \(Y\times_S Y\), so \(U_0\) is a closed subprescheme of
\(U\) containing all the \(Z_i\cap U\). Thus schematic density gives
\(U_0=U\), hence \(u=v\). Conversely, take
\(Y=\operatorname{Spec}\mathscr O_S[T]\).

In the terminology introduced at the end of EGA I, 9.5, saying that the
subprescheme \(Z\) is schematically dense in \(X\) also means that \(X\)
is the \emph{closure subprescheme} of \(Z\) in \(X\).

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:lemma:4:2}\textbf{Lemma 4.2.}
\textit{Let \(X\) be a flat \(S\)-prescheme, and let
\((Z_i)_{i\in I}\) be a family of subpreschemes of \(X\), flat over
\(S\). Let \(S_0\) be a subprescheme of \(S\) defined by a nilpotent
ideal \(\mathscr J\), let \(X_0,Z_{i0}\) be obtained from \(X,Z_i\) by
the base change \(S_0\to S\), and suppose finally that the modules
\(\mathscr J^n/\mathscr J^{n+1}\) are locally free over \(S_0\).
If \((Z_{i0})_{i\in I}\) is schematically dense in
\(X_0\), then \((Z_i)_{i\in I}\) is schematically dense in \(X\).}

\emph{Proof.}
Suppose \(\mathscr J^{n+1}=0\), where \(n\geq0\), and argue by induction
on \(n\); the assertion is trivial for \(n=0\). Define
\(S_m,X_m,Z_{im}\) by reduction modulo \(\mathscr J^{m+1}\), as usual.
The induction hypothesis already implies that
\((Z_{i,n-1})_{i\in I}\) is schematically dense in \(X_{n-1}\).
After replacing \(X\) by an induced open subset, it is enough to prove
that every section \(f\) of \(\mathscr O_X\) that vanishes on all the
\(Z_i\) is zero.

The section \(f_{n-1}\) of \(\mathscr O_{X_{n-1}}\)
defined by \(f\) vanishes on the \(Z_{i,n-1}\), hence is zero.
Consequently, \(f\) is a section of
\(\mathscr J^n\mathscr O_X\). Since \(X\) is flat over \(S\), there is
an isomorphism
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:lemma:4:2:display:01}
\[
  \mathscr J^n\mathscr O_X
  \xleftarrow{\ \sim\ }
  \mathscr E\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{X_0},
  \qquad
  \mathscr E=\mathscr J^n=\mathscr J^n/\mathscr J^{n+1}.
\]
Similarly, for every \(i\in I\), the restriction
\(f_i\) of \(f\) to \(Z_i\) may be regarded as a section of
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:lemma:4:2:display:02}
\[
  \mathscr J^n\mathscr O_{Z_i}
  \xleftarrow{\ \sim\ }
  \mathscr E\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{Z_{i0}}.
\]
By hypothesis, all the \(f_i\) are zero. The module \(\mathscr E\) is
locally free by assumption, and therefore so is
\[
  \mathscr F
  =\mathscr E\otimes_{\mathscr O_{S_0}}\mathscr O_{X_0}.
\]
Thus \(f\) is a section of the locally free module \(\mathscr F\) on
\(X_0\) whose restriction to every \(Z_{i0}\) is zero. Since the family
\((Z_{i0})_{i\in I}\) is schematically dense in \(X_0\), it follows at
once that \(f=0\). \qed

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:corollary:4:3}\textbf{Corollary 4.3.}
\textit{Let \(X\) be a locally Noetherian \(S\)-prescheme, flat over
\(S\), and let \((Z_i)_{i\in I}\) be a family of subpreschemes of \(X\),
flat over \(S\). Suppose that, for every \(s\in S\), the family of fibers
\((Z_{is})_{i\in I}\) is schematically dense in \(X_s\). Then
\((Z_i)_{i\in I}\) is schematically dense in \(X\).}

\emph{Proof.}
After replacing \(X\) by an induced open subset, it is enough to prove
that every section \(f\) of \(\mathscr O_X\) whose restriction to all
the \(Z_i\) is zero is itself zero.
For this, it suffices to prove that, for every \(s\in S\) and every
integer \(n\), the section induced on
\[
  X\times_S
  \operatorname{Spec}\bigl(\mathscr O_{S,s}/\mathfrak m_s^{n+1}\bigr)
\]
is zero, where \(\mathfrak m_s\) is the maximal ideal of
\(\mathscr O_{S,s}\) (this is where the hypothesis that \(X\) is locally
Noetherian is used).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo draft
re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, spells this step out. If \(x\in X\) lies
over \(s\), the image of \(f\) in the Noetherian local ring
\(\mathscr O_{X,x}\) then lies in \(\mathfrak m_s^{n+1}\mathscr O_{X,x}\),
hence in the \((n+1)\)-st power of its maximal ideal, for every \(n\); by
Krull's intersection theorem it is zero, so that \(f=0\). In the case
reached, 4.2 applies with \(S_0=\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s)\): the
\(\mathscr J^k/\mathscr J^{k+1}\) are vector spaces over \(\kappa(s)\), and
the fibers \(Z_{is}\) form a schematically dense family in \(X_s\) by
hypothesis.}
This reduces us to the case in which \(S\) is the spectrum of a local
ring whose maximal ideal \(\mathscr J\) is nilpotent. We are then under
the conditions of application of \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:lemma:4:2}{4.2},
whence the conclusion. \qed

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:lemma:4:4}\textbf{Lemma 4.4.}
\textit{Under the hypotheses of \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:3}{4.3},
and supposing \(S\) locally Noetherian, for every base change
\(S'\to S\), with \(S'\) not necessarily locally Noetherian, the family
\((Z_i')_{i\in I}\) is schematically dense in \(X'\).}

\emph{Proof.}\footnote{Translator's note: in the terminology of
EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.10.8, the lemma says that \((Z_i)_{i\in I}\) is
universally schematically dense in \(X\) relative to \(S\); the
Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, adds this to the
statement. It also points to another proof, that of EGA~IV\(_3\),
11.10.9, which rests on 11.9.16: one first treats base changes
\(S'\to S\) locally of finite type, for which \(X'\) is locally
Noetherian and the analogues of 4.3 and 4.5 for separating families
apply, and then an arbitrary affine \(S'=\operatorname{Spec}(A')\), \(A'\)
being the filtered union of its \(A\)-subalgebras of finite type
(11.9.12). In the last line of the proof of 11.9.16, the reference
(11.9.5) should read (11.9.12).}
Let \(f'\) be a section of \(\mathscr O_{X'}\) over an open subset
\(U'\) of \(X'\), vanishing on the \(Z_i'\); we must show that \(f'=0\).
Fix \(s'\in S'\). It is enough to prove that \(f'\) vanishes at every
point of \(U'\) above \(s'\). If \(s\) is the image of \(s'\) in \(S\),
then, after replacing \(S,S'\) by the spectra of their local rings at
\(s,s'\), we may suppose that \(S,S'\) are local and that \(s,s'\) are
their closed points. We may also suppose that \(X\) is affine, so that
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:lemma:4:4:display:01}
\[
  \begin{gathered}
    S=\operatorname{Spec}(A),\qquad
    S'=\operatorname{Spec}(A'),\\
    X=\operatorname{Spec}(B),\qquad
    X'=\operatorname{Spec}(B'),\qquad
    B'=B\otimes_A A',
  \end{gathered}
\]
where \(A\to A'\) is a local homomorphism. We may further suppose that
\(U'\) is affine, of the form \(X'_{g'}\), with \(g'\in B'\).

For every \(A\)-subalgebra \(A''\) of \(A'\), put
\[
  S''=\operatorname{Spec}(A'')
\]
and let \(X'',Z_i''\) be obtained from \(X,Z_i\) by the base change
\(S''\to S\). We obtain the diagram
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:lemma:4:4:diagram:01}
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
  Z_i \arrow[d] & Z_i'' \arrow[l]\arrow[d]
    & Z_i' \arrow[l]\arrow[d] \\
  X \arrow[d] & X'' \arrow[l]\arrow[d] & X' \arrow[l]\arrow[d] \\
  S & S'' \arrow[l] & S' \arrow[l].
\end{tikzcd}
\]

In what follows, we restrict to those \(A''\) that are localizations of
\(A\)-subalgebras \(A_1''\) of \(A'\), of finite type over \(A\), at
\(\mathfrak m'\cap A_1''\), where \(\mathfrak m'\) is the maximal ideal
of \(A'\). Thus the homomorphisms \(A\to A''\to A'\) are local. These
\(A''\) form an increasing filtered family of subalgebras whose union,
and hence whose direct limit, is \(A'\). Likewise,
\[
  B'=\varinjlim_{A''}\bigl(B\otimes_A A''\bigr).
\]
There therefore exist such an \(A''\) and an element
\(g''\in B''=B\otimes_A A''\) whose image in \(B'\) is \(g'\).
Replacing \(S\) by \(S''\) (and \(X,Z_i\) by \(X'',Z_i''\)),\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, checks that
this replacement preserves the hypotheses of the lemma. In our words:
\(A''\), a localization of an \(A\)-algebra of finite type, is
Noetherian; so is \(B\otimes_AA''\), a localization of a \(B\)-algebra of
finite type; flatness is preserved by base change; and if \(s''\in S''\)
lies over \(t\in S\), the fibers \(X''_{s''}\) and \(Z''_{i,s''}\) are
obtained from \(X_t\) and \(Z_{i,t}\) by the extension
\(\kappa(s'')/\kappa(t)\), so that the \(Z''_{i,s''}\) form a
schematically dense family in \(X''_{s''}\) by 4.5 below.} we may
therefore suppose that \(g'\) comes from an element \(g\in B\).
After replacing \(X\) by the open subset \(X_g=U\), we may suppose that
\(U'=X'\). In the same way, we may suppose that \(f'\) comes from a
section \(f\) of \(\mathscr O_X\) over \(X\).

Let \(Y=V(f)\) be the subscheme of \(X\) defined by \(f\), and put
\[
  Y_i=Z_i\times_XY.
\]
Thus \(Y_i\) is the closed subscheme \(V(f_i)\) of \(Z_i\), where \(f_i\)
is the section of \(\mathscr O_{Z_i}\) induced by \(f\). Denoting by
\(Y',Y_i'\) the \(S'\)-schemes obtained by base change, we have
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:lemma:4:4:display:02}
\[
  Y'=V(f'),
  \qquad
  Y_i'=V(f_i'),
\]
and analogous relations hold for \(Y'',Y_i''\). The hypothesis on
\(f'\) is expressed by the relations
\[
  Y_i'=Z_i'\qquad\text{for every }i,
\]
at least at every point \(x'\) of \(X'\) above \(s'\).

Let \(\mathfrak m\) be the maximal ideal of \(A\). For every
\(n\geq0\), introduce the closed subscheme
\[
  S_n=\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak m^{n+1})
\]
of \(S\), and let \(X_n,Y_n,Z_n^i,Y_n^i\) be obtained from
\(X,Y,Z_i,Y_i\) by the base change \(S_n\to S\). More generally, for
every \(S\)-prescheme \(P\), write \(P_n=P\times_SS_n\).

By VIII, \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:theorem:6:4}{6.4}, we have the following:
for every \(n\) and every \(i\in I\), there is a subprescheme \(T_n^i\) of
\(S_n\) that represents the following functor
\(F_n^i:(\mathbf{Sch})_{/S_n}^{\circ}\to\mathbf{Set}\): for \(P\) over
\(S_n\), \(F_n^i(P)\) is empty if \((Z_n^i)_P\ne(Y_n^i)_P\), and consists
of one element otherwise. This being so, one sees at once that, for fixed
\(i\) and for \(m\geq n\),
\[
  T_n^i=T_m^i\times_{S_m}S_n.
\]
Let \(\widehat S=\operatorname{Spec}(\widehat A)\), where \(\widehat A\)
is the completion of \(A\); it follows at once that there is a closed
subscheme \(T^i\) of \(\widehat S\) that reduces to the \(T_n^i\).\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, gives the
details and states the conclusion for the formal scheme
\(\operatorname{Spf}(\widehat A)\); the statement holds as printed.
VIII, \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:theorem:6:4}{6.4} applies because \(Z_n^i\),
flat over the Artinian scheme \(S_n\), is essentially free over it
(VIII, \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:proposition:6:2:item:a:01}{6.2(a)}), and
gives \(T_n^i\) closed in \(S_n\). For \(P\) over \(S_n\) one has
\((Y_n^i)_P=Y_i\times_SP=(Y_m^i)_P\), and likewise for \(Z_i\), whence
\(T_n^i=T_m^i\times_{S_m}S_n\): the ideal \(K_m^i\) of \(T_m^i\) in
\(A/\mathfrak m^{m+1}\) maps onto the ideal \(K_n^i\) of \(T_n^i\).
Lifting step by step, the ideal \(K^i=\varprojlim_nK_n^i\) of
\(\widehat A\) maps onto every \(K_n^i\), so that
\(T^i=\operatorname{Spec}(\widehat A/K^i)\) satisfies
\(T^i\times_{\widehat S}S_n=T_n^i\).}
Let \(T\) be the closed subscheme of \(\widehat S\) that is the
intersection of the \(T^i\). Since \(\widehat S\) is Noetherian, there is
a finite subset \(J\) of \(I\) such that
\[
  T=\bigcap_{i\in J}T^i.
\]

Since \(Y_i'=Z_i'\), there is an \(A''\) such that \(Y_i''=Z_i''\)
(where \(A''\) satisfies the conditions made explicit above). A priori,
\(A''\) depends on \(i\), but one can find an \(A''\) that works for all
\(i\in J\), since \(J\) is finite. Since \(A''\to A'\) is local, \(s'\)
maps to the closed point \(s''\) of \(S''\); let \(\mathfrak m''\) be the
maximal ideal of \(A''\), let
\(S_n''=\operatorname{Spec}(A''/\mathfrak m''^{\,n+1})\) be the \(n\)-th
infinitesimal neighborhood of \(s''\) in \(S''\), which lies over \(S_n\)
because \(\mathfrak mA''\subset\mathfrak m''\), and let
\(S_0''=\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s'')\), \(X_n''=X\times_SS_n''\) and
\(Z_n^{i''}=Z_i\times_SS_n''\); let \(f''\) be the image of \(f\) in
\(B''=B\otimes_AA''\), and \(f_n''\) its image in
\(B''/\mathfrak m''^{\,n+1}B''\).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text
does not define \(S_n''\); with the notation \(P_n=P\times_SS_n\) it is
\(S''\times_SS_n=\operatorname{Spec}(A''/\mathfrak m^{n+1}A'')\), and the
parenthetical remark at the end of the proof accordingly uses the
topology defined by the powers of \(\mathfrak m\). For that scheme the
hypotheses of 4.2 may fail: \(S''\times_SS_0\) need not be the spectrum
of a field, and the \(\mathfrak m^kA''/\mathfrak m^{k+1}A''\) need not be
locally free over \(A''/\mathfrak mA''\). The Gille--Polo draft
re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, corrects this by using the infinitesimal
neighborhoods of \(s''\), as here. The factorization below is not
affected: for \(i\in J\), the equality \(Y_i''=Z_i''\) persists after the
base change \(S_n''\to S''\).} By the definition of the \(T^i\), it
follows that \(S_n''\to S_n\) factors through \(S_n''\to T_n^i\) for every
\(i\in J\), hence also through
\[
  T_n=\bigcap_{i\in J}T_n^i,
\]
hence also through \(T_n^i\) for every \(i\in I\). This means that, for
every \(n\), \(f_n''\) vanishes on the \(Z_n^{i''}\). By
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:lemma:4:2}{4.2} applied to \(S_n''\), \(X_n''\),
\((Z_n^{i''})_{i\in I}\), \(S_0''\), and \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:lemma:4:5}{4.5}
below, it follows that \(f_n''=0\). Since this holds for every \(n\), and
\(X''\) is Noetherian, so that its local rings are separated for their
usual topology, it follows that \(f''\) is zero at the points \(x''\) of
\(X''\) above \(s''\). (Note that, for every \(x''\) above \(s''\), the
topology of \(\mathscr O_{X'',x''}\) defined by the
\(\mathfrak m''^{\,n}\mathscr O_{X'',x''}\) is separated, because
\(\mathfrak m''\mathscr O_{X'',x''}\) is contained in the maximal ideal of
\(\mathscr O_{X'',x''}\).) A fortiori, \(f'\) is zero at the points
\(x'\) of \(X'\) above \(s'\). \qed

\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009),
Expos\'e~IX, 4.5.0; our formulation). Let \(X\) be an \(S\)-prescheme,
\((Z_i)_{i\in I}\) a family of subpreschemes of \(X\), and \(S'\to S\) a
faithfully flat morphism, and let \(X'\), \(Z_i'\) be obtained by base
change. If \((Z_i')_{i\in I}\) is schematically dense in \(X'\), then
\((Z_i)_{i\in I}\) is schematically dense in \(X\). Indeed, let \(f\) be a
section of \(\mathscr O_X\) over an open set \(U\) of \(X\) that vanishes
on the \(Z_i\cap U\). Its inverse image \(f'\) on \(U'=U\times_SS'\)
vanishes on the \(Z_i'\cap U'\), hence is zero. For \(x\in U\) choose
\(x'\in U'\) above \(x\), as \(U'\to U\) is surjective; the local
homomorphism \(\mathscr O_{X,x}\to\mathscr O_{X',x'}\) is flat, hence
faithfully flat and injective, and it maps the germ of \(f\) at \(x\) to
that of \(f'\) at \(x'\), which is zero. Hence \(f=0\). No
quasi-compactness or Noetherian hypothesis is needed. This is the fact
used in the ``if'' part of the proof of 4.5 and in the reductions in the
proof of 4.7; see also EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.10.5(i).

It remains to give the proof of \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:lemma:4:5}{the following lemma}.

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:lemma:4:5}\textbf{Lemma 4.5.}
\textit{Let \(X\) be a locally Noetherian prescheme}\footnote{Translator's
note: this hypothesis is not needed, as the Gille--Polo draft re-edition
(2009), Expos\'e~IX, observes: for any prescheme \(X\) over \(k\),
EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.10.6, shows that schematic density is preserved by any
extension of the base field, and the converse is the faithfully flat case
(11.10.5(i), or the Addition after the proof of 4.4).}%
\textit{ over a field \(k\), let \((Z_i)_{i\in I}\) be a family of
subpreschemes of \(X\), let \(k'/k\) be a field extension, and let
\(X',Z_i'\) be obtained from \(X,Z_i\) by base change. Then
\((Z_i)_{i\in I}\) is schematically dense in \(X\) if and only if
\((Z_i')_{i\in I}\) is schematically dense in \(X'\).}

\emph{Proof.}
The ``if'' is nearly trivial from the definitions, and follows from the
mere fact that \(k'\) is faithfully flat over \(k\) (without any
Noetherian or other hypothesis). Let us prove the ``only if''. Regarding
\(k'\) as the direct limit of its subextensions \(k''\) of finite type,
and proceeding as at the beginning of the preceding proof, one easily
reduces to the case in which \(k'\) is an extension of finite type of
\(k\). (Note that every section \(f''\) of \(\mathscr O_{X''}\) whose
inverse image \(f'\in\Gamma(X',\mathscr O_{X'})\) is zero is itself
zero.) Then \(k'\) is the direct limit of its subalgebras \(A\) of finite
type over \(k\), and we are reduced to seeing that the family of the
\(Z_{iA}\) is schematically dense in \(X_A\). (Note that every section of
\(\mathscr O_{X_A}\) whose inverse image on \(X_{k'}\) vanishes on the
\(Z_{ik'}\) vanishes on the \(Z_{iA}\): for every flat \(A\)-prescheme
\(Z\), such as \(Z_{iA}\), every section of \(\mathscr O_Z\) that is zero
on \(Z_{k'}\) is zero, as one sees for example by reducing to the case in
which \(Z\) is affine.)\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo draft
re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, concludes differently from this point:
\(A\) being of finite type over \(k\), the projection \(X_A\to X\) is
flat and of finite presentation, and such a morphism carries
schematically dense families to schematically dense families by inverse
image (EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.10.5(ii), which rests on 11.9.10(ii)); thus the
\(Z_{iA}\) are at once schematically dense in \(X_A\), without the
reduction to a finite extension.} Using \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:3}{4.3} for
the \(X_{A'}\), where \(A'\) is a quotient of \(A\) by a power of a
maximal ideal, and Hilbert's Nullstellensatz, one is reduced to the case
in which \(k'\) is a finite extension of \(k\). Note that the hypothesis
made on \((Z_i)_{i\in I}\) also means that the intersection of the
kernels of the canonical homomorphisms
\[
  u_i:\mathscr O_X\longrightarrow (v_i)_*(\mathscr O_{Z_i})
\]
is zero, where \(v_i:Z_i\to X\) is the canonical immersion. In this form
one sees that%
\begingroup
\renewcommand{\thefootnote}{\(\ast\)}%
\footnote{This is brief! For a proof, cf. EGA IV\(_3\), 11.10.6.}%
\addtocounter{footnote}{-1}%
\endgroup\ this hypothesis is stable under a finite extension \(k'/k\),
which completes the proof. \qed

Let us record in passing \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:6}{the following result}, which will be used in a
later expos\'e.%

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:corollary:4:6}\textbf{Corollary 4.6.}
\textit{Let \(X\) be a flat \(S\)-prescheme and \(U\) an open subset of
\(X\). Suppose that \(U_s\) is schematically dense in \(X_s\) for every
\(s\in S\), and that either \(X\) is locally Noetherian or \(X\) is
locally of finite presentation over \(S\). Then \(U\) is schematically
dense in \(X\).}

\emph{Proof.}
For simplicity, suppose \(U\) is retrocompact in \(X\) (cf. EGA
IV\(_3\), 11.10.10 for the general case). The locally
Noetherian case is included only for reference; it is contained in
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:3}{4.3}.

In the second case, we may plainly suppose \(S\) and \(X\) affine. Then
\(X\) and \(U\) are of finite presentation over
\(S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\). The ring \(A\) is the direct limit of its
finite-type \(\mathbb Z\)-subalgebras \(A_i\). The ``patented
procedure'' already used (spelled out at length in EGA IV\(_3\), \S8)
shows that there are an index \(i\), an affine scheme \(X_i\) over
\[
  S_i=\operatorname{Spec}(A_i),
\]
and an open subset \(U_i\) of \(X_i\), from which \(X,U\) are obtained
by the base change \(S\to S_i\). Let \(E_i\) be the subset of \(S_i\)
consisting of those \(s\in S_i\) for which \((U_i)_s\) is schematically
dense in \((X_i)_s\). This condition also means that \((U_i)_s\)
contains the set \(\operatorname{Ass}\mathscr O_{(X_i)_s}\) of points
``associated'' with the structure sheaf of \((X_i)_s\), and the results of
EGA IV\(_3\), \S9 then show that this condition is of a constructible
nature, that is, that \(E_i\) is a constructible subset of \(S_i\).
Moreover, the condition considered on the fibers is invariant under
extension of the base field, which proves, by the hypothesis made, that
the inverse image \(E\) of \(E_i\) under \(S\to S_i\) is \(S\), and
implies, by EGA IV\(_3\), \S8, that there is a \(j\geq i\) such that the
inverse image \(E_j\) of \(E_i\) in \(S_j\) is \(S_j\), that is, that
\((U_j)_s\) is schematically dense in \((X_j)_s\) for every
\(s\in S_j\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo draft re-edition
(2009), Expos\'e~IX, gives precise references: for the ``patented
procedure'', EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.8.2 and 8.10.5(iii); for the description of
\(E_i\) by associated points, EGA~IV\(_2\), 5.9.9 and 5.10.2; for its
constructibility, EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.9.17.1; for the existence of \(j\),
EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.3.11; for the general case, EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.10.10 and
11.9.17. The invariance under extension of the base field is
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:lemma:4:5}{4.5}.}

Apply \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:lemma:4:4}{4.4} to
\((S_j,X_j,U_j)\) and the base change \(S\to S_j\). It follows that
\(U\) is schematically dense in \(X\). Notice that here
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:lemma:4:4}{4.4} is used only
for a family with a finite index set (in fact, a singleton); in this
case its proof simplifies considerably. \qed

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:theorem:4:7}\textbf{Theorem 4.7.}
\textit{Let \(S\) be a prescheme, and let \(G\) be a group of
multiplicative type and of finite type over \(S\). For every integer
\(n>0\), let \({}_nG\) be the kernel of \(n\cdot\operatorname{id}_G\).
Then the family of subpreschemes \(({}_nG)_{n>0}\) is schematically
dense in \(G\) in the sense of
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:definition:4:1}{Definition~4.1}.}

\emph{Proof.}
We distinguish two cases.

\smallskip
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:theorem:4:7:proof:item:a}\noindent\textit{(a) \(S\) locally Noetherian.}
By \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:3}{4.3}, we are reduced to the case
in which \(S\) is the spectrum of a field \(k\), and, after an extension
of the latter, to the case in which \(k\) is algebraically closed and
\(G\) is diagonalizable, say
\[
  G=D_k(M),
\]
where \(M\) is a commutative group of finite type. Then
\[
  M\simeq\Gamma\times\mathbb Z^r
\]
with \(\Gamma\) finite, and consequently
\[
  G\simeq G_1\times G_2,
  \qquad
  G_1=D(\Gamma),
  \qquad
  G_2=\mathbf G_m^r.
\]
For \(n\) sufficiently divisible---namely, for \(n\) a multiple of the
order of \(\Gamma\)---one has
\[
  {}_nG=G_1\times{}_nG_2,
  \qquad\text{since}\qquad
  {}_nG_1=G_1.
\]
Applying \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:3}{4.3} again to the projection
\(G\to G_1\), we are reduced to
\(G_2\), hence to the case \(G=\mathbf G_m^r\). Since \(G\) is then
reduced, schematic density of \(({}_nG)_{n>0}\) is equivalent to
density of \(\bigcup_n{}_nG\) in the ordinary topology. As
\[
  {}_nG=({}_n\mathbf G_m)^r,
\]
we are reduced to \(G=\mathbf G_m\), an irreducible group of dimension
one. The assertion now follows because
\(\bigcup_n{}_nG\), the set of roots of unity in \(k\), is infinite.

\smallskip
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:theorem:4:7:proof:item:b}\noindent\textit{(b) The general case.}
For every \(s\in S\), there are an open neighborhood \(U\) of \(s\)
and a faithfully flat morphism \(S'\to U\) such that
\(G'=G_{S'}\) is diagonalizable, say \(G'=D_{S'}(M)\). It is immediate
that we may reduce to the case of the \(G'\); hence we may suppose \(G\)
diagonalizable, so that it comes from the absolute group
\(D_{\mathbb Z}(M)\) over \(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb Z)\) by the base
change \(S\to\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb Z)\). It now suffices to combine
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:4:7:proof:item:a}{(a)} and
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:lemma:4:4}{4.4}. \qed

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:corollary:4:8}\textbf{Corollary 4.8.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)]\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:corollary:4:8:item:a}
  \textit{Let \(u,v:H\to G\) be two homomorphisms of \(S\)-preschemes
  in groups, with \(H\) diagonalizable}\footnote{Translator's note: the
  Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, states (a) for \(H\)
  of multiplicative type and of finite type. The proof below gives this
  too: the graphs of \(u\) and \(v\) are subgroups of \(H\times_SG\)
  isomorphic to \(H\), hence of multiplicative type and of finite type,
  and (b) applies to them.}%
  \textit{ and of finite type. Suppose that, for every integer \(n>0\),
  the restrictions of \(u\) and \(v\) to \({}_nH\) are equal. Then
  \(u=v\).}

  \item[(b)]\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:corollary:4:8:item:b}
  \textit{Let \(H_1,H_2\) be two subgroups of multiplicative type and
  of finite type of a prescheme in groups \(G\). If
  \({}_nH_1={}_nH_2\) for every integer \(n>0\), then \(H_1=H_2\).}
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.}
Part~\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:8:item:a}{(a)} follows from part~\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:8:item:b}{(b)} by considering the subgroups \(H_1,H_2\)
of \(H\times G\) that are the graphs of \(u,v\). For part~\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:8:item:b}{(b)}, put
\[
  H=H_1\cap H_2.
\]
This is a subgroup prescheme of \(H_i\), for \(i=1,2\), and we must show
that it equals \(H_i\). The hypothesis says precisely that \(H\)
contains all the \({}_nH_i\). We are therefore reduced to \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:9}{the next
corollary}. \qed

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:corollary:4:9}\textbf{Corollary 4.9.}
\textit{Let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group of multiplicative type and of finite
type, and let \(H\) be a subgroup prescheme containing \({}_nG\) for
every \(n>0\). Then \(H=G\).}

\emph{Proof.}
By \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:4:7}{4.7}, it is enough to prove that
\(H\) is closed, or equivalently that it equals \(G\) set-theoretically.
This reduces us to the case in which \(S\) is the spectrum of a field.
Every subgroup prescheme of \(G\) is then closed, by VI\(_B\), \SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2}{1.4.2}.
\qed

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:remark:4:10}\textbf{Remark 4.10.}
Under the hypotheses of \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:4:7}{4.7}, let
\(m>0\) be an integer with the following properties: for every
\(s\in S\), \(m\) is not a power of the characteristic of \(\kappa(s)\);
and if \(G_s\) has type \(M\), every prime divisor of the torsion
subgroup of \(M\) divides \(m\). The second condition is automatically
satisfied if \(G\) is a torus. The proof shows that in
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:4:7}{Theorem~4.7} and
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:8}{Corollary~4.8} and
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:9}{Corollary~4.9} one may restrict to
the subgroups
\[
  {}_{m^r}G,\qquad r>0.
\]

Moreover, if \(G\) is smooth over \(S\), then for every \(s\in S\) there
are an open neighborhood \(U\) of \(s\) and an integer \(m>0\), prime
to every residue characteristic on \(U\), that satisfy the preceding
conditions for \(G_U\). For instance, take \(m\) to be the order of the
torsion subgroup of the type of \(G\) at \(s\).\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, refers here to
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:remark:1:4:1:item:a}{the remark following 1.4} and to
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:proposition:2:1:item:e:01}{2.1(e)}: the type is
locally constant, so on a neighborhood \(U\) of \(s\) it is the type \(M\)
of \(G\) at \(s\), and, \(G\) being smooth, the order of the torsion
subgroup of \(M\) is prime to every residue characteristic on \(U\). When
this torsion subgroup is trivial (for instance for a torus), the rule
gives \(m=1\), for which the groups \({}_{m^r}G\) are trivial; one may
then take for \(m\) a prime number invertible on a neighborhood of
\(s\).} After restriction to
\(U\), the groups \({}_{m^r}G\) are then
finite and \'etale over \(S\), and their family is schematically dense.
In certain applications---in particular those involving
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:3:2}{Theorem~3.2} and
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:3:2:bis}{Theorem~3.2~bis} (cf. XI, 6),
though not those involving \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:3:6}{Theorem~3.6}
and \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:3:6:bis}{Theorem~3.6~bis}---this
observation allows one to avoid
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:3:1}{Theorem~3.1} and its use of Hochschild
cohomology.

\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009),
Expos\'e~IX, 5.0; our formulation). The following fact is used implicitly
in the proof of 5.1~(a). Let \(A\) be a Noetherian local ring with maximal
ideal \(\mathfrak m\), let \(S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\) and
\(S_n=\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak m^{n+1})\), let \(H\) be a finite
\(S\)-scheme, and let \(K\) be a subprescheme of \(H\) such that
\(K\times_SS_n=H\times_SS_n\) for every \(n\). Then \(K=H\). Indeed, \(K\)
is a closed subprescheme of an open subset \(V\) of \(H\), and \(V\)
contains the closed fiber \(H\times_SS_0\); since \(H\) is finite over the
local scheme \(S\), every non-empty closed subset of \(H\) meets that
fiber, so the closed set \(H-V\) is empty. Thus \(K\) is defined by an
ideal \(I\) of the finite \(A\)-algebra \(B=\Gamma(H,\mathscr O_H)\), and
\(I\) is contained in \(\bigcap_n\mathfrak m^{n+1}B\), which is zero by
Krull's intersection theorem.

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:theorem:5:1}\textbf{Theorem 5.1.}
\textit{Let \(u,v:H\to G\) be two homomorphisms of \(S\)-preschemes in
groups, where \(H\) is of multiplicative type and of finite type.
Suppose either that \(S\) is locally Noetherian or that \(G\) is of
finite presentation over \(S\). Let \(s\in S\) satisfy \(u_s=v_s\), and
suppose that \(u_s\) is central. Then there is an open neighborhood
\(U\) of \(s\) such that \(u_U=v_U\).}

\emph{Proof.}
We distinguish the two cases.

\smallskip
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:theorem:5:1:proof:item:a}\noindent\textit{(a) \(S\) locally Noetherian.}
Let \(K=\operatorname{Ker}(u,v)\); we want to find \(U\) with
\(K_U=H_U\). This formulation and EGA IV\(_3\), \S8 allow us to replace
\(S\) by the spectrum of \(\mathscr O_{S,s}\), and hence to suppose \(S\)
local with closed point \(s\).\footnote{Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, details this reduction.
In our words: \(K\), the inverse image of the diagonal of \(G\times_SG\)
under \((u,v)\), is a subgroup prescheme of \(H\); \(H\), of finite type
over the locally Noetherian \(S\), is locally Noetherian, so the immersion
\(K\to H\) is of finite type and \(K\) is of finite presentation over
\(S\); if \(K\to H\) is an isomorphism over
\(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathscr O_{S,s})\), it is one over a neighborhood
of \(s\) (EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.8.2.4).} Replacing the local ring \(A\) by its
completion, which introduces a faithfully flat and quasi-compact base
change, we may even suppose \(A\) complete.\footnote{Translator's note:
this is legitimate since an immersion that becomes an isomorphism after a
faithfully flat quasi-compact base change is an isomorphism
(EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.7.1); as the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009),
Expos\'e~IX, observes, the completeness of \(A\) is not used below.}

By \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:3:4}{3.4}, one has \(u_n=v_n\) for every
\(n\), where the subscript denotes reduction modulo
\(\mathfrak m^{n+1}\), with \(\mathfrak m\) the maximal ideal of \(A\).
For every \(m>0\), let
\[
  {}_mu,{}_mv:{}_mH\longrightarrow G
\]
be the homomorphisms induced by \(u,v\). Then
\[
  ({}_mu)_n=({}_mv)_n
  \qquad\text{for every }n.
\]
This being true for every \(n\), and \({}_mH\) being finite over \(S\)
by \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:proposition:2:2}{2.2}, it follows that
\({}_mu={}_mv\).\footnote{Translator's note: by the Addition at the
beginning of this section, applied to the subprescheme
\(\operatorname{Ker}({}_mu,{}_mv)\) of the finite \(S\)-scheme
\({}_mH\).} This being true for every \(m\), one has \(u=v\) by
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:4:7:proof:item:a}{4.7~(a)}.

\smallskip
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:theorem:5:1:proof:item:b}\noindent\textit{(b) \(G\) of finite presentation.}
We may again suppose \(S\) local with closed point \(s\), and prove that
then \(u=v\). After a faithfully flat and quasi-compact extension of the
base, we may moreover suppose \(H\) diagonalizable, hence of the form
\(D_S(M)\), with \(M\) a commutative group of finite type.\footnote{Translator's
note: such an extension \(S'\to S\) is allowed because \(H'\to H\) is then
faithfully flat and quasi-compact, hence an epimorphism, so that
\(u'=v'\) implies \(u=v\); and \(H\) is of finite presentation over \(S\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:proposition:2:1:item:b:01}{2.1(b)}), which the
reduction to the local case uses (EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.8.2). The Gille--Polo
draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, spells this out.} As in the proof
of \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:6}{4.6}, introduce the increasing
filtered family of the \(\mathbb Z\)-subalgebras of finite type \(A_i\) of
\(A\). Since \(G\) is of finite presentation over \(S\), it comes from a
group prescheme \(G_i\) of finite presentation over some
\(S_i=\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)\). Moreover, \(H\) comes from
\(H_i=D_{S_i}(M)\). Further, for \(j\) large, by EGA IV\(_3\), \S8, the
homomorphisms \(u,v\) come from homomorphisms of \(S_j\)-groups
\(u_j,v_j:H_j\to G_j\), and, if \(s_j\) is the image of \(s\) in \(S_j\),
\((u_j)_{s_j}\) is central.\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text says
that \(u_j\) will be central for \(j\) large. What is needed, and what
holds, is that the fiber \((u_j)_{s_j}\) is central (\(u\) itself need not
be central); the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX,
corrects this and proves it, in substance as follows. The morphisms
\((h,g)\mapsto u_j(h)\,g\,u_j(h)^{-1}\) and \((h,g)\mapsto g\) from
\((H_j\times_{S_j}G_j)_{s_j}\) to \((G_j)_{s_j}\) become equal after the
faithfully flat quasi-compact base change
\(\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s)\to\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s_j)\), because
\(u_s\) is central; hence they are equal, and this holds for every \(j\).}
Moreover, \((u_j)_{s_j}=(v_j)_{s_j}\). We can then apply
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:5:1:proof:item:a}{(a)} to the situation over
\(S_j\), whence at once the stated conclusion. \qed

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:corollary:5:2}\textbf{Corollary 5.2.}
\textit{Let \(u:H\to G\) be a homomorphism of \(S\)-preschemes in
groups, where \(H\) is of multiplicative type and of finite type, and
suppose either that \(S\) is locally Noetherian or that \(G\) is of
finite presentation over \(S\). If \(s\in S\) and \(u_s\) is the unit
homomorphism, then there is an open neighborhood \(U\) of \(s\) on
which \(u_U\) is the unit homomorphism.}

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:corollary:5:3}\textbf{Corollary 5.3.}
\textit{Let \(u,H,G\) be as in
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:5:2}{5.2}, and suppose in addition that \(G\)
is separated over \(S\). Let \(U\) be the set of points \(s\in S\) for
which \(u_s\) is the unit homomorphism. Then \(U\) is open and closed
in \(S\), and \(u_U:H_U\to G_U\) is the unit homomorphism.}

\emph{Proof.}
Only closedness remains to be proved. Put \(K=\operatorname{Ker}(u)\).
Since \(G\) is separated over \(S\), \(K\) is a closed subprescheme of
\(H\), and \(U\) is the set of points \(s\in S\) for which \(K_s=H_s\).
It follows readily that \(U\) is closed, for example by
\SGARefLink{sga3:viii:theorem:6:4}{VIII, 6.4}.\footnote{Translator's note: VIII, 6.4 applies because \(H\) is
essentially free over \(S\) (VIII,
\SGARefLink{sga3:viii:proposition:6:3}{6.3}), as the Gille--Polo draft
re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, points out; the closed subprescheme of
\(S\) that it provides, representing the condition \(K_{S'}=H_{S'}\), has
\(U\) as underlying set.} Alternatively, we may suppose
that \(S\) is reduced and that \(U\) is dense in \(S\), hence
schematically dense. Then \(H_U\) is schematically dense in \(H\)
because \(H\) is flat over \(S\),\footnote{Translator's note: and of
finite presentation over \(S\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:proposition:2:1:item:b:01}{2.1(b)}), which makes the
density of \(H_U\) a consequence of EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.10.5(ii): for a flat
morphism locally of finite presentation, inverse images of schematically
dense families are schematically dense. The Gille--Polo draft re-edition
(2009), Expos\'e~IX, adds this.}
and \(u\) and the unit homomorphism, being equal on \(H_U\), are equal
on \(H\). \qed

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:corollary:5:4}\textbf{Corollary 5.4.}
\textit{Let \(S\) be a prescheme; let \(H,G\) be \(S\)-groups, with
\(H\) of multiplicative type and of finite type and \(G\) separated
and of finite presentation over \(S\); and let
\(S'\to S\) be a morphism covering for the faithfully flat quasi-compact
topology (for example, faithfully flat and quasi-compact) and with
geometrically connected fibers.}\footnote{Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, replaces this
hypothesis by ``universal effective epimorphism'' (for instance a
faithfully flat quasi-compact morphism, or a morphism admitting a
section), so as to apply the corollary to the morphism \(K\to S\) of 5.5,
and adapts the beginning of the proof. The printed hypothesis already
covers that case: a morphism admitting a section is covering for every
topology, its image being the whole functor (IV,
\SGARefLink{sga3:iv:definition:4:2:3}{4.2.3}). The proof uses only that
\(S'\to S\) is a universal effective epimorphism, so the corollary holds
for such morphisms as well. An example is \(S=\operatorname{Spec}(k)\),
\(S'=\operatorname{Spec}(k')\) with \(k'/k\) radicial, for which
\(S'\otimes_k\overline k\) is a single point; this case is used in X,
\SGARefLink{sga3:x:proposition:1:4}{1.4}.}

\textit{Let \(u':H'\to G'\) be a central homomorphism of the
\(S'\)-groups obtained from \(H,G\) by base change. Then there is a
unique homomorphism \(u:H\to G\) of \(S\)-groups such that
\(u\times_SS'=u'\). If \(S'/S\) admits a section \(g\), then \(u\) is
the morphism obtained from \(u'\) by the base change
\(g:S\to S'\).}

\emph{Proof.}
The uniqueness of \(u\), and the last assertion concerning \(g\), are
trivial, given that the faithfully flat quasi-compact topology is less
fine than the canonical topology of \((\mathbf{Sch})\). For the existence
of \(u\), we are reduced for the same reason to showing that the two
homomorphisms
\[
  u_1'',u_2'':H''\longrightarrow G''
\]
of \(S''\)-groups obtained from \(u'\) by the two base changes
\[
  \operatorname{pr}_1,\operatorname{pr}_2:
  S''=S'\times_SS'\longrightarrow S'
\]
are equal. Now they coincide on the diagonal of \(S''\); precisely,
their pullbacks along the diagonal morphism \(S'\to S''\) are equal,
since both are \(u'\). By \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:5:3}{5.3},\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, adds how 5.3
applies: as \(u_1''\) and \(u_2''\) are central,
\(h\mapsto u_1''(h)\,u_2''(h)^{-1}\) is a homomorphism \(H''\to G''\),
equal to the unit homomorphism exactly over the points where \(u_1''\)
and \(u_2''\) coincide, and 5.3 applies to it over \(S''\).}
there is therefore an open-and-closed subset \(U\) of \(S''\),
containing the diagonal, over which \(u_1''\) and \(u_2''\) coincide.
The fibers of \(S'/S\) are geometrically connected, hence so are those
of \(S''/S\), and in particular they are connected. Since \(U\)
contains the diagonals of these fibers, it contains every fiber and
therefore equals \(S''\). This proves existence. \qed

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:corollary:5:5}\textbf{Corollary 5.5.}
\textit{Let \(S\) be a prescheme, let \(K\) be an \(S\)-group prescheme
with connected fibers,}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo draft
re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, adds the hypothesis that \(K\) be locally
of finite type, so that VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-2.4}{2.4},
cited in the proof, applies. It is not needed: a connected prescheme over a
field \(k\) that has a \(k\)-rational point is geometrically connected
(EGA~IV\(_2\), 4.5.13), and each fiber \(K_s\) has the rational point
given by the unit section.}%
\textit{ and let \(H\) be an invariant
subgroup of \(K\), of multiplicative type and of finite type. Then
\(H\) is a central subgroup of \(K\).}

\emph{Proof.}
We apply \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:5:4}{5.4} with \(S'=K\) to the
homomorphism \(H_K\to H_K\) deduced from the operations of \(K\) on \(H\)
induced by the inner automorphisms of \(K\). (This is possible because,
for a group scheme over a field, connected implies geometrically
connected (VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-2.4}{2.4}).)\footnote{Translator's
note: in more detail, after the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009),
Expos\'e~IX: 5.4 is applied with \(H\) in the place of \(G\) (\(H\) is
affine and of finite presentation over \(S\)) to the homomorphism of
\(K\)-groups \(H_K\to H_K\), \((h,k)\mapsto(khk^{-1},k)\), which is central
because \(H\) is commutative; the morphism \(K\to S\), having the unit
section, is covering (see the note to 5.4).} The inverse image of this
homomorphism under the unit section of \(S'=K\) over \(S\) is the identity
homomorphism; hence, by \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:5:4}{5.4}, so is
\(H_K\to H_K\), and \(H\) is central in \(K\). \qed

Let us state the variants of the preceding results for central subgroups
of multiplicative type. Arguing as above, and using
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:3:2:bis}{3.2~bis}, gives:

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:theorem:5:1:bis}\textbf{Theorem 5.1 bis.}
\textit{Let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group prescheme, and let \(H_1,H_2\) be
two subgroup preschemes of multiplicative type and of finite type, with
\((H_1)_s\) central. Suppose either that \(S\) is locally Noetherian or
that \(G\) is of finite presentation over \(S\). For every \(s\in S\)
such that \((H_1)_s=(H_2)_s\), there is an open neighborhood \(U\) of
\(s\) for which \(H_1|_U=H_2|_U\).}

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:corollary:5:3:bis}\textbf{Corollary 5.3 bis.}
\textit{Under the preceding hypotheses, suppose moreover that \(G\) is
separated over \(S\). Let \(U\) be the set of points \(s\in S\) for
which \((H_1)_s=(H_2)_s\). Then \(U\) is open and closed in \(S\), and
\(H_1|_U=H_2|_U\).}

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:corollary:5:4:bis}\textbf{Corollary 5.4 bis.}
\textit{Let \(S\) be a prescheme, let \(G\) be a group prescheme
separated and of finite presentation over \(S\), let \(S'\to S\) be a
covering morphism for the faithfully flat quasi-compact topology with
geometrically connected fibers, and let \(H'\) be a subgroup of
multiplicative type and of finite type of \(G'=G\times_SS'\).
Then there is a unique subgroup \(H\) of \(G\) defining \(H'\) by the
base change \(S'\to S\) (and \(H\) is of multiplicative type and of finite
type over \(S\)).\footnote{Translator's note: by
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:definition:1:1}{1.1} and
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:proposition:2:1:item:b:01}{2.1(b)}, as the Gille--Polo
draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, specifies.} If \(S'\to S\) admits a section \(g\), then
\(H=g^*H'\).}

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:theorem:5:6}\textbf{Theorem 5.6.}
\textit{Let \(S\) be a prescheme and let \(u:H\to G\) be a homomorphism
of \(S\)-preschemes in groups, where \(H\) is of multiplicative type and
of finite type and \(G\) is of finite presentation over \(S\).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)]\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:theorem:5:6:item:a}
  \textit{Suppose that \(G\) has connected fibers. If \(s\in S\) and
  \(u_s:H_s\to G_s\) is central, then there is an open neighborhood
  \(U\) of \(s\) such that the homomorphism \(H|_U\to G|_U\) induced by
  \(u\) is central.}

  \item[(b)]\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:theorem:5:6:item:b}
  \textit{If \(u_s:H_s\to G_s\) is central for every \(s\in S\), then
  \(u\) is central.}
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.}
The standard procedure reduces us, in
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:5:6:item:a}{(a)} and
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:5:6:item:b}{(b)}, to the case in which \(S\)
is locally Noetherian.\footnote{Translator's note: for (a), the
Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, carries this out as in
the proof of 5.1~(b): one may suppose \(S\) local with closed point \(s\)
and, after a faithfully flat quasi-compact extension, \(H=D_S(M)\); then
\(u\) comes from a homomorphism \(u_i:D_{S_i}(M)\to G_i\) over the
spectrum \(S_i\) of a \(\mathbb Z\)-subalgebra of finite type \(A_i\) of
\(\mathscr O_{S,s}\), and \((u_i)_{s_i}\) is central at the image \(s_i\)
of \(s\), by the argument of the note to 5.1~(b).} Moreover, in
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:5:6:item:a}{(a)} we may suppose \(S\) local,
with closed point \(s\), and we must then prove that \(u\) is central.

Let \(K=\operatorname{Ker}(v,w)\), where
\[
  v,w:H\times_SG\rightrightarrows G
\]
are defined by
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ix:theorem:5:6:display:01}
\[
  v(h,g)=g,
  \qquad
  w(h,g)=\operatorname{int}(u(h))\cdot g
        =u(h)g\,u(h)^{-1}.
\]
Then \(K\) is a subgroup over \(G\) of
\[
  H_G=H\times_SG,
\]
and we must prove that \(K=H_G\). By
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:9}{4.9}, it is enough to show that \(K\)
contains
\[
  {}_m(H_G)=({}_mH)\times_SG
\]
for every \(m>0\). We are thus reduced to the case \(H={}_mH\), so that
\(H\) is finite over \(S\).

One can also say that the operation of \(H\) on \(G\), via \(u\) and the
inner automorphisms of \(G\), defines an automorphism \(\theta\) of the
\(H\)-group \(G_H=G\times_SH\), and we want to prove that this
automorphism is the identity. By \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:3:9}{3.9},
this is so in any case if \(S\) is Artinian local. In case
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:5:6:item:a}{(a)}, we conclude that, for every
integer \(n\), the automorphism \(\theta_n\) of \((G_H)_n\) deduced from
\(\theta\) by the base change \(H_n\to H\) (where \(H_n=H\times_SS_n\) and
\(S_n=\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak m^{n+1})\)) is the identity. Hence,
if \(K=\operatorname{Ker}(\theta,\operatorname{id}_{G_H})\), then \(K\) is
a subgroup prescheme of \(G_H\) that contains \((G_H)_n\) for every
\(n\),\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has ``the unit section of
\((G_H)_n\) over \(H_n\)''; every subgroup contains that section, and what
\(\theta_n=\operatorname{id}\) gives is \((G_H)_n\subset K\). The
Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~IX, corrects this, working
over the local scheme of \(G\) at the unit element of \(G_s\). In our
words, \(V\) is obtained as follows: at a point \(x\) of the unit section
above \(s\), the ideal defining \(K\) near \(x\) lies in
\(\mathfrak m^{n+1}\mathscr O_{G_H,x}\) for every \(n\); it therefore
vanishes at \(x\) by Krull's theorem (\(G_H\) is Noetherian), hence near
\(x\); and the open set so obtained contains the whole unit section,
since \(H\) is finite over the local scheme \(S\), so that every point of
\(H\) specializes to a point above \(s\).} and therefore contains an
induced open neighborhood \(V\) of the unit section of \(G_H\) over \(H\).
Now, the fibers of \(G_H\) over \(H\) being connected, and therefore
(being group preschemes) having a single associated point (VI\(_A\),
\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-1.1.1}{1.1.1}), it follows that, for every
\(t\in H\), \(V_t\) is schematically dense in \(G_t\);\footnote{Translator's
note: in more detail, after the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009),
Expos\'e~IX: \(G_t\) is a group of finite type over \(\kappa(t)\), hence
Cohen--Macaulay (VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-1.1.1}{1.1.1}) and
without embedded associated points; being connected, it is irreducible
(VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-2.4}{2.4}), so its only associated
point is its generic point, which lies in the non-empty open set \(V_t\);
by EGA~IV\(_2\), 3.1.8, \(V_t\) is therefore schematically dense in
\(G_t\).} hence, by
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:4:3}{4.3}, \(V\) is schematically dense in
\(G_H\).

Moreover, since \(K\) is an \(H\)-subgroup of \(G_H\), it induces on
each fiber \(G_h\) a subgroup \(K_h\). This subgroup contains an open
neighborhood of the unit element; because \(G_h\) is connected, it
follows that \(K_h=G_h\). Thus \(K=G_H\) set-theoretically. Finally,
\(K\) is a closed subprescheme of \(G_H\) containing the schematically
dense subprescheme \(V\), and hence \(K=G_H\). This proves \SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:5:6:item:a}{(a)}.

Part~\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:theorem:5:6:item:b}{(b)} follows directly from
\SGARefLink{sga3:ix:corollary:3:9}{3.9}: to verify that a subprescheme
\(K\) of \(P=G\times_SH\) equals \(P\), it is enough to verify, after
every base change \(S'\to S\) in which \(S'\) is the spectrum of an
Artinian quotient of a local ring of \(S\), that \(K'=P'\). \qed
