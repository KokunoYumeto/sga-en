\begin{proposition}\label{sga3:XXI:4.1}
Let \(n\) be the order of \(s_\alpha s_\beta\). Put \(u_0=0\), and,
for \(p\in\mathbb N\), define
\[
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
u_{2p+1}&=u_{2p}+(s_\alpha s_\beta)^p(\alpha),\\
u_{2p+2}&=u_{2p+1}+(s_\alpha s_\beta)^p s_\alpha(\beta).
\end{aligned}
\right.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:XXI:4.1:item:i}(i)] \(u_{k+2n}=u_k\), for every \(k\in\mathbb N\).
\item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:XXI:4.1:item:ii}(ii)] \(u_0=0\), \(u_1=\alpha\), \(u_{2n-1}=\beta\),
and \(u_{2n}=0\).
\item[(iii)] If \(1<k<2n-1\), then
\[
 u_k=a_k\alpha+b_k\beta,
 \qquad a_k,b_k\in\mathbb N.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Assertion \SGARefLink{sga3:XXI:4.1:item:i}{(i)} follows at once from \((s_\alpha s_\beta)^n=1\) and
\(u_{2n}=0\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXI, gives another description of the \(u_k\). In
our formulation: let \(\rho\) be half the sum of the positive roots, so that
\((\alpha^\ast,\rho)=(\beta^\ast,\rho)=1\) by 3.5.1, and let \(w_k\) be as in 4.2 below.
Then \(u_k=\rho-w_k^{-1}(\rho)\) for all \(k\): this holds for \(k=0\), and
\(w_{k+1}^{-1}=w_k^{-1}s_\alpha\) or \(w_k^{-1}s_\beta\) according as \(k\) is even or
odd, while \(\rho-s_\alpha(\rho)=\alpha\) and \(\rho-s_\beta(\rho)=\beta\), which gives
the recursion defining the \(u_k\). As \(w_{k+2n}=w_k\), this gives (i), and also
\(u_1=\alpha\) and \(u_{2n-1}=\beta\), since \(w_{2n-1}=s_\beta\).}

We prove \SGARefLink{sga3:XXI:4.1:item:ii}{(ii)} and (iii). Direct calculation gives, in the four cases,
the following sequences of values:\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text gives
these sequences for types \(B_2\) and \(G_2\) with the roles of \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\)
interchanged (for instance \(0,\alpha,2\alpha+\beta,3\alpha+3\beta,\ldots\) for \(B_2\)),
that is, for \(\ell(\alpha)\geq\ell(\beta)\), contrary to the convention at the beginning
of this section; the corrected values are those of the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~XXI.}
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
A_1\times A_1&0,\alpha,\beta+\alpha,\beta,0,\\[2pt]
A_2&0,\alpha,\beta+2\alpha,2\beta+2\alpha,
       2\beta+\alpha,\beta,0,\\[2pt]
B_2&0,\alpha,\beta+3\alpha,2\beta+4\alpha,
       3\beta+4\alpha,3\beta+3\alpha,2\beta+\alpha,\beta,0,\\[2pt]
G_2&0,\alpha,\beta+4\alpha,2\beta+6\alpha,
       4\beta+9\alpha,5\beta+10\alpha,6\beta+10\alpha,\\
   &\quad 6\beta+9\alpha,5\beta+6\alpha,4\beta+4\alpha,
       2\beta+\alpha,\beta,0.
\end{array}
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{sga3:XXI:4.2}\footnote{The following lemmas 4.2 and 4.4 are used in the
proof of Theorem~\ref{sga3:XXI:5.1}. There are at present simpler proofs of 5.1 (see
Bourbaki, \emph{Groupes et algèbres de Lie}, Chapter~IV). (Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXI, places this note at 4.1, extends it to 4.1--4.4,
and gives the reference as Chapter~V, \S3, Theorem~1, of Bourbaki's book.)}
Put
\[
 w_{2p}=(s_\beta s_\alpha)^p,
 \qquad
 w_{2p+1}=s_\alpha(s_\beta s_\alpha)^p,
\]
so that the \(w_i\), \(i=0,\ldots,2n-1\), are the distinct elements of
\(W\). Let \(x\in V^\ast\). One has
\[
 w_k(x)-x=n_k\alpha^\ast+m_k\beta^\ast,
 \qquad n_k,m_k\in\mathbb Q,
\]
with
\[
 n_k+m_k=-(x,u_k).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The proof is by induction on \(k\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXI, derives the lemma
directly from the description of the \(u_k\) given in the note to 4.1. In our
formulation: pairing with \(\rho\), which takes the value \(1\) on \(\alpha^\ast\) and
\(\beta^\ast\), and using (4), one finds
\(n_k+m_k=(w_k(x)-x,\rho)=(x,w_k^{-1}(\rho)-\rho)=-(x,u_k)\).} If \(k=0\), the formula is
trivial. We carry out, for example, the passage from \(w_{2p}\) to
\(w_{2p+1}\). One has \(w_{2p+1}=s_\alpha w_{2p}\), whence
\[
\begin{aligned}
w_{2p+1}(x)-x
 &=s_\alpha w_{2p}(x)-w_{2p}(x)+w_{2p}(x)-x\\
 &=-(w_{2p}(x),\alpha)\alpha^\ast+n_{2p}\alpha^\ast+m_{2p}\beta^\ast.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
n_{2p+1}+m_{2p+1}
 &=n_{2p}+m_{2p}
   -\bigl((s_\beta s_\alpha)^p(x),\alpha\bigr)\\
 &=-(x,u_{2p})-\bigl(x,(s_\alpha s_\beta)^p(\alpha)\bigr)\\
 &=-(x,u_{2p+1}).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}
