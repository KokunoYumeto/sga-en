\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{sga3:XXI:3.3.6}]
Choose \(w\in W\) such that
\(\operatorname{Card}(w(R_+)\cap P')\) is maximal. Replacing \(R_+\)
by \(w(R_+)\), we may assume that
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Card}(R_+\cap P')
 \geq
 \operatorname{Card}\bigl(R_+\cap s_\alpha(P')\bigr)
 \quad\text{for every }\alpha\in\mathscr{S}(R_+)=\Delta.
 \tag{\(\ast\)}\label{sga3:XXI:3.3.6:star}
\end{equation}
We show that \(R_+\subset P'\). Otherwise, since \(P'\) is closed,
there is an \(\alpha\in\Delta\) such that \(\alpha\notin P'\). As
\(P'\) satisfies (P 2), one then has \(-\alpha\in P'\), and also
\(-2\alpha\in P'\) if \(2\alpha\) is a root.

For every \(\beta\in R_+\cap P'\), one has \(\beta\neq\alpha\). If
\(2\alpha\) is not a root, then
\(s_\alpha(\beta)\in R_+\) by Lemma~\ref{sga3:XXI:3.3.1}; hence
\[
 s_\alpha(R_+\cap P')
 \subset R_+\cap s_\alpha(P').
\]
But \(R_+\cap s_\alpha(P')\) also contains
\(\alpha=s_\alpha(-\alpha)\), contradicting
\eqref{sga3:XXI:3.3.6:star}. If \(2\alpha\) is a root, the same
argument applies.
\end{proof}

Thus, to study closed sets of roots satisfying (P 2), it is enough to
consider those that contain the set of positive roots.

\begin{proposition}\label{sga3:XXI:3.3.10}
Let \(R_+\) be a system of positive roots. Let \(P'\) be closed,
\(R_+\subset P'\subset R\). If one writes
\(\Delta'=\mathscr{S}(R_+)\cap(-P')\), then \(P'\) is the union of
\(R_+\) and the set of roots that are linear combinations of the
elements of \(\Delta'\) with negative coefficients.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We prove the assertion by induction on the order of a root
\(\gamma\in P'\setminus R_+\). Put \(\Delta=\mathscr{S}(R_+)\). If
\(\operatorname{ord}_\Delta(\gamma)=-1\), then
\(-\gamma\in\mathscr{S}(R_+)\) and \(\gamma\in-\Delta'\). If
\(\operatorname{ord}_\Delta(\gamma)<-1\), there exists
\(\beta\in\mathscr{S}(R_+)\) such that \(\gamma+\beta\in R\) and
\(\operatorname{ord}_\Delta(\gamma+\beta)>\operatorname{ord}_\Delta(\gamma)\)
(apply Proposition~\ref{sga3:XXI:3.1.2} to \(-\gamma\)).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXI,
makes two points explicit. In our formulation:
\(\operatorname{ord}_\Delta(\gamma+\beta)=\operatorname{ord}_\Delta(\gamma)+1\leq-1\),
so \(\gamma+\beta\) is again a root of \(P'\setminus R_+\), of larger order, to which the
induction hypothesis applies; and the combination it gives has negative coefficients,
so that the same holds for \(\gamma=(\gamma+\beta)-\beta\) once \(\beta\in\Delta'\).}
Since
\(\gamma+\beta\) is the sum of two roots of \(P'\), it is an element of
\(P'\), hence, by the induction hypothesis, a linear combination of the
elements of \(\Delta'\). It suffices to check that \(\beta\in-P'\).
Now \(-\beta=\gamma-(\gamma+\beta)\); but \(\gamma\in P'\) and
\(-(\gamma+\beta)\) is positive, hence belongs to \(P'\).
\end{proof}
