\chapter*{Exposé XXV\\The Existence Theorem}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Exposé XXV. The Existence Theorem}
\begin{center}
\textit{by M. Demazure}
\end{center}

For completeness, in this Exposé we give a proof of the existence theorem for split
groups. Like Chevalley's original proof (``Sur certains schémas de groupes
semi-simples'', \textit{Séminaire Bourbaki}, May 1961, no.~219), it rests on the
existence of complex semisimple algebraic groups of every possible type. Cartier
gave the principle of a more satisfactory proof, establishing directly the
existence of a simply connected split semisimple \(\mathbb Z\)-group associated
with a given Cartan matrix (unpublished).
Let us point out, however, that the difficulty is not to give an explicit
construction of a group scheme, but to verify that the group so constructed
really satisfies the required conditions---essentially, that its fibers are
indeed smooth and reductive.

\SGASection{1}{Statement of the theorem}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxv:section:1}

\begin{sgastatement}{Theorem 1.1}
Let \(S\) be a nonempty scheme. The functor
\[
  G\longmapsto R(G)
\]
is an equivalence from the category of pinned reductive \(S\)-group schemes to
the category of pinned reduced root data.
\end{sgastatement}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxv:theorem:1:1}

By the uniqueness theorem (Exposé XXIII, 4.1), the preceding statement is
equivalent to the following.

\begin{sgastatement}{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:xxv:corollary:1-2}Corollary 1.2 (Existence of split groups)}
For every reduced root datum \(R\), there exists a split reductive
\(\mathbb Z\)-group \(G\) such that \(R(G)\simeq R\).
\end{sgastatement}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxv:corollary:1:2}

In particular:

\begin{sgastatement}{Corollary 1.3}
Let \(k\) be a field. For every splittable reductive \(k\)-group \(G_k\), there
exists a reductive \(\mathbb Z\)-group \(G\) such that
\[
  G\otimes_{\mathbb Z}k\simeq G_k.
\]
\end{sgastatement}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxv:corollary:1:3}

Conversely, first observe that to prove \SGARefLink{sga3:xxv:corollary:1-2}{1.2} it is enough, by \SGARefLink{sga3:xxii:prop:4.3.1}{Exposé XXII, 4.3.1},
\SGARefLink{sga3:XXI:6.5.10}{Exposé XXI, 6.5.10}, and the uniqueness theorem, to consider the case in which the root datum \(R\) is
simply connected (and even irreducible, if desired, by \SGARefLink{sga3:XXI:7.1.6}{Exposé XXI, 7.1.6}).

On the other hand, under the hypotheses of \SGARefLink{sga3:xxv:corollary:1:3}{1.3} the group scheme \(G\) has
constant type (since \(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb Z)\) is connected), hence type
\(R(G_k)\). By \SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:5.9}{Exposé XXIII, 5.9}, the validity of \SGARefLink{sga3:xxv:corollary:1:3}{1.3} for a given group \(G_k\)
therefore entails the existence of a split \(\mathbb Z\)-group of type
\(R(G_k)\).

Thus, to prove \SGARefLink{sga3:xxv:corollary:1-2}{1.2} and hence \SGARefLink{sga3:xxv:theorem:1:1}{1.1}, it is enough to prove \SGARefLink{sga3:xxv:corollary:1:3}{1.3} when \(k\) has
characteristic zero (for example, \(k=\mathbb C\)) and \(G_k\) is simply
connected (and in particular semisimple), together with the following
proposition.

\begin{sgastatement}{Proposition 1.4}
For every simply connected reduced root datum \(R\), there exists a semisimple
algebraic \(\mathbb C\)-group of type \(R\).
\end{sgastatement}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxv:proposition:1:4}

One may prove \SGARefLink{sga3:xxv:proposition:1:4}{1.4} as follows. First, one knows (see, for example,
Jacobson, \textit{Lie Algebras}, Chapter VII, Theorem 5) that there exists a
complex semisimple Lie algebra of type \(R\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXV, adds a
description of this Lie algebra by generators and relations, due to Serre
(J.-P.~Serre, \textit{Algèbres de Lie semi-simples complexes}, Benjamin, 1966,
Chapter~VI, \S5). In our formulation: let \(\Delta\) be a system of simple
roots of \(R\), and put \(n_{\alpha\beta}=(\alpha^\ast,\beta)\) for
\(\alpha,\beta\in\Delta\). The complex Lie algebra with generators
\(e_\alpha,f_\alpha,h_\alpha\) (\(\alpha\in\Delta\)) and relations
\[
 [h_\alpha,h_\beta]=0,\quad
 [e_\alpha,f_\beta]=\delta_{\alpha\beta}h_\alpha,\quad
 [h_\alpha,e_\beta]=n_{\alpha\beta}e_\beta,\quad
 [h_\alpha,f_\beta]=-n_{\alpha\beta}f_\beta
\]
for all \(\alpha,\beta\in\Delta\), together with
\[
 \operatorname{ad}(e_\alpha)^{1-n_{\alpha\beta}}(e_\beta)=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{ad}(f_\alpha)^{1-n_{\alpha\beta}}(f_\beta)=0
\]
for \(\alpha\neq\beta\), is a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra of
type \(R\). The structure constants of a Chevalley basis, and in particular
their signs, are treated in \SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:6.5}{Exposé XXIII, 6.5} and \SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:6.7}{6.7}, and in
J.~Tits, \textit{Sur les constantes de structure et le théorème d'existence
des algèbres de Lie semi-simples}, Publ.\ Math.\ IHÉS \textbf{31} (1966),
21--58.}
Taking the identity component of the group of automorphisms of this algebra,
one obtains a semisimple algebraic \(\mathbb C\)-group of type
\(\operatorname{ad}(R)\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXV, adds a proof
of this step. In our formulation: let \(\mathfrak g\) be the Lie algebra in
question and \(G\) the identity component of the algebraic group
\(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak g)\subset\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak g)\). An
automorphism of \(\mathfrak g\otimes_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C[\varepsilon]\)
reducing to the identity modulo \(\varepsilon\) has the form
\(1+\varepsilon D\) with \(D\) a derivation of \(\mathfrak g\), so
\(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\) is the Lie algebra of derivations of
\(\mathfrak g\); since every derivation of the semisimple algebra
\(\mathfrak g\) is inner, \(\operatorname{ad}\) identifies \(\mathfrak g\)
with \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\), and the adjoint representation of \(G\)
becomes its given faithful representation on \(\mathfrak g\). The Lie algebra
of the radical of \(G\) is a solvable ideal of \(\mathfrak g\), hence zero, so
\(G\) is semisimple. The Lie algebra of a maximal torus \(T\) of \(G\) is a
Cartan subalgebra of \(\mathfrak g\), and differentiation identifies the roots
of \(G\) with respect to \(T\) with those of \(\mathfrak g\) with respect to
\(\operatorname{Lie}(T)\); so the root system of \(G\) is that of \(R\).
Finally, the center of \(G\) acts trivially in the adjoint representation;
as this representation is faithful, the center is trivial, and \(G\) is
adjoint, of type \(\operatorname{ad}(R)\).}
By \textit{Bible}, 23-01, Proposition 1, one deduces the existence of a group
of type \(R\).

The remainder of this Exposé is devoted to the proof of \SGARefLink{sga3:xxv:corollary:1:3}{1.3} when \(k\) has
characteristic zero and \(G_k\) is semisimple. The proof proceeds in two
stages: the construction of a ``piece of \(\mathbb Z\)-group scheme''
(\S2), and the study of the group obtained by applying ``Weil's theorem''
(\S3).

To avoid confusion, in \S2 we shall not use the abbreviated notation \(X_k\)
for the \(k\)-scheme \(X\otimes_{\mathbb Z}k\), where \(X\) is a
\(\mathbb Z\)-scheme.
