\SGASection{4}{Appendix}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxv:section:4}

We used the following proposition in the course of the proof.

\begin{sgastatement}{Proposition 4.1}
Let \(S\) be a locally noetherian scheme of dimension at most \(1\);
let \(G\) and \(H\) be two \(S\)-group schemes of finite type; and let
\(f\colon G\to H\) be a quasi-finite separated group homomorphism.
Then \(f\) is affine.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXV, adds the
hypothesis that \(G\) is flat over \(S\), which is used in 4.3. The statement
as printed in 1970 holds without it. For every \(s\in S\), the kernel of
\(f_s\colon G_s\to H_s\) is finite, since \(f\) is quasi-finite; so \(f_s\) is
finite, as the composite of the quotient morphism
\(G_s\to G_s/\operatorname{Ker}(f_s)\), which is finite (Exposé
VI\textsubscript{B}, 9.2(vii)), and of a homomorphism with trivial kernel,
which is a closed immersion (Exposé VI\textsubscript{B}, 1.4.2). Hence \(f\)
is affine by \SGARefLink{sga3:xvi:lemma:4:2}{Exposé XVI, Lemma 4.2}.}
\end{sgastatement}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxv:proposition:4:1}

We shall give the proof only when \(G\) is smooth over \(S\), which is
indeed the hypothesis satisfied in the application above.

\SGANextSectionTarget{sga3:xxv:subsection:4-2}\SGASubsection{4.2}{}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxv:paragraph:4:2}

By EGA II, 1.6.4, we may suppose that \(S\) is reduced. By the usual
techniques of passage to the limit, we may suppose \(S\) local. If
\(\dim(S)=0\), the assertion is trivial.
Suppose therefore that \(\dim(S)=1\). By faithfully flat descent, we may
suppose that \(S\) is complete with algebraically closed residue field.
After replacing \(S\) by its normalization, if
necessary, we may suppose \(S\) normal (EGA II, 6.7.1, and
EGA \(0_{\mathrm{IV}}\), 23.1.5).
We are thus reduced to the case in which \(S\) is the spectrum of a
complete discrete valuation ring \(A\) with algebraically closed
residue field.

\SGANextSectionTarget{sga3:xxv:subsection:4-3}\SGASubsection{4.3}{}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxv:paragraph:4:3}

Let \(\eta\) (respectively \(s\)) be the generic (respectively closed)
point of \(S\). Consider the image \(f_\eta(G_\eta)\) of \(G_\eta\)
in \(H_\eta\). It is a closed subgroup scheme of \(H_\eta\). Let
\(H'\) be the schematic closure of \(f_\eta(G_\eta)\) in \(H\).
Since \(H'\to H\) is affine (it is a closed immersion), we may replace
\(H\) by \(H'\), and thus suppose that \(H\) is flat over \(S\) and
that \(f_\eta\) is surjective. Since \(f_\eta\) is finite and \(G,H\)
are flat over \(S\), we have
\[
 \dim(G_s)=\dim(G_\eta)=\dim(H_\eta)=\dim(H_s).
\]

\SGANextSectionTarget{sga3:xxv:subsection:4-4}\SGASubsection{4.4}{}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxv:paragraph:4:4}

Let \(H_s^0,\ldots,H_s^n\) be the irreducible components of \(H_s\),
where \(H_s^0\) denotes the identity component, and let
\(z_0,\ldots,z_n\) be their generic points. Since each local ring
\(\mathcal O_{H,z_i}\) has dimension at most \(1\), the morphism
\[
 G\times_H\mathcal O_{H,z_i}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{H,z_i}
\]
is affine, being quasi-finite and separated (see \SGARefLink{sga3:xvi:lemma:4:2}{Exposé XVI, Lemma 4.2}).\footnote{Translator's note: the
1970 text leaves the number of the lemma blank (``cf.\ lemme \ldots,
Exp.~XVI''); the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXV, supplies it.}
Thus \(G\) is affine over \(H\) in a neighborhood of \(z_i\). Let \(V\)
be the largest open subset of \(H\) over which \(G|_V\) is affine.
Then \(V\) contains every \(z_i\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXV, makes
explicit a step used below. Since \(V\) is open and contains the generic point
\(z_i\) of \(H_s^i\), the set \(V\cap H_s^i\) is a nonempty open subset of
\(H_s^i\); as \(\kappa(s)\) is algebraically closed, it contains a rational
point \(y_i\). The translations by the sections \(f(g)\), \(g\in G(S)\), used
below carry \(y_i\) to every rational point of \(H_s^i\), and \(V\) is stable
under them.}

On the other hand, \(V\) is plainly stable under translation by any
element \(g\in G(S)\). We have \(\dim(G_s)=\dim(H_s)\), and \(f_s\) is
finite, so
\[
 f(s)\colon G_s^0(s)\longrightarrow H_s^0(s)
\]
is surjective. Since \(A\) is complete and \(G\) is smooth over \(S\),
the canonical map \(G^0(S)\to G_s^0(s)\) is surjective; one deduces that,
for every \(h_0\in H_s^0(s)\), there exists \(g\in G(S)\) such that
\(f(g_s)=h_0\). Since
\(H_s^0(s)\) acts transitively on each \(H_s^i(s)\), it follows that
\(V\supset H_s(s)\), and therefore, since \(\kappa(s)\) is algebraically
closed, \(V\supset H_s\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXV, extends this
step to the case where \(G\) is flat, but not smooth, over \(S\). In our
formulation: smoothness serves only to lift a rational point \(g_0\) of
\(G_s^0\) with \(f(g_0)=h_0\) to a section of \(G\). For \(G\) flat over \(S\),
Exposé VI\textsubscript{B}, Lemma 5.6.1, and the henselian property of \(A\)
give a finite surjective morphism \(S_1\to S\), with \(S_1\) local of residue
field \(\kappa(s)\), and a section \(g\in G(S_1)\) whose value at the closed
point is \(g_0\). Translating by \(f(g)\) as above shows that
\(G\times_SS_1\) is affine over an open subset of \(H\times_SS_1\) containing
the point above \(h_0y_i\), where \(y_i\) is a rational point of
\(V\cap H_s^i\). That point is the only one above \(h_0y_i\), and
\(H\times_SS_1\to H\) is finite; so \(h_0y_i\) has an affine open
neighborhood \(W\) in \(H\) over whose inverse image \(G\times_SS_1\) is
affine. As \((G\times_HW)\times_SS_1\to G\times_HW\) is finite and
surjective, \(G\times_HW\) is affine by Chevalley's theorem (EGA II, 6.7.1).
Hence \(h_0y_i\in V\) for every \(h_0\in H_s^0(s)\), and the argument
concludes as before. See also the note to 4.1.}

Clearly \(V\supset H_\eta\), since \(f_\eta\) is finite. Consequently,
\(V=H\). \SGAQED
