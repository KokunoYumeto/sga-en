\SGARefTarget{sga3:xxvi:subsec:7:10}

One may study the relative positions of two minimal parabolic
subgroups; the results are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item Let \(P,P_1,P',P'_1\) be four minimal parabolic subgroups of
\(G\).  Then
\[
 t_2(P,P_1)=t_2(P',P'_1)
\]
\textup{(cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:xxvi:paragraph:4-5-2}{4.5.2})}, that is, the pairs \((P,P_1)\) and \((P',P'_1)\) are fpqc-locally
conjugate, if and only if there is a \(g\in G(S)\) such that
\[
 \Int(g)P=P',\qquad \Int(g)P_1=P'_1.
\]

\item Fix a minimal parabolic subgroup \(P\) of \(G\) with Levi
subgroup \(L\), and let \(T\) be a maximal torus of \(L\).  Consider
the scheme
\[
 \Par_{t_{\min}}(G;P)
\]
of minimal parabolic subgroups of \(G\) in standard position
relative to \(P\).  There is a morphism \textup{(cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:xxvi:paragraph:4-5-5}{4.5.5})}
\[
 f:\Par_{t_{\min}}(G;P)
   \longrightarrow W_P(T)\backslash W_G(T)/W_P(T)
\]
whose fibers are the orbits of \(P\) in
\(\Par_{t_{\min}}(G;P)\).  By \textup{(1)}, \(f\) therefore induces
a monomorphism
\[
 P(S)\backslash\Par_{t_{\min}}(G;P)(S)
 \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow
 \bigl(W_P(T)\backslash W_G(T)/W_P(T)\bigr)(S).
\]
Its image is identified with \(W\).  This is the Bruhat theorem:
each orbit of \(P(S)\) in \(\Par_{t_{\min}}(G;P)(S)\) contains
exactly one parabolic subgroup of \(G\) with Levi subgroup \(L\),
that is, one of the form
\[
 \Int(n)P,\qquad n\in\Norm_G(L)(S).
\]

\item In other words, let \(E\) be the set of elements \(g\in G(S)\)
such that \(\Int(g)P\) and \(P\) are in mutual standard position.
Then \(E\) has a partition into double cosets modulo \(P(S)\),
indexed by \(W\):
\[
 E=P(S)\,W\,P(S)\qquad\text{(obvious notation)}.
\]
One may also write, with \(U=\rad^u(P)\),
\[
 E=U(S)\cdot\Norm_G(L)(S)\cdot U(S),
\]
thus exhibiting a partition of \(E\) into double cosets modulo
\(U(S)\), indexed by \(\Norm_G(L)(S)\).

\item If \(S\) is the spectrum of a field, then \(E=G(S)\), and
one recovers \textup{[BT]}, 5.15.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{sgastatement}{Counter\SGARefLink{sga3:xxvi:counterexamples:7:11}{examples 7.11}}
Let
\[
 S=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf Z/4\mathbf Z),
 \qquad G=\operatorname{SL}_{2,S}.
\]
Let \(B\) be the usual Borel subgroup consisting of the matrices
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}
 \qquad\text{with }c=0,
\]
and put
\[
 g=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\2&1\end{pmatrix}\in G(S),
 \qquad B'=\Int(g)B.
\]
Then
\[
 B(S)=B'(S),
\]
whereas \(B\cap B'\) contains no maximal torus.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXVI, adds
the reason. In our formulation: \(B'\) is the stabilizer of the line spanned
by the first column \((-1,2)\) of \(g\), that is, the subgroup defined by
\(c=2(a+d)\). So \(B\cap B'\) is defined in \(B\) by \(2(a+a^{-1})=0\); it is
not flat over \(S\), since \(a+a^{-1}\) is killed by \(2\) without being a
multiple of \(2\), hence not smooth, and by 4.5.1 it contains no maximal torus
of \(G\), even locally. In particular \(B\) and \(B'\) are not in standard
position.}

This shows both that two distinct minimal parabolic subgroups may
have the same group of sections and that, in general, one cannot
decide from the groups \(P(S)\) and \(P'(S)\) alone whether two
minimal parabolic subgroups \(P,P'\) are in standard position.  In
particular, the subset \(E\) of \(G(S)\) does not appear to be
definable solely from the configuration
\[
 \{G(S),P(S),\Norm_G(L)(S)\}.
\]
In the example above, this subset is defined by \(c\ne2\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXVI,
considers \(E\) as a functor on \(S\)-schemes. In our formulation: for an
\(S\)-scheme \(S'\) and \(g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in G(S')\),
the subgroups \(\Int(g)B\) and \(B\) are in standard position if and only if,
locally on \(S'\), \(c\) is either zero or invertible. Indeed, over a local
base every maximal torus of \(B\) is conjugate under \(\rad^u(B)\) to the
diagonal torus, so it stabilizes exactly two lines, one of them that of the
first basis vector, the other spanned by a vector \((y,1)\); it lies in
\(\Int(g)B\) if and only if the first column \((a,c)\) of \(g\) spans one of
them, that is, \(c=0\) or \(c\) is invertible.}
\end{sgastatement}
