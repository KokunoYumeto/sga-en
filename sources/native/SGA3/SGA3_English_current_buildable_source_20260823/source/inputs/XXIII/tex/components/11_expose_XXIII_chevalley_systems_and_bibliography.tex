\SGANextSectionTarget{sga3:xxiii:section:6}\SGASection{6}{Chevalley Systems}

The explicit calculations of \SGARefLink{sga3:xxiii:section:3}{Section 3} have important numerical
consequences.

\SGARefTarget{sga3:XXIII:6.1}
\SGAStatement{Definition 6.1}{Let \(S\) be a scheme and
\((G,T,M,R)\) a split \(S\)-group. A \emph{Chevalley system} of
\(G\) is a family
\[
  (X_\alpha)_{\alpha\in R},
  \qquad
  X_\alpha\in\Gamma(S,\mathfrak g^\alpha)^\times,
\]
satisfying:
\begin{description}
  \item[(SC)] for every pair \(\alpha,\beta\in R\),
  \[
    \operatorname{Ad}(w_\alpha(X_\alpha))X_\beta
      =\pm X_{s_\alpha(\beta)}.
  \]
\end{description}}

Recall (\SGARefLink{sga3:xx:3.1}{Exposé~XX, 3.1}) that
\[
  w_\alpha(X_\alpha)
    =\exp(X_\alpha)\exp(-X_\alpha^{-1})\exp(X_\alpha).
\]
Condition (SC) implies, in particular,\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has
\(X_{-\alpha}=\pm X_\alpha\) here, and refers for the relation below, which it writes
\(\operatorname{Ad}(w_\alpha(X_\alpha))X_\alpha=-X_{-\alpha}\) with \(X_{-\alpha}\) as in
1.2, to Exp.~XX, 2.10; the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXIII, corrects this
reference to XX, 3.7.}
\[
  X_{-\alpha}=\pm X_\alpha^{-1}\qquad(\alpha\in R)
\]
by the relation (\SGARefLink{sga3:xx:3.7}{Exposé~XX, 3.7})
\[
  \operatorname{Ad}(w_\alpha(X_\alpha))X_\alpha=-X_\alpha^{-1}.
\]

\SGARefTarget{sga3:XXIII:6.2}
\SGAStatement{Proposition 6.2}{Every split group admits a Chevalley
system. More precisely, if
\[
  \bigl(\Delta,(X'_\alpha)_{\alpha\in\Delta}\bigr)
\]
is a pinning (\SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:1.1}{1.1}) of \((G,T,M,R)\), there is a Chevalley system
\((X_\alpha)_{\alpha\in R}\) with \(X_\alpha=X'_\alpha\) for
\(\alpha\in\Delta\).}

First, it suffices to verify (SC) for \(\alpha\in\Delta\). Indeed,
for every \(\alpha\in R\), choose a sequence
\(\alpha_i\in\Delta\) such that
\[
  \alpha=s_{\alpha_1}\cdots s_{\alpha_n}(\alpha_{n+1}).
\]
Repeated use of (SC) gives
\[
 X_\alpha
  =\pm\operatorname{Ad}\!\left(
    w_{\alpha_1}(X_{\alpha_1})\cdots
    w_{\alpha_n}(X_{\alpha_n})\right)X_{\alpha_{n+1}}.
\]
By \SGARefLink{sga3:xxiii:lemma:3-1-1:item:iii}{Lemma 3.1.1(iii)},
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &w_{\alpha_1}(X_{\alpha_1})\cdots w_{\alpha_n}(X_{\alpha_n})
  w_{\alpha_{n+1}}(X_{\alpha_{n+1}})
  w_{\alpha_n}(X_{\alpha_n})^{-1}\cdots
  w_{\alpha_1}(X_{\alpha_1})^{-1}\\
 &\hspace{42mm}=\alpha^*(\pm1)w_\alpha(X_\alpha).
\end{aligned}
\]
Now
\[
 w_{\alpha_i}(X_{\alpha_i})^{-1}
  =\alpha_i^*(-1)w_{\alpha_i}(X_{\alpha_i}),
\]
and
\[
 \beta(\gamma^*(-1))=(-1)^{(\gamma^*,\beta)}=\pm1.
\]
It follows that (SC) for every pair \((\alpha_i,\gamma)\) implies
(SC) for every \((\alpha,\beta)\).

For each \(\alpha\in R\), choose such a sequence and define
\[
 X_\alpha=
 \operatorname{Ad}\!\left(
  w_{\alpha_1}(X_{\alpha_1})\cdots
  w_{\alpha_n}(X_{\alpha_n})\right)X'_{\alpha_{n+1}}.
\]
It remains to prove the following lemma.

\SGARefTarget{sga3:XXIII:6.3}
\SGAStatement{Lemma 6.3}{Let
\[
 (G,T,M,R,\Delta,(X_\alpha)_{\alpha\in\Delta})
\]
be a pinned \(S\)-group. If \(\alpha_i\), \(0\le i\le n+1\), are
simple roots such that
\[
  s_{\alpha_1}\cdots s_{\alpha_n}(\alpha_{n+1})=\alpha_0,
\]
then
\[
  \operatorname{Ad}(w_{\alpha_1}\cdots w_{\alpha_n})
    X_{\alpha_{n+1}}=\pm X_{\alpha_0}.
\]}

Arguing as in \SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:2.3.4}{2.3.4}, using Tits's lemma, reduces the proof to:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:XXIII:6.3:item:a}(a)] \(G\) has semisimple rank at most \(2\); or
  \item[\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:XXIII:6.3:item:b}(b)] \(w_{\alpha_1}\cdots w_{\alpha_n}\) is a section of
    \(T\).
\end{enumerate}
In case \SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:6.3:item:a}{(a)}, note that 6.3 is a consequence of 6.2, and that 6.2 has
been verified in \SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:3.1.2:item:ii}{3.1.2} (respectively \SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:3.2.1:item:ii}{3.2.1}, \SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:3.3.1:item:ii}{3.3.1},
\SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:3.4.1:item:ii}{3.4.1}), (ii).

In case \SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:6.3:item:b}{(b)}, it suffices to show that if
\[
  s_{\alpha_1}\cdots s_{\alpha_n}=1,
\]
then \(t=w_{\alpha_1}\cdots w_{\alpha_n}\) satisfies
\(\alpha(t)=\pm1\) for every root \(\alpha\). By the structure of the
Weyl group (\SGARefLink{sga3:XXI:5.1}{Exposé~XXI, 5.1}), the word in the free group generated by the
\(s_\alpha\) lies in the normal subgroup generated by
\[
  (s_\alpha s_\beta)^{n_{\alpha\beta}}.
\]
Thus one reduces to a word
\[
 s_{\alpha_1}\cdots s_{\alpha_i}
 (s_{\alpha_{i+1}}s_{\alpha_{i+2}})
 ^{n_{\alpha_{i+1}\alpha_{i+2}}}
 s_{\alpha_i}\cdots s_{\alpha_1},
\]
for which
\[
  t=s_{\alpha_1}\cdots s_{\alpha_i}
      (t_{\alpha_{i+1}\alpha_{i+2}}).
\]
The assertion is now immediate from the values of
\(t_{\alpha_1\alpha_2}\) computed in \SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:3.1.2:item:i}{3.1.2(i)}, \SGARefLink{sga3:xxiii:proposition:3-2-1:item:i}{3.2.1(i)},
\SGARefLink{sga3:xxiii:proposition:3-3-1:item:i}{3.3.1(i)}, and \SGARefLink{sga3:xxiii:proposition:3-4-1:item:i}{3.4.1(i)}.

\SGARefTarget{sga3:XXIII:6.4}
\SGAStatement{Proposition 6.4}{Let \(S\) be a scheme, \((G,T,M,R)\) a split
\(S\)-group, and \((X_\alpha)_{\alpha\in R}\) a Chevalley system of \(G\). Let
\(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) be two nonproportional roots, and suppose
\[
  \operatorname{length}(\alpha)\le\operatorname{length}(\beta),
  \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
  |(\beta^*,\alpha)|\le|(\alpha^*,\beta)|.
\]
Let \(p\) and \(q\) be the integers \(\ge0\) such that the set of roots of the
form \(\beta+k\alpha\), \(k\in\mathbf Z\), is
\[
  \{\beta-(p-1)\alpha,\ldots,\beta,\ldots,\beta+q\alpha\}
\]
(cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:XXI:2.3.5}{Exposé~XXI, 2.3.5}; by \textit{loc.\ cit.}, one
therefore has \(-(\alpha^*,\beta)=q-p+1\)). Then the commutation relation between
\(U_\alpha\) and \(U_\beta\) is given by the following table (which exhausts the
possible cases, since the length \(p+q-1\) of the preceding string of roots is
\(\le3\)), where for each \(\gamma\in R\) we put \(p_\gamma(x)=\exp(xX_\gamma)\):
\begin{description}
  \item[\(p\) arbitrary, \(q=0\):]
  \[
    p_\beta(y)p_\alpha(x)=p_\alpha(x)p_\beta(y).
  \]
  \item[\(p=1\), \(q=1\):]
  \[
    p_\beta(y)p_\alpha(x)=p_\alpha(x)p_\beta(y)p_{\alpha+\beta}(\pm xy).
  \]
  \item[\(p=1\), \(q=2\):]
  \[
    p_\beta(y)p_\alpha(x)
     =p_\alpha(x)p_\beta(y)p_{\alpha+\beta}(\pm xy)p_{2\alpha+\beta}(\pm x^2y).
  \]
  \item[\(p=1\), \(q=3\):]
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    p_\beta(y)p_\alpha(x)
     ={}&p_\alpha(x)p_\beta(y)p_{\alpha+\beta}(\pm xy)p_{2\alpha+\beta}(\pm x^2y)\\
     &{}\cdot p_{3\alpha+\beta}(\pm x^3y)p_{3\alpha+2\beta}(\pm x^3y^2).
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  \item[\(p=2\), \(q=1\):]
  \[
    p_\beta(y)p_\alpha(x)=p_\alpha(x)p_\beta(y)p_{\alpha+\beta}(\pm2xy).
  \]
  \item[\(p=2\), \(q=2\):]
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
    p_\beta(y)p_\alpha(x)
     ={}&p_\alpha(x)p_\beta(y)p_{\alpha+\beta}(\pm2xy)\\
     &{}\cdot p_{2\alpha+\beta}(\pm3x^2y)p_{\alpha+2\beta}(\pm3xy^2).
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  \item[\(p=3\), \(q=1\):]
  \[
    p_\beta(y)p_\alpha(x)=p_\alpha(x)p_\beta(y)p_{\alpha+\beta}(\pm3xy).
  \]
\end{description}}

\SGAProof
By \SGARefLink{sga3:XXI:3.5.4}{Exposé XXI, 3.5.4}, there is a system of simple roots \(\Delta\)
such that \(\alpha\in\Delta\), and there are
\(\alpha'\in\Delta\), \(a,b\ge0\), with
\[
  \beta=a\alpha+b\alpha'.
\]
Consider the pinning \((\Delta,(X_\alpha)_{\alpha\in\Delta})\) of \(G\). The desired relation
lies in \(U_{\alpha\alpha'}\), so it follows
from the rank-two calculations of Section 3 and condition (SC).

\SGARefTarget{sga3:XXIII:6.5}
\SGAStatement{Corollary 6.5 (Chevalley's rule)}{Let
\((X_\alpha)_{\alpha\in R}\) be a Chevalley system of the split
\(S\)-group \(G\). If \(\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\in R\), then
\[
  [X_\alpha,X_\beta]=\pm pX_{\alpha+\beta},
\]
where \(p\) is the least positive integer for which
\(\beta-p\alpha\) is not a root.}

The assertion is symmetric in \(\alpha,\beta\); after arranging
\(\operatorname{length}(\alpha)\le\operatorname{length}(\beta)\),
it follows from \SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:6.4}{Proposition 6.4}.

\SGARefTarget{sga3:XXIII:6.6}
\SGAStatement{Corollary 6.6}{Let \(S\) be a scheme such that
\(6\cdot1_S\ne0\), and let \((G,T,M,R)\) be a split \(S\)-group.
If \(R'\subset R\) is such that
\[
  \mathfrak g_{R'}
   =\mathfrak t\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R'}\mathfrak g^\alpha
\]
is a Lie subalgebra of \(\mathfrak g\), then \(R'\) is a closed
subset of \(R\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:XXI:3.1.4}{Exposé~XXI, 3.1.4}).}

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXIII; our
formulation). A proof of 6.6, under a weaker hypothesis. Let
\((X_\gamma)_{\gamma\in R}\) be a Chevalley system of \(G\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:6.2}{6.2}), and let \(\alpha,\beta\in R'\) with
\(\alpha+\beta\in R\). By \SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:6.5}{6.5},
\([X_\alpha,X_\beta]=\pm pX_{\alpha+\beta}\), where \(p\) is the least integer \(>0\)
such that \(\beta-p\alpha\) is not a root; as the \(\alpha\)-string through \(\beta\) has
at most four terms (\SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:6.4}{6.4}), \(p\in\{1,2,3\}\). Since
\(\mathfrak g_{R'}\) is a Lie subalgebra, \([X_\alpha,X_\beta]\) is a section of
\(\mathfrak g_{R'}\); its component in \(\mathfrak g^{\alpha+\beta}\) for the
decomposition \(\mathfrak g=\mathfrak t\oplus\bigoplus_{\gamma\in R}\mathfrak g^\gamma\)
is \(\pm p\,X_{\alpha+\beta}\), which is nonzero as soon as \(p\cdot1_S\ne0\), because
\(X_{\alpha+\beta}\) is a basis of \(\mathfrak g^{\alpha+\beta}\). In that case
\(\mathfrak g^{\alpha+\beta}\subset\mathfrak g_{R'}\), that is, \(\alpha+\beta\in R'\).
Hence \(R'\) is closed (\SGARefLink{sga3:XXI:3.1.3}{Exposé~XXI, 3.1.3}) as soon as
\(2\cdot1_S\ne0\) and \(3\cdot1_S\ne0\); this follows from \(6\cdot1_S\ne0\), but also
holds, for example, for \(S=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf Z/6\mathbf Z)\). Some such
hypothesis is needed. Suppose that \(2\cdot1_S=0\) and that \(R\) is of type \(B_n\) or
\(C_n\) (\(n\ge2\)) or \(F_4\) (respectively that \(3\cdot1_S=0\) and that \(R\) is of
type \(G_2\)), and let \(R'\) be the set of short roots. If \(\alpha,\beta\in R'\) and
\(\alpha+\beta\) is a long root, then \(p=2\) (respectively \(p=3\)) in 6.5, so that
\([X_\alpha,X_\beta]=0\); hence \(\mathfrak g_{R'}\) is a Lie subalgebra of \(\mathfrak g\),
but \(R'\) is not closed. For example, over a field of characteristic \(2\), the special
orthogonal group of the quadratic form \(x_1y_1+\cdots+x_ny_n\) is contained in the
symplectic group of its polar form, and its roots with respect to the diagonal maximal
torus are the short roots of the root system \(C_n\) of the latter.

\SGARefTarget{sga3:XXIII:6.7}
\SGAParagraph{6.7}
The exact signs in this section can be determined by studying
\(\operatorname{Norm}_G(T)\), and more precisely the extended Weyl
group
\[
  W^*=\operatorname{Norm}_{\operatorname{\acute Ep}(R)}(T(R))(\mathbf Z),
\]
which is an extension of \(W(R)\) by an abelian group of type
\((2,2,\ldots,2)\), and which accounts for the signs.\footnote{J.~Tits, \textit{Sur les constantes de
structure et le théorème d'existence des algèbres de Lie semi-simples}, Publ.\ Math.\
I.H.É.S., no.~31.}

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~XXIII; our
formulation). If \(\alpha\ne\beta\) are simple roots of a pinned group, the elements
\(a=w_\alpha\) and \(b=w_\beta\) satisfy the braid relation \(aba\cdots=bab\cdots\),
with \(n_{\alpha\beta}\) factors on each side; this applies in particular to the elements
\(w_\alpha\) of \(W^*\). Since \(a\) and \(b\) lie in \(Z_{\alpha\beta}\), it suffices to
treat the four types of no.~3; recall that \(a^2=t_\alpha\) and \(b^2=t_\beta\), with
\(t_\alpha^2=t_\beta^2=e\). Type \(A_1\times A_1\): \(ab=ba\)
(\SGARefLink{sga3:xxiii:lemma:3-1-1:item:ii}{3.1.1~(ii)}). Type \(A_2\): by
\SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:3.2.1:item:i}{3.2.1~(i)}, \(bab=(aba)^{-1}=a^{-1}b^{-1}a^{-1}\), so
\(aba=bab\) amounts to \(t_\alpha bt_\alpha=b^{-1}\), that is, to
\(s_\beta(t_\alpha)\,t_\alpha=t_\beta\); this holds, since
\(s_\beta(t_\alpha)=t_\alpha t_\beta^{-1}\) by
\SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:3.1.1:item:iv}{3.1.1~(iv)}. Type \(B_2\): the first two formulas of
\SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:3.3.6}{3.3.6} give \(babab=(aba)\,t_\beta\), hence
\(baba=aba\,t_\beta b^{-1}=abab\). Type \(G_2\): the second and third formulas of
\SGARefLink{sga3:XXIII:3.4.9}{3.4.9} give \(abababa=(babab)\,t_\alpha\), hence
\(ababab=babab\,t_\alpha a^{-1}=bababa\). See also J.~Tits, \textit{Normalisateurs de
tores I. Groupes de Coxeter étendus}, J.~Algebra \textbf{4} (1966), 96--116.
