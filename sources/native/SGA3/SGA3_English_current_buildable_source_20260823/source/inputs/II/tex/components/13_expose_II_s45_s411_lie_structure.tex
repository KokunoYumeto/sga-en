\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:proposition:4-5}\textbf{Proposition 4.5.}
\textit{Let \(F\) be a good \(\mathcal O_S\)-module. There is a functorial
isomorphism}
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{Lie}}\!
   \left(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)/S,
         \mathcal M\right)
 \xrightarrow{\sim}
 \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}
   \left(F,\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(F/S,\mathcal M)\right)
\]
\textit{which respects the \(\mathcal O_S\)-module structures. In
particular, there is an isomorphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules}
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{Lie}}\!
   \left(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)/S\right)
 \xrightarrow{\sim}
 \underline{\operatorname{End}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F).
\]

This follows immediately from \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:subsection:4-3}{4.3} and from (i), (ii), (iii).\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~II, adds to the statement
that \(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)/S\)
satisfies (E) and, as a part (ii), that
\(\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)\) is a good
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module; both are proved in the proof of 4.7 below. Its
proof of the isomorphism uses the compatibility of the morphisms \(d\) for
\(F\) and for \(\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)\)
recorded in our note to 4.3.}

Let us then make the following definition.

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:definition:4-6}\textbf{Definition 4.6.}
We say that the functor in groups \(G\) over \(S\) is \emph{good} if
\(G\) satisfies condition \((E)\) relative to \(S\) and if
\(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\) is a good
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~II, adds that a good \(\mathcal O_S\)-module
\(F\) is a good \(S\)-group: by the Corollary of 4.4, \(F/S\) satisfies (E)
and \(d\colon F\to\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(F/S)\) is an isomorphism
of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules.}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:example:4-6-1}\textbf{Example.}
If \(G\) is representable, it is good: \(G\) satisfies \((E)\), and
\(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\) is of the form \(V(\mathcal E)\),
hence good.

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~II; our
formulation). If \(G/S\) satisfies (E), then
\(F=\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\) satisfies (E), the morphism
\(d\colon F\to\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(F/S)\) is
\(\mathcal O_S\)-linear, and \(G\) is good if and only if \(d\) is
bijective. Indeed, write \(F(\mathcal N)=\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S,\mathcal N)\).
A point of \(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(F(\mathcal N)/S,\mathcal M)\)
over \(S'\) is a morphism
\(\Phi\colon I_{S'}(\mathcal N)\times_{S'}I_{S'}(\mathcal M)\to G\) whose
restrictions along the zero sections of \(I_{S'}(\mathcal N)\) and of
\(I_{S'}(\mathcal M)\) are both the unit; this description is symmetric,
whence \(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(F(\mathcal N)/S,\mathcal M)\simeq
\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(F(\mathcal M)/S,\mathcal N)\), functorially
in \(\mathcal M\) and \(\mathcal N\). As \(G/S\) satisfies (E),
\(F(\mathcal M_1\oplus\mathcal M_2)\simeq F(\mathcal M_1)\times_S
F(\mathcal M_2)\), and with 3.8 this gives
\(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(F(\mathcal N)/S,\mathcal M_1\oplus\mathcal M_2)
\simeq\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(F(\mathcal N)/S,\mathcal M_1)\times_S
\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(F(\mathcal N)/S,\mathcal M_2)\); so
\(F(\mathcal N)/S\) satisfies (E) by Remark~1) of 4.1. The morphism \(d\)
is then \(\mathcal O_S\)-linear by 4.3, and \(F\) is good if and only if
\(d\) is bijective (our note to the Corollary of 4.4).
\par\medskip

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:theorem:4-7}\textbf{Theorem 4.7.}
\textit{If \(F\) is a good \(\mathcal O_S\)-module, then the
\(S\)-group
\(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)\)
is good.}

In any case there is an isomorphism
\[
 T_{\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)/S}(\mathcal M)
 \xrightarrow{\sim}
 \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}
   \bigl(F,T_{F/S}(\mathcal M)\bigr)
\]
if \(F/S\) satisfies (E) and \(T_{F/S}(\mathcal M)\) is given the
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module structure deduced from that of \(F\). If \(F\) is
good, then it satisfies (E), and there are isomorphisms
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}
   \bigl(F,T_{F/S}(\mathcal M)\bigr)
 \xrightarrow{\sim}
 \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}
   \bigl(F,F\otimes T_{\mathcal O_S/S}(\mathcal M)\bigr).
\]
It follows first that
\(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)\) satisfies
(E) relative to \(S\). On the other hand,
\(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)/S)
\simeq\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)\), and this
last module is good if \(F\) is good (because
\(T_{\mathcal O_S/S}(\mathcal M)\) is ``free of finite type'' over
\(\mathcal O_S\)).

\SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:subsection:4-7-1}%
Now let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group and \(F\) a good
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module. Suppose given a linear representation of
\(G\) in \(F\), that is (\SGARefLink{sga3:i:definition:4-7-1}{I~4.7.1}), a morphism of \(S\)-groups
\[
 \rho:G\longrightarrow
 \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F).
\]
One deduces from \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:proposition:4-5}{4.5} a morphism
\[
 \rho':\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)
 \longrightarrow
 \underline{\operatorname{End}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)
\]
which (if \(G/S\) satisfies (E)) is a morphism of
\(\mathcal O_S\)-modules.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~II, writes \(\rho'\), also denoted \(d\rho\), as
the composite of \(L(\rho)\colon\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\to
\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)/S)\)
with the isomorphism of 4.5 (linear under (E) by our note to 4.1 and by
4.7), and adds the formula it uses in the bracket construction below:
writing \(\mathcal O(I_{S'})=\mathcal O(S')[t]\), an element \(X\) of
\(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)(S')\subset G(I_{S'})\) acts on
\(F_{I_{S'}}\) by \(\rho(X)=\mathrm{id}+t\rho'(X)\), since an endomorphism
\(Y\) of \(F_{S'}\) corresponds under 4.5 to the automorphism
\(\mathrm{id}+tY\) (see the proof of 4.8).}

\SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:definition:4-7-2}%
In particular, let \(G\) be a good \(S\)-group. Then
\(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\) is a good
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module, and there is a morphism of \(S\)-groups
\[
 \operatorname{Ad}:G\longrightarrow
 \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}
   \bigl(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\bigr).
\]
By the preceding construction, it induces a morphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules
\[
 \operatorname{ad}:
 \underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)
 \longrightarrow
 \underline{\operatorname{End}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}
   \bigl(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\bigr),
\]
or, equivalently, a bilinear morphism
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)
 \times_S
 \underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)
 \longrightarrow
 \underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S),
 \qquad
 (x,y)\longmapsto[x,y]=\operatorname{ad}(x)\cdot y.
\]
Here \(x,y\) are arbitrary elements of
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)(S')
 =
 \underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G_{S'}/S')(S').
\]
If \(G\) is commutative, then \([x,y]=0\).

note first that it suffices to do so for
\[
 x,y\in\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)(S).
\]
Let us note next that there is a canonical isomorphism
\(I_S\times_SI_S\simeq I_{I_S}\); to avoid confusion, let \(I\) and
\(I'\) denote two copies of \(I_S\). There is then a commutative square

Now in such a diagram, if one takes two elements \(x\) and \(y\) as
indicated and lifts them arbitrarily to \(x'\) and \(y'\) in

\[
 x'y'x'^{-1}y'^{-1}

of the two elements obtained does not depend on the chosen lifts and is

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~II; our
formulation; see also M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel, \emph{Groupes
alg\'ebriques}, Masson/North-Holland, 1970, II, \S4, 4.2). Here is the
verification. Write \(\mathcal O(I)=\mathcal O(S)[t]\) and
\(\mathcal O(I')=\mathcal O(S)[t']\), with \(t^2=t'^2=0\); thus
\(y\in L(S)\subset G(I)\) and \(x\in L(S)\subset G(I')\). As \(G\) and
\(L\) are good, \(L(I')=L(S)\oplus t'L(S)\), and the ninth piece
\(L(S)\) maps to \(L(I')\) by \(u\mapsto t'u\) (the morphism \(d\), our note
to 4.3). Take for \(y'\) the image of \(y\) under the map
\(L(S)\to L(I')\) induced by \(I'\to S\). Write \(x'=s(x)\,\ell\), where
\(s\colon G(I')\to G(I\times I')\) is induced by the projection
\(I\times I'\to I'\) and \(\ell\in L(I')\); as \(L(I')\) is commutative,
\(x'y'x'^{-1}=s(x)\,y'\,s(x)^{-1}=\operatorname{Ad}(x)(y')\), the adjoint
action over the base \(I'\). By the formula recalled in our note to the
construction of \(\rho'\), \(\operatorname{Ad}(x)=\mathrm{id}+t'\operatorname{ad}(x)\)
on \(L(I')\), so \(x'y'x'^{-1}y'^{-1}=t'\,[x,y]\), which is the image of
\(z=[x,y]\).
\par\medskip

Two properties appear on this construction:

\textit{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:subsection:4-7-3:item:i}(i)} the bracket is ``functorial in \(G\)'': precisely,
\[
 G\longmapsto\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)
\]
is a functor from the category of good \(S\)-groups to the category of
good \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules equipped with a bilinear composition law;

\textit{(ii)} \([x,y]+[y,x]=0\): indeed, the diagram is symmetric about
the first diagonal.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~II, spells this out: exchanging the roles of \(I\) and
\(I'\), and of \(x\) and \(y\), replaces the commutator
\(x'y'x'^{-1}y'^{-1}\) by its inverse \(y'x'y'^{-1}x'^{-1}\). It also notes
that \([x,x]=0\) does not follow: lifting \(x\) once into \(L(I)\) and once
into \(L(I')\), one does not know a priori that the two lifts commute
(cf.\ 4.10).}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:proposition:4-8}\textbf{Proposition 4.8.}
\textit{Let \(F\) be a good \(\mathcal O_S\)-module. Under the
identification}
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{Lie}}\!
  \left(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)/S\right)
 \xrightarrow{\sim}
 \underline{\operatorname{End}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F),
\]
\textit{\(\operatorname{Ad}(x)\cdot X\) is transformed into
\(x\circ X\circ x^{-1}\), and \([X,Y]\) into \(X\circ Y-Y\circ X\).}

Let us consider first
\(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_{I_S}\text{-mod.}}(F_{I_S})\).
An element \(x\) of this group is, by the definition of base change for
functors, the giving, for every \(S'\to I_S\), of an
\(\mathcal O(S')\)-automorphism \(x_{S'}\) of \(F(S')\). Now let us remark
that giving \(S'\to I_S\) is equivalent to giving \(S'\to S\) together with
a section \(s:S'\to I_{S'}\). One then sees at once that \(x_{S'}\) is
determined by \(x_{I_{S'}}\), so that it suffices to verify the required
formulas on the \(F(I_{S'})\). Now
\(F(I_{S'})=F(S')\cdot\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(F/S)(S')=F(S')\cdot F(S')\),
since \(F\) is good. Write \(\mathcal O(I_{S'})=\mathcal O(S')[t]\),
\(t^2=0\). \SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:scholium:4-5-1}It is immediate that the endomorphism \(X\) of \(F\)
corresponds, under the given identification, to the automorphism
\(\mathrm{id}+tX\) of \(F(I_{S'})\). It only remains to translate the
definitions to reduce the announced formulas to
\[
 x\circ(\mathrm{id}+tX)\circ x^{-1}=\mathrm{id}+t\,(x\circ X\circ x^{-1})
\]
and
\[
 (\mathrm{id}+tX)\circ(\mathrm{id}+t'Y)\circ(\mathrm{id}+tX)^{-1}
 \circ(\mathrm{id}+t'Y)^{-1}=\mathrm{id}+tt'(X\circ Y-Y\circ X)
\]
when \(t^2=t'^2=0\).

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:corollary:4-8-1}\textbf{Corollary 1.}
\textit{Let \(G\) be a good \(S\)-group and let
\(x,y,z\in\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)(S')\). Then}
\[
 [x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0.
\]
Indeed, if \(G\) is good, then \(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\) is a
good \(\mathcal O_S\)-module, and the morphism of \(S\)-groups
\[
 \operatorname{Ad}:G\longrightarrow
 \underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}
  \bigl(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\bigr)
\]
gives by functoriality
\[
 [\operatorname{ad}x,\operatorname{ad}y]
 =\operatorname{ad}x\circ\operatorname{ad}y
  -\operatorname{ad}y\circ\operatorname{ad}x
 =\operatorname{ad}[x,y],
\]
which, applied to an element \(z\), gives the Jacobi relation.\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~II, makes explicit that
the functoriality used is (i) above, applied to \(\operatorname{Ad}\), which
is legitimate because
\(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S))\)
is good by 4.7, and that the first equality is Proposition 4.8.}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:corollary:4-8-2}\textbf{Corollary 2.}
\textit{Let \(G\) be a good \(S\)-group acting linearly on a good
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(F\); equivalently, let \(F\) be a
\(G\)-\(\mathcal O_S\)-module, with \(G\) and \(F\) good. Then the
linear map}
\[
 \rho':\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)
 \longrightarrow
 \underline{\operatorname{End}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)
\]
\textit{is a representation; that is,}
\[
 \rho'([x,y])
 =\rho'(x)\circ\rho'(y)-\rho'(y)\circ\rho'(x).
\]

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:scholium:4-9}\textbf{Scholium 4.9.}
To every good \(S\)-group \(G\) (for example, every representable
one) we have functorially associated a good \(\mathcal O_S\)-module
\(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\) equipped with a bilinear map
satisfying
\[
 [x,y]+[y,x]=0,
 \qquad
 [x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0.
\]
We shall call \(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\), equipped with this
structure, the ``Lie algebra'' of \(G\) over \(S\) (the quotation marks
being justified by the fact that \(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\)
is not, strictly speaking, an \(\mathcal O_S\)-Lie algebra). To every
linear representation of \(G\) in a good \(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(F\) is
associated a representation of its ``Lie algebra''. In particular, to the
adjoint representation of \(G\) is associated the adjoint representation of
its ``Lie algebra''.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~II, writes instead that it is not known whether
\(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\) is an \(\mathcal O_S\)-Lie algebra in
the strict sense: the axiom in question is \([x,x]=0\), which does not
follow from \([x,y]+[y,x]=0\) when \(2\) is not invertible on \(S\)
(see 4.10).}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:definition:4-10}\textbf{Definition 4.10.}
An \(S\)-functor in groups \(G\) is called \emph{very good} if it is
good and \(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\) is an
\(\mathcal O_S\)-Lie algebra, that is, if \([x,x]=0\) identically.

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:example:4-10-1}\textbf{Examples of very good \(S\)-groups.}
The group \(\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-mod.}}(F)\)
for every good \(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(F\). Every representable group
(see below). Every good \(S\)-group admitting a monomorphism into a very
good \(S\)-group, for example every good subgroup functor of a
representable group, or every good \(S\)-group admitting a faithful
linear representation in a good \(\mathcal O_S\)-module, for example
every good \(S\)-group such that \(\operatorname{Ad}\) is a
monomorphism\dots

\subsection*{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:subsection:4-11}4.11.}
Suppose now that \(G\) is a group scheme over \(S\). By
\SGARefLink{sga3:ii:theorem:4-1-4}{4.1.4}, \(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)(S)\) identifies with the
group of infinitesimal automorphisms of \(G/S\) invariant on the right,
that is, by \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:subsection:3-14}{3.14}, with the group of derivations of
\(\mathcal O_G\) over \(\mathcal O_S\) invariant under right translation.
Moreover, this identification respects the module structure, and even, up
to sign, the Lie algebra structure, the bracket in
\(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)(S)\) corresponding to the opposite
of the bracket of derivations, as one sees by reasoning as in
Proposition~\SGARefLink{sga3:ii:proposition:4-8}{4.8}: one must compute the commutator of the
automorphisms of \(G_{I_S\times I_S}\) that correspond to the automorphisms
\(u\) and \(v\) of \(\mathcal O_{G_{I_S\times I_S}}\) defined by
\[
 u=\mathrm{id}+t\,d,\qquad v=\mathrm{id}+t'd';\qquad t^2=t'^2=0.
\]
As this correspondence reverses the order of composition, one finds at
once that this commutator corresponds to
\(v^{-1}u^{-1}vu=\mathrm{id}+tt'(d'd-dd')\).\footnote{Translator's note: the
1970 text says that the identification respects the Lie algebra structure,
the bracket corresponding to the bracket of derivations, computes
\(uvu^{-1}v^{-1}=\mathrm{id}+tt'(dd'-d'd)\), and recovers
\(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\) as the Lie algebra of
right-invariant derivations without a sign. Since an automorphism of
\(G_{I_S\times I_S}\) acts on functions by composition, the correspondence
reverses composition; hence \(x\mapsto d_x\) reverses brackets and
\(x\mapsto-d_x\) is an isomorphism of Lie algebras. The Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~II, corrects the sign in this way, referring to
M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel, \emph{Groupes alg\'ebriques}, Masson/North-Holland,
1970, II, \S4, 4.4 and 4.6.}

\SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:scholium:4-11-1}%
One then recovers the classical definition: by \(x\mapsto-d_x\),
\(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\) is the functor that associates
with every \(S'\) over \(S\) the \(\mathcal O(S')\)-Lie algebra of
derivations of \(G_{S'}\) relative to \(S'\) invariant under right
translation. \SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:corollary:4-11-2}It follows in particular that every representable
group is very good.

Let us recall, on the other hand (\SGARefLink{sga3:ii:proposition:3-3}{3.3}), that
\(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\) is representable by the vector
bundle \(V(\omega^1_{G/S})\), where \(\omega^1_{G/S}\) is the inverse image
under the unit section of \(G\) of the sheaf \(\Omega^1_{G/S}\) of relative
differentials of \(G\) over \(S\).
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:remark:4-11-5}The preceding properties show that the
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(\omega^1_{G/S}\) identifies with the sheaf of
differentials of \(G\) relative to \(S\) invariant on the right, that is,
with the sheaf whose sections over an open set \(U\) of \(S\) are the
sections of \(\Omega^1_{G/S}\) over the inverse image of \(U\) that are
invariant under right translation.

\SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:remark:4-11-4}%
Let us note finally that it follows from the decomposition of the tangent
bundle as a semidirect product that the \(\mathcal O_G\)-module
\(\Omega^1_{G/S}\) is the inverse image, under the projection
\(G\to S\), of the \(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(\omega^1_{G/S}\). It follows,
for example, that \(\Omega^1_{G/S}\) is locally free (respectively locally
free of finite type) if \(\omega^1_{G/S}\) is, which is the case in
particular if \(S\) is the spectrum of a field (respectively if \(S\) is the
spectrum of a field and \(G\) is locally of finite type over \(S\)).

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~II; our
formulation). The re-edition obtains
\(\Omega^1_{G/S}\simeq\pi^*(\omega^1_{G/S})\), \(\pi\colon G\to S\), from
the \((G\times_SG)\)-equivariance of \(\Omega^1_{G/S}\) and its additions to
Expos\'e~I, 6.8, which also identify \(\omega^1_{G/S}\) with the sheaf of
right-invariant differentials (I, 6.8.3; compare VII\textsubscript{A}, 2.4).
It adds: (b) \(\omega^1_{G/S}\) carries a natural structure of
\(G\)-\(\mathcal O_S\)-module, which induces on
\(V(\omega^1_{G/S})=\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\) the adjoint representation
(it still calls the action on \(\omega^1_{G/S}\) adjoint); more generally,
given for every \(f\colon Y\to S\) an \(\mathcal O_Y\)-module
\(\mathcal N(Y)\), functorially in \(Y\) (for instance
\(\mathcal N(Y)=\mathcal J\mathcal O_Y\) for a quasi-coherent ideal
\(\mathcal J\) of \(\mathcal O_S\)), \(G\) acts on the functor
\(Y\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_Y}(f^*\omega^1_{G/S},\mathcal N(Y))\),
an action used in Expos\'e~III; (c) the unit section
\(\varepsilon\colon S\to G\) is an immersion (EGA~I, 5.3.11), closed if \(G\)
is separated over \(S\) (EGA~I, 5.4.6), so that
\(\omega^1_{G/S}=\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2\), where \(\mathcal I\) is the ideal
of \(\varepsilon(S)\) in an open subscheme of \(G\) in which it is closed;
if \(G=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A(G)\) is affine over \(S\), \(\mathcal I\)
is the augmentation ideal, the kernel of
\(\varepsilon^\natural\colon\mathcal A(G)\to\mathcal O_S\).
\par\medskip

\SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:scholium:4-11-3}%
We have thus associated functorially with every \(S\)-group scheme \(G\) a
vector bundle \(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\) over \(S\), endowed with a
structure of \(S\)-scheme in Lie algebras over the \(S\)-scheme of rings
\(\mathcal O_S\). Let us recall (\SGARefLink{sga3:ii:proposition:3-4}{3.4} and \SGARefLink{sga3:ii:proposition:3-8}{3.8}) that this
construction commutes with finite products and with base extension.

\SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:notation:4-11-6}%
Let \(\mathop{\mathscr L\!ie}(G/S)\) be the sheaf of sections of this
vector bundle (EGA~II, 1.7.9). It is endowed with a structure of
\(\mathcal O_S\)-Lie algebra. As this construction does not commute with
base extension (in general), the Lie algebra structure on this module does
not make it possible to reconstruct the structure of scheme in Lie
algebras on \(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\).\footnote{Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~II, gives the reason, that
\(\mathop{\mathscr L\!ie}(G/S)=(\omega^1_{G/S})^\vee\) need not determine
\(\omega^1_{G/S}\), and an example over the affine line. In our
formulation: over \(S=\operatorname{Spec}k[s]\), \(k\) a field, the closed
subgroup \(G=\operatorname{Spec}k[s,x]/(sx)\) of \(\mathbb G_{a,S}\) has
fibre \(\mathbb G_{a,k}\) at \(s=0\) and trivial fibres elsewhere; here
\(\omega^1_{G/S}\simeq k[s]/(s)\), so \(\mathop{\mathscr L\!ie}(G/S)=0\),
whereas the Lie algebra of the fibre at \(0\) is \(k\).}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:lemma:4-11-7}This is, however, the case when
\(\omega^1_{G/S}\) is locally free of finite type (which happens in
particular if \(G\) is smooth over \(S\), or if \(S\) is the spectrum of a
field and \(G\) is locally of finite type over \(S\)), since one then has
\[
 \operatorname{Lie}(G/S)=V(\omega^1_{G/S})
 =W\bigl(\mathop{\mathscr L\!ie}(G/S)\bigr)
 \qquad\textup{(\SGARefLink{sga3:i:corollary:4-6-5-1}{I~4.6.5, Cor.})}.\footnotemark
\]
\footnotetext{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~II, spells this out: as \(\omega^1_{G/S}\) is locally free of
finite type, \(\mathop{\mathscr L\!ie}(G/S)^\vee=(\omega^1_{G/S})^{\vee\vee}
=\omega^1_{G/S}\), so \(V(\omega^1_{G/S})=V(\mathop{\mathscr L\!ie}(G/S)^\vee)
=W(\mathop{\mathscr L\!ie}(G/S))\). For the smooth case it refers to
EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 17.2.4; the local freeness of \(\Omega^1\) for a
smooth morphism is EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 17.2.3~(i).}

\SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:corollary:4-11-8}%
Let us note finally that if \(G\to H\) is a monomorphism of functors in
groups, then \(\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(G/S)\to
\underline{\operatorname{Lie}}(H/S)\) is also a monomorphism. If \(G\) and
\(H\) are representable, then \(\omega^1_{H/S}\to\omega^1_{G/S}\) is an
epimorphism and \(\operatorname{Lie}(G/S)\to\operatorname{Lie}(H/S)\) is a
closed immersion; indeed, a vector monomorphism of vector bundles is a
closed immersion.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~II, justifies the epimorphism. In our words: let
\(f\colon\mathcal M\to\mathcal N\) be a morphism of quasi-coherent
\(\mathcal O_S\)-modules with cokernel \(\mathcal P\) such that
\(V(f)\colon V(\mathcal N)\to V(\mathcal M)\) is a monomorphism. The
inclusion \(V(\mathcal P)\to V(\mathcal N)\) and the zero morphism
\(V(\mathcal P)\to V(\mathcal N)\) have the same composite with \(V(f)\),
since \(\mathcal M\to\mathcal P\) is zero; hence they are equal, so the
surjection \(\mathcal N\to\mathcal P\) is zero and \(\mathcal P=0\).} In particular, one has the following result: let
\(G\to H\) be a monomorphism of group schemes over \(S\); if
\(\omega^1_{G/S}\) is locally free of finite type, then the corresponding
morphism \(\mathop{\mathscr L\!ie}(G/S)\to\mathop{\mathscr L\!ie}(H/S)\) is
an isomorphism of \(\mathop{\mathscr L\!ie}(G/S)\) onto a submodule of
\(\mathop{\mathscr L\!ie}(H/S)\) that is locally a direct summand.
