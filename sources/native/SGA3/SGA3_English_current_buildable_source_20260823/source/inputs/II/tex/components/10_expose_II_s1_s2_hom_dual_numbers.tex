
\chapter*{Expos\'e II\\Tangent Bundles -- Lie Algebras}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Expos\'e II. Tangent Bundles -- Lie Algebras}
\markboth{EXPOS\'E II. TANGENT BUNDLES -- LIE ALGEBRAS}%
  {EXPOS\'E II. TANGENT BUNDLES -- LIE ALGEBRAS}

\begin{center}
  \textit{by M. Demazure}
\end{center}

We propose in this expos\'e to construct, in the theory of schemes, the
analogues of the tangent bundles and Lie algebras of classical theory. It
will, however, be useful not to restrict ourselves to schemes proper, but
to consider the functors on the category of schemes that are not
necessarily representable (for example, the functors
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}\), \(\underline{\operatorname{Norm}}\),
and so forth). As announced in the preceding expos\'e, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:1-1}{I~1.1}, we shall
identify a scheme with its associated functor.

On the other hand, the constructions presented below go beyond the setting
of scheme theory. They are equally valid, for example, in the theory of
analytic spaces with nilpotent elements, apart from a few changes of detail.

Before beginning this construction, we must give a few general definitions
that supplement those of \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:1:7}{I~1.7}.

\SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:section:1}\section*{1. The functors
  \(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,Y)\)}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{1. The functors Hom Z/S (X,Y)}

We resume the notation of \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:1-1}{I~1.1}. We identify the category \(\mathscr C\)
with a full subcategory of
\[
  \widehat{\mathscr C}
  =\operatorname{Hom}(\mathscr C^{\circ},\mathbf{Set})
\]
(in particular, we suppress the underlinings that allowed us to distinguish
graphically an object of \(\widehat{\mathscr C}\) from an object of
\(\mathscr C\)).

Consider the following situation: four objects of
\(\widehat{\mathscr C}\), denoted by \(S,X,Y,Z\), the first of which is in
fact an object of \(\mathscr C\), with \(X\) and \(Y\) over \(Z\), and
\(Z\) over \(S\).

\SGAThreeNativeDiagram[0.24\textwidth]
  {native_diagrams/exp2/exp2_p002_XY_over_ZS.tex}
  {The objects \(X\) and \(Y\) over \(Z\), and \(Z\) over \(S\).}
  {fig:exp2-xy-over-zs}
  {LNM 151, p.~44}

\SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:definition:1-1}%
Let us then define an object of \(\widehat{\mathscr C}_{/S}\), denoted
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,Y)\), by
\[
  \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,Y)(S')
  =\operatorname{Hom}_{Z_{S'}}(X_{S'},Y_{S'}).
\]
One sees at once that
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,Y)\) is simply the subobject of
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(X,Y)\) formed by the morphisms compatible
with \(p_X\) and \(p_Y\); that is, the kernel of the pair of morphisms
\[
  \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(X,Y)
  \rightrightarrows
  \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(X,Z),
\]
the first defined by composition with \(p_Y\), and the second being the
constant morphism whose ``image'' is \(p_X\). It follows from the
definition that there are isomorphisms
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{Hom}_S
    \bigl(S',\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,Y)\bigr)
  &\simeq \operatorname{Hom}_Z(X\times_SS',Y)\\
  &\simeq \operatorname{Hom}_Z\bigl(Z\times_SS',
      \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_Z(X,Y)\bigr)\\
  &\simeq \operatorname{Hom}_Z\bigl(X,
      \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_Z(Z\times_SS',Y)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Let us note in passing that the last two isomorphisms extend to the case
where \(S'\) is no longer in \(\mathscr C\), by the procedure indicated in
\SGARefLink{sga3:i:proposition:1-7-1}{I~1.7.1}.\footnote{Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~II, expands this passage. In our
words: the first bijection holds as well for every object \(T\) of
\(\widehat{\mathscr C}\) over \(S\) (cf.\ \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:1:7}{I~1.7}), and all of them are
functorial in \(T\); replacing \(T\) by \(T\times_SS''\) for variable
\(S''\to S\), one obtains isomorphisms of \(S\)-functors
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(T,\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,Y))
\simeq\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_Z(Z\times_ST,Y))
\simeq\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X\times_ST,Y)\), where
\(X\times_ST\) lies over \(Z\) through \(X\); the first is the one used in
the proof of 3.11.}

Let us point out two special cases of the definition. If \(Z=S\), then
\[
  \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{S/S}(X,Y)
  =\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(X,Y).
\]

On the other hand, we set
\[
  \prod_{Z/S}Y
  =\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(Z,Y),
\]
so that, by definition,
\[
  \left(\prod_{Z/S}Y\right)(S')
  \simeq \Gamma(Y_{S'}/Z_{S'}).\footnotemark
\]
\footnotetext{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~II, adds here that \(\prod_{Z/S}\colon\widehat{\mathscr C}_{/Z}\to
\widehat{\mathscr C}_{/S}\) is right adjoint to the base change
\(U\mapsto U\times_SZ\), and that for schemes it is Weil's restriction of
scalars. In our words: taking \(X=Z\) in the bijection
\(\operatorname{Hom}_S(T,\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,Y))\simeq
\operatorname{Hom}_Z(X\times_ST,Y)\), valid for every \(S\)-functor \(T\),
gives \(\operatorname{Hom}_S(U,\prod_{Z/S}Y)\simeq
\operatorname{Hom}_Z(U\times_SZ,Y)\), functorially in \(U\) and \(Y\).}
One also has an isomorphism
\[
  \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,Y)
  \simeq
  \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{X/S}(X,Y\times_ZX)
  =\prod_{X/S}(Y\times_ZX),
\]
which gives in particular, when \(Z=S\), an isomorphism
\[
  \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(X,Y)
  \simeq \prod_{X/S}Y_X.
\]

\SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:remark:1-2}%
A last remark: the functor
\(Y\mapsto\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,Y)\) commutes with
products in the following sense: there is a functorial isomorphism
\[
  \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,Y\times_ZY')
  \simeq
  \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,Y)
  \times_S
  \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,Y').
\]
It follows that if \(Y\) is a \(Z\)-group, respectively a \(Z\)-ring,
and so on, then \(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,Y)\) is an
\(S\)-group, respectively an \(S\)-ring, and so on.

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~II; our
formulation). Let \(\pi\colon M\to Y\) be a \(Y\)-functor in
\(\mathcal O_Y\)-modules (\SGARefLink{sga3:i:paragraph:4-3-3-1}{I~4.3.3.1}), and put
\(H=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,Y)\). By the isomorphisms above,
an \(S\)-functor \(H'\) over \(H\) amounts to a \(Z\)-morphism
\(f\colon X\times_SH'\to Y\), and an \(H\)-morphism
\(H'\to\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,M)\) amounts to a
\(Y\)-morphism \(X\times_SH'\to M\), the source lying over \(Y\) through
\(f\). These \(Y\)-morphisms form a module over \(\mathcal O(X\times_SH')\):
two of them are added by means of the addition \(M\times_YM\to M\), and one
of them is multiplied by \(a\colon X\times_SH'\to\mathcal O_S\) by means of
the scalar multiplication \(\mathcal O_Y\times_YM\to M\), since
\(\mathcal O_Y=\mathcal O_S\times_SY\). This structure is functorial in
\(H'\); restricting scalars along
\(\mathcal O(H')\to\mathcal O(X\times_SH')\), it makes
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,M)\) an \(\mathcal O_H\)-module over
\(H\). Likewise, if \(M\) is only a \(Y\)-group, its multiplication makes
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{Z/S}(X,M)\) a group over \(H\).

\section*{\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:section:2}2. The schemes \(I_S(\mathcal M)\)}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{2. The schemes I S (M)}

\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:definition:2-1}\subsection*{\SGARefLink{sga3:ii:definition:2-1}{Definition 2.1}.}
Let \(S\) be a scheme and \(\mathcal M\) a quasi-coherent
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module. Denote by
\(\mathscr D_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal M)\) the quasi-coherent
\(\mathcal O_S\)-algebra \(\mathcal O_S\oplus\mathcal M\), where
\(\mathcal M\) is regarded as an ideal of square zero. Denote by
\[
  I_S(\mathcal M)=\operatorname{Spec}
    \mathscr D_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal M)
\]
the corresponding \(S\)-scheme.
In particular, put
\[
  \mathscr D_{\mathcal O_S}
  =\mathscr D_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal O_S),
  \qquad I_S=I_S(\mathcal O_S),
\]
and call these, respectively, the algebra of dual numbers over \(S\) and
the scheme of dual numbers over \(S\).

The assignment \(\mathcal M\mapsto I_S(\mathcal M)\) is a contravariant
functor from quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules to \(S\)-schemes.
In particular, the morphisms \(0\to\mathcal M\) and
\(\mathcal M\to0\) define, respectively, the structural morphism
\[
  \rho:I_S(\mathcal M)\longrightarrow I_S(0)=S
\]
and a section \(\varepsilon_{\mathcal M}\) of it, called the
\emph{zero section}.

\SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:subsection:2-1-1}%
On the other hand, \(\mathcal O(S)=\Gamma(S,\mathcal O_S)\) is a set of
operators on the module \(\mathcal M\), hence on the \(S\)-scheme
\(I_S(\mathcal M)\), and the operations of \(\mathcal O(S)\) commute with
the \(S\)-morphisms \(I_S(\mathcal M)\to I_S(\mathcal M')\) arising, by the
preceding functoriality, from morphisms \(\mathcal M'\to\mathcal M\); in
particular, these operations preserve the zero section of
\(I_S(\mathcal M)\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~II, lets the whole multiplicative monoid
\(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal M)\) act. In our words: by
contravariance each \(a\) gives an \(S\)-endomorphism \(a^*\) of
\(I_S(\mathcal M)\), with \(1^*=\operatorname{id}\), \((ab)^*=b^*\circ a^*\),
\(0^*=\varepsilon_{\mathcal M}\circ\rho\) and
\(a^*\circ\varepsilon_{\mathcal M}=\varepsilon_{\mathcal M}\), so that
\(m\cdot a=a^*(m)\) is a right action on points; the homotheties give back
the operators above. For instance, if \(\mathcal M=\mathcal O_S^r\), an
\(S'\)-point of \(I_S(\mathcal M)\) is an \(r\)-tuple
\((\epsilon_1,\dots,\epsilon_r)\) of elements of \(\mathcal O(S')\) with
\(\epsilon_i\epsilon_j=0\) for all \(i,j\), on which \(a\in\mathcal O(S)\)
acts by \((a\epsilon_1,\dots,a\epsilon_r)\). The re-edition also notes that
\(I_S(\mathcal M)\) has the same underlying space as \(S\), \(\mathcal M\)
being a nil ideal.}

\SGARefTarget{sga3:ii:remark:2-1-2}%
Formation of \(I_S(\mathcal M)\) commutes with base extension: there are
canonical isomorphisms
\[
  \bigl(I_S(\mathcal M)\bigr)_{S'}
  \simeq I_{S'}(\mathcal M\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal O_{S'}).\footnotemark
\]
\footnotetext{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~II, adds a paragraph here, used in its additions to 3.4 and 4.6. In
our words: for an \(S\)-functor \(X\), write
\(I_X(\mathcal M)=I_S(\mathcal M)\times_SX\). Since \(\mathcal O(S')\)
operates on \((I_S(\mathcal M))_{S'}\), functorially in \(S'\) and
\(\mathcal M\), every \(a\in\mathcal O(X)=\operatorname{Hom}_S(X,\mathcal O_S)\)
defines an \(X\)-endomorphism \(a^*\) of \(I_X(\mathcal M)\), given on
points by \((m,x)\mapsto(m\cdot a(x),x)\), where \(a(x)=a\circ x\). This is
an action of the multiplicative monoid \(\mathcal O(X)\), functorial in
\(\mathcal M\), which preserves the zero section \(X\to I_X(\mathcal M)\);
and a morphism \(\pi\colon Y\to X\) carries \(a^*\) to \((a\circ\pi)^*\).}

\subsection*{Proposition 2.2.}
For every \(S\)-scheme \(X\), the diagram of functors over \(S\) obtained
by applying \(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(-,X)\) to the preceding
diagram is cartesian.

\SGAThreeNativeDiagram[0.58\textwidth]
  {native_diagrams/exp2/exp2_p006_Hom_cartesian_diagram.tex}
  {The cartesian diagram of \(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S\)-functors.}
  {fig:exp2-hom-cartesian}
  {LNM 151, p.~47}

Indeed, for every \(S'\to S\), one must verify that the diagram of sets
obtained by evaluating the functors on \(S'\) is cartesian. Since formation
of \(I_S(\mathcal P)\) commutes with base extension, it is enough to take
\(S'=S\), and hence to verify that the following diagram of sets is
cartesian.

\SGAThreeNativeDiagram[0.58\textwidth]
  {native_diagrams/exp2/exp2_p006_points_cartesian_diagram.tex}
  {The cartesian diagram after evaluation on \(S\).}
  {fig:exp2-points-cartesian}
  {LNM 151, p.~47}

If \(x\in X(S)\), it follows from SGA~1, III~5.1 that
\(X(\varepsilon_{\mathcal M})^{-1}(x)\) is isomorphic, functorially in
\(\mathcal M\), to
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}
    \bigl(x^*(\Omega^1_{X/S}),\mathcal M\bigr),
\]
where \(\Omega^1_{X/S}\) denotes the sheaf of relative differentials of
\(X\) over \(S\). This latter functor in \(\mathcal M\) plainly carries a
direct sum of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules to the product of the corresponding
sets, which proves the proposition.

\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:corollary:2-2-1}\subsection*{Corollary.}
If \(\mathcal M\) is free of finite type, the functor
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(I_S(\mathcal M),X)\) is isomorphic
\textup{(}as a functor over \(X\)\textup{)} to a finite product
\textup{(}over \(X\)\textup{)} of copies of
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(I_S,X)\).

\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:ii:nota:2-2-2}\subsection*{Nota.}
It follows from the proof of the proposition that
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(I_S,X)\), as an \(X\)-functor, is
isomorphic to \(\mathbf V(\Omega^1_{X/S})\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:i:definition:4-6-1}{I~4.6.1}), and is therefore
represented by the vector bundle
\[
  V(\Omega^1_{X/S}).
\]
