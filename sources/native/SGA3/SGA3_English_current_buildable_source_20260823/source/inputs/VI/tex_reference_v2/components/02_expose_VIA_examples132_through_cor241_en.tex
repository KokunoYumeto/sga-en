\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}; our
formulation). The re-edition gives here two examples, due to M.~Raynaud, of a group scheme \(G\)
over a non-perfect field \(k\) for which \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is not a subgroup scheme of \(G\)
(compare 0.2). In both, \(k\) has characteristic \(p>0\), \(t\in k-k^p\), \(\overline k\) is an
algebraic closure of \(k\), and \(\alpha\in\overline k\) satisfies \(\alpha^p=t\). We use the fact,
seen in the proof of 1.3.1, that the local rings of a group scheme locally of finite type over
\(\overline k\) at its closed points are all isomorphic; if \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) were a subgroup
scheme of \(G\), this would apply to \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\otimes_k\overline k\).

(a) The additive polynomial \(X^{p^2}-tX^p\) defines a finite subgroup scheme \(G\) of
\(\mathbb G_{a,k}=\operatorname{Spec}k[X]\). As \(X^{p^2}-tX^p=X^p(X^{p(p-1)}-t)\) and the second
factor does not vanish at \(0\), the local ring of \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) at the origin is \(k\). If
\(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) were a subgroup scheme of \(G\), it would thus be smooth over \(k\) at the
origin, hence smooth (1.3.1), and \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\otimes_k\overline k\) would be the reduced
subscheme of \(G\otimes_k\overline k\). But \(X^{p^2}-tX^p=(X^p-\alpha X)^p\) in \(\overline k[X]\),
and \(X^p-\alpha X\) has simple roots, so this reduced subscheme is defined by the ideal
\((X^p-\alpha X)\), which is not generated by polynomials with coefficients in \(k\), because
\(\alpha\notin k\). Hence \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is not a subgroup scheme of \(G\).

(b) In \(\mathbb G^4_{a,k}=\operatorname{Spec}k[X,Y,U,V]\), let \(G\) be the subgroup scheme defined
by the ideal \(I\) generated by the additive polynomials \(P=X^p-tY^p\) and \(Q=U^p-tV^p\). Over
\(\overline k\), \(P=(X-\alpha Y)^p\) and \(Q=(U-\alpha V)^p\), so the underlying space of
\(G\otimes_k\overline k\) is the plane \(\Pi\) defined by \(X=\alpha Y\) and \(U=\alpha V\); in
particular \(G\) is irreducible of dimension~\(2\). Let \(h\in\sqrt I\), with linear part
\(aX+bY+cU+dV\) (\(a,b,c,d\in k\)). Since \(h\) vanishes on \(\Pi\), \(h(\alpha Y,Y,\alpha V,V)=0\),
whence \(a\alpha+b=c\alpha+d=0\), and \(a=b=c=d=0\) because \(\alpha\notin k\). Thus \(\sqrt I\),
the ideal of \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\), is contained in \(\mathfrak m^2\), where \(\mathfrak m=(X,Y,U,V)\),
and the tangent space of \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\otimes_k\overline k\) at the origin has dimension~\(4\).
On the other hand, \(R=XV-YU\) satisfies \(R^p=PV^p-QY^p\), so \(R\in\sqrt I\); at the point
\(z=(\alpha,1,\alpha,1)\) of \(\Pi\), its differential \(dX-\alpha\,dY-dU+\alpha\,dV\) is not zero, so
the tangent space of \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\otimes_k\overline k\) at \(z\) has dimension at most \(3\).
Hence \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is not a subgroup scheme of \(G\). (The re-edition also notes that, near
every point other than the origin, \(\sqrt I\) is generated by \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\).)

\section*{2. Connected components of an \(A\)-group locally of finite type}
\phantomsection\label{sec:sga3-VIA-2}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{2. Connected components of an
  \texorpdfstring{\(A\)}{A}-group locally of finite type}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-VIA-2.1}%
\noindent\textbf{2.1.}
Let us first consider an arbitrary \(A\)-group \(G\), and let \(G'\) be
the connected component of the origin \(e\) in \(G\). This connected
component is plainly closed, so we may identify it with the reduced closed
subscheme of \(G\) having \(G'\) as its underlying space.%

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIA-2.1.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 2.1.1.}
\textit{For every extension \(K\) of the residue field \(k\) of \(A\),
the underlying space of \(G'\otimes_AK\) is the connected component of the
origin in the \(K\)-group \(G\otimes_AK\) \textup{(}\(G'\) is
geometrically connected\textup{)}.}

\medskip
Indeed, let \((G\otimes_AK)'\) be the connected component of the origin in
\(G\otimes_AK\). Since the image of \((G\otimes_AK)'\) in \(G\) is
connected and contains the unit element of \(G\), that image is contained
in \(G'\). Thus \((G\otimes_AK)'\) is contained in the inverse image
\(G'\otimes_AK\) of \(G'\) in \(G\otimes_AK\). The proposition therefore
follows from the connectedness of \(G'\otimes_AK\), which is proved in the
following lemma.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-VIA-2.1.2}%
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 2.1.2.}
\textit{Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be two connected schemes over a field \(k\).
If \(X\) contains a rational point, then \(X\times_kY\) is connected.}

\medskip
We give a direct proof below of this result from EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}
(4.5.8 and 4.5.14).

Suppose first that \(Y\) is nonempty, connected, and affine, with algebra
\(B\). Then \(X\times_kY\) is the spectrum of the quasi-coherent
\(\mathcal O_X\)-algebra
\[
  \mathcal B=\mathcal O_X\otimes_kB.
\]
We wish to show that every nonempty subset \(U\) of \(X\times_kY\) that is
both open and closed equals \(X\times_kY\). And indeed, \(U\) is affine over \(X\),
and its affine \(\mathcal O_X\)-algebra is a direct factor of
\(\mathcal B\). It follows from \hyperref[lem:sga3-VIA-2.1.3]{Lemma~2.1.3} below that the image of \(U\)
in \(X\) is open and closed, and hence is all of \(X\). In particular,
this image contains a rational point \(x\) of \(X\), so \(U\) meets the
inverse image of \(x\) in \(X\times_kY\). Since this inverse image is
isomorphic to \(Y\), and hence connected, \(U\) contains the whole inverse
image. The same conclusion would hold for the complement of \(U\) in
\(X\times_kY\) if \(U\) were different from \(X\times_kY\), which is
absurd.

If \(Y\) is now an arbitrary \(k\)-scheme, the preceding argument shows
that the fibers of the canonical projection
\[
  X\times_kY\longrightarrow Y
\]
are connected. If \(x\) is a rational point of \(X\), all these fibers
meet the subscheme \(\{x\}\times_kY\), which is itself connected. This
proves the assertion.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-VIA-2.1.3}%
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 2.1.3.}
\textit{Let \(X\) be a scheme, and let \(\mathcal A\) be a
quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_X\)-algebra that is
a direct factor of a free \(\mathcal O_X\)-module. Then the image of
\(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A\) in \(X\) is open and closed.}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, writes ``locally a direct factor''. The statement as printed is the one
needed in 2.1.2, where the algebra is a direct factor of the free \(\mathcal O_X\)-module
\(\mathcal O_X\otimes_kB\); as its conclusion is local on \(X\), it also holds when \(\mathcal A\) is
only locally a direct factor of a free module. The theorem of Kaplansky used in the proof (a projective
module over a local ring is free) is in I.~Kaplansky, \emph{Projective modules}, Ann.\ of Math.\
\textbf{68} (1958), 372--377.}

\medskip
Let \(V\) be this image. It is clear that \(V\) is contained in the
support of \(\mathcal A\). Conversely, if \(x\) belongs to the support of
\(\mathcal A\), then \(\mathcal A_x\) is nonzero and is a free
\(\mathcal O_{X,x}\)-module (Kaplansky). Consequently, the fiber of
\(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A\) at \(x\), which is affine with algebra
\[
  \mathcal A_x\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}\kappa(x),
\]
is nonempty. Thus the image of \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A\)
coincides with the support of \(\mathcal A\).

If \(s\) is the unit section of \(\mathcal A\), the equality \(s_x=0\)
implies that \(s\) is zero in a neighborhood of the point \(x\), so that
the support of \(\mathcal A\) is closed. We may suppose, on the other
hand, that \(\mathcal A\) is a direct factor of the direct sum of a family
\((\mathcal O_X^\alpha)\) of copies of \(\mathcal O_X\). Let then
\(\varphi^\alpha\) be the restriction to \(\mathcal A\) of the canonical
projection of \(\bigoplus_\alpha\mathcal O_X^\alpha\) onto
\(\mathcal O_X^\alpha\); if \(x\) is a point of \(X\), every homomorphism
of \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\)-modules
\(\psi_x:\mathcal A_x\to\mathcal O_{X,x}\) is of the form
\[
  a\longmapsto\sum_\alpha\xi^\alpha\varphi^\alpha(a),
\]
where \((\xi^\alpha)\) is a family of elements of \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\).
Since \(\mathcal A_x\) is free and nonzero, we may choose the
\(\xi^\alpha\) and \(a\) in such a way that
\[
  1=\sum_\alpha\xi^\alpha\varphi^\alpha(a)
   =\xi^{\alpha_1}\varphi^{\alpha_1}(a)
    +\cdots+
    \xi^{\alpha_n}\varphi^{\alpha_n}(a).
\]
The equality
\(1=\xi^{\alpha_1}\varphi^{\alpha_1}(a)+\cdots+\xi^{\alpha_n}\varphi^{\alpha_n}(a)\)
then remains valid in a neighborhood of \(x\), which shows that \(a\),
and hence \(\mathcal A\), are nonzero in a neighborhood of \(x\)
(``the support of a projective module is open'').

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-VIA-2.2}%
\noindent\textbf{2.2.}
The notation remains that of \hyperref[par:sga3-VIA-2.1]{2.1}. It is clear that \(G'\) is a reduced
scheme over \(\operatorname{Spec}k\subset\operatorname{Spec}A\). \hyperref[lem:sga3-VIA-2.1.2]{Lemma~2.1.2} shows that \(G'\times_kG'\) is connected, so
\((G'\times_kG')_{\mathrm{red}}\) is the reduced subscheme of
\(G\times_AG\) whose underlying space is the connected component of the
origin. In particular, the multiplication morphism
\[
  \mu:G\times_AG\longrightarrow G
\]
induces a morphism
\[
  \mu':(G'\times_kG')_{\mathrm{red}}\longrightarrow G'
\]
that makes \(G'\) a group in the full subcategory of
\(\mathbf{Sch}_{/k}\) consisting of the reduced \(k\)-schemes.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, adds here, with a view to Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, \S~3, a subgroup
functor attached to an arbitrary \(A\)-group \(G\): for an \(A\)-scheme \(S\), let \(G^0(S)\) be the
set of \(u\in G(S)\) mapping the underlying space of \(S\) into \(G'\). It is stable under inversion,
since inversion maps \(G'\) onto itself, and under products: the points of \(G\times_AG\) whose two
projections lie in \(G'\) form the underlying space of \(G'\times_kG'\), which is connected by 2.1.2
and contains the origin, so the multiplication maps it into \(G'\). When \(G'\) is open in \(G\),
this functor is represented by the open subscheme of \(G\) with underlying space \(G'\), which is
then a subgroup scheme \(G^0\) of \(G\), and \(G'=(G^0)_{\mathrm{red}}\).}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-VIA-2.3}%
\noindent\textbf{2.3.}
In accordance with our conventions of \SGARefLink{par:sga3-VIA-1.1}{1.1}, we again assume from now on that
\(G\) is locally of finite type over \(A\). Then \(G\) is locally
Noetherian, hence locally connected, so that the connected component of
the unit element is open in \(G\). We shall denote by \(G^0\) the
subscheme induced by \(G\) on this open subset, so that, with the
notation of \hyperref[par:sga3-VIA-2.1]{2.1}, \(G'=(G^0)_{\mathrm{red}}\). It follows evidently from
\hyperref[par:sga3-VIA-2.2]{2.2} that \(G^0\) is a group subscheme of \(G\), which we shall call the
\emph{neutral component} of \(G\).

Let \(G^\alpha\) be an arbitrary connected component of \(G\), and let
\[
  \nu^\alpha:G^\alpha\times_AG^0\longrightarrow G
\]
be the morphism defined by
\[
  \nu^\alpha(S)(g,\gamma)=g\gamma g^{-1}
\]
for every \(S\in(\mathbf{Sch}_{/A})\), \(g\in G^\alpha(S)\), and
\(\gamma\in G^0(S)\). If \(e\) is the origin of \(G\), the restriction of
\(\nu^\alpha\) to \(G^\alpha\times_A\{e\}\) is the trivial morphism;
since \(G^\alpha\times_AG^0\) is connected by \hyperref[lem:sga3-VIA-2.1.2]{2.1.2}, we see that
\(\nu^\alpha\) factors through \(G^0\),
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIA-2.3.1}%
so that \(G^0\) is a normal \(A\)-subgroup of \(G\) (for the preceding
implies that, for every scheme \(S\) over \(A\), \(G^0(S)\) is a normal
subgroup of \(G(S)\)).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, states this conclusion as Proposition~2.3.1 (\(G^0\) is an open normal
subgroup scheme of \(G\)), the number used in references to it in other Exposés; it notes that \(G^0\)
is even a characteristic subgroup (see the second Addition after 2.4.1, (e)).}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIA-2.4}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 2.4.}
\textit{The neutral component of an \(A\)-group \(G\) locally of finite
type is geometrically irreducible and is of finite type over \(A\).}

\medskip
Let \(\overline k\) be the algebraic closure of the residue field \(k\)
of \(A\), and let us first show that \(G^0\) is geometrically
irreducible, that is, that \(G^0\otimes_A\overline k\) is irreducible.
By \hyperref[par:sga3-VIA-2.2]{2.2}, \((G\otimes_A\overline k)_{\mathrm{red}}\) is a
\(\overline k\)-group locally of finite type and reduced, hence smooth
over \(\overline k\) (\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-1.3.1}{1.3.1}). A fortiori, the local rings of
\((G\otimes_A\overline k)_{\mathrm{red}}\) are integral domains, so that
the connected components of \(G\otimes_A\overline k\), in particular
\(G^0\otimes_A\overline k\), are irreducible.\footnote{Translator's note: a locally noetherian scheme whose local rings
are integral domains has irreducible connected components (EGA~I, 6.1.10); the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, adds this reference.}

Let us now show that \(G^0\) is of finite type over \(A\). Since \(G^0\)
is locally of finite type over \(A\), it suffices to prove that \(G^0\) is
quasi-compact. For this, let \(V\) be an affine open subset of \(G^0\).
Since \(G^0\) is irreducible, this open subset is dense in \(G^0\), so
that \(V\cdot V\) coincides with \(G^0\) (\hyperref[sga3:VIA:0.5]{0.5}); since \(V\cdot V\) is the
image of \(V\times_AV\) under the multiplication morphism, \(V\cdot V\)
is quasi-compact.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{cor:sga3-VIA-2.4.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Corollary 2.4.1.}
\textit{Every connected component of \(G\) is irreducible and of finite
type over \(A\).}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, adds that every connected component \(H\) has the same dimension as
\(G^0\) (compare Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, \SGARefLink{lem:sga3-VIB-1.5}{1.5}): in the notation of
the proof, \(H\otimes_kK\) is a finite disjoint union of translates of \(G^0\otimes_kK\), and
dimension does not change under extension of the base field (EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 4.1.4). It also
warns that a connected component other than \(G^0\) need not be geometrically connected: in
\(\mu_{3,\mathbb R}=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb R[X]/(X^3-1)\), the component
\(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb R[X]/(X^2+X+1)\simeq\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C\) splits into two
points over \(\mathbb C\).}

\medskip
Indeed, we may suppose that \(A\) is equal to its residue field \(k\).
Let then \(H\) be a connected component of \(G\), \(x\) a closed point of
\(H\), \(\kappa(x)\) the residue field of \(x\), and \(K\) the normal
extension of \(k\) generated by \(\kappa(x)\). The canonical projection
\(\pi:H\otimes_kK\to H\) is open and closed, so that every connected
component of \(H\otimes_kK\) is the sum of the images of
\(G^0\otimes_kK\) under the translations defined by the points of
\(\pi^{-1}(x)\). Since \(H\otimes_kK\) is of finite type, so is \(H\).

If \(H\) were not irreducible, we could choose \(x\) in such a way that
\(x\) belongs to two distinct irreducible components. The local ring
\(\mathcal O_{H,x}\) would then have two distinct minimal prime ideals,
as would the local ring of every point of \(H\otimes_kK\) above \(x\)
(``going-down theorem'' of Cohen--Seidenberg, Bourbaki,
\emph{Alg.\ comm.}, chap.~VI); but we have just seen that
\(H\otimes_kK\) is the direct sum of a certain number of copies of
\(G^0\otimes_kK\), which is irreducible\ldots
