
\section*{1. Local properties of an \(A\)-group locally of finite type}
\phantomsection\label{sec:sga3-VIA-1}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{1. Local properties of an
  \texorpdfstring{\(A\)}{A}-group locally of finite type}

We shall first see that, if \(G\) is locally of finite type and flat over
\(A\), one can ``make every closed point of \(G\) strictly rational'' by
means of a finite flat extension of the base.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-VIA-1.1}%
\noindent\textbf{1.1.}
Unless expressly stated otherwise, from now on we assume that \(G\) is an
\(A\)-group locally of finite type. When \(A\) is a field, we shall
obtain in Expos\'e~VII\textsubscript{B} very precise results on the local
rings of \(G\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, mentions two of them here: when \(A\) is a field \(k\), the local rings of
\(G\) are complete intersections (cf.\ Exposé~VII\textsubscript{B},
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:5.5.1}{5.5.1}), and \(G\) is smooth over \(k\) if \(k\) has characteristic
zero (Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, \SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.6.1}{1.6.1}; see also Exposé~VII\textsubscript{B},
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIB:3.3.1}{3.3.1}).}
Here we content ourselves with a few elementary results.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIA-1.1.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1.1.1.}
\textit{Let \(x\) be a point of an \(A\)-group \(G\) locally of finite type
and flat over \(A\). Then the local ring \(\mathcal O_{G,x}\) is
Cohen--Macaulay, and there is a system of parameters
\(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) of \(\mathcal O_{G,x}\) such that
\(\mathcal O_{G,x}/(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) is a flat \(A\)-module.}

\medskip
We first suppose that \(A\) is equal to its residue field \(k\). It then
suffices to prove that \(\mathcal O_{G,x}\) is Cohen--Macaulay, and we may
restrict to the case in which \(x\) is a closed point
(EGA~\(0_{\mathrm{IV}}\), 16.5.13). By
\hyperref[lem:sga3-VIA-1.1.2]{Lemma~1.1.2} below, \(G\) contains a closed
point \(y\) such that \(\mathcal O_{G,y}\) is Cohen--Macaulay. By
SGA~1, I, \S~9, this is equivalent to saying that, for every finite
extension \(K\) of the base field \(k\) and every point \(\overline y\) of
\(\overline G=G\otimes_k K\) lying above \(y\), the local ring
\(\mathcal O_{\overline G,\overline y}\) is Cohen--Macaulay. If \(K\) has
been chosen sufficiently large (that is, if \(K\) contains the normal
extension of \(k\) generated by the residue fields \(\kappa(y)\) and
\(\kappa(x)\)), \(\overline y\) is (strictly) rational over \(K\), as
is every point \(\overline x\) of \(\overline G\) lying above \(x\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, explains why. If \(z\) is \(x\) or \(y\), the residue field of a point of
\(\overline G\) over \(z\) is generated over \(K\) by the image of a \(k\)-embedding of \(\kappa(z)\)
into an extension of \(K\). Such an embedding takes its values in \(K\): each element of \(\kappa(z)\)
has a minimal polynomial over \(k\) with a root in the normal extension of \(k\) contained in \(K\),
so that this polynomial splits there. Hence that residue field is \(K\).}
Since the automorphism
\[
  r_{\overline x}\circ(r_{\overline y})^{-1}
\]
sends \(\overline y\) to \(\overline x\), it follows that
\(\mathcal O_{\overline G,\overline x}\), and hence
\(\mathcal O_{G,x}\) (SGA~1, I, \S~9), are Cohen--Macaulay.

Now let \(A\) again be arbitrary. The preceding argument applies to
\(k\otimes_A G\), so that \(k\otimes_A\mathcal O_{G,x}\) is
Cohen--Macaulay. If \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) is a sequence of elements of
\(\mathcal O_{G,x}\) whose image in
\(k\otimes_A\mathcal O_{G,x}\) is a system of parameters, it follows from
SGA~1, IV, 5.7, or EGA~\(0_{\mathrm{IV}}\), 15.1.16, that
\(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) is an \(\mathcal O_{G,x}\)-regular sequence and that
\(\mathcal O_{G,x}/(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) is finite and flat over \(A\).

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-VIA-1.1.2}%
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 1.1.2.}
\textit{Every nonempty scheme \(X\), locally of finite type over an
Artinian ring \(A\), contains a closed point \(x\) whose local ring is
Cohen--Macaulay.}

\medskip
We may plainly suppose that \(X\) is affine, with algebra \(B\), and argue
by induction on \(\dim X\) (the assertion is clear if \(X\) is discrete,
since all its local rings are then Artinian). Since \(B\) is of finite type
over \(A\), if \(\dim B>0\), then \(B\) contains an element \(a\) that is
not invertible and is not a zero divisor.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, justifies this; here is an argument. The zero divisors of the noetherian
ring \(B\) form the union of its finitely many associated primes. If every non-invertible element of
\(B\) were a zero divisor, each maximal ideal of \(B\) would be contained in that union, hence (prime
avoidance) would be an associated prime, and \(B\) would have only finitely many maximal ideals. Now
\(\operatorname{Spec}B=\operatorname{Spec}(B/\mathfrak m_AB)\), and \(B/\mathfrak m_AB\) is a
\(k\)-algebra of finite type, so by the Nullstellensatz every prime ideal of \(B\) is the intersection
of the maximal ideals containing it; with finitely many maximal ideals, every prime ideal would be
maximal, and \(\dim B=0\).}
The closed subscheme
\[
  X'=\operatorname{Spec} B/(a)
\]
of \(X\) then has dimension strictly less than \(\dim X\), and by the
induction hypothesis it contains a closed point \(x\) such that
\(\mathcal O_{X',x}\) is Cohen--Macaulay. Since
\(\mathcal O_{X',x}=\mathcal O_{X,x}/(a)\), and \(a\) is neither invertible
nor a zero divisor in \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\), the ring
\(\mathcal O_{X,x}\) is Cohen--Macaulay (see also EGA~IV\textsubscript{2},
6.11.3).

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIA-1.2}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1.2.}
\textit{Let \(A\) be an Artinian ring, let \(G\) be an \(A\)-group
locally of finite type and flat over \(A\), and let \(x\) be a closed point
of \(G\). Then there exists a local \(A\)-algebra \(A'\), finite and free
over \(A\), such that every point of \(G\otimes_A A'\) lying above \(x\)
is strictly rational over \(A'\).}

\medskip
We know, indeed, that there exists a local \(A\)-algebra \(A_1\), finite
and free over \(A\), whose residue field contains the normal extension of
\(k\) generated by the residue field \(\kappa(x)\) of \(x\).\footnote{Translator's note: see EGA~\(0_{\mathrm{III}}\), 10.3.1. For a simple
extension \(k(\theta)\) of \(k\), one may take \(A[T]/(F)\), where \(F\) is a monic polynomial lifting
the minimal polynomial of \(\theta\): it is free of finite rank over \(A\) and local with residue
field \(k(\theta)\); in general one repeats this. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, refers here to its Lemma~V~4.1.2, which this translation gives in the
Addition at the end of Exposé~V, 4~e).} In this case, all the
points \(g_1,\ldots,g_n\) of \(G\otimes_A A_1\) lying above \(x\in G\)
have the same residue field as \(A_1\) (that is, \(g_1,\ldots,g_n\) are rational
over \(A_1\) in the sense of Expos\'e~V, \S~4, e)).
Let then \(B_1,\ldots,B_n\) be the local rings of \(g_1,\ldots,g_n\).
By \hyperref[prop:sga3-VIA-1.1.1]{1.1.1}, the rings
\(B_1,\ldots,B_n\) have quotients \(B'_1,\ldots,B'_n\) that are Artinian
and flat over \(A_1\). If we put
\[
  A'=B'_1\otimes_{A_1}\cdots\otimes_{A_1}B'_n,
\]
the algebras \(B_i\otimes_{A_1}A'\) are all local and have a quotient
isomorphic to \(A'\), so that \(A'\) answers the question.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-VIA-1.3}%
\noindent\textbf{1.3.}
Let \(e\) be the unit element (or origin) of \(G\), that is, the image of
the unique point of \(\operatorname{Spec}A\) under the unit section
\(\operatorname{Spec}A\to G\). By definition, \(e\) is strictly rational
over \(A\).

\par\medskip
\noindent\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIA-1.3.1}%
\textbf{Proposition 1.3.1.}
\textit{Let \(G\) be a group locally of finite type and flat over an
Artinian ring \(A\), and let \(K\) be the perfect closure of the residue
field \(k\) of \(A\). The following assertions are equivalent:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \textit{\(G\otimes_A K\) is reduced.}
\item[(i bis)] \textit{If \(\mathcal O_{G,e}\) is the local ring of the
origin of \(G\), \(\mathcal O_{G,e}\otimes_A K\) is reduced.}
\item[(ii)] \textit{\(G\) is smooth over \(A\).}
\item[(ii bis)] \textit{\(G\) is smooth over \(A\) at the origin.}
\end{enumerate}

By means of SGA~1, II.2.1, we reduce at once to the case in which
\(A\) is a field (\(A=k\)). The implications
\[
  \text{(i)}\Rightarrow\text{(i bis)},\qquad
  \text{(ii)}\Rightarrow\text{(ii bis)},\qquad
  \text{(ii)}\Rightarrow\text{(i)},\qquad
  \text{(ii bis)}\Rightarrow\text{(i bis)}
\]
are evident, so it suffices to prove that (i bis) implies (ii).
Now, if \(\overline k\) denotes the algebraic closure of \(k\), it
follows from (i bis) that \(\mathcal O_{G,e}\otimes_k\overline k\) is
reduced. Moreover, if \(x\) is an arbitrary closed point of
\(\overline G=G\otimes_k\overline k\), there is exactly one
\(\overline k\)-morphism
\[
  s:\operatorname{Spec}\overline k\longrightarrow G\otimes_k\overline k
\]
whose image is \(x\); right translation \(r_s\) then induces an
isomorphism of \(\mathcal O_{G,e}\otimes_k\overline k\) onto
\(\mathcal O_{\overline G,x}\), so that \(\mathcal O_{\overline G,x}\),
and hence \(\overline G\), are reduced. But, since \(\overline G\) is also
locally of finite type over \(\overline k\), \(\overline G\) contains at
least one point \(y\) closed in \(\overline G\) and smooth over
\(\overline k\). Since, on the other hand, the local rings of the closed
points of \(\overline G\) are all isomorphic to
\(\mathcal O_{G,e}\otimes_k\overline k\), we see that all these local
rings are regular, so that \(\overline G\) is smooth over
\(\overline k\), hence \(G\) smooth over \(k\).
