\phantomsection\label{ctr:sga3-VIB-1.2.2}%
\noindent\textbf{Counter\SGARefLink{ctr:sga3-VIB-1.2.2}{example 1.2.2}.---}
\emph{Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic zero, let \(G\) be
the constant \(k\)-group \(\mathbb Z\), and let \(H\) be the
additive \(k\)-group \(\mathbb G_{a,k}\). Let \(u:G\to H\) be a
morphism of \(k\)-groups. If \(u\ne0\), then \(u(G)\) is not closed
in \(H\).}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-1.3}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1.3.---}
\emph{Let \(k\) be a field, let \(G\) be a \(k\)-group locally of finite
type, let \(u:G\to H\) be a morphism of \(k\)-groups, and let \(x\) be a
point of \(G\). The following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-1.3-i} \(u\) is quasi-finite (respectively, unramified,
  flat, smooth, \'etale) at the point \(x\);
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{prop:sga3-VIB-1.3:item:ii}(ii)}] \(u\) is locally quasi-finite (respectively,
  unramified, flat, smooth, \'etale).
\end{enumerate}}

It is enough to prove that \textup{\SGARefLink{concl:sga3-VIB-1.3-i}{(i)}} implies \textup{\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-1.3:item:ii}{(ii)}}. By
EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.7.1, and EGA~IV\(_4\), 17.7.1, we may suppose that \(k\)
is algebraically closed and that \(x\in G(k)\). Let \(U\) then be the
nonempty open subset of \(G\) consisting of the points at which \(u\) is
quasi-finite (respectively, unramified, flat, smooth, \'etale).\footnote{Translator's note: for the
properties other than flatness this set is open: by definition for unramified, smooth and \'etale,
which at a point mean the property on a neighborhood of the point, and by EGA~IV\(_3\), 13.1.4, for
quasi-finite. For flatness, openness is not clear a priori: \(H\) being arbitrary, \(u\) need not be
locally of finite presentation (cf.\ EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.3.1). But the set of points at which \(u\) is
flat is stable under generization, and the argument needs only this, since in the Jacobson scheme
\(G\) every point is a generization of a closed point. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, makes this correction.} It is
enough to show that every closed point \(y\) of \(G\) belongs to \(U\),
since \(G\) is a Jacobson scheme (EGA~IV\(_3\), 10.4.6). There is then a
translation sending \(x\) to \(y\), which shows that \(u\) is
quasi-finite (respectively, unramified, flat, smooth, \'etale) at \(y\).
