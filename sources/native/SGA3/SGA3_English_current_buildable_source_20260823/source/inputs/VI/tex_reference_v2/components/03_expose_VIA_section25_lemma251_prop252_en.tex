\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}; our
formulation). The re-edition inserts here a paragraph 2.5 (Lemmas 2.5.1 and 2.5.3, Propositions
2.5.2 and 2.5.4), with results that also appear in Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B} and that it uses to
complete Theorem~3.2. In (a)--(d), \(A\) is an Artinian local ring with maximal ideal
\(\mathfrak m\) and residue field \(k\), and \(\overline k\) is an algebraic closure of \(k\). We use
the following remark: \emph{if \(N\) is an \(A\)-submodule of an \(A\)-module \(M\) and
\(M=N+\mathfrak mM\), then \(M=N\)}, since \(M=N+\mathfrak m^rM\) for every \(r\) and \(\mathfrak m\)
is nilpotent.

\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-VIA-2.5.1}%
(a) (Lemma~2.5.1 of the re-edition.) \emph{Let \(X\) be an \(A\)-scheme. If \(X\otimes_Ak\) is
locally of finite type \textup{(}resp.\ of finite type\textup{)} over \(k\), so is \(X\) over \(A\).
If \(u\colon X\to Y\) is a morphism of \(A\)-schemes such that \(u\otimes_Ak\) is an immersion
\textup{(}resp.\ a closed immersion\textup{)}, so is \(u\).} If \(\operatorname{Spec}B\) is an affine
open subset of \(X\) and \(b_1,\dots,b_n\in B\) have images generating the \(k\)-algebra
\(B/\mathfrak mB\), then \(B=A[b_1,\dots,b_n]+\mathfrak mB\), so \(B=A[b_1,\dots,b_n]\) by the remark;
and \(X\) is quasi-compact if \(X\otimes_Ak\) is, the two having the same underlying space. Next,
\(u\) has the same underlying continuous map as \(u\otimes_Ak\), so it is a homeomorphism onto a
locally closed \textup{(}resp.\ closed\textup{)} subset of \(Y\); for \(x\in X\), the image \(N\) of
\(\mathcal O_{Y,u(x)}\) in \(M=\mathcal O_{X,x}\) satisfies \(M=N+\mathfrak mM\) because
\(u\otimes_Ak\) is an immersion, so \(\mathcal O_{Y,u(x)}\to\mathcal O_{X,x}\) is surjective, and
\(u\) is an immersion \textup{(}resp.\ a closed immersion\textup{)} (EGA~I, 4.2.2).

(b) (Lemma~2.5.3 of the re-edition, which makes no finiteness assumption.) \emph{Let \(G\) be an
\(A\)-group, flat over \(A\), acting on an \(A\)-scheme \(X\); let \(x_0\in X(A)\) and let
\(\varphi\colon G\to X\) be the orbit map \(g\mapsto gx_0\). If \(\varphi\) is flat at one point of
\(G\), it is flat.} Let \(\varphi\) be flat at \(g\). The action \(a\colon G\times_AX\to X\) is flat,
being the composite of the automorphism \((h,x)\mapsto(h,hx)\) of \(G\times_AX\) and of the
projection onto \(X\). (1) \emph{\(X\) is flat over \(A\) at every point of \(\varphi(G)\).} At
\(\varphi(g)\) it is, since \(\mathcal O_{G,g}\) is flat over \(A\) and faithfully flat over
\(\mathcal O_{X,\varphi(g)}\). Let \(h\in G\); choose a field \(K\) and points \(g',h'\in G(K)\) lying
over \(g\) and \(h\). The point \((h'g'^{-1},\varphi(g'))\) of \(G\times_AX\) with values in \(K\) lies
over a point \(z\) such that \(\operatorname{pr}_2(z)=\varphi(g)\) and \(a(z)=\varphi(h)\); since
\(G\times_AX\) is flat over \(A\) at \(z\) and \(a\) is flat at \(z\), \(X\) is flat over \(A\) at
\(\varphi(h)\). (2) \emph{Reduction to \(A=k\).} Let \(h\in G\), \(B=\mathcal O_{X,\varphi(h)}\) and
\(C=\mathcal O_{G,h}\). Both are flat over \(A\), so
\(\operatorname{Tor}^B_1(B/\mathfrak mB,C)=\operatorname{Tor}^A_1(k,C)=0\); by the flatness criterion
for the nilpotent ideal \(\mathfrak mB\) (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\ comm.}, III, \S\,5, no.~2, th.~1),
\(C\) is flat over \(B\) as soon as \(C/\mathfrak mC\) is flat over \(B/\mathfrak mB\), that is, as
soon as \(\varphi\otimes_Ak\) is flat at \(h\). (3) \emph{The case \(A=k\).} With \(K\), \(g'\),
\(h'\) as in (1), \(\varphi_K\colon G_K\to X_K\) is flat at the point defined by \(g'\); it commutes
with the left translations by \(h'g'^{-1}\) of \(G_K\) and of \(X_K\), so it is flat at the point
\(h''\) defined by \(h'\). Then \(\mathcal O_{G_K,h''}\) is flat over \(\mathcal O_{X,\varphi(h)}\)
and faithfully flat over \(\mathcal O_{G,h}\), so \(\varphi\) is flat at \(h\).

(c) (Proposition~2.5.2 of the re-edition.) \emph{Let \(u\colon G\to H\) be a quasi-compact morphism
of \(A\)-groups locally of finite type. Then \(u(G)\) is closed in \(H\), its connected components
are irreducible and all of the same dimension, and
\(\dim G=\dim u(G)+\dim\operatorname{Ker}u\). If \(u\) is a monomorphism, it is a closed
immersion.} These assertions are unchanged if \(A\) is replaced by \(k\) (for the last one, by (a)),
and then if \(k\) is replaced by \(\overline k\): the projection \(H_{\overline k}\to H\) is
surjective, open and closed, the image of \(G_{\overline k}\) is the inverse image of \(u(G)\),
dimensions do not change (EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 4.1.4), and being a closed immersion descends by
faithfully flat base change. So let \(A=k\) be algebraically closed, and let \(H'\) be the closure of
\(u(G)\), with its reduced structure. The points of \(H'\times_kH'\) whose two projections lie in
\(u(G)\) form the image of \(G\times_kG\) (EGA~I, 3.4.8), which the multiplication maps into
\(u(G)\); they are dense in \(H'\times_kH'\), the projections being open. As \(H'\times_kH'\) is
reduced, the multiplication maps it into \(H'\), and \(H'\) is a closed subgroup scheme of \(H\). A
connected component \(D\) of \(H'\) is open, so \(u^{-1}(D)\) is a nonempty open subset of \(G\); it
contains a rational point \(g\), and left translation by \(u(g)\) maps the neutral component
\(H'^0\) onto \(D\). As \(u\) is quasi-compact and \(H'^0\) is of finite type (2.4),
\(u(G)\cap H'^0\) is constructible (EGA~IV\textsubscript{1}, 1.8.5); it is dense in the irreducible
space \(H'^0\), so it contains a dense open subset \(W\) of \(H'^0\), and \(H'^0=W\cdot W\subset u(G)\)
by 0.5. Hence \(D=u(g)\cdot H'^0\subset u(G)\) for every \(D\): \(u(G)=H'\) is closed, and its
connected components, being translates of \(H'^0\), are irreducible and of the same dimension. For
the dimension formula, replace \(G\) by \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\), \(H\) by \(H'\), and
\(\operatorname{Ker}u\) by a subscheme with the same space; as \(G\times_kH\) is reduced, \(G\) acts
on \(H\) by \(g\cdot h=u(g)h\), and \(u\) is the orbit map of the origin. It is surjective, and flat
at the points lying over a maximal point of \(H\), where the local ring of \(H\) is a field; so it is
flat by (b). By EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 6.1.2,
\(\dim\mathcal O_{G,e}=\dim\mathcal O_{H,e}+\dim\mathcal O_{N,e}\), where \(N=u^{-1}(e)\); as \(G\),
\(H\) and \(N\) are equidimensional (2.4.1 and the note to it),
\(\dim G=\dim H+\dim N\). Finally, let \(u\) be a monomorphism; its kernel is trivial. By
Theorem~3.2 below, whose proof does not use this Addition, the quotient \(Q=G\backslash H\) exists,
and by 3.2~(iv), \((g,h)\mapsto(u(g)h,h)\) is an isomorphism of \(G\times_kH\) onto
\(H\times_QH\). Restricted to \(h=e\), it shows that \(u\) maps \(G\) isomorphically onto the fibre
of \(H\to Q\) at the image of the origin, which is a closed subscheme of \(H\), a rational point of
\(Q\) being closed. So \(u\) is a closed immersion.

\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIA-2.5.4}%
(d) (Proposition~2.5.4 of the re-edition.) \emph{Let \(G\) be an \(A\)-group locally of finite type
acting on a nonempty \(A\)-scheme \(X\) locally of finite type, in such a way that
\((g,x)\mapsto(gx,x)\) maps \(G\times_AX\) onto \(X\times_AX\). Then the connected components of
\(X\) are of finite type, irreducible, and all of the same dimension;}
\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIA-2.5.4-ii}%
\emph{more precisely, this dimension is \(\dim G-\dim F\), where \(F\) is the stabilizer, in
\(G\otimes_A\overline k\), of a closed point \(x\) of \(X\otimes_A\overline k\).} As in (c), we may
suppose \(A=k\) (using (a) for the finiteness); the hypothesis is preserved by base change. Suppose
first \(k\) algebraically closed; we may replace \(G\) and \(X\) by \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) and
\(X_{\mathrm{red}}\), on which \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) acts. For a rational point \(x\) of \(X\), the
orbit map \(\varphi_x\colon g\mapsto gx\) is surjective: a point \(y\) of \(X\) is the image of a
point of \(G\times_kX\) lying over the point \((y,x)\) of \(X\times_kX\), and such a point lies in
\(G\times\{x\}\). As in (c), \(\varphi_x\) is flat by (b), hence open
(EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 2.4.6). The open sets \(G^0x\) (\(x\in X(k)\)) are irreducible and
quasi-compact (2.4); two of them that meet are equal, since they then meet in a rational point
\(z=gx=g'x'\) with \(g,g'\in G^0(k)\), and \(G^0x=G^0z=G^0x'\); and they cover \(X\), since
\(X=\varphi_x(G)\) and \(G\) is the union of the translates \(G^0g\) by rational points \(g\), with
\(\varphi_x(G^0g)=G^0(gx)\). They are therefore the connected components of \(X\), irreducible and of
finite type. If \(x,y\in X(k)\), the points of \(G\) carrying \(x\) to \(y\) form a nonempty closed
subscheme of \(G\), which has a rational point \(g\); as \(G^0\) is normal in \(G\) (2.3), \(g\) maps
\(G^0x\) onto \(G^0y\), so all the components have the same dimension. The stabilizer \(F\) of \(x\)
is the fibre \(\varphi_x^{-1}(x)\), a closed subgroup scheme of \(G\); since \(\varphi_x\) is flat,
EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 6.1.2, gives
\(\dim\mathcal O_{G,e}=\dim\mathcal O_{X,x}+\dim\mathcal O_{F,e}\), that is,
\(\dim G=\dim G^0x+\dim F\). In general, if \(C\) is a connected component of \(X\) and \(C'\) a
connected component of \(C\otimes_k\overline k\), then \(C'\) is a connected component of
\(X\otimes_k\overline k\) and maps onto \(C\), the projection being open and closed; so \(C\) is
irreducible and quasi-compact, hence of finite type, and \(\dim C=\dim C'\)
(EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 4.1.4).
