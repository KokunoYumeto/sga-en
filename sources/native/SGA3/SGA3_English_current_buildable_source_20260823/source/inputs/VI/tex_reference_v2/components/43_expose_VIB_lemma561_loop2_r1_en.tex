\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-VIB-5.6.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 5.6.1.---}
\emph{Let \(S\) be a scheme, let \(f:X\to S\) be an \(S\)-scheme
locally of finite presentation, let \(s\) be a point of \(S\), let \(x\)
be a closed point of \(X_s\), and let \(z\) be the generic point of an
irreducible component \(Z\) of \(X_s\) containing \(x\) and such that
\[
  \dim_x(X_s)=\dim Z;
\]
suppose that \(f\) is universally open at \(z\) \textup{(}hence also at
\(x\)\textup{)}. Then there exist a morphism
\[
  g:S''\longrightarrow S'\longrightarrow S,
\]
with \(S'\to S\) \'etale of finite presentation and covering \(s\), and
\(S''\to S'\) finite, of finite presentation and surjective, such that
\(g^{-1}(s)\) consists of a single point \(s''\), and an
\(S\)-morphism \(h:S''\to X\) such that \(h(s'')=x\).}

It is clear that we may suppose \(X\) affine and of finite presentation
over \(S\), then, by passage to the limit, replace \(S\) by
\(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_{S,s}\), and then suppose \(S\) strictly
henselian, with closed point \(s\). Let then \(n=\dim_xX_s\). The choice of
a system of parameters of \(\mathcal O_{X_s,x}\) and the ``Main Theorem''
then show that, after shrinking \(X\), there exists a quasi-finite
\(S\)-morphism of finite presentation
\[
  u:X\longrightarrow S[T_1,\ldots,T_n]=Y.
\]
Moreover, \(u(x)\) is a closed point of \(Y_s\), and we may suppose that
\(u^{-1}(u(x))\) has an underlying space reduced to a point. Let \(V\) be
the subscheme of \(X\) that is the inverse image under \(u\) of a section
of \(Y\) over \(S\) passing through \(u(x)\). Then \(V\) is quasi-finite
over \(S\) and contains \(x\) as its only point above \(s\). Since \(S\)
is henselian, \(V=V'\amalg V''\), with \(V'\) finite over \(S\) and
\(V''\) above \(S-s\), so that \(V'\) is the spectrum of a local ring.
Let us show that \(V'\to S\) is surjective. For this, we may replace \(S\)
by the reduced closed subscheme underlying an irreducible component of
\(S\), and then suppose \(S\) normal, hence geometrically unibranch. Since
\(f\) is universally open at \(z\), there exists an irreducible component
\(\mathfrak z\) of \(X\) containing \(z\) and equidimensional \emph{over}
\(S\) (of relative dimension \(n\)) at the point \(z\). Since \(u\) is
quasi-finite and \(Y\) is of relative dimension \(n\), \(\mathfrak z\)
necessarily dominates \(Y\). Again because \(u\) is quasi-finite,
\(\mathfrak z\) is also equidimensional \emph{over} \(Y\) at the point
\(x\). Since \(\mathcal O_{Y,u(x)}\) is geometrically unibranch
(EGA~IV\(_3\), 14.4.1.1), Chevalley's theorem tells us that \(u\) is
universally open at \(x\); hence \(V\), and also \(V'\), are universally
open over \(S\) at \(x\), and a fortiori \(V'\) dominates \(S\).

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
The re-edition proves 5.6.1 without Chevalley's theorem (EGA~IV\(_3\), 14.4.1), using the following lemma of
O.~Gabber, which strengthens EGA~IV\(_3\), 14.4.1.2, and gives a proof of loc.\ cit., 14.4.1.3 (compare the
erratum ErrIV~38 in EGA~IV\(_4\)).
(a) \emph{Let \(A\subset B\) be integral domains, with \(B\) integral over \(A\), let \(\mathfrak q\) be a prime
ideal of \(B\), and suppose that \(A\) is unibranch at \(\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap A\). Then
\(\pi\colon\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A\) is open at \(\mathfrak q\).} Let \(A'\) be the
integral closure of \(A\) in its field of fractions and \(B'\) the ring generated by \(A'\) and \(B\) in the
field of fractions of \(B\). The morphisms \(\operatorname{Spec}B'\to\operatorname{Spec}B\) and
\(\phi\colon\operatorname{Spec}A'\to\operatorname{Spec}A\) are integral and surjective, hence closed, and
\(\phi^{-1}(\mathfrak p)\) is a single point \(\mathfrak p'\). So, if \(W'\) is an open neighbourhood of
\(\mathfrak p'\), the complement of the closed set \(\phi(\operatorname{Spec}A'-W')\) is a neighbourhood of
\(\mathfrak p\) contained in \(\phi(W')\). Hence \(\pi\) is open at \(\mathfrak q\) as soon as
\(\operatorname{Spec}B'\to\operatorname{Spec}A'\) is open at a point above \(\mathfrak q\), and we are reduced to
the case where \(A\) is integrally closed. Let then \(N\) be a quasi-Galois extension of the
field of fractions \(K\) of \(A\) containing that of \(B\), \(C\) the integral closure of \(A\) in \(N\), which
contains \(B\), and \(\Gamma=\operatorname{Aut}(N/K)\). As \(\operatorname{Spec}C\to\operatorname{Spec}B\) is
surjective, it is enough that \(\rho\colon\operatorname{Spec}C\to\operatorname{Spec}A\) be open. If \(W\) is
open in \(\operatorname{Spec}C\), so is \(W^*=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma W\), and \(\rho(W)=\rho(W^*)\);
as \(\Gamma\) permutes transitively the prime ideals of \(C\) above each prime ideal of \(A\) (Bourbaki,
\emph{Alg\`ebre commutative}, V, \S\,2, no.\,2, th.\,2), \(W^*\) is a union of fibres of \(\rho\), so
\(\rho(W^*)\) is the complement of the closed set \(\rho(\operatorname{Spec}C-W^*)\).
(b) \emph{Let \(f\colon X\to Y\) be a dominant morphism of irreducible schemes, locally of finite type and
quasi-finite at a point \(x\) such that \(y=f(x)\) is a unibranch point of \(Y\). Then \(f\) is open at
\(x\).} We may suppose \(X\) and \(Y\) reduced. By Zariski's Main Theorem (EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.12.9), there are
affine open neighbourhoods \(U\) of \(x\) and \(V=\operatorname{Spec}A\) of \(y\) such that \(f|_U\) factors as
an open immersion \(U\to V'\) followed by a finite morphism \(V'\to V\); replacing \(V'\) by the closure of the
image of \(U\), with its reduced structure, \(V'=\operatorname{Spec}B\) with \(B\) an integral domain finite over
\(A\), and \(A\to B\) is injective since \(f\) is dominant. By (a), \(V'\to V\) is open at the image of \(x\),
hence \(f\) is open at \(x\).

In the proof of 5.6.1, this lemma is used as follows. The hypotheses on \((f,x,z)\) are preserved by every base
change \(S_1\to S\), \(s_1\) and \(x_1\) being points above \(s\) and \(x\): indeed \(\dim_{x_1}X_{1,s_1}=n\)
(EGA~IV\(_2\), 4.1.4), and as generizations lift along \(X_{1,s_1}\to X_s\), a flat morphism, there is a
maximal point \(z_1\) of \(X_{1,s_1}\) above \(z\) specializing to \(x_1\), whose closure has dimension \(n\),
and \(f_1\) is universally open at \(z_1\). By EGA~IV\(_3\), 14.3.3.1~(i), applied to the base changes
\(S[T'_1,\ldots,T'_r]\to S\), it is therefore enough to prove that \(u\) is open at \(x\), that is
(EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.10.3), that every generization of \(u(x)\) lifts to a generization of \(x\). For this, we may
replace \(S\) by an irreducible component, with its reduced structure, and then by its normalization, the
generizations of \(u(x)\) lifting by going-up along the integral surjective morphism so obtained; so \(S\) is
integral and normal. Then, \(f\) being universally open at \(z\), some irreducible component \(\mathfrak z\) of
\(X\) containing \(z\) dominates \(S\) with generic fibre of dimension \(n\) (EGA~IV\(_3\), 14.3.13); as \(u\)
is quasi-finite, \(u\) maps its generic point to that of \(Y\), which is normal, and \(u|_{\mathfrak z}\), and
hence \(u\), is open at \(x\) by (b). Finally, the re-edition uses the henselization of \(\mathcal O_{S,s}\)
instead of its strict henselization, and makes the passage to the limit explicit (EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.10.5); this
gives in addition \(S'\) affine and \(\kappa(s'')=\kappa(x)\), the residue field being unchanged by
henselization. Note also that \(h\) defines a section of \(X\times_SS''\) over \(S''\) passing through a point
above \(x\) (compare EGA~IV\(_3\), 14.5.10).
