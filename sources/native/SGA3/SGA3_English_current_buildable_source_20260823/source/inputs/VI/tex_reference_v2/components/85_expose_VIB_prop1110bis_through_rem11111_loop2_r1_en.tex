\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
The re-edition adds here (its 11.10.bis) the following result, a special case of R.~W.~Thomason,
\emph{Equivariant resolution, linearization, and Hilbert's fourteenth problem over arbitrary base schemes},
Adv.\ Math.\ \textbf{65} (1987), 16--34, 1.4--1.5, who refers to an argument of P.~Deligne (see D.~Knutson,
\emph{Algebraic spaces}, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 203, Springer, 1971, III, Th.~1.1); the argument is close to
Serre's, (b) of the Addition after 11.8. It gives the conclusion of 11.10 over every noetherian \(S\), with no
hypothesis on \(\mathcal A(G)\). \emph{Let \(S\) be a noetherian scheme, \(G\) an \(S\)-group flat, quasi-compact
and quasi-separated over \(S\), \(\mathcal E\) a quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_S\)-module with an operation of \(G\),
and \(\mathcal M\) a coherent submodule of \(\mathcal E\). Then \(\mathcal M\) is contained in a coherent submodule
of \(\mathcal E\) stable under \(G\).}
Let \(f\colon G\to S\) be the structural morphism. The operation is given by an automorphism \(\theta\) of the
\(\mathcal O_G\)-module \(f^*(\mathcal E)\), the action of the point \(\mathrm{id}_G\in G(G)\); let
\(\delta\colon\mathcal E\to f_*f^*(\mathcal E)\) be the composite of the adjunction morphism and \(f_*(\theta)\), so
that \(\delta(x)\) is the section \(g\mapsto g\cdot x\). As \(f\) is flat, \(f^*\) is exact, and for a
quasi-coherent submodule \(\mathcal E_0\) of \(\mathcal E\), \(f^*(\mathcal E_0)\) is a submodule of
\(f^*(\mathcal E)\). If \(\theta(f^*\mathcal E_0)\subset f^*\mathcal E_0\), that is, if
\(\delta(\mathcal E_0)\subset f_*f^*(\mathcal E_0)\), then \(\mathcal E_0\) inherits an operation of \(G\): the
inverse of \(\theta\), which is the pull-back of \(\theta\) by the inversion of \(G\), also preserves
\(f^*(\mathcal E_0)\), and the identities expressing that \(\theta\) is an operation restrict to \(\mathcal E_0\),
since \(G\times_SG\) is flat over \(S\) (the re-edition's Remark 11.7.bis, which extends 11.7). Call such an
\(\mathcal E_0\) \emph{stable}. For a coherent submodule \(\mathcal F\) of \(\mathcal E\), let
\(\mathcal E_{\mathcal F}\) be the kernel of
\(\mathcal E\xrightarrow{\delta}f_*f^*(\mathcal E)\to f_*f^*(\mathcal E/\mathcal F)\), formed by the sections \(x\)
all of whose transforms \(g\cdot x\) lie in \(\mathcal F\). Pulling \(\delta(x)\) back along the unit section gives
\(x\); hence \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal F}\subset\mathcal F\), and \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal F}\) is coherent. By EGA~I,
9.4.9, \(\mathcal E\) is the filtered union of its coherent submodules \(\mathcal F\); so, \(f\) being
quasi-compact and quasi-separated, \(f_*f^*(\mathcal E)\) is the filtered union of the \(f_*f^*(\mathcal F)\) ((a) of
the Addition after 11.0), and \(\mathcal E\) is the filtered union of the
\(\mathcal E_{\mathcal F}=\delta^{-1}(f_*f^*\mathcal F)\). As \(\mathcal M\) is coherent and \(S\) noetherian, the
increasing family of the \(\mathcal M\cap\mathcal E_{\mathcal F}\) is stationary, so that
\(\mathcal M\subset\mathcal E_{\mathcal F}\) for some \(\mathcal F\). It remains to see that \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal F}\)
is stable. As \(f^*\) is exact, \(f^*(\mathcal E_{\mathcal F})\) is the kernel of the pull-back
\(f^*(\mathcal E)\to f^*f_*f^*(\mathcal E/\mathcal F)\) of the map above, and
\(f^*f_*f^*(\mathcal E/\mathcal F)=p_*q^*f^*(\mathcal E/\mathcal F)\) by (c) of the Addition after 11.0, where \(p\) and
\(q\) are the two projections of \(G\times_SG\). In terms of points, this map sends a section \(y\colon g\mapsto
y(g)\) of \(f^*(\mathcal E)\) to \((g,h)\mapsto h\cdot y(g)\bmod\mathcal F\); for \(y=\delta(x)\), with \(x\) a section
of \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal F}\), this is \((g,h)\mapsto hg\cdot x\bmod\mathcal F=0\). Hence
\(\delta(\mathcal E_{\mathcal F})\subset f_*f^*(\mathcal E_{\mathcal F})\).

\phantomsection\label{rem:sga3-VIB-11.10.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Remark 11.10.1.---}
J.-P.~Serre has remarked that in
\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-11.9]{11.9} \textup{(}hence also in
\hyperref[cor:sga3-VIB-11.10]{11.10}\textup{)} it was enough to
assume that, for every affine open subset \(U\) of \(S\), there
is a faithfully flat morphism \(U'\to U\), with \(U'\) affine
with ring \(A'\), such that
\(\mathcal A\otimes_{\mathcal O_U}\mathcal O_{U'}\) is defined by
a \emph{projective} \(A'\)-module \textup{(}not necessarily
free\textup{)}.\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has
\(\mathcal E\otimes_UU'\); the hypothesis of 11.9 concerns
\(\mathcal A=\mathcal A(G)\).} Moreover, when \(S\) is locally
noetherian, regular and of dimension~\(1\), this hypothesis can
be replaced, in \hyperref[cor:sga3-VIB-11.10]{11.10}, by the sole hypothesis that \(G\) be flat over
\(S\)\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text makes this assertion for 11.9 as well (``dans 11.9 (donc aussi
dans 11.10)''). For 11.9 it is false; the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, replaces the
sentence by counterexamples, given in the Addition below. For 11.10 it holds, even over every noetherian base, by
the Addition after 11.10.}
\textup{(}which implies that \(\mathcal A(G)\) is flat\textup{)};
see on this subject J.-P.~Serre, \emph{Groupes de Grothendieck
des sch\'emas en groupes r\'eductifs d\'eploy\'es}, Publ.\
Math.\ IH\'ES \textbf{34} (1968), 37--52.\footnote{Translator's
note: the 1970 text has ``Pub.\ Math.\ n\textsuperscript{o}~39''.}

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
The re-edition replaces the second sentence of 11.10.1 by counterexamples, found independently by O.~Gabber and
J.-P.~Serre, which show that in 11.8 and 11.9 the flatness of the coalgebra, even over a discrete valuation ring,
does not ensure the existence of a smallest stable submodule containing a given one. Let \(A\) be an integral
domain with field of fractions \(K\neq A\); the intersection of the nonzero ideals of \(A\) is \(0\), since an element
\(a\neq0\) of \(a^2A\) is a unit. Let \(\mathcal B=\{P\in K[T]\mid P(0)\in A\}\). As an \(A\)-module,
\(\mathcal B=A\oplus TK[T]\) is flat, and \(\mathcal B\otimes_A\mathcal B\) is the ring of polynomials in
\(K[T_1,T_2]\) with constant term in \(A\); so \(\Delta(P)=P(T_1+T_2)\), \(\eta(P)=P(0)\) and \(P(T)\mapsto P(-T)\)
make \(\mathcal B\) a Hopf algebra, and \(G=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal B\) is a flat affine \(A\)-group with
\(G_K=\mathbb G_{a,K}\). On \(V=Av_1\oplus Av_2\), the map \(\rho(v)=1\otimes v+T\otimes u(v)\), where \(u(v_1)=v_2\)
and \(u(v_2)=0\), is a comodule structure, because \(u^2=0\). A submodule of \(V\) containing \(v_1\) has the form
\(Av_1\oplus Jv_2\) with \(J\) an ideal of \(A\), and, \(\mathcal B\) being flat, it is stable if and only if
\(T\in J\mathcal B\), that is, if and only if \(J\neq0\) (for \(J\neq0\) one has \(JK=K\)). The intersection of these
stable submodules is \(Av_1\), which is not stable: there is no smallest stable submodule containing \(v_1\). (The
same holds for the subcoalgebra \(A\oplus K\cdot T\) of \(\mathcal B\), through which \(\rho\) factors.) For \(A\) a
discrete valuation ring, the re-edition gives a second example: the \(A\)-group obtained from the constant group
\((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)_A\) by removing the closed point of its non-neutral component, acting on \(V\) by
exchanging \(v_1\) and \(v_2\). Both examples are instances of a general construction: if \(B\) is a Hopf
\(A\)-algebra, free as an \(A\)-module, with augmentation ideal \(I\), then
\(B'=\{b\in B\otimes_AK\mid\eta(b)\in A\}=A\oplus(I\otimes_AK)\) is a Hopf algebra containing \(B\); and if \(V\) is a
\(B\)-comodule, free over \(A\) with basis \((v_1,\ldots,v_n)\), in which \(Av_1\) is not a subcomodule, then, as a
\(B'\)-comodule, \(V\) has the subcomodules \(Av_1+JV\) for all nonzero ideals \(J\) of \(A\), whose intersection
\(Av_1\) is not a subcomodule. (The re-edition states the last condition as \(\mu_V(v_1)\neq v_1\otimes1\), which
does not suffice: for \(n=1\), \(Av_1=V\).)

\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-11.11}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 11.11.---}
\emph{Let \(G\) be an algebraic group over a field \(k\).
The following conditions are equivalent:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-i}
  \emph{\(G\) is affine.}
\item[\textup{(ii)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-ii}
  \emph{\(G\) is quasi-affine.}
\item[\textup{(iii)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-iii}
  \emph{One can make \(G\) act faithfully on a quasi-affine
  \(k\)-scheme.}
\item[\textup{(iv)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-iv}
  \emph{One can make \(G\) act faithfully on a vector space
  over \(k\) \textup{(not necessarily of finite dimension)}.}
\item[\textup{(v)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-v}
  \emph{\(G\) is isomorphic to a closed subgroup of a group
  \(\operatorname{GL}(n)_k\).}
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.}
We have \hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-i]{\textup{(i)}}
\(\Rightarrow\)
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-ii]{\textup{(ii)}} trivially, and
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-ii]{\textup{(ii)}}
\(\Rightarrow\)
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-iii]{\textup{(iii)}}, because
\(G\) acts faithfully on itself by left translations. We have
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-iii]{\textup{(iii)}}
\(\Rightarrow\)
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-iv]{\textup{(iv)}}, for if \(G\)
acts on \(X\) quasi-compact and quasi-separated over \(k\), it
also acts on the vector space \(\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)\), thanks
to the fact that the formation of \(f_*(\mathcal O_X)\)
\textup{(}\(f:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k\) the structural
morphism\textup{)} commutes with every base change
\(S\to\operatorname{Spec}k\) \textup{(EGA~IV\(_1\),
1.7.21)};\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text cites EGA~IV
1.7.12, which concerns EGA~II, 3.8.5; EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.7.21 extends
to quasi-separated morphisms the base change theorem EGA~III,
1.4.15 used here, all base changes over a field being flat.}
hence it acts on the affine envelope
\(X'=\operatorname{Spec}(\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X))\), and the
canonical morphism \(X\to X'\) is obviously compatible with the
action of \(G\). This said, if \(X\) is quasi-affine, i.e.\
if \(X\to X'\) is an open immersion,\footnote{Translator's note: a scheme is quasi-affine if it is isomorphic to a
quasi-compact open subscheme of an affine scheme (EGA~II, 5.1.1); for a quasi-compact scheme \(X\) this holds if
and only if \(X\to X'\) is an open immersion (EGA~II, 5.1.2). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, recalls the definition.}
and if \(G\) acts faithfully on \(X\), it acts faithfully on
\(X'\), or, what amounts to the same, on the vector space
\(V=\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)\), which proves the implication
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-iii]{\textup{(iii)}}
\(\Rightarrow\)
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-iv]{\textup{(iv)}}.

Suppose now \hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-iv]{\textup{(iv)}},
that is, that \(G\) acts faithfully on the vector space \(V\).
Then, by \hyperref[cor:sga3-VIB-11.10]{11.10}, \(V\) is the
direct limit of finite-dimensional vector subspaces \(V_i\),
stable under the action of \(G\). If \(K_i\) is the kernel of
the induced action of \(G\) on \(V_i\), that is, of
\(G\to\operatorname{Aut}(V_i)\), then \(K_i\) is a closed
subscheme of \(G\), and the hypothesis that \(G\) acts
faithfully is expressed at once by the fact that the
intersection of the \(K_i\) is the unit subgroup of \(G\). As
\(G\) is noetherian, it follows that one of the \(K_i\) is
already reduced to the unit group, hence that
\(G\to\operatorname{Aut}(V_i)\) is a monomorphism. It is
therefore a closed immersion by
\hyperref[cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2]{1.4.2}, which proves that
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-iv]{\textup{(iv)}}
\(\Rightarrow\)
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-v]{\textup{(v)}}. As
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-v]{\textup{(v)}}
\(\Rightarrow\)
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.11-i]{\textup{(i)}} trivially, this
proves 11.11.

\phantomsection\label{rem:sga3-VIB-11.11.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Remark 11.11.1.---}
M.~Raynaud informs us that 11.11 can be generalized to the
case of a quasi-compact and quasi-separated group scheme \(G\)
over a locally noetherian regular scheme \(S\) of dimension
\(\leq1\), assuming moreover that \(G\) is \emph{flat} over
\(S\). One then has the equivalence of the following
conditions:\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, proves the
equivalence of (i)--(iv) in its added Section~12, assuming in (iii) that the quasi-affine \(S\)-scheme \(X\) is flat
over \(S\): its argument makes \(G\) act on the flat \(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(\mathcal A(X)\) by 11.6. It also notes
that \(\mathcal A(G)\) is here torsion-free, hence flat, the local rings of \(S\) being fields or discrete valuation
rings.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] \(G\) is affine over \(S\).
\item[\textup{(ii)}] \(G\) is quasi-affine over \(S\).
\item[\textup{(iii)}] One can make \(G\) act faithfully on a
  quasi-affine \(S\)-scheme.
\item[\textup{(iv)}] One can make \(G\) act faithfully on a
  flat quasi-coherent Module over \(S\).
\item[\textup{(v)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.11.1-b}
  \textup{(}If \(G\) is of finite type over \(S\) and \(S\) is
  noetherian\textup{)} \(G\) is isomorphic to a closed subgroup
  of an \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal E)\), where \(\mathcal E\)
  is a locally free Module of finite type over \(S\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
  Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, proves in its added Section~13, with various generalizations, that (i)--(iv) imply
  this condition; the converse holds because \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal E)\) is affine over \(S\).}
\end{enumerate}
