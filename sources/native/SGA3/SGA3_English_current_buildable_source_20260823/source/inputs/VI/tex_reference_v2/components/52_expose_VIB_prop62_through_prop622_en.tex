\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-6.2}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 6.2.---}%
\emph{Let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group. For a subfunctor \(T\) of the
functor \(G\), consider the following property:}
\[
  (+_{\!f})\qquad
  \text{for every \(s\in S\), \(T_s\) is representable by a
  closed subscheme of \(G_s\).}
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}]
  \emph{Let \(X\) be an \(S\)-scheme, and let \(u\) and \(v\) be two
  \(S\)-morphisms from \(X\) to \(G\). Then
  \(\underline{\operatorname{Transp}}(u,v)\) satisfies condition
  \((+_{\!f})\).}
\item[\textup{(ii)}]
  \emph{Let \(u:X\to G\) and \(v:Y\to G\) be two monomorphisms of
  schemes. Suppose that \(v_s\) is a closed immersion for every
  \(s\in S\); then \(\underline{\operatorname{Transp}}_G(X,Y)\)
  satisfies condition \((+_{\!f})\). Suppose that \(u_s\) and \(v_s\)
  are closed immersions for every \(s\in S\); then
  \(\underline{\operatorname{Transp}}^{\mathrm{str}}_G(X,Y)\)
  satisfies condition \((+_{\!f})\).}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{prop:sga3-VIB-6.2:item:iii}(iii)}]
  \emph{Let \(u:H\to G\) be a monomorphism; then
  \(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_G H\) satisfies condition
  \((+_{\!f})\); if, moreover, \(u_s\) is a closed immersion for every
  \(s\in S\), then \(\underline{\operatorname{Norm}}_G H\) satisfies
  condition \((+_{\!f})\).}
\item[\textup{(iv)}]
  \emph{Let \(u:X\to G\) be a morphism; then
  \(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}(u)\) satisfies condition
  \((+_{\!f})\).}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B},
  states 6.2 for arbitrary morphisms of \(S\)-schemes \(u,w\colon X\to G\) and \(v\colon Y\to G\), with the
  transporters of images of the note to 6.1:
  \(\underline{\operatorname{Transp}}(u,w)\) and \(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}(u)\) satisfy \((+_{\!f})\);
  so do \(\underline{\operatorname{Transp}}_G(u(X),v(Y))\) and \(\underline{\operatorname{Norm}}_G\,v(Y)\) when
  every \(v_s\) is a closed immersion, and
  \(\underline{\operatorname{Transp}}^{\mathrm{str}}_G(X,Y)\) when every \(u_s\) and \(v_s\) is one. These follow
  in the same way from VIII, 6.5, the formation of transporters commuting with base change.}
\end{enumerate}

It is enough to prove \textup{(i)} and \textup{(ii)}; assertion \textup{(i)} follows from
VIII, \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:examples:6:5:item:b:01}{6.5~(b)},%

  q_1,q_2:G\rightrightarrows\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(X,G)
\]
defined by \(q_1(g)=(\operatorname{int}g)\circ u\) and \(q_2(g)=v\), taking into account that \(G_s\) is

6.2.2 is VIII, 6.2, in whose proof it shows that a flat module \(M\) over an Artinian local ring \((A,\mathfrak m)\)
is free: if \(F\to M\) maps a basis of a free module onto elements of \(M\) lifting a basis of
\(M/\mathfrak mM\), its cokernel \(Q\) satisfies \(Q=\mathfrak mQ\), hence \(Q=0\), \(\mathfrak m\) being
nilpotent, and its kernel \(K\) satisfies \(K\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak m)=0\), \(M\) being flat, hence \(K=0\);

\phantomsection\label{ex:sga3-VIB-6.2.4-a}%
its 6.2.4 is VIII, \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:examples:6:5}{6.5}, with the items (a),
\phantomsection\label{ex:sga3-VIB-6.2.4-b}%

\phantomsection\label{ex:sga3-VIB-6.2.4-e}%
(e); and its 6.2.5 is VIII, \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:corollary:6:7}{6.7}, which needs no closedness hypothesis on
the group subscheme \(H\), every group subscheme of a group scheme over a field being closed

finitely many coordinates of finitely many generators, but not in general: if \(k\) is a field,
\(S=\operatorname{Spec}k[t_1,t_2,\ldots]\), \(Z\) the disjoint union of copies \(Z_n\) of \(S\) (\(n\geq1\)) and
\(Y\subset Z\) the union of the closed subschemes \(V(t_n)\) of the \(Z_n\), then \(T=V(t_1,t_2,\ldots)\).
\phantomsection\label{cor:sga3-VIB-6.2.6}%
Finally, the re-edition inserts here, as its 6.2.6, Expos\'e~XII, \SGARefLink{sga3:xii:6.1}{6.1}: if \(G\) is a
connected algebraic group over a field \(k\), its center \(Z\) is represented by a closed subgroup scheme, and
\(G/Z\) is affine. It details the step of the proof given there which says that \(Z\) is the intersection of the
kernels \(Z_n\) of the adjoint representations of \(G\) on the
\(P^n=\mathcal O_{G,e}/\mathfrak m_e^{n+1}\). In our words: the \(Z_n\) decrease, so their intersection \(K\) is
one of them, \(G\) being noetherian, and \(Z\subset K\). As \(K\) acts trivially on every \(P^n\), its centralizer
\(C\), a closed subgroup scheme of \(G\) (VIII, 6.5~(e)), contains every infinitesimal neighbourhood of \(e\); so
the ideal of \(C\) vanishes at \(e\) by Krull's intersection theorem, hence on an open neighbourhood \(U\) of \(e\),
being of finite type. Then \(C\) contains the image of the flat morphism \(U\times_kU\to G\), \((g,h)\mapsto gh\),
which is surjective (VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{sga3:VIA:0.5}{0.5}); so \(C=G\), and \(K\subset Z\).
