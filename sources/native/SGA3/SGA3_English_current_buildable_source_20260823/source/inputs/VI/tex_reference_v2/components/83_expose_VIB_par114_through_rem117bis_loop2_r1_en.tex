\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-VIB-11.4}%
\noindent\textbf{11.4.---}
Let \(\mathcal E\) and \(\mathcal F\) be two quasi-coherent
\(\mathcal O_S\)-modules. Denote by
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))\)
the \(S\)-functor of morphisms of \(S\)-functors from
\(\mathrm V(\mathcal F)\) to \(\mathrm V(\mathcal E)\), and by
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))\)
the sub-\(S\)-functor of
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))\)
formed by the \(\mathcal O_S\)-homomorphisms of
\(\mathcal O_S\)-functors in modules \textup{(cf.\ I)}. Then
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))(T)\)
is in canonical one-to-one correspondence with
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-Mod.}}
(\mathcal E\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal O_T,\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal O_T)\);
that is,
\[
  \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}
    \bigl(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E)\bigr)
  \simeq
  \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}
    \bigl(\mathrm W(\mathcal E),\mathrm W(\mathcal F)\bigr).
\]

Let \(X\) be an \(S\)-scheme. Then the set of morphisms of
\(S\)-functors from \(X\) to
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))\)
is in one-to-one correspondence with
\(\operatorname{Hom}_S(X\times_S\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))\);
hence the set of morphisms of \(S\)-functors from \(X\) to
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))\)
is in one-to-one correspondence with a subset of
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_S\bigl(X\times_S\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E)\bigr)
  =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-Mod.}}
    \bigl(\mathcal E,\mathcal A(X\times_S\mathrm V(\mathcal F))\bigr)
\]
\textup{(EGA~II, 1.7.13)}.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-11.5}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 11.5.---}
\emph{Let \(X\) be an \(S\)-scheme that is quasi-compact and
quasi-separated over \(S\), let \(f:X\to S\) be the
structural morphism, and let \(\mathcal E\) and
\(\mathcal F\) be two quasi-coherent
\(\mathcal O_S\)-modules. Suppose that one of the following
two conditions holds:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{a)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.5-a}
  \emph{\(f\) is affine;}

\item[\textup{b)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.5-b}
  \emph{\(\mathcal F\) is flat over \(\mathcal O_S\).}
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Then:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.5-i}
  \emph{the canonical homomorphism}
  \[
    \varphi:\mathcal A(X)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal S(\mathcal F)
      \longrightarrow\mathcal A\bigl(X\times_S\mathrm V(\mathcal F)\bigr)
  \]
  \emph{is an isomorphism \textup{(}where \(\mathcal S(\mathcal F)\) denotes
  the symmetric algebra of \(\mathcal F\)\textup{);}}

\item[\textup{(ii)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.5-ii}
  \emph{the set of morphisms of \(S\)-functors from \(X\) to
  \(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))\)
  is in one-to-one correspondence with
  \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-Mod.}}(\mathcal E,\mathcal A(X)\otimes\mathcal F)\).}
\end{enumerate}

To prove \textup{(i)}, by 11.1~\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.1-a]{\textup{a)}}
and~\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.1-b]{\textup{b)}}, it suffices to show that, if
\(\mathcal F\) is flat over \(\mathcal O_S\), then \(\mathrm V(\mathcal F)\) is flat
over \(S\). By EGA~III, 1.4.15.5, it suffices to see that, if \(\mathcal F\) is
flat over \(\mathcal O_S\), then so is \(\mathcal S(\mathcal F)\); this is a corollary
of a result of Daniel Lazard, according to which every flat module over a ring is a
filtered direct limit of free modules of finite type.

Let us now prove \textup{(ii)}.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, works in 11.4--11.6 with
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathrm W(\mathcal E),\mathrm W(\mathcal F))\), isomorphic to
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))\) (I,
\SGARefLink{sga3:i:proposition:4-6-2}{4.6.2} and \SGARefLink{sga3:i:proposition:4-6-3}{4.6.3}), and obtains (ii)
without (i): a morphism of \(S\)-functors from \(X\) to this functor is an element of
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_X}(f^*\mathcal E,f^*\mathcal F)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathcal E,f_*f^*\mathcal F)\),
and the canonical morphism \(\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal A(X)\to f_*f^*(\mathcal F)\) is an isomorphism
when \(f\) is affine (EGA~II, 1.4.7) or \(\mathcal F\) is flat ((b) of the Addition after 11.0).} Let \(\alpha\) be a morphism of \(S\)-functors from
\(X\) to \(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))\);
there corresponds to it a homomorphism \(\rho\) of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules from
\(\mathcal E\) to
\(\mathcal A(X\times_S\mathrm V(\mathcal F))=\mathcal A(X)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal S(\mathcal F)\)
such that, if \(\eta\) is an \(S\)-section of \(X\), the homomorphism
\[
  (\mathcal A(\eta)\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal S(\mathcal F)})\circ\rho:
  \mathcal E\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal S(\mathcal F)
  =\mathcal S(\mathcal F)
\]
corresponds to \(\alpha(\eta)\). But since
\(\alpha(\eta)\in\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))(S)\),
\(\mathcal A(\eta)\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal S(\mathcal F)}\) factors through the
canonical injective homomorphism \(\mathcal F\to\mathcal S(\mathcal F)\). We
therefore have, for every \(S\)-section \(\eta\) of \(X\), a commutative diagram
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=large,column sep=large]
    \mathcal E \arrow[r,"\rho"] \arrow[d] &
    \mathcal A(X)\otimes\mathcal S(\mathcal F)
      \arrow[d] \arrow[dl,"\mathcal A(\eta)\otimes\mathrm{id}" description] \\
    \mathcal S(\mathcal F) & \mathcal F \arrow[l]
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
which shows that \(\rho\) factors through the canonical injective homomorphism
\(\mathcal A(X)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal F\to
\mathcal A(X)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal S(\mathcal F)\), so that \(\rho\) may be
regarded as a homomorphism of \(\mathcal O_S\)-modules from \(\mathcal E\) to
\(\mathcal A(X)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal F\).

Conversely, let
\[
  \rho\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-Mod.}}
    \bigl(\mathcal E,\mathcal A(X)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal F\bigr)
  \subset\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_S\text{-Mod.}}
    \bigl(\mathcal E,\mathcal A(X\times_S\mathrm V(\mathcal F))\bigr);
\]
there corresponds to it a morphism \(\alpha\) from \(X\) to
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))\). For
every \(S\)-scheme \(T\) and every \(T\)-section \(\eta\) of \(X\times_ST\),
\(\alpha(\eta)\) corresponds to
\(\mathcal A(\eta)\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal S(\mathcal F)\otimes\mathcal O_T}\), a
homomorphism from \(\mathcal E\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal O_T\) to
\(\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal O_T\); hence
\(\alpha(\eta)\in\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))(T)\),
which shows that \(\alpha\) is a morphism from \(X\) to
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))\);
this proves \textup{(ii)}.\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has ``si \(\rho\) est une
\(S\)-section de \(X\)'' for \(\eta\) in the first paragraph of this proof of (ii), and
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathrm V(\mathcal E),\mathrm V(\mathcal F))\) for
\(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathrm V(\mathcal F),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))\) at its end.}

\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-VIB-11.6}%
\noindent\textbf{11.6.---}
Let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group that is flat, quasi-compact, and
quasi-separated over \(S\), such that \(\mathcal A=\mathcal A(G)\)
is a flat \(\mathcal O_S\)-module, and let \(\mathcal E\) be a
quasi-coherent and flat \(\mathcal O_S\)-module. Note that a
morphism of \(S\)-functors from \(G\) to
\(\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathrm W(\mathcal E))
=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal O_S}(\mathrm V(\mathcal E),\mathrm V(\mathcal E))\)
defines an \emph{operation} of \(G\) on \(\mathcal E\) if and only
if the corresponding morphism
\[
  \theta:G\times_S\mathrm V(\mathcal E)\longrightarrow\mathrm V(\mathcal E)
\]
satisfies the following two conditions:

\smallskip
\phantomsection\label{diag:sga3-VIB-11.6-1}%
\noindent 1) if \(\mu:G\times_SG\to G\) denotes the structural
morphism, the diagram
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=large,column sep=large]
    G\times_SG\times_S\mathrm V(\mathcal E)
      \arrow[r,"\mathrm{id}_G\times\theta"]
      \arrow[d,"\mu\times\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm V(\mathcal E)}"'] &
    G\times_S\mathrm V(\mathcal E) \arrow[d,"\theta"] \\
    G\times_S\mathrm V(\mathcal E) \arrow[r,"\theta"'] &
    \mathrm V(\mathcal E)
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
is commutative;

\smallskip
\phantomsection\label{diag:sga3-VIB-11.6-2}%
\noindent 2) if \(\varepsilon:S\to G\) is the unit section of
\(G\), the diagram
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=large,column sep=large]
    S\times_S\mathrm V(\mathcal E)
      \arrow[rr,"\varepsilon\times\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm V(\mathcal E)}"] & &
    G\times_S\mathrm V(\mathcal E) \arrow[dl,"\theta"] \\
    & \mathrm V(\mathcal E) \arrow[ul,"\simeq"] &
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
is commutative.

It then amounts to the same to say that the morphism
\(\rho:\mathcal E\to\mathcal A(G)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal E\)
deduced from \(\theta\) satisfies the following two axioms:

\smallskip
\phantomsection\label{cond:sga3-VIB-11.6-CM1}%
\noindent\textup{(CM 1)} If
\(\Delta:\mathcal A\to\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal A=\mathcal A(G\times_SG)\)
is the homomorphism \(\mathcal A(\mu)\),\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970
text has \(\mathcal A(\theta)\).} the diagram
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection\label{diag:sga3-VIB-11.6-CM1}%
  \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=large,column sep=large]
    \mathcal A\otimes\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal E &
    \mathcal A\otimes\mathcal E
      \arrow[l,"\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal A}\otimes\rho"'] \\
    \mathcal A\otimes\mathcal E
      \arrow[u,"\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal E}"] &
    \mathcal E \arrow[l,"\rho"] \arrow[u,"\rho"']
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
is commutative.

\smallskip
\phantomsection\label{cond:sga3-VIB-11.6-CM2}%
\noindent\textup{(CM 2)} If \(\eta:\mathcal A\to\mathcal O_S\) is
the homomorphism \(\mathcal A(\varepsilon)\), the diagram
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection\label{diag:sga3-VIB-11.6-CM2}%
  \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=large,column sep=large]
    \mathcal O_S\otimes\mathcal E & &
    \mathcal A\otimes\mathcal E
      \arrow[ll,"\eta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal E}"'] \\
    & \mathcal E \arrow[ul,"\simeq"] \arrow[ur,"\rho"'] &
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
is commutative.

It amounts to the same to say that the morphism
\(\theta_{\mathrm{af}}:G_{\mathrm{af}}\times_S\mathrm V(\mathcal E)\to\mathrm V(\mathcal E)\)
corresponding to \(\rho\) defines an operation of \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\)
on \(\mathcal E\) \textup{(compare I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:4-2}{4.2})}.

Consequently, there is a one-to-one correspondence between
operations of \(G\) on \(\mathcal E\) and operations of
\(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) on \(\mathcal E\).

\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-11.6.1}%
\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
The re-edition expresses 11.4--11.6 by means of \(\mathrm W(\mathcal E)\) and states the outcome of 11.6 as a
proposition (its 11.6.1). Let \(G\) be flat, quasi-compact and quasi-separated over \(S\), with \(\mathcal A(G)\)
flat over \(\mathcal O_S\), and let \(\mathcal E\) be a quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_S\)-module, flat or not.
(i) \emph{An \(\mathcal A(G)\)-comodule structure on \(\mathcal E\) is the same as an operation of
\(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) on \(\mathcal E\), and therefore defines, through the morphism of groups
\(\tau_G\colon G\to G_{\mathrm{af}}\) of the Addition after 11.3, an operation of \(G\) on \(\mathcal E\).} This
holds because \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) is affine over \(S\), with algebra \(\mathcal A(G)\) (I,
\SGARefLink{sga3:i:paragraph:4-7-2}{4.7.2}). In particular, 11.7 below holds for every quasi-coherent
\(\mathcal E\) whose operation is given by a comodule structure, without assuming \(G\) affine or \(\mathcal E\)
flat; the re-edition states 11.7 in this form.
\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.6.1-ii}%
(ii) \emph{If \(\mathcal E\) is flat, every operation of \(G\) on \(\mathcal E\) factors through
\(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) and comes from a unique \(\mathcal A(G)\)-comodule structure}: this is the last assertion of
11.6. The argument of 11.6 gives (ii) also when \(G\) is affine over \(S\) and \(\mathcal E\) is arbitrary, by
11.5, case~\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.5-a]{\textup{a)}}; this case is used in 11.7--11.10.

\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-VIB-11.7}%
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 11.7.---}
\emph{Let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group that is quasi-compact and
quasi-separated over \(S\), flat, and such that
\(\mathcal A(G)\) is a flat \(\mathcal O_S\)-module
\(\mathcal A\), and let \(\mathcal E\) be a quasi-coherent
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module. Suppose either that \(G\) is affine
over \(S\) or that \(\mathcal E\) is flat over \(S\). Let
\(\rho:\mathcal E\to\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal E\) be a
homomorphism defining an operation of \(G\) on \(\mathcal E\).
Let \(\mathcal E_0\) be a quasi-coherent
sub-\(\mathcal O_S\)-module of \(\mathcal E\) such that the
homomorphism \(\mathcal E_0\to\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal E\)
deduced from \(\rho\) factors through the canonical
homomorphism \textup{(}injective, thanks to the fact that
\(\mathcal A\) is flat!\textup{)}
\(\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal E_0\to\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal E\)
by means of a homomorphism
\(\rho_0:\mathcal E_0\to\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal E_0\). Then
\(\rho\) defines an operation of \(G\) on \(\mathcal E_0\)
\textup{(}which we shall call the \emph{operation induced} on
\(\mathcal E_0\) by \(\rho\), and we shall say that
\(\mathcal E_0\) is \emph{stable} under \(\rho\)\textup{)}.}

This follows immediately from the definitions and from
\hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-11.6]{11.6}. Notice, however, that in
general the canonical map
\[
  \mathrm W(\mathcal E_0)\longrightarrow\mathrm W(\mathcal E)
\]
is not injective.
