
\section*{7. Generated subgroups; commutator group}
\phantomsection\label{sec:sga3-VIB-7}%
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{7. Generated subgroups; commutator group}

Throughout this section, \(k\) denotes a fixed field.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-7.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 7.1.---}
\emph{Let \(G\) be a \(k\)-group, let
\((X_i)_{i\in I}\) be a family of geometrically reduced
\(k\)-schemes} \textup{(EGA~IV\(_2\), 4.6.2)}\emph{;}\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text says ``séparable''
(separable) here and throughout this section. In EGA~IV\(_2\), 4.6.2, ``séparable'', ``géométriquement
réduit'' and ``universellement réduit'' are synonyms; this translation uses ``geometrically reduced'', as does
the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}.}
\emph{for every \(i\in I\), let \(f_i:X_i\to G\) be a
\(k\)-morphism.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{prop:sga3-VIB-7.1:item:i}(i)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-7.1-i}
  \emph{There exists a smallest closed \(k\)-group subscheme of
  \(G\) through which every \(f_i\) factors. It is denoted
  \[
    \Gamma_G\bigl((f_i)_{i\in I}\bigr).
  \]
  It is a geometrically reduced \(k\)-scheme, and hence smooth
  when \(G\) is locally of finite type over \(k\).}\footnote{Translator's note: a geometrically reduced \(k\)-scheme
  locally of finite type is smooth over \(k\) at the generic points of its irreducible components, and a
  \(k\)-group locally of finite type that is smooth at one point is smooth
  (\hyperref[cor:sga3-VIB-1.3.1]{1.3.1}); the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, adds
  the reference to 1.3.1.}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{prop:sga3-VIB-7.1:item:ii}(ii)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-7.1-ii}
  \emph{Put
  \[
    X=\coprod_{i\in I}X_i,
  \]
  and let \(f:X\to G\) be the morphism whose restriction to
  \(X_i\) is \(f_i\), for every \(i\in I\). Put
  \[
    X^1=X\amalg X,
  \]
  and let \(f^1:X^1\to G\) be the morphism whose restrictions to
  the two copies of \(X\) are \(f\) and \(c\circ f\),
  respectively. For every \(n>1\), put
  \[
    X^n=X^1\times_kX^{n-1},
    \qquad
    f^n=\mu\circ(f^1\times_k f^{n-1}):X^n\longrightarrow G.
  \]
  Then \(\Gamma_G((f_i)_{i\in I})\) is the reduced subscheme of
  \(G\) whose underlying space is the closure of
  \[
    \bigcup_{n\geq1}f^n(X^n).
  \]}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{prop:sga3-VIB-7.1:item:iii}(iii)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-7.1-iii}
  \emph{For every \(k\)-scheme \(S\),
  \(\Gamma_G((f_i)_{i\in I})_S\) is the smallest closed group
  subscheme of \(G_S\) through which every
  \[
    f_{i,S}:(X_i)_S\longrightarrow G_S
  \]
  factors. If \(G\) is assumed to be locally of finite type
  over \(k\), then \(\Gamma_G((f_i)_{i\in I})_S\) is the smallest
  group subscheme of \(G_S\) through which every \(f_{i,S}\)
  factors.}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, states this
  second assertion without assuming \(G\) locally of finite type over \(k\). In the proof below the hypothesis
  serves only to know that the group subscheme \(G'_s\) of \(G_s\) is closed, and every group subscheme of a
  group scheme over a field is closed (Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A},
  \hyperref[sga3:VIA:0.5.2]{Addition after 0.5, (c)}).}
\end{enumerate}

First note that, to prove \textup{\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-7.1:item:i}{(i)}} and \textup{\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-7.1:item:iii}{(iii)}}, after defining \(X\) and \(f\) as
in \textup{\SGARefLink{concl:sga3-VIB-7.1-ii}{(ii)}}, we are reduced to the case in which \(I\)
consists of a single element.

Let \(H\) be the reduced subscheme of \(G\) whose underlying set
is
\[
  \overline{\bigcup_{n\geq1}f^n(X^n)}.
\]
Then \(H\) is geometrically reduced (EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.10.7). It
is clear that every closed subscheme of \(G\) through which all
the \(f^n\) factor also contains \(H\).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has ``tout sous-préschéma fermé de
\(G\) qui domine \(f\)'' (every closed subscheme of \(G\) through which \(f\) factors). This is true of closed
group subschemes, the case needed here, since every \(f^n\) factors through a group subscheme through which
\(f\) factors; but the closure of \(f(X)\), with its reduced structure, need not contain \(H\). The
reading above is that of the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, which adds that the
family of the \(f^n\colon X^n\to H\) is schematically dominant (EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.10.4).} To prove
\textup{\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-7.1:item:i}{(i)}} and \textup{\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-7.1:item:ii}{(ii)}}, it is therefore enough to show
that \(H\) is a group subscheme of \(G\), that is, that the
restriction of \(c\) to \(H\) and the restriction of \(\mu\) to
\(H\times_kH\) factor through the inclusion \(H\to G\).

Since \(H\) is geometrically reduced, \(H\times_kH\) is reduced,
and it is enough to verify, set-theoretically, that
\[
  c(H)\subset H,
  \qquad
  \mu(H\times_kH)\subset H.
\]
By EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.10.6, the union of the
\[
  f^n_{(H)}(X^n\times_kH)
\]
is dense in \(H\times_kH\). Similarly, for every
\(n\geq1\), the union of the
\[
  f^m_{(X^n)}(X^n\times_kX^m),
  \qquad m\geq1,
\]
is dense in \(X^n\times_kH\). It is therefore
enough to prove
\[
  \mu\!\left(
    f^n_{(H)}
      \bigl(f^m_{(X^n)}(X^n\times_kX^m)\bigr)
  \right)\subset H
\]
and \(c(f^n(X^n))\subset H\). But
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \mu\!\left(
    f^n_{(H)}
      \bigl(f^m_{(X^n)}(X^n\times_kX^m)\bigr)
  \right)
  &=
  \mu\!\left((f^n\times_k f^m)(X^n\times_kX^m)\right)\\
  &=f^{n+m}(X^{n+m})\subset H.
\end{aligned}
\]
Also, since \(c(f^1(X^1))\subset f^1(X^1)\), for every \(n\)
one has
\[
  c(f^n(X^n))\subset f^n(X^n)\subset H.
\]
This proves \textup{\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-7.1:item:i}{(i)}} and \textup{\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-7.1:item:ii}{(ii)}}.

We now prove \textup{\SGARefLink{concl:sga3-VIB-7.1-iii}{(iii)}}. Let \(G'\) be a closed group
subscheme of \(G_S\) through which \(f_S\) factors. We must show
that \(G'\) contains \(H_S\), or equivalently that
\[
  H_S=H_S\times_{G_S}G'.
\]
Put
\[
  H'_S=H_S\times_{G_S}G'=G'\cap H_S.
\]
Every \(f^n_S\) factors through both \(G'\) and \(H_S\), and
hence through \(H'_S\). Since the family
\[
  f^n:X^n\longrightarrow H
\]
is schematically dominant, EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.10.6 implies that
the base-changed family
\[
  f^n_S:X^n_S\longrightarrow H_S
\]
is schematically dominant as well. Because every \(f^n_S\)
factors through \(H'_S\), EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.10.1\textup{(c)}
gives
\[
  H'_S=H_S.
\]
We now prove the second assertion of \textup{\SGARefLink{concl:sga3-VIB-7.1-iii}{(iii)}} when \(G\) is
locally of finite type over \(k\). Let \(G'\) be a group
subscheme of \(G_S\) through which \(f_S\) factors. Again we must
show that, if we put
\[
  H'_S=H_S\times_{G_S}G',
\]
then \(H'_S=H_S\). For this it is enough to show that \(H'_S\) is
closed in \(H_S\) and to apply the preceding argument. It is
therefore enough to show that \(H_S\) and \(H'_S\) have the same
underlying set; a fortiori, it is enough to show that, for every
\(s\in S\),
\[
  H_s=H'_s,
  \qquad\text{where}\qquad
  H'_s:=H'_S\times_S\kappa(s)=H_s\times_{G_s}G'_s.
\]
Now \(G'_s\), being a \(\kappa(s)\)-group subscheme of \(G_s\), is
closed (\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-1.4]{1.4}); hence \(H'_s\) is closed
in \(H_s\), and the argument at the beginning, applied to
\(H'_s\), \(H_s\), and the \(f^n_s\), shows that \(H'_s=H_s\).
