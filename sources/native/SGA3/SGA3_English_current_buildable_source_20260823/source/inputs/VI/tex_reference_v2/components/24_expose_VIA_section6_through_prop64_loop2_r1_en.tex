\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}; our
formulation). The re-edition ends the Exposé with a section~6, ``Complements on \(k\)-groups not
necessarily of finite type'', whose results are taken from D.~Perrin, \emph{Schémas en groupes
quasi-compacts sur un corps}, Publ.\ Math.\ Orsay, 1975, and \emph{Approximation des schémas en
groupes, quasi-compacts sur un corps}, Bull.\ Soc.\ Math.\ France \textbf{104} (1976), 323--335; they
are used in Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, \S\,12. Here \(k\) is a field, \(G\) and \(H\) are \(k\)-group
schemes, not assumed of finite type, and \(\mu_G\) is the multiplication of \(G\).

(a) (Its Lemma~6.1.) \emph{For every point \(x\) of \(G\) there is a point \(w\) of \(G\times_kG\)
with \(\mu_G(w)=x\) whose two projections are maximal points of \(G\)}: in A.~Weil's language, every
point of \(G\) is a product of two generic points. Let \(K=\kappa(x)\) and let \(\gamma\in G(K)\) be
the point defined by \(x\). The fibre of \(\mu_G\) at \(x\) is the fibre of \(\mu_{G_K}\) at \(\gamma\),
which the second projection maps isomorphically onto \(G_K\), with inverse
\(g\mapsto(\gamma g^{-1},g)\). Take for \(w\) a maximal point of this fibre. Its two projections are
the images in \(G\) of maximal points of \(G_K\), namely of the point corresponding to \(w\) and of its
image under the automorphism \(g\mapsto\gamma g^{-1}\) of the \(K\)-scheme \(G_K\); and the flat
projection \(G_K\to G\) maps maximal points to maximal points.

\phantomsection\label{cor:sga3-VIA-6.2}%
(b) (Its Corollary~6.2.) \emph{Let \(f\colon G\to H\) be a quasi-compact dominant morphism of
\(k\)-groups. Then \(f\) is surjective, and if \(H\) is reduced, \(f\) is faithfully flat.} As \(f\) is
quasi-compact, the closure of \(f(G)\), which is \(H\), consists of the specializations of points of
\(f(G)\) (EGA~IV\textsubscript{1}, 1.10.1), so \(f(G)\) contains every maximal point of \(H\). Given
\(h\in H\), choose by (a) a point \(w\) of \(H\times_kH\) with \(\mu_H(w)=h\) whose projections
\(\alpha\), \(\beta\) are maximal points of \(H\); as \(\alpha,\beta\in f(G)\), \(w=(f\times f)(v)\) for
a point \(v\) of \(G\times_kG\) (EGA~I, 3.4.8), and \(f(\mu_G(v))=h\). If \(H\) is reduced, its local
ring at \(\alpha\) is a field; so \(f\), which is the orbit map of the rational point \(e\) of \(H\) for
the action \(g\cdot y=f(g)y\) of \(G\) on \(H\), is flat at the points over \(\alpha\), hence flat by
(b) of the first Addition after 2.4.1.

(c) (Its 6.3 and Proposition~6.4.) Recall (EGA~IV\textsubscript{3}, 11.10.1) that a morphism
\(f\colon X\to Y\) is \emph{schematically dominant} if, for every open subset \(U\) of \(Y\), the only
closed subscheme of \(U\) through which \(f^{-1}(U)\to U\) factors is \(U\) itself; when \(f\) is
quasi-compact and quasi-separated, this means that the closed image of \(f\) is \(Y\) (loc.\ cit.,
11.10.3, and EGA~I, 9.5.8).
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIA-6.4}%
\emph{If \(f\colon H\to G\) is a quasi-compact morphism of \(k\)-groups, its closed image \(H'\) is a
closed subgroup scheme of \(G\), and \(f\) factors as \(H\xrightarrow{\;f'\;}H'\hookrightarrow G\) with
\(f'\) schematically dominant, quasi-compact and surjective.} The morphism \(f\) is quasi-compact and
separated (\(H\) is separated, 0.3), so \(H'\) is defined by the quasi-coherent ideal
\(\operatorname{Ker}(\mathcal O_G\to f_*\mathcal O_H)\) (EGA~I, \S\,9.5), and its formation commutes
with flat base change (EGA~IV\textsubscript{1}, 1.7.21). It contains the unit section, which factors
through \(f\). The inversion of \(G\) maps \(H'\) onto the closed image of \(c_G\circ f=f\circ c_H\)
(\(c_G\), \(c_H\) the inversions), that is, onto \(H'\). The closed image of \(f\times f\) is
\(H'\times_kH'\): by flat base change, \(\mathrm{id}_H\times f\) has closed image \(H\times_kH'\), and
\(f\times\mathrm{id}_{H'}\colon H\times_kH'\to G\times_kH'\) has closed image \(H'\times_kH'\), whence
the assertion by the transitivity of closed images (EGA~I, 9.5.5). As
\(\mu_G\circ(f\times f)=f\circ\mu_H\) factors through \(H'\), \(f\times f\) factors through the closed
subscheme \(\mu_G^{-1}(H')\), which therefore contains \(H'\times_kH'\). So \(H'\) is a closed subgroup
scheme of \(G\). Finally, \(f'\) is schematically dominant by construction, quasi-compact because
\(H'\to G\) is separated, and surjective by (b).

(d) (Its Theorem~6.5, due to D.~Perrin, Publ.\ Math.\ Orsay, loc.\ cit., V, 3.1 and 3.2, and
Bull.\ Soc.\ Math.\ France, loc.\ cit., 0.0 and 0.1.) \emph{Let \(G\) be a quasi-compact \(k\)-group.
\textup{(i)} \(G\) is the projective limit of a filtered system \((G_i)\) of \(k\)-groups of finite
type, the transition morphisms being affine for \(i\) large enough, and the morphisms \(G\to G_i\) are
faithfully flat \textup{(}and affine for \(i\) large enough\textup{)}. \textup{(ii)} If \(N\) is a closed
subgroup of \(G\), the \textup{(}fpqc\textup{)} quotient sheaf \(G/N\) is a \(k\)-scheme when the
immersion \(N\to G\) is of finite presentation \textup{(}\(G/N\) is then of finite type over \(k\)\textup{)},
and when \(N\) is normal in \(G\).} For the proof, which uses several intermediate theorems, the
re-edition refers to Perrin.

\phantomsection\label{cor:sga3-VIA-6.6}%
(e) (Its Corollary~6.6.) \emph{A quasi-compact morphism \(f\colon G\to H\) of \(k\)-groups that is
schematically dominant is faithfully flat; this applies in particular when \(H\) is affine and
\(\mathcal O(H)\to\mathcal O(G)\) is injective.} By (b), \(f\) is surjective, and by (b) of the first
Addition after 2.4.1 it suffices that \(f\) be flat at the origin. The subgroup \(H^0\) of (e) of the
second Addition after 2.4.1 has the same local ring at the origin as \(H\); replacing \(H\) by \(H^0\)
and \(G\) by \(f^{-1}(H^0)\), which keeps \(f\) schematically dominant (\(H^0\to H\) being flat;
EGA~IV\textsubscript{3}, 11.10.5), we may suppose \(H\), and hence \(G\), quasi-compact. Write
\(H=\varprojlim H_i\) as in (d)~(i). The kernel \(N_i\) of \(G\to H_i\) is a closed normal subgroup,
and its immersion into \(G\) is of finite presentation, as is the unit section of \(H_i\); so
\(G/N_i\) is a \(k\)-group of finite type by (d)~(ii), and \(G\to H_i\) induces a monomorphism
\(f_i\colon G/N_i\to H_i\), which is a closed immersion ((c) of the first Addition after 2.4.1). The
composite of \(G\to G/N_i\) and \(f_i\) equals that of \(f\) and of the faithfully flat morphism
\(H\to H_i\); this composite is schematically dominant, hence so is \(f_i\), which is therefore an
isomorphism. Thus every \(G\to H_i\) is faithfully flat, and \(f\) is flat at the origin, the local
ring of \(H\) at the origin being the inductive limit of those of the \(H_i\) (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\
comm.}, I, \S\,2, no.~7, prop.~9). Finally, if \(H\) is affine and \(\mathcal O(H)\to\mathcal O(G)\) is
injective, the closed image of \(f\) is \(H\).

(f) (Its Corollary~6.7.) \emph{Let \(u\colon G\to H\) be a quasi-compact morphism of \(k\)-groups,
with kernel \(N\). Then the \textup{(}fpqc\textup{)} quotient sheaf \(G/N\) is represented by a
\(k\)-group, and \(u\) factors as \(G\to G/N\to H\), the first morphism being faithfully flat and the
second a closed immersion. In particular, a quasi-compact monomorphism of \(k\)-groups is a closed
immersion.} By (c) and (e), the closed image \(G'\) of \(u\) is a closed subgroup of \(H\), and
\(G\to G'\) is faithfully flat and quasi-compact, hence a covering for the (fpqc) topology; its kernel
is \(N\), so \(G'\) represents \(G/N\) (Exposé~IV, 3.3.2.1 and 5.1.7, which the re-edition cites).
If \(u\) is a monomorphism, so is \(G\to G'\), which, being also an effective epimorphism, is an
isomorphism (Exposé~IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:remark:1:14}{1.14}).

(g) (Its Corollaries~6.8--6.10; Perrin, Bull.\ Soc.\ Math.\ France, loc.\ cit., 4.2.6--4.2.8, and
Publ.\ Math.\ Orsay, loc.\ cit., V, 3.7 and 3.9.) \emph{The category of commutative quasi-compact
\(k\)-group schemes is abelian}: the argument of 5.4 applies, with (f) in place of Theorem~3.2.
\emph{If \(k\) has characteristic \(0\), every \(k\)-group scheme \(G\) is geometrically reduced.}
Translations by points with values in extensions of \(k\) reduce this to the assertion that
\(\mathcal O_{G,e}\otimes_kL\) is reduced for every extension \(L\) of \(k\); now
\(\mathcal O_{G,e}\) is the local ring at the origin of the quasi-compact group \(G^0\) ((e) of the
second Addition after 2.4.1), and writing \(G^0=\varprojlim G_i\) as in (d)~(i), it is the inductive
limit of the local rings at the origin of the \(G_i\), which are smooth (Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B},
\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.6.1}{1.6.1}), so that their tensor products with \(L\) are reduced.
\emph{If \(k\) is algebraically closed and \(G\) is quasi-compact, a faithfully flat morphism
\(G\to H\) of \(k\)-groups induces a surjection \(G(k)\to H(k)\), and the rational points of \(G\) are
dense in \(G\)}; for these two assertions the re-edition refers to Perrin.
