
\medskip
\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-VIA-5.5}%
\noindent\textbf{5.5.---}
Let \(G\) be a group locally of finite type and flat over an Artinian
local ring \(A\). We know that the connected component of the origin
\(G^0\) is a normal subgroup of \(G\) (\hyperref[par:sga3-VIA-2.3]{2.3}). This subgroup \(G^0\)
acts on the left on all the connected components \(G^\alpha\) of \(G\),
so that \(G^0\backslash G\) is the direct sum of the
\(G^0\backslash G^\alpha\). In particular, the connected component of
the origin in \(G^0\backslash G\) is none other than
\(G^0\backslash G^0\simeq\operatorname{Spec}A\), and
\(G^0\backslash G\to\operatorname{Spec}A\) is a local isomorphism at the
origin; consequently, \(G^0\backslash G\) is unramified over
\(\operatorname{Spec}A\) (Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-1.3}{1.3});\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text refers here to ``3.3'', a
number that does not occur in this Exposé; the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A},
cites Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, 1.3, by which a morphism of groups locally of finite type that is
unramified at one point is unramified everywhere.} since \(G^0\backslash G\) is
also flat over \(\operatorname{Spec}A\) by Theorem~\SGARefLink{concl:sga3-VIA-3.2-iv}{3.2~(iv)},\footnote{Translator's note: the printing has ``théorème 4.2 (iv)'' here, and
``théorème 4.2'' twice in 5.6 below; Theorem 3.2 is meant.} we see that
\(G^0\backslash G\) is an \'etale group over \(\operatorname{Spec}A\).

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}; our
formulation). The re-edition states this result as its Proposition~5.5.1:
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIA-5.5.1}%
\emph{if \(G\) is an \(A\)-group locally of finite type and flat, \(G^0\backslash G\) is an étale
\(A\)-group; if \(A=k\) is an algebraically closed field, \(G^0\backslash G\) is the constant
\(k\)-group on \(G(k)/G^0(k)\), which acts simply transitively on the set of connected components of
\(G\), and it is finite if \(G\) is of finite type} (for the second part it refers to Demazure--Gabriel,
loc.\ cit., II, \S\,5, 1.7--1.10). Indeed, an étale scheme over an algebraically closed field is the
disjoint union of copies of \(\operatorname{Spec}k\) indexed by its rational points; those of
\(G^0\backslash G\) are the images of the cosets \(G^0g\), \(g\in G(k)\); and
\(G^0g\mapsto G^0g\) identifies the set of these cosets with the set of connected components of \(G\),
each component having a rational point. A group of finite type has finitely many components.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-VIA-5.6}%
\noindent\textbf{5.6.---}
Now let \(k\) be a perfect field and \(G\) a group locally of
finite type over \(k\). We have seen (\SGARefLink{sga3:VIA:0.2}{0.2}) that \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is then a group
subscheme of \(G\). Moreover, the equivalence class of the origin of \(G\)
for the left action of \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) on \(G\) is the whole
underlying space of \(G\). It therefore follows from Theorem~\hyperref[thm:sga3-VIA-3.2]{3.2} that
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIA-5.6.1}%
the quotient \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\backslash G\) is the spectrum of a finite local \(k\)-algebra.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, states this as its Proposition~5.6.1 and adds that the residue field of this
algebra is \(k\): the quotient has a single point, image of the rational point \(e\), and is of finite
type over \(k\) (3.2), hence is the spectrum of a finite local \(k\)-algebra with residue field \(k\).}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIA-5.6.2}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition.---}
\emph{Let \(u:F\to G\) be a homomorphism of groups locally of
finite type over a perfect field \(k\). The following assertions
are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{prop:sga3-VIA-5.6.2:item:i}(i)}] \(u\) is flat.
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{prop:sga3-VIA-5.6.2:item:ii}(ii)}] \(u^0:F^0\to G^0\) is dominant, and the morphism
\(v:F_{\mathrm{red}}\backslash F\to G_{\mathrm{red}}\backslash G\)
induced by \(u\) is flat.
\end{enumerate}}

Consider indeed the following commutative diagram:
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection\label{diag:sga3-VIA-5.6.2-quotients}%
  \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=large,column sep=large]
    F_{\mathrm{red}}
      \arrow[r,"i"]
      \arrow[d,"u_{\mathrm{red}}"'] &
    F
      \arrow[r,"p"]
      \arrow[d,"u"] &
    F_{\mathrm{red}}\backslash F
      \arrow[d,"v"] \\
    G_{\mathrm{red}}
      \arrow[r,"j"'] &
    G
      \arrow[r,"q"'] &
    G_{\mathrm{red}}\backslash G
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
where \(i\) and \(j\) denote the inclusions, \(p\) and \(q\) the canonical
projections. By Theorem~\SGARefLink{concl:sga3-VIA-3.2-iv}{3.2~(iv)}, \(p\) and \(q\) are faithfully flat;
consequently, if \(u\) is flat, \(qu=vp\) is flat, and hence so is \(v\).

Conversely, if \(v\) is flat, \(F\) and \(G\) are flat over
\(G_{\mathrm{red}}\backslash G\); by SGA~1, IV, 5.9, it therefore suffices to show that
the morphism \(u^{-1}(G_{\mathrm{red}})\to G_{\mathrm{red}}\) induced by \(u\) is flat;
this is true because \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is reduced (3.3.1).\footnote{Translator's note: no 3.3.1 occurs in this Exposé. The step may be
justified as follows. \(H=u^{-1}(G_{\mathrm{red}})\) is a closed subgroup scheme of \(F\) with the same
underlying space; since \(u^0\) is dominant, \(u\) maps the generic point of \(F^0\) to that of \(G^0\),
where the local ring of \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is a field, so the homomorphism \(H\to G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is
flat at that point, hence everywhere, by the homogeneity of flatness ((b) of the first Addition after
2.4.1; compare Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, 1.3). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, proves (ii)~\(\Rightarrow\)~(i) instead by the local flatness criterion at
the generic point of \(F^0\).}
