
\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-7.8}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 7.8.---}
\emph{Let \(G\) be a \(k\)-group locally of finite type, and
let \(A\) and \(B\) be two geometrically reduced
\textup{(}that is, smooth\textup{)} \(k\)-group subschemes of
\(G\). Suppose that one of the following conditions holds:}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, justifies ``that is, smooth'' as in our note to
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-7.1-i]{7.1~(i)}: a geometrically reduced group scheme locally of finite type
over \(k\) is smooth at the generic points of its irreducible components, hence smooth
(\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-1.3]{1.3}).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{a)}]\phantomsection\label{cond:sga3-VIB-7.8-a}
  \emph{\(A\) and \(B\) are invariant and of finite type over
  \(k\);}
\item[\textup{b)}]\phantomsection\label{cond:sga3-VIB-7.8-b}
  \emph{\(A\) is connected and \(B\) is of finite type over
  \(k\).}
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Then \((A,B)\) is of finite type over \(k\), and
represents the sheaf associated, for the \((\mathrm{fppf})\)
topology (or the \((\mathrm{fpqc})\) topology), with the group
presheaf of commutators of \(A\) and \(B\) in \(G\).
Moreover,}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, adds here
to the statement that the \(k\)-groups \((A,B^0)\) and \((A^0,B)\) are connected, which the proof below
establishes.}
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-7.8-identity-component}%
\[
  (A,B)^0=(A,B^0)\cdot(A^0,B).
\]

\emph{Proof.}
By \hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-7.6]{7.6}, to show that \((A,B)\)
is the desired associated sheaf, it is enough to show that
there exists an integer \(n\) such that
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-7.8-finite-generation}%
\[
  \nu^n\bigl((A\times_kB)^n\bigr)=(A,B)
\]
\textup{(}notation of
\hyperref[def:sga3-VIB-7.2-vii]{7.2\textup{(vii)}}\textup{)}. To
prove this, as well as the other two assertions, we may suppose
that \(k\) is algebraically closed
(\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-7.1-iii]{7.1\textup{(iii)}},
EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.6.1 and 2.7.1, and
\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIA-2.1.1]{VI\(_A\), 2.1.1}).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, details this reduction. Let \(\overline k\) be an algebraic closure of \(k\).
By 7.1~(iii), the formation of \((A,B)\), \((A,B^0)\) and \((A^0,B)\) commutes with the extension
\(k\to\overline k\), and so does that of identity components (VI\textsubscript{A}, 2.1.1). Being of finite type
descends from \(\overline k\) to \(k\) (EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.7.1), and a \(k\)-morphism is surjective as soon as it
is so after extension to \(\overline k\); this applies to \(\nu^n\colon(A\times_kB)^n\to(A,B)\) and to the
product morphism \((A,B^0)\times_k(A^0,B)\to(A,B)^0\).}

Let then \(B^0,\ldots,B^p\) be the connected components of
\(B\), and \(A^0,\ldots,A^q\) those of \(A\) (these components
are finite in number, since \(A\) and \(B\) are supposed of
finite type over \(k\), hence Noetherian). Let
\(\nu_{ij}\) be the restriction of \(\nu\) to
\[
  A^i\times_kB^j.
\]
Each \(A^i\) and \(B^j\) is irreducible, by
\hyperref[cor:sga3-VIA-2.4.1]{VI\(_A\), 2.4.1}; hence so are
\(A^0\times_kB^j\) and \(A^i\times_kB^0\). Since the unit
element of \(G\) belongs to \(A^0\) and to \(B^0\), it belongs
to
\[
  \nu_{0j}(A^0\times_kB^j)
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \nu_{i0}(A^i\times_kB^0).
\]
Thus every \(\Gamma_G(\nu_{0j})\) and every
\(\Gamma_G(\nu_{i0})\) is connected
(\hyperref[rem:sga3-VIB-7.2-vi]{7.2\textup{(vi)}}). Likewise, if
\(u_{0j}\), respectively \(u_{i0}\), denotes the inclusion
of \(\Gamma_G(\nu_{0j})\), respectively
\(\Gamma_G(\nu_{i0})\), in \(G\), then
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-7.8-generated-connected-groups}%
\[
  (A^0,B)=\Gamma_G\bigl((u_{0j})_{j=0}^{p}\bigr),
  \qquad
  (A,B^0)=\Gamma_G\bigl((u_{i0})_{i=0}^{q}\bigr),
\]
and these groups are connected. Moreover, it follows from
\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-7.4]{7.4} and the preceding
constructions that there is an integer \(r\) such that both
\((A^0,B)\) and \((A,B^0)\) are contained in
\[
  \nu^r\bigl((A\times_kB)^r\bigr).
\]
In case \textup{b)}, one has \((A,B)=(A^0,B)\), and the proof
is complete.

We now consider case \textup{a)}. Let \(H\) be the group
subscheme of \(G\) generated by the union of \((A,B^0)\) and
\((A^0,B)\); again by \hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-7.4]{7.4}, \(H\)
is connected, and there exists an integer \(m\) such that
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-7.8-H-contained}%
\[
  \nu^m\bigl((A\times_kB)^m\bigr)\supset H.
\]
Hence the underlying set of \(H\) is none other than
\((A,B^0)\cdot(A^0,B)\).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text gives no argument for this; the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, derives it from its Corollary 7.1.1 (see the Addition after the
proof of 7.1). In case a), \(A^0\) and \(B^0\) are characteristic subgroups of \(A\) and \(B\)
(Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, \hyperref[thm:sga3-VIA-2.6.5]{second Addition after 2.4.1, (e)}), hence invariant in
\(G\), so \((A,B^0)\) and \((A^0,B)\) are invariant in \(G\) by
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-7.3-v]{7.3~(v)}. They are smooth (7.1~(i)) and connected, hence of finite type
(VI\textsubscript{A}, \hyperref[prop:sga3-VIA-2.4]{2.4}), and \((A^0,B)\) normalizes \((A,B^0)\); by that
Addition, the set \((A,B^0)\cdot(A^0,B)\) is closed and underlies the subgroup scheme generated by
\((A,B^0)\) and \((A^0,B)\), which is \(H\).}

If \(T\) is a subset of \(G\) stable under the group law
(cf.\ \(3.0\)), write \(T_0\) for the group of closed points of
\(G\) belonging to \(T\). Put
\[
  \Gamma'
    =\bigcup_{q\geq1}\nu^q\bigl((A\times_kB)^q\bigr).
\]
By \hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-7.9]{Proposition 7.9} below,
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-7.8-rational-commutators}%
\[
  (A^0,B)_0=(A_0^0,B_0),
  \qquad
  (A,B^0)_0=(A_0,B_0^0),
  \qquad
  \Gamma'_0=(A_0,B_0).
\]
Therefore
\[
  H_0=(A_0^0,B_0)\cdot(A_0,B_0^0)
\]
has finite index in \(\Gamma'_0\) (Bible, Expos\'e~III,
Appendix), since \(A_0\) and \(B_0\) are invariant and
\(A_0^0\), respectively \(B_0^0\), has finite index in
\(A_0\), respectively \(B_0\).

We are then in the situation of
\hyperref[lem:sga3-VIB-7.7]{Lemma 7.7}: since
\[
  \nu^m\bigl((A\times_kB)^m\bigr)\supset H,
\]
there exists an integer \(p\) such that
\[
  \nu^{mp}\bigl((A\times_kB)^{mp}\bigr)=(A,B),
\]
and there exists a finite sequence \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) of closed
points of \(G\) such that
\[
  (A,B)=a_1H\cup\cdots\cup a_nH.
\]
Since \(H\) is of finite type over \(k\), every \(a_iH\) is
quasi-compact. Their union \((A,B)\) is therefore
quasi-compact, hence of finite type over \(k\). Finally, \(H\)
is irreducible, so each \(a_iH\) is irreducible; and because
the unit \(e\) belongs to \(H\), it follows that
\[
  H=(A,B)^0.
\]
