\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-1.4}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1.4.---}
\emph{Let \(A\) be an Artinian local ring, let \(G\) and \(H\) be two
\(A\)-groups locally of finite type, and let \(u:G\to H\) be a
quasi-compact morphism of \(A\)-groups. The following assertions are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{prop:sga3-VIB-1.4:item:i}(i)}] \(u\) is proper;
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{prop:sga3-VIB-1.4:item:ii}(ii)}] there exists \(h\in H\) such that the fiber
  \(u^{-1}(h)\) is nonempty and proper over \(\kappa(h)\);
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{prop:sga3-VIB-1.4:item:iii}(iii)}] \(\ker u\) is proper.
\end{enumerate}}

It is clear that \textup{\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-1.4:item:i}{(i)}} implies \textup{\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-1.4:item:iii}{(iii)}}, and that
\textup{\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-1.4:item:iii}{(iii)}} implies \textup{\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-1.4:item:ii}{(ii)}}. On the other hand, it follows
from the hypotheses that \(u\) is of finite type and separated
(EGA~I, 5.5.1) (since \(G\) is separated (Expos\'e~VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{sga3:VIA:0.3}{0.3})). It
therefore remains to prove that assertion \textup{(ii)} implies that \(u\)
is universally closed, so that we may suppose that \(A\) is equal to its
residue field \(k\), and even that \(k\) is algebraically closed and that
\(h\in H(k)\) (EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.6.4).\footnote{Translator's note: to make \(h\) rational, one
replaces \(k\) by an algebraic closure \(k'\) of \(\kappa(h)\). The point \(h\) defines a point \(h'\) of
\(H'=H\otimes_kk'\), rational over \(k'\), and the fiber of \(u'=u\otimes_kk'\) at \(h'\) is
\(u^{-1}(h)\otimes_{\kappa(h)}k'\), which is nonempty and proper; and \(u\) is universally closed if
\(u'\) is, by EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.6.4. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, spells
this out.} We have seen (\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-1.2]{1.2}) that \(u(G)\)
is then the underlying set of a closed reduced group subscheme of \(H\);
since every closed immersion is proper (EGA~II, 5.4.2), we may suppose
that \(u\) is surjective and that \(H\) is reduced. Since \(u\) is
surjective, the group \(G(k)\) acts transitively on the set of closed
points of \(H\); for every closed point \(y\) of \(H\), \(u^{-1}(y)\) is
therefore proper over \(\kappa(y)\). By EGA~IV\(_3\), 9.6.1, the set of
\(y\in H\) such that \(u^{-1}(y)\) is not proper over \(\kappa(y)\) is
locally constructible; since it contains no closed point, it is empty
(EGA~IV\(_3\), 10.1.2). Now consider the generic point of \(H^0\). By
EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.10.5, used according to the method of 8.1.2~a),\footnote{Translator's note: the
1970 text has ``la méthode 8.1.2~b)''; the method meant is that of EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.1.2~a), where the
local ring \(\mathcal O_{H,\eta}\) at the generic point \(\eta\) of \(H^0\) is the inductive limit of the
rings of the affine open neighborhoods of \(\eta\); the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, corrects it. The fiber of \(u\) at \(\eta\) is proper by what precedes, and
since \(H\) is reduced, \(\mathcal O_{H,\eta}=\kappa(\eta)\), so that 8.10.5~(xii) applies, \(u\) being of
finite presentation.} there is a
nonempty open subset \(V\) of \(H^0\) such that the restriction of \(u\)
over the open set \(V\) is proper. It is then clear that the
\(g\cdot V\) \((g\in G(k))\) form an open covering of \(H\) such that, for
every \(g\in G(k)\), the restriction of \(u\) over the open set
\(g\cdot V\) is proper; we conclude that \(u\) is proper
(EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.4.3~(vii)).

\phantomsection\label{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Corollary 1.4.1.---}
\emph{Let \(A\) be an Artinian local ring, let \(G\) and \(H\) be two
\(A\)-groups locally of finite type, and let \(u:G\to H\) be a morphism
of \(A\)-groups. The following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] \(u\) is locally quasi-finite;
\item[\textup{(ii)}] \(u\) is quasi-finite at some point;
\item[\textup{(iii)}] \(\ker u\) is discrete;
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.1:item:iv}(iv)}] the restriction of \(u\) to each connected
  component of \(G\) is finite.
\end{enumerate}
If \(u\) is quasi-compact, these assertions are also equivalent to
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(v)}] \(u\) is finite.
\end{enumerate}}

It is clear that \textup{(iv)} implies \textup{(iii)}, that
\textup{(iii)} implies \textup{(ii)} (EGA~I, 6.4.4), and that,
when \(u\) is quasi-compact, \textup{(iv)} and \textup{(v)} are
equivalent. We have already seen that assertions \textup{(i)} and
\textup{(ii)} are equivalent (\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-1.3]{1.3}).

Let us finally prove that \textup{(i)} implies \textup{\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.1:item:iv}{(iv)}}. Let
\(C\) be a connected component of \(G\). Since \(C\) is of finite
type over \(A\) (Expos\'e~VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIA-2.4.1}{2.4.1}) and \(H\) is
separated (Expos\'e~VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{sga3:VIA:0.3}{0.3}),%
\begingroup
\renewcommand{\thefootnote}{\fnsymbol{footnote}}%
\footnote[1]{\label{note:sga3-VIB-1.4.1-separated-xref}Translator's
note: the French source cites Expos\'e~VI\(_A\), 0.2 here; the
separation statement is \SGARefLink{sga3:VIA:0.3}{0.3}.}%
\endgroup{}
the restriction \(u'\) of \(u\) to \(C\) is of finite type and
separated, since \(C\) is separated over \(A\) (EGA~I, 5.5.1). It is
therefore enough to prove that \(u'\) is universally closed; we may
therefore suppose that \(A\) is equal to its residue field \(k\), and
even that \(k\) is algebraically closed (EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.6.4), since
\(C\otimes_A\bar k\), where \(\bar k\) denotes the algebraic closure of
\(k\), is the sum of a finite number of connected components of
\(G\otimes_A\bar k\). Let then \(g\) be a closed point of \(C\); if
\(u^0:G^0\to H\) is the restriction of \(u\) to \(G^0\), we have
\[
  u'=r_{u(g)}\circ u^0\circ r_{g^{-1}},
\]
where \(r_g\) denotes right translation by \(g\),\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has
\(u'=r_{u(g^{-1})}\circ u^0\circ r_g\); since \(r_{g^{-1}}\) maps \(C\) onto \(G^0\), the formula above,
which the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, gives, is the correct one.} and to prove that
\(u'\) is proper it is enough to prove that \(u^0\) is proper. By
hypothesis \(u\) is locally quasi-finite, so the fiber \(\ker u\) is
discrete (and nonempty); we have seen that \(u^0\) is of finite type, so
the fiber \(\ker u^0\) is finite (EGA~II, 6.2.2), hence proper, and
nonempty; therefore \(u^0\) is proper (\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-1.4]{1.4}), hence finite
(EGA~III\(_1\), 4.4.2) since it is quasi-finite.

\phantomsection\label{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2}%
\noindent\textbf{Corollary 1.4.2.---}
\emph{Let \(A\) be an Artinian local ring, let \(G\) and \(H\) be two
\(A\)-groups locally of finite type, and let \(u:G\to H\) be a
quasi-compact morphism of \(A\)-groups. The following assertions are
equivalent:%
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2:item:i}(i)}] \(u\) is a closed immersion;
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2:item:ii}(ii)}] \(u\) is a monomorphism;\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, observes that over a field \(k\) the hypothesis that \(G\) and \(H\) be
locally of finite type can be dropped in the equivalence of (i) and (ii): every quasi-compact
monomorphism of \(k\)-group schemes is a closed immersion (D.~Perrin, \emph{Approximation des
schémas en groupes, quasi-compacts sur un corps}, Bull.\ Soc.\ Math.\ France \textbf{104} (1976),
323--335, 4.2.4); for affine \(G\) and \(H\), where every morphism \(G\to H\) is affine, hence
quasi-compact, see also M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel, \emph{Groupes algébriques}~I, Masson and
North-Holland, 1970, III, \S3, 7.2~(b).}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2:item:iii}(iii)}] \(\ker u\) is isomorphic to the unit
  \(A\)-group.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, every group subscheme
of \(H\) is closed.}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, adds that this last assertion holds for every \(A\)-group \(H\), without
finiteness hypotheses; see the Addition after \SGARefLink{sga3:VIA:0.5.2}{0.5} in Expos\'e~VI\(_A\), assertion~(c).}

\begingroup
\renewcommand{\thefootnote}{\fnsymbol{footnote}}%
\footnote[1]{\label{note:sga3-VIB-1.4.2-unit-base}Translator's note:
the French source calls the trivial kernel the ``unit \(k\)-group''
both in the statement and in the proof. Here the base is the
Artinian local ring \(A\), and \(\ker u\) is an \(A\)-group; the
unit object required by the monomorphism criterion is therefore
\(\operatorname{Spec}A\), the unit \(A\)-group.}%
\endgroup{}
It is clear that \textup{\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2:item:i}{(i)}} implies \textup{\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2:item:ii}{(ii)}}. Considering
the functors represented by \(G\) and \(H\), one sees immediately
that \textup{\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2:item:ii}{(ii)}} and \textup{\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2:item:iii}{(iii)}} are equivalent. Finally, if
\(\ker u\) is the unit \(A\)-group, \(\ker u\) is a proper nonempty fiber,
so (\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-1.4]{1.4}) \(u\) is a proper monomorphism, and of finite presentation since
\(H\) is locally Noetherian (EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.6.1),\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has
``EGA~IV 6.1''; the statement used, that a morphism of finite type to a locally noetherian scheme is of
finite presentation, is EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.6.1, as in the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011).} hence is a closed
immersion (EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.11.5).

The final assertion follows because \(H\) is locally Noetherian,
so every immersion \(G\to H\) is quasi-compact (EGA~I, 6.6.4).
