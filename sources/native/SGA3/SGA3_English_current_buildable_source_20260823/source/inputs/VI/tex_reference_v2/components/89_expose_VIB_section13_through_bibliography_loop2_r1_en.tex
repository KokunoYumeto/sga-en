
\par\bigskip\noindent\phantomsection\label{sec:sga3-VIB-13}%
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B},
\S\,13, ``Groupes affines plats sur une base r\'eguli\`ere de dimension \(\leq2\)''; our
formulation). The re-edition shows that the usual proof that an affine algebraic group over a
field is linear, and Lemma~\SGARefLink{lem:sga3-VIB-11.12}{11.12}, extend to affine flat groups
over a noetherian regular base of dimension \(\leq2\); in dimension \(\leq1\) this gives the
last assertion of Remark~\SGARefLink{rem:sga3-VIB-11.11.1}{11.11.1}. We give its content in our
own terms; the numbers in brackets are those of the re-edition.

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(a) A lemma of O.~Gabber} [13.1]. \emph{Let \(S\) be a normal
noetherian scheme, \(\mathcal A\) a flat quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_S\)-module,
\(\mathcal F\subset\mathcal A\) a coherent submodule, \(U\) the set of points at which
\(\mathcal F\) is flat and \(j:U\to S\) the inclusion. Then \(U\) is open and contains the
points of codimension \(\leq1\), \(\mathcal F^{**}=j_*j^*\mathcal F\), the canonical morphism
\(j_*(\mathcal E)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal A\to
j_*(\mathcal E\otimes_{\mathcal O_U}j^*\mathcal A)\) is an isomorphism for every
quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_U\)-module \(\mathcal E\), and \(\mathcal V=j_*j^*\mathcal F\) is a
submodule of \(\mathcal A=j_*j^*\mathcal A\), with
\(\mathcal V\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal A=j_*j^*(\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal A)\).}
A coherent module is flat exactly where it is locally free, so \(U\) is open. The stalks of
\(\mathcal F\), submodules of the flat, hence torsion-free, stalks of \(\mathcal A\), are
torsion-free, hence free at the points of codimension \(\leq1\), where the local rings are
fields or discrete valuation rings. So \(S-U\) has codimension \(\geq2\) in the normal scheme
\(S\); hence the reflexive module \(\mathcal F^{**}\), which agrees with \(\mathcal F\) on
\(U\), is \(j_*j^*\mathcal F\), and \(\mathcal O_S=j_*j^*\mathcal O_S\)
(EGA~IV\(_2\), 5.10.5). The morphism in question is an isomorphism when \(\mathcal A\) is free
of finite rank, hence when \(\mathcal A\) is flat, which is locally a filtered inductive limit
of such modules (D.~Lazard's theorem), both sides commuting with these limits, \(j\) being
quasi-compact and quasi-separated. For \(\mathcal E=\mathcal O_U\) it gives
\(\mathcal A=j_*j^*\mathcal A\), so that \(\mathcal V\subset\mathcal A\); for
\(\mathcal E=j^*\mathcal F\), the last isomorphism.

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(b) Linearity} [13.2, 13.3]. If \(R\) is a noetherian regular
ring of dimension \(\leq2\), the dual \(N^*\) of a finitely generated \(R\)-module \(N\) is
projective: for a presentation \(L_1\to L_0\to N\to0\) by free modules of finite rank, \(N^*\)
is the kernel of \(L_0^*\to L_1^*\), and \(R\) has global dimension \(\leq2\). So a reflexive
finitely generated module, being the dual of its dual, is projective, and in (a), when \(S\)
is regular of dimension \(\leq2\), \(\mathcal V\) is locally free of finite rank. \emph{Let
\(S\) be a noetherian regular scheme of dimension \(\leq2\) and \(G\) an affine flat
\(S\)-group, with algebra \(\mathcal A=\mathcal A(G)\), comultiplication \(\Delta\) and counit
\(\varepsilon\). \textup{(i)} If \(G\) is of finite type, it is isomorphic to a closed subgroup
of \(\operatorname{GL}(\mathcal V)\) for a locally free \(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(\mathcal V\)
of finite rank, and to a closed subgroup of \(\operatorname{GL}_{d,S}\) for some \(d\) if \(S\)
is affine. \textup{(ii)} \(\mathcal A\) is the filtered union of sub-Hopf algebras of finite
type.} First, every coherent submodule \(\mathcal M\) of \(\mathcal A\) lies in a coherent
\(G\)-stable one: choose a coherent \(\mathcal M'\supset\mathcal M\) with
\(\Delta(\mathcal M)\subset\mathcal M'\otimes\mathcal A\) (\(\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal A\) is the
union of the \(\mathcal M''\otimes\mathcal A\), \(\mathcal M''\) coherent; EGA~I, 9.4.9), and
put \(\mathcal F=\Delta^{-1}(\mathcal M'\otimes\mathcal A)\); then
\(\mathcal M\subset\mathcal F\subset\mathcal M'\), as \(x=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varepsilon)\Delta(x)\),
and \(\Delta(\mathcal F)\subset\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal A\) by the coassociativity of \(\Delta\)
and the flatness of \(\mathcal A\) (compare the re-edition's
\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-11.10.bis}{11.10.bis}, after R.~W.~Thomason). Now let
\(\mathcal B\subset\mathcal A\) be a subalgebra of finite type, generated by a coherent
submodule \(\mathcal M\), and \(\mathcal F\supset\mathcal M\) a coherent \(G\)-stable
submodule. By (a), with its notation, \(\mathcal V=j_*j^*\mathcal F\) is a locally free
submodule of \(\mathcal A\) containing \(\mathcal F\), and
\(\Delta(\mathcal V)\subset j_*j^*(\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal A)=\mathcal V\otimes\mathcal A\):
\(\mathcal V\) is \(G\)-stable. Let \(\mathcal A_{\mathcal V}\) be the image of the morphism of
Hopf algebras \(\mathcal A(\operatorname{GL}(\mathcal V))\to\mathcal A\) defined by the action of
\(G\) on \(\mathcal V\). If \((v_i)\) is a local basis of \(\mathcal V\) and
\(\Delta(v_j)=\sum_iv_i\otimes a_{ij}\), the \(a_{ij}\) are the images of the matrix
coefficients, and \(v_j=\sum_i\varepsilon(v_i)a_{ij}\); so
\(\mathcal B\subset\mathcal A_{\mathcal V}\). If \(G\) is of finite type, take
\(\mathcal B=\mathcal A\): \(G\to\operatorname{GL}(\mathcal V)\) is a closed immersion; if
moreover \(S\) is affine, \(\mathcal V\oplus\mathcal V'\simeq\mathcal O_S^d\) for some locally
free \(\mathcal V'\), on which \(G\) is made to act trivially, and \(G\to\operatorname{GL}_{d,S}\)
is a closed immersion. In general, \(\mathcal A\) is the filtered union of the sub-Hopf algebras
of finite type \(\mathcal A_{\mathcal V}\). The re-edition states that these may moreover be
taken flat; when \(S\) has dimension \(\leq1\) this is clear, \(\mathcal A_{\mathcal V}\) being
torsion-free, but in dimension \(2\) the argument does not show it. Example [13.3]: if \(R\) is
a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer \(\pi\) and fraction field \(K\), the projective
limit \(G\) of the system
\(\cdots\to\mathbb G_{a,R}\xrightarrow{\ \pi\ }\mathbb G_{a,R}\xrightarrow{\ \pi\ }\mathbb G_{a,R}\)
is a flat affine \(R\)-group, not of finite type: \(\mathcal A(G)=R[X/\pi^n:n\geq0]\) is the
ring of the polynomials of \(K[X]\) with constant term in \(R\), \(G_K=\mathbb G_{a,K}\), the
special fibre is trivial, and \(G(B)\) is the set of sequences \((x_n)_{n\geq0}\) of elements
of \(B\) with \(x_n=\pi x_{n+1}\) for all \(n\) (the re-edition also uses this example in its
Remark~\SGARefLink{rem:sga3-VIB-11.10.1}{11.10.1}).

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(c) Equivariant resolution} [13.4--13.6]. Let \(S\) be
noetherian and \(G\) an affine flat \(S\)-group. Following R.~W.~Thomason (\emph{Equivariant
resolution, linearization, and Hilbert's fourteenth problem over arbitrary base schemes},
Adv.\ Math.\ \textbf{65} (1987), 16--34, 2.1), \((G,S)\) has the \emph{equivariant resolution
property} \textup{(RE)} if every coherent \(G\)-\(\mathcal O_S\)-module is a \(G\)-equivariant
quotient of a locally free \(G\)-\(\mathcal O_S\)-module of finite rank. (Without a group, this
resolution property holds for separated noetherian regular schemes, SGA~6, II, 2.1.1 and
2.2.7, and for noetherian regular schemes of dimension \(\leq1\), but not for the affine plane
with a doubled origin, loc.\ cit., 2.2.7.2.) \emph{If \(G\) is of finite type and \((G,S)\)
satisfies \textup{(RE)}, \(G\) is isomorphic to a closed subgroup of
\(\operatorname{GL}(\mathcal E)\) for a locally free \(\mathcal E\) of finite rank, and to one
of \(\operatorname{GL}_{d,S}\) if \(S\) is affine.} The re-edition gives a proof by O.~Gabber,
simpler than Thomason's (loc.\ cit., 3.1), which uses \(G\) essentially free over \(S\). In our
words: let \(\mathcal F\) be a coherent \(G\)-stable submodule of \(\mathcal A(G)\) generating it
as an algebra (as in (b)), and \(p:\mathcal E\to\mathcal F\) an equivariant epimorphism from a
locally free \(\mathcal E\) of finite rank. For a local basis \((v_i)\) of \(\mathcal E\), with
coaction \(v_j\mapsto\sum_iv_i\otimes a_{ij}\), equivariance gives
\(\Delta(p(v_j))=\sum_ip(v_i)\otimes a_{ij}\), hence
\(p(v_j)=\sum_i\varepsilon(p(v_i))a_{ij}\): the image of
\(\mathcal A(\operatorname{GL}(\mathcal E))\to\mathcal A(G)\) contains \(\mathcal F\), so it is
\(\mathcal A(G)\), and \(G\to\operatorname{GL}(\mathcal E)\) is a closed immersion; for \(S\)
affine, one adds a complement as in (b). Thomason's argument: \(p\) gives a closed immersion
\(\tau\) of \(G\) into the vector bundle \(\mathbb V(\mathcal E)\), compatible with the right
translations of \(G\) and its action on \(\mathbb V(\mathcal E)\); the representation \(\rho\)
of \(G\) takes its values in the strict transporter \(N\) of \(\tau(G)\) into itself in
\(\operatorname{GL}(\mathcal E)\), a closed subgroup when \(G\) is essentially free over \(S\)
(6.2.4~(e) of the re-edition, in \SGARefLink{ex:sga3-VIB-6.2.4-e}{the Addition after 6.2}),
and \(h\mapsto\tau(1)h\) is a retraction of \(N\) onto \(\tau(G)\simeq G\); \(N\) being
separated, \(\rho\) is a closed immersion. The re-edition remarks that Thomason's proof seems to
need this hypothesis, which he does not state, but which holds for reductive \(G\)
(Expos\'e~XXII, \SGARefLink{sga3:xxii:cor:5.7.8}{5.7.8}), the case of his Corollary~3.2; and that
Thomason proves (loc.\ cit., 2.5) that \((G,S)\) satisfies \textup{(RE)} when \(S\) is
separated, noetherian, regular of dimension \(\leq2\) and \(G\) is affine of finite presentation
with \(\mathcal A(G)\) locally projective, which with the preceding result gives (b)~(i) under a
slightly stronger hypothesis.

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(d) Comodules over a regular ring of dimension \(\leq2\)}
[13.7, simplifying Thomason, loc.\ cit., 2.5]. \emph{Let \(R\) be a noetherian regular ring of
dimension \(\leq2\), \(C\) an \(R\)-coalgebra which is projective as an \(R\)-module, and \(F\)
a \(C\)-comodule of finite type over \(R\). Then \(F\) is a quotient of a \(C\)-comodule which
is projective of finite type over \(R\).} So, if \(S\) is affine, regular of dimension
\(\leq2\), and \(\mathcal A(G)\) is projective, \((G,S)\) satisfies \textup{(RE)}. We may
suppose \(R\) a domain, with fraction field \(K\). The coaction \(\rho:F\to F\otimes C\) is a
morphism of comodules for the coaction \(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta\) of \(F\otimes C\), split by
\(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varepsilon\). Choose a surjection \(q:W\to F\), \(W\) free of finite type,
and let \(W'\subset W\otimes C\) be the inverse image of \(\rho(F)\) under
\(q\otimes\mathrm{id}\): a subcomodule, mapped onto \(F\) by \(q\otimes\varepsilon\). By local
finiteness (\SGARefLink{lem:sga3-VIB-11.8}{11.8} and
Remark~\SGARefLink{rem:sga3-VIB-11.10.1}{11.10.1}), a subcomodule \(V'\) of \(W'\), finitely
generated over \(R\), still maps onto \(F\); it is torsion-free, as \(W\otimes C\) is.
Replacing \(F\) by \(V'\), we may suppose \(F\) torsion-free, and take \(W\), \(W'\), \(V'\) as
above for it. Let \(V\) be the saturation of \(V'\) in \(W\otimes C\), that is, the kernel of
\(W\otimes C\to E=(W\otimes C\otimes K)/(V'\otimes K)\): it is a subcomodule, and it is
contained in \(W'\), because \((F\otimes C)/\rho(F)\), a direct summand of the torsion-free
\(F\otimes C\), is torsion-free; so \(V\) maps onto \(F\). With \(Q=(W\otimes C)/V\), which
embeds in the \(K\)-vector space \(E\), one has
\(\operatorname{Tor}^R_1(V,N)\simeq\operatorname{Tor}^R_2(Q,N)\simeq\operatorname{Tor}^R_3(E/Q,N)=0\)
for every \(R\)-module \(N\): \(V\) is flat. And \(V\) is finitely generated: \(V/V'\) is the
torsion submodule of \((W\otimes C)/V'\), which is that of \(L'/V'\) for a free direct summand
\(L'\) of finite rank, containing \(V'\), of a free module \(L=(W\otimes C)\oplus P\). So \(V\)
is projective of finite type.
