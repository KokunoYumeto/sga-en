\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
\emph{Complements and counterexamples}, due to O.~Gabber (the re-edition's 5.8).
(a) Corollary 5.4~(i) remains true when the fibres of \(X\) are assumed quasi-separated and connected (instead of
locally of finite type and connected). Such a fibre \(X_s\) is irreducible by the re-edition's VI\(_A\),
\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-2.6.6}{2.6.6} (in this edition (f) of the second Addition after VI\(_A\), 2.4.1), so
that every affine open subset of \(X\) meeting \(X_s\) satisfies the condition (i\('\)) of the Addition after the
proof of 5.6.2. Quasi-compactness then follows as in 5.4~(ii) when an open subset \(V\) as there exists; the
re-edition states it without that condition, which the example after the proof of 5.4 shows to be needed.
(b) Without a finiteness condition on the fibres, 5.3 and 5.4 fail, even over a field \(k\). If every point of a
topological space \(Z\) has an open neighbourhood homeomorphic to a Boolean space (compact, Hausdorff, totally
disconnected), then \(Z\), with the sheaf of locally constant \(k\)-valued functions, is a \(k\)-scheme: a Boolean
space \(B\) is the spectrum of the ring of locally constant functions \(B\to k\). The local rings of such schemes
are equal to \(k\), the \(k\)-morphisms between them are the continuous maps, the product of two of them over
\(k\) has the product space as underlying space, so that such a scheme is separated over \(k\) if and only if it is
Hausdorff, and a discrete group acting on \(Z\) by homeomorphisms is a \(k\)-group locally of finite type acting on
the scheme \(Z\), with \(\Phi\) surjective if and only if the action is transitive. Let \(C=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb
Z)^{\mathbb N}\), the Cantor space, a compact Hausdorff totally disconnected group. First example: for each
\(p\in C\), glue to \(C\) a copy of \(C\) along \(C-\{p\}\), and call \(p'\) the new point. In the resulting space
\(X\), the neighbourhoods of \(p'\) are the sets \(\{p'\}\cup(N-\{p\})\), \(N\) a neighbourhood of \(p\) in
\(C\), so \(C\) is a dense open subset, Hausdorff, but \(p\) and \(p'\) cannot be separated. The group of
homeomorphisms of \(X\) acts transitively on \(X\): a homeomorphism \(\varphi\) of \(C\) extends to \(X\) by
\(\varphi(p')=\varphi(p)'\), the translations of \(C\) act transitively on \(C\), and the exchange of \(p\) and
\(p'\), fixing the other points, is a homeomorphism. This contradicts 5.3 without its hypothesis~(ii). But \(X\)
is not connected; here is a connected example. For \(i\in\mathbb N\), the sets
\(C_i=\{u\mid u_j=0\ \text{for}\ j<i,\ u_i=1\}\) are open and closed, homeomorphic to \(C\), and their disjoint
union is \(C-\{0\}\); it follows, using translations, that \(C-F\) is homeomorphic to \(C-\{0\}\) for every
two-element subset \(F\) of \(C\). For each such \(F=\{x,y\}\), glue to \(C\) a copy \(C(F)\) of \(C\), with base
point \(q_F=0\), by a homeomorphism \(C(F)-\{q_F\}\to C-F\), and let \(X'\) be the resulting space. If \(N\) is
an open neighbourhood of \(q_F\) in \(C(F)\), the image of \(N-\{q_F\}\) is the complement in \(C-F\) of a compact
set, the image of \(C(F)-N\), so it contains points arbitrarily near \(x\) and \(y\): a locally constant function
on \(X'\) takes the same value at \(x\), \(y\) and \(q_F\). Hence \(X'\) is connected, and not separated; each of
its points has an open neighbourhood homeomorphic to \(C\). Let \(T\) be the group of translations of \(C\), \(L\)
the free group on symbols \(\lambda_F\), and \(\Gamma=T*L\) their free product; choose homeomorphisms
\(h_F\colon C\to C(F)\) with \(h_F(0)=q_F\). Let \(E\) be the set of partial homeomorphisms of \(X'\) formed by
the translations, the \(h_F\) and their inverses, and \(\sigma\colon E\to\Gamma\) the map with \(\sigma(t)=t\),
\(\sigma(h_F^{\pm1})=\lambda_F^{\pm1}\). On \(\Gamma\times X'\), \(\Gamma\) discrete, let \(\sim\) be the
equivalence relation generated by \((\gamma\sigma(e),x)\sim(\gamma,e(x))\), for \(e\in E\) and \(x\) in the source
of \(e\), let \(Z\) be the quotient space and \(\pi\colon\Gamma\times X'\to Z\) the projection; \(\Gamma\) acts on
\(Z\) by left translations. The relation is generated by homeomorphisms between open subsets, so \(\pi\) is open.
The class of \((1,x)\) consists of the points \((\sigma(e_1)\cdots\sigma(e_n),e_n^{-1}\cdots e_1^{-1}(x))\); if
\(\sigma(e_1)\cdots\sigma(e_n)=1\) with \(n>0\), the normal form in the free product shows that the word can be
shortened by merging two consecutive translations, dropping the identity of \(C\), or cancelling a pair
\(h_F^{\pm1},h_F^{\mp1}\), which does not change the composite map; so \(\pi\) is injective on \(\{1\}\times X'\),
hence on each \(\{\gamma\}\times X'\), and maps it homeomorphically onto an open subset of \(Z\). Thus \(Z\) is
again a \(k\)-scheme of the above kind, not separated; it is connected, \(\pi(\{\gamma\}\times X')\) meeting
\(\pi(\{\gamma\sigma(e)\}\times X')\) and \(\sigma(E)\) generating \(\Gamma\); and \(\Gamma\) acts transitively,
since \((1,x)\sim(\sigma(t_x),0)\) for \(x\in C\), \(t_x\) the translation by \(x\), and
\((1,q_F)\sim(\sigma(h_F),0)\). This contradicts 5.4 without the finiteness condition on the fibres.
