
\section*{5. Separation of groups and homogeneous spaces}
\phantomsection\label{sec:sga3-VIB-5}%
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{5. Separation of groups and homogeneous spaces}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-5.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 5.1.---}
\emph{For an \(S\)-group \(G\) to be separated, it is necessary
and sufficient that the identity section of \(G\) be a closed
immersion.}

The condition is necessary (EGA~I, 5.4.6); it is sufficient by
virtue of the following cartesian diagram (VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{sga3:VIA:0.3}{0.3}):
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection\label{diag:sga3-VIB-5.1-cartesian}%
  \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=large,column sep=huge]
    G\times_S G
      \arrow[r,"{\mu\circ(\operatorname{id}_G\times c)}"] &
    G \\
    G
      \arrow[u,"\Delta_{G/S}"]
      \arrow[r,"\pi"] &
    S
      \arrow[u,"\varepsilon"']
  \end{tikzcd}\,.
\end{center}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-5.2}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 5.2.---}
\emph{If \(S\) is discrete, every \(S\)-group is separated.}

Indeed, \(S\) is then equal to
\[
  \coprod_{s\in S}\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_{S,s},
\]
and it is enough to show that, for every \(s\in S\),
\[
  G\times_S\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_{S,s}
\]
is separated (EGA~I, 5.5.5); this follows from VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{sga3:VIA:0.3}{0.3}, since
\(\mathcal O_{S,s}\) is a local ring of dimension \(0\).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text refers
to VI\(_A\), 0.2, and calls \(\mathcal O_{S,s}\) an Artinian local ring; the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, refers to VI\(_A\), 0.3, where separation is proved, and writes ``of dimension
\(0\)''. As \(S\) is discrete, \(\{s\}\) is open, so \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_{S,s}\) is the open
subscheme \(\{s\}\) of \(S\) and \(\mathcal O_{S,s}\) has only one prime ideal; it need not be noetherian
(take \(S=\operatorname{Spec}k[T_1,T_2,\ldots]/(T_1,T_2,\ldots)^2\), \(k\) a field), hence need not be
Artinian. The argument of VI\(_A\), 0.3 only uses that the base has a single point (see the note there).}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-VIB-5.3}%
\noindent\textbf{Theorem 5.3.---}
\emph{Let \(S\) be a scheme, let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group scheme
locally of finite presentation over \(S\) and universally open over
\(S\) in a neighborhood of the identity section, and let \(X\) be
an \(S\)-scheme on which \(G\) acts in such a way that the morphism}
\[
  \Phi:G\times_S X\longrightarrow X\times_S X,
  \qquad (g,x)\longmapsto(gx,x),
\]
\emph{is surjective \textup{(}intuitively, \(X\) is a homogeneous space under \(G\)\textup{)}. Suppose
moreover that, for every \(s\in S\):}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] \emph{there exists an open subscheme \(U\) of
  \(X\), separated over \(S\), such that \(U_s\) is dense in
  \(X_s\);}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{thm:sga3-VIB-5.3:item:ii}(ii)}] \emph{the fiber \(X_s\) is locally of finite
  type over \(\kappa(s)\).}%
\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text assumes \(G\) universally open over \(S\) along the identity
section, and has no hypothesis~(ii); both changes are due to the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}. Without (ii) the theorem fails, even over a field (second Addition at the end
of this section). The proof of 5.6.2 uses a neighbourhood of the identity section on which \(G\) is universally
open, which openness along the section does not give: over a discrete valuation ring \(R\) with uniformizer
\(t\) and residue field \(k\), the closed subgroup \(G=\operatorname{Spec}R[u]/(tu)\) of \(\mathbb G_{a,R}\),
with trivial generic fibre and special fibre \(\mathbb G_{a,k}\), is universally open at the points of its
identity section (they are rational in their fibres, so after any base change the points above them lie on the
identity section), but not at the generic point of \(\mathbb G_{a,k}\), which lies in every neighbourhood of
the identity section (compare \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-4.2}{4.2}).}
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Then \(X\) is separated over \(S\).}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{cor:sga3-VIB-5.4}%
\noindent\textbf{Corollary 5.4.---}
\emph{Let \(S,G,X\) be as above.
Suppose moreover that \(X\) has fibers locally of finite type and
connected.\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 statement assumes only that the fibres of \(X\) are connected,
and asserts that \(X\) is separated and quasi-compact over \(S\); its proof, given below, concerns separation.
The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, adds the finiteness condition on the fibres,
without which the statement fails (second Addition at the end of this section), and the condition on \(V\) in
(ii), without which quasi-compactness fails (Addition after the proof).} Then:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{cor:sga3-VIB-5.4:item:i}(i)}] \emph{\(X\) is separated over \(S\).}
\item[\textup{(ii)}] \emph{If there exists an open subset \(V\) of
  \(X\), quasi-compact over \(S\), which meets every nonempty fiber
  \(X_s\), then \(X\) is quasi-compact over \(S\).}
\end{enumerate}

Indeed, since \(X_s\) is connected, \(X_s\) is also irreducible (if it is nonempty),\footnote{Translator's
note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, justifies this by its VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-2.5.4}{2.5.4} (in
this edition (d) of the first Addition after VI\(_A\), 2.4.1): if a group
locally of finite type over a field acts on a scheme \(Y\) locally of finite type in such a way that
\((g,y)\mapsto(gy,y)\) is surjective, the connected components of \(Y\) are irreducible. This applies to
\(G_s\) acting on \(X_s\), the morphism \(\Phi_s\) deduced from \(\Phi\) by base change being surjective.} so
that if \(U\) is an affine open subset of \(X\) such that \(U_s\) is nonempty, \(U_s\) is dense in \(X_s\), and
the theorem applies.

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
\emph{Proof of} (ii). The question being local on \(S\), let \(S\) be affine; then \(V\) is quasi-compact, and
by \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-3.5}{3.5} some quasi-compact open subset \(U\) of \(G\) contains every
\(\underline{G^0_s}\). If \(X_s\) is not empty, it is irreducible, as we have just seen, and \(V_s\) is a
nonempty open subset of it, so that \(\underline{G^0_s}\cdot V_s=X_s\) by the re-edition's
VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-2.6.4}{2.6.4} (in this edition (d) of the second Addition after
VI\(_A\), 2.4.1). Hence
\((g,x)\mapsto gx\) maps the quasi-compact scheme \(U\times_SV\) onto \(X\), and \(X\) is quasi-compact over the
affine scheme \(S\). The condition on \(V\) cannot be omitted. The following example is due to O.~Gabber: let
\(A\) be the ring \(k[T_1,T_2,\ldots]\), \(k\) a field, localized at the ideal \((T_1,T_2,\ldots)\), let
\(S=\operatorname{Spec}A\), with closed point \(s\), and \(S^*=S-\{s\}\), which is not quasi-compact (it is the
union of the \(D(T_i)\), and the prime ideal \((T_1,\ldots,T_n)\) lies in none of \(D(T_1),\ldots,D(T_n)\)). Let
\(X\) be the disjoint union of the open subscheme \(S^*\) and of the closed point \(\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s)\),
and let the trivial group \(G=S\) act on \(X\). Then \(X\times_SX=X\), \(\Phi\) is the identity, every fibre of
\(X\) is one rational point, and \(X\) is separated, but not quasi-compact, over \(S\).

The re-edition also points out that 5.3 is a reworked form of a theorem of M.~Raynaud, given in the 1965 edition
(SGAD) of this seminar, to which the footnotes to Expos\'e~X, \SGARefLink{sga3:x:lemma:8-5}{8.5} and
\SGARefLink{sga3:x:theorem:8-8}{8.8} refer: \emph{if \(G\) is locally of finite
presentation and universally open over \(S\), with connected fibres, every \(S\)-scheme \(X\) locally of finite
presentation over \(S\) on which \(G\) acts with \(\Phi\) surjective is separated over \(S\)}; for instance
\(X=G\). This follows from (i). Indeed, \(G_s\) is geometrically connected, having a rational point; so if \(F\)
is open and closed in \(X_s\), the image of \(G_s\times F\) under the action lies in \(F\), each
\(G_s\times C\), for \(C\) a connected component of \(F\), being connected (EGA~IV\(_2\), 4.5.8) and containing
\(\epsilon(s)\times C\). As \(\Phi_s\) is surjective, \(F\) or \(X_s-F\) is empty: the fibres of \(X\) are
connected.
