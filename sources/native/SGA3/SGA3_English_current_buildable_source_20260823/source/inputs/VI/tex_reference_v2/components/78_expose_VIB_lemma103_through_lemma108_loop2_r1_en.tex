
\medskip
\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-VIB-10.3}%
\noindent\textbf{10.3.---}
\emph{In the situation recalled at the beginning of
\hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-10.0]{10.0}, let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group of
finite presentation; then there are an index \(j\in I\), an
\(S_j\)-group \(G_j\) of finite presentation, and an
\(S\)-isomorphism of groups}
\[
  G\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }G_j\times_{S_j}S.
\]

Assertions \hyperref[lem:sga3-VIB-10.2]{10.2} and
\hyperref[lem:sga3-VIB-10.3]{10.3} are easy consequences of
\hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-10.0]{10.0} and
\hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-10.1]{10.1}, taking into account the
interpretation
of the structure of an \(S\)-group given in EGA~0\(_{\mathrm{III}}\),
8.2.5 and 8.2.6.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-VIB-10.4}%
\noindent\textbf{10.4.---}
\emph{Let \(j\in I\), let \(G_j\) and \(H_j\) be two
\(S_j\)-groups of finite presentation, and let
\(u_j:G_j\to H_j\) be a morphism of \(S_j\)-groups. For
\(u=u_j\times_{S_j}S\) to be a central monomorphism
\textup{(respectively, an invariant monomorphism)}, it is
necessary and sufficient that there exist \(i\in I\),
\(i\geq j\), such that}
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-10.4-base-change}%
\[
  u_i=u_j\times_{S_j}S_i
\]
\emph{have the same property.}

This follows immediately from
\hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-10.0]{10.0} and
\hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-10.1]{10.1}, in view of the
characterization in \hyperref[rem:sga3-VIB-6.7]{6.7} of
central and invariant group monomorphisms.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{cor:sga3-VIB-10.5}%
\noindent\textbf{Corollary 10.5.---}
\emph{Let \(j\in I\), and let \(G_j\) be an \(S_j\)-group of
finite presentation. For \(G=G_j\times_{S_j}S\) to be
commutative, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist
\(i\in I\), \(i\geq j\), such that
\(G_i=G_j\times_{S_j}S_i\) be commutative.}

Indeed, to say that an \(S\)-group is commutative is the same
as saying that it is central when regarded as a group
subscheme of itself.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-10.6}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 10.6.---}
\emph{In the situation recalled at the beginning of
\hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-10.0]{10.0}, let \(j\in I\), let \(G_j\)
be an \(S_j\)-group of finite presentation, and let \(G'_j\) be
a group subscheme of \(G_j\), flat and of finite presentation
over \(S_j\). For}
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-10.6-quotient-over-S}%
\[
  (G_j\times_{S_j}S)/(G'_j\times_{S_j}S)
\]
\emph{to be representable \textup{(for the (fpqc) topology)}, it
is necessary and sufficient that there exist \(i\in I\),
\(i\geq j\), such that}
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-10.6-quotient-over-Si}%
\[
  (G_j\times_{S_j}S_i)/(G'_j\times_{S_j}S_i)
\]
\emph{be representable.}

This is a consequence of the following more general lemma.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-VIB-10.7}%
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 10.7.---}
\emph{Let \(\mathcal T\) be the topology \textup{(fppf)} or
\textup{(fpqc)}; let \(j\in I\), let \(X_j\) be an
\(S_j\)-scheme of finite presentation, and let \(R_j\) be an
equivalence relation on \(X_j\) defined by an
\(S_j\)-monomorphism \(v_j:R_j\to X_j\times_{S_j}X_j\) such that
the morphism \(\operatorname{pr}_{1,j}\circ v_j:R_j\to X_j\),
deduced from \(v_j\) by composition with the first projection
\(X_j\times_{S_j}X_j\to X_j\), is flat and of finite
presentation. Then, for the quotient sheaf}
\[
  (X_j\times_{S_j}S)/(R_j\times_{S_j}S)
\]
\emph{for the topology \(\mathcal T\) to be representable, it is
necessary and sufficient that there exist \(i\in I\),
\(i\geq j\), such that the quotient sheaf}
\[
  (X_j\times_{S_j}S_i)/(R_j\times_{S_j}S_i)
\]
\emph{for the topology \(\mathcal T\) be representable.}

In view of the statements EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.8.2, 8.8.3,
8.10.5, and 11.2.6 recalled in
\hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-10.0]{10.0}, this lemma follows from
the next result.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-VIB-10.8}%
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 10.8.---}
\emph{Let \(\mathcal T\) be the \textup{(fppf)} or
\textup{(fpqc)} topology. Let \(X\) be an \(S\)-scheme of
finite presentation \textup{(respectively, locally of finite
presentation)}, and let \(R\) be an equivalence relation on
\(X\) defined by a monomorphism}
\[
  v:R\longrightarrow X\times_S X
\]
\emph{such that \(\operatorname{pr}_1\circ v\) is flat and of
finite presentation \textup{(respectively, flat and locally
of finite presentation)}. Then the following conditions are
equivalent:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-10.8-i}
  \emph{The quotient sheaf \(X/R\) for the topology
  \(\mathcal T\) is representable.}

\item[\textup{(ii)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-10.8-ii}
  \emph{There are an \(S\)-scheme \(Y\) of finite presentation
  \textup{(respectively, locally of finite presentation)} and
  a faithfully flat morphism \(p:X\to Y\) such that the
  diagram}
\end{enumerate}
\begin{center}
  \phantomsection\label{diag:sga3-VIB-10.8-D}%
  \begin{tabular}{c@{\qquad}c}
    \textup{(D)} &
    \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=large,column sep=large]
      R
        \arrow[r,"{\operatorname{pr}_1\circ v}"]
        \arrow[d,"{\operatorname{pr}_2\circ v}"'] &
      X \arrow[d,"p"] \\
      X \arrow[r,"p"'] &
      Y
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{tabular}
\end{center}
\emph{\textup{(}where \(\operatorname{pr}_1\) and
\(\operatorname{pr}_2\) are the projections
\(X\times_SX\rightrightarrows X\)\textup{)} is cartesian.}

First note that, by IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:definition:3:3:2}{3.3.2} and 4.4.3, the sheaf \(X/R\) for
the topology \(\mathcal T\) is representable by \(Y\) if and
only if diagram~\hyperref[diag:sga3-VIB-10.8-D]{\textup{(D)}}
is cartesian and \(p\) is a covering for the topology
\(\mathcal T\).

Let us prove that
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-10.8-i]{\textup{(i)}} implies
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-10.8-ii]{\textup{(ii)}}.
Hypothesis~\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-10.8-i]{\textup{(i)}}
implies that diagram~\hyperref[diag:sga3-VIB-10.8-D]%
{\SGARefLink{diag:sga3-VIB-10.8-D}{\textup{(D)}}} is cartesian, hence that
\(\operatorname{pr}_1\circ v\) is deduced from \(p\) by base
change by \(p\), and that \(p\) is a covering for the
\textup{(fpqc)} topology. Since \(\operatorname{pr}_1\circ v\)
is faithfully flat and of finite presentation
\textup{(respectively, faithfully flat and locally of finite
presentation)}, so is \(p\) (EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.7.1), and since
\(X\) is of finite presentation \textup{(respectively, locally
of finite presentation)} over \(S\), so is \(Y\)
(EGA~IV\(_4\), 17.7.5).\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text refers to EGA~IV 17.7.4, which
concerns flat base change. The statement used, that \(g\) is (locally) of finite presentation when \(g\circ f\)
is and \(f\) is faithfully flat and locally of finite presentation (and quasi-compact, for finite
presentation), is EGA~IV\(_4\), 17.7.5, to which the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, corrects the reference.}

Let us prove that
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-10.8-ii]{\textup{(ii)}} implies
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-10.8-i]{\textup{(i)}}. It is enough
to show that \(p\) is a covering for the \textup{(fppf)}
topology. But \(p\) is faithfully flat by hypothesis, and it
is locally of finite presentation because \(X\) and \(Y\) are
locally of finite presentation over \(S\).\footnote{Translator's note: see EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.4.3~(v),
which the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, cites here.}
