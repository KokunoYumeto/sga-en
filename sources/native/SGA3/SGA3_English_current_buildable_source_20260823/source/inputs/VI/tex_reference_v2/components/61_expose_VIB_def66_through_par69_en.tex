
\medskip
\phantomsection\label{def:sga3-VIB-6.6}%
\noindent\textbf{Definition 6.6.---}
Let \(G\) be an \(S\)-functor in groups and let \(H\) be a group
sub-\(S\)-functor of \(G\). One says that \(H\) is \emph{invariant}
(respectively, \emph{central}, respectively,
\emph{characteristic}) in \(G\) if
\[
  \underline{\operatorname{Norm}}_G H=G
\]
(respectively, if
\(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}_G H=G\), respectively, if for
every \(S\)-scheme \(T\) and every automorphism
\[
  a\in\operatorname{Aut}_{T\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(G_T)
\]
one has \(a(H_T)\subset H_T\)). In other words, for every
\(S\)-scheme \(T\), the subgroup \(H(T)\) of \(G(T)\) is normal
in \(G(T)\) (respectively, central in \(G(T)\), respectively,
invariant under every automorphism of \(G_T\)).

If \(H\) is central (respectively, characteristic), then it is
invariant.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{rem:sga3-VIB-6.7}%
\noindent\textbf{Remark 6.7.---}
Let \(G\) and \(H\) be two \(S\)-groups and let \(u:H\to G\) be
a monomorphism. For \(H\) to be invariant (respectively,
central, respectively, characteristic) in \(G\), it is necessary and
sufficient that the morphism
\[
  \mu\circ
  \bigl(c\circ\operatorname{pr}_2,\,
        \mu\circ(u\times\operatorname{id}_G)\bigr)
  :H\times_S G\longrightarrow G
\]
(defined by
\[
  (h,g)\longmapsto g^{-1}\cdot hg
\]
for \(g\in G(S')\) and \(h\in H(S')\)) factor through \(u\)
(respectively, be equal to \(u\circ\operatorname{pr}_1\), respectively,
that for every \(S\)-scheme \(T\) and every \(T\)-automorphism of groups
\(a\) of \(G\times_ST\), \(a\circ u(T)\) factor through \(u(T)\)).

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{ex:sga3-VIB-6.8}%
\noindent\textbf{Example 6.8.---}
Let \(G\) be an \(S\)-functor in groups. Then
\(\underline{\operatorname{Centr}}G\) is characteristic and
central. If \(G_s\) is representable for every \(s\in S\), then
\(G^0\) is characteristic. This follows from the definitions and
\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-3.3]{3.3}.

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
The re-edition ends this section (its 6.9) with results of M.~Raynaud and L.~Gruson, \emph{Crit\`eres de platitude
et de projectivit\'e}, Invent.\ Math.\ \textbf{13} (1971), 1--89, on \(S\)-schemes \emph{pure} over \(S\)
(loc.\ cit., I, 3.3.3), a notion local on \(S\) for the \'etale topology: a flat \(A\)-algebra \(B\) of finite
presentation is a projective \(A\)-module if and only if \(\operatorname{Spec}B\) is pure over
\(\operatorname{Spec}A\) (loc.\ cit., I, 3.3.5); and an \(S\)-scheme locally of finite type and flat over \(S\),
whose fibres are geometrically irreducible and without embedded components, is pure over \(S\)
(loc.\ cit., I, 3.3.4~(iii)). The re-edition concludes that a flat, pure \(S\)-scheme locally of finite
presentation is essentially free over \(S\) (in the sense of Expos\'e~VIII, 6.1, with ``projective'', see the
Addition after 6.2), and that an \(S\)-group scheme \(G\) locally of finite presentation and flat over \(S\), with
connected fibres, is pure over \(S\), hence essentially free: the fibres of \(G\) are geometrically irreducible
(VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-2.4}{2.4}) and, being Cohen--Macaulay
(VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-1.1.1}{1.1.1}), have no embedded components. The statements of VIII, 6.5~(e)
then apply to such groups.
