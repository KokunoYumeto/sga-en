\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-VIB-11.17}%
\noindent\textbf{Theorem 11.17 \textup{(Chevalley)}.---}
\emph{Let \(k\) be a field, let \(G\) be an affine
\(k\)-group \textup{(not necessarily algebraic)}, and
let \(H\) be a closed group subscheme of \(G\), invariant
in \(G\). Then the \textup{(fpqc)} sheaf quotient \(G/H\)
is representable by an affine \(k\)-group.}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, refers to another proof of this theorem, which does not use the results of
Expos\'e~VI\(_A\): M.~Takeuchi, \emph{A correspondence between Hopf ideals and sub-Hopf algebras}, Manuscripta
Math.\ \textbf{7} (1972), 251--270, Theorem~5.2.}

\emph{First case}: suppose first that \(G\) is algebraic. By
\SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIA-3.2}{VI\(_A\), 3.2}, the cokernel \(K\) of the injection
\(H\to G\) exists, the canonical morphism \(p:G\to K\) is a covering for the
\textup{(fpqc)} topology, and \(K\) represents the quotient sheaf \(G/H\) for the
\textup{(fpqc)} topology; it therefore remains to show that \(K\) is affine.

Suppose first that \(k\) is algebraically closed, that \(G\) is reduced and
connected, and that \(H\) is reduced. By the \emph{Bible} \textup{(Expos\'e~4,
Corollary to Theorem~1)}, there is a finite-dimensional linear representation
\[
  \rho:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}(V)
\]
such that \((\operatorname{Ker}\rho)_{\mathrm{red}}=H\). By
\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-11.11}{11.11}, \(G/\operatorname{Ker}\rho\) is affine;
hence \((G/H)/(\operatorname{Ker}\rho/H)\) is affine, and therefore so is \(G/H\),
the group \(\operatorname{Ker}\rho/H\) being finite.

If \(k\) is algebraically closed, and \(G\) and \(H\) are reduced, then
\((G/H)/(G^0/H\cap G^0)\) is finite, as a quotient of \(G/G^0\), and
\(G^0/H\cap G^0\) is affine; hence \(G/H\) is affine.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, spells out this inference. In our words: the subgroup \(G^0H\) is
invariant in \(G\), and the quotient of \(G/H\) by its image \(G^0H/H\simeq G^0/H\cap G^0\)
(VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{par:sga3-VIA-5.3.3}{5.3.3}) is \(G/G^0H\)
(VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{par:sga3-VIA-5.3.2}{5.3.2}), a quotient of the finite constant group \(G/G^0\)
(VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-5.5.1}{5.5.1}; \(k\) is algebraically closed). Hence \(G/H\) is the disjoint
union of the finitely many fibres of \(G/H\to G/G^0H\), each of which is a translate of \(G^0H/H\) by an element of
\(G(k)\), hence affine.}

In the case where \(k\) is algebraically closed, and \(G\) and \(H\) are
arbitrary, \((G\times_kH)_{\mathrm{red}}\) is isomorphic to
\((G_{\mathrm{red}})\times_k(H_{\mathrm{red}})\). Consequently \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is a
closed subgroup of \(G\), and \(H_{\mathrm{red}}\) is a closed subgroup of
\(G_{\mathrm{red}}\), invariant in \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\): the conjugation morphism
\(G_{\mathrm{red}}\times_kH_{\mathrm{red}}\to H\) has a reduced source, so it factors
through \(H_{\mathrm{red}}\). Put \(H'=H\cap G_{\mathrm{red}}\); it is a closed subgroup
of \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\), invariant in \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\), which contains
\(H_{\mathrm{red}}\) and has the same underlying space as \(H\). The morphism
\(G_{\mathrm{red}}/H'\to G/H\) induced by the inclusion is a monomorphism of algebraic
\(k\)-groups, hence a closed immersion
\textup{(\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2}{1.4.2})}; it is surjective, and
\(G_{\mathrm{red}}/H'\) is reduced
\textup{(\SGARefLink{concl:sga3-VIB-9.2-ix}{9.2~(ix)})}, so that it identifies
\(G_{\mathrm{red}}/H'\) with \((G/H)_{\mathrm{red}}\). On the other hand,
\(H'/H_{\mathrm{red}}\) is a \(k\)-group of finite type with only one point, hence
finite, so that the projection
\(G_{\mathrm{red}}/H_{\mathrm{red}}\to G_{\mathrm{red}}/H'\), which is the quotient by
\(H'/H_{\mathrm{red}}\) \textup{(VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{par:sga3-VIA-5.3.2}{5.3.2})},
is finite and surjective. Since \(G_{\mathrm{red}}/H_{\mathrm{red}}\) is affine by the
preceding case, so is \((G/H)_{\mathrm{red}}\) \textup{(EGA~II, 6.7.1)}, and hence so is
\(G/H\) \textup{(EGA~I, 5.1.10)}, since, \(G/H\) being of finite type over \(k\), its
nilradical is nilpotent.\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text concludes from the
first isomorphism that \((G_{\mathrm{red}})/(H_{\mathrm{red}})\) is isomorphic to
\((G/H)_{\mathrm{red}}\), and applies EGA~I, 5.1.10 to it. The canonical morphism
\(G_{\mathrm{red}}/H_{\mathrm{red}}\to(G/H)_{\mathrm{red}}\) need not be an isomorphism: if
\(k\) has characteristic \(p>0\), \(G=\mathbb G_{a,k}\) and \(H=\alpha_p\), it is the
Frobenius morphism \(x\mapsto x^p\). The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, corrects the step through \(G_{\mathrm{red}}/(H\cap
G_{\mathrm{red}})\), after M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel, \emph{Groupes alg\'ebriques}~I,
Masson and North-Holland, 1970, III, \S\,3, 5.6; the argument above is ours. It also notes
that \(H_{\mathrm{red}}\) need not be invariant in a non-reduced \(G\): in characteristic
\(p\), \(H=\alpha_p\times\mathbb G_a\) is invariant in \(G=\mathbb G_a^2\rtimes\alpha_p\),
where \(t\in\alpha_p\) acts by \((x,y)\mapsto(x+ty,y)\), but \(H_{\mathrm{red}}=0\times\mathbb
G_a\) is not.}

In the case where \(k\) is arbitrary,
\((G\otimes_k\overline{k})/(H\otimes_k\overline{k})\) is isomorphic to
\((G/H)\otimes_k\overline{k}\)
\textup{(\SGARefLink{concl:sga3-VIB-9.2-v}{9.2~(v)})}; hence, since the first is
affine, so is the second, and therefore \(G/H\) is affine.\footnote{Translator's note: the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, names the descent result used here: a morphism
\(X\to S\) is affine if it becomes affine after a faithfully flat quasi-compact base change \(S'\to S\)
(EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.7.1); here \(S'=\operatorname{Spec}\overline{k}\).}

\emph{Second case}: the general case. By \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-11.13}{11.13}, the
algebra \(A\) of \(G\) is the inductive limit of an increasing filtered family
\((A_i)_{i\in I}\) of subalgebras of finite type of \(A\), stable under the diagonal map
and the involution. By EGA~I, 4.2.3, \(H\) is affine, and if \(B\) denotes the algebra
of \(H\), the homomorphism \(A\to B\) deduced from the injection \(H\to G\) is
surjective. Put \(I=\operatorname{Ker}(A\to B)\), \(I_i=I\cap A_i\) and
\(B_i=A_i/I_i\); since the structure of augmented associative involutive hyperalgebra
of \(A\) passes to the quotient by \(I\), one checks easily that that of \(A_i\) passes
to the quotient by \(I_i\), so that \(B_i\) is the algebra of an affine \(k\)-group of
finite type \(H_i\); it is clear that \(B=\varinjlim B_i\). By EGA~I, 4.2.3, \(H_i\) is
isomorphic to a closed group subscheme of \(G_i\). By
\SGARefLink{rem:sga3-VIB-6.7}{6.7}, the fact that \(H\) is invariant in \(G\) is
expressed by saying that a certain morphism \(H\times_kG\to G\) factors through the
injection \(H\to G\), in other words that the corresponding homomorphism
\(A\to B\otimes_kA\) factors through the canonical homomorphism \(A\to B\); one checks
easily that the homomorphism \(A_i\to B_i\otimes_kA_i\) defined in the analogous way
factors through \(A_i\to B_i\), so that \(H_i\) is invariant in \(G_i\). By what we
have seen above, \(G_i\) being of finite type over \(k\), \(G_i/H_i\) is representable
by an affine \(k\)-group \(K_i\), whose algebra we denote by \(C_i\). Now put
\(C=\varinjlim C_i\), and let \(K=\varprojlim K_i\) be the affine \(k\)-scheme with
algebra \(C\) \textup{(EGA~IV, 8.2.3)}.

Let us show that \(K\) represents \(G/H\); for this it suffices to check that
\(H\times_kG\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }G\times_KG\), and that the morphism \(G\to K\) is a
covering for the \textup{(fpqc)} topology
\textup{(IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:4:4:3}{4.4.3})}. Now, for each \(i\),
\(H_i\times_kG_i\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }G_i\times_{K_i}G_i\), so the same holds for the
morphism obtained by passing to the projective limit; finally, each of the morphisms
\(G_i\to K_i\) is faithfully flat
\textup{(\SGARefLink{concl:sga3-VIB-9.2-xi}{9.2~(xi)})}, in other words \(A_i\) is
faithfully flat over \(C_i\);\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text says here that \(C_i\) is faithfully flat
over \(A_i\), and just below that \(C\) is faithfully flat over \(A\); as the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, writes, it is \(A_i\) that is faithfully flat over \(C_i\), and \(A\) over \(C\).} since \(A=\varinjlim A_i\) and \(C=\varinjlim C_i\), it
follows that \(A\) is faithfully flat over \(C\),\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, proves this step (after M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel, \emph{Groupes
alg\'ebriques}~I, III, \S\,3, 7.1). Our argument: for every \(C\)-module \(M\),
\(A\otimes_CM=\varinjlim_i(A_i\otimes_{C_i}M)\), because \(\varinjlim_i(A_i\otimes_{C_i}C)=A\) (an element
\(a\otimes c\) with \(c\) the image of \(c_j\in C_j\), \(j\geq i\), equals \(ac_j\otimes1\) in
\(A_j\otimes_{C_j}C\)). As each \(A_i\) is flat over \(C_i\) and filtered inductive limits are exact, \(A\) is
flat over \(C\). If \(\mathfrak m\) is a maximal ideal of \(C\), the rings \(A_i\otimes_{C_i}C/\mathfrak m\) are
nonzero, \(A_i\) being faithfully flat over \(C_i\); so their inductive limit \(A/\mathfrak mA\) is nonzero, and
\(A\) is faithfully flat over \(C\).} so that \(G\to K\) is a faithfully flat
morphism. Since this morphism is affine, it is quasi-compact, hence a covering for the
\textup{(fpqc)} topology, which completes the proof.

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B};
our formulation). The re-edition proves the first step of the first case (\(k\)
algebraically closed, \(G\) reduced and connected, \(H\) reduced) without the
\emph{Bible}, from 11.16, by an argument of M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel
(\emph{Groupes alg\'ebriques}~I, Masson and North-Holland, 1970, III, \S\,3, 5.6). In
our words: by the proof of \SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIB-11.16}{11.16}, there are a
finite-dimensional \(G\)-module \(E\) and a vector \(e_0\in E\) such that \(H\) is the
largest closed subgroup scheme \(H_1\) of \(G\) with \(he_0\in Re_0\) for every
\(k\)-algebra \(R\) and every \(h\in H_1(R)\); thus \(he_0=\chi(h)e_0\) for a character
\(\chi:H\to\mathbb G_{m,k}\). Let \(E'\) be the subspace of \(E\) spanned by the vectors
\(ge_0\), \(g\in G(k)\). (a) \(E'\) is a sub-\(G\)-module of \(E\): the action morphism
\(G\times_kE'\to E\) (vector spaces being regarded as affine spaces) maps the
\(k\)-points into \(E'\), and these are dense in the reduced \(k\)-scheme of finite type
\(G\times_kE'\), so that it factors through the closed subscheme \(E'\). (b) Let
\(h\in H(k)\). For \(g\in G(k)\), \(h(ge_0)=g(g^{-1}hg)e_0=\chi(g^{-1}hg)\,ge_0\), so the
morphism \(G\to\mathbb A^1_k\), \(g\mapsto\chi(g^{-1}hg)\), takes at the \(k\)-points
values among the finitely many eigenvalues of \(h\) on \(E\); as these points are dense
and \(G\) is irreducible, it is constant, equal to \(\chi(h)\). Hence \(h\) acts on
\(E'\) as the scalar \(\chi(h)\), and the two morphisms \(H\times_kE'\to E'\),
\((h,x)\mapsto hx\) and \((h,x)\mapsto\chi(h)x\), agree at the \(k\)-points, which are
dense in the reduced scheme \(H\times_kE'\); so they are equal. (c) Let \(K\) be the
kernel of the representation of \(G\) on \(\operatorname{End}_k(E')\) by conjugation,
\(u\mapsto gug^{-1}\). By (b), \(H\subset K\). Conversely, a point \(g\in K(R)\) commutes
with every \(R\)-endomorphism of \(E'\otimes_kR\), so it acts on \(E'\otimes_kR\) as
multiplication by a unit of \(R\), and \(ge_0\in Re_0\); hence \(K\subset H\) by the
maximality of \(H\). Thus \(H=K\), the morphism
\(G/H\to\operatorname{GL}(\operatorname{End}_k(E'))\) is a monomorphism of algebraic
\(k\)-groups, hence a closed immersion
\textup{(\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2}{1.4.2})}, and \(G/H\) is affine.

\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B},
11.18, ``Compl\'ements''; our formulation). The re-edition ends \S\,11 with consequences
of 11.17 and of its proof, drawn from M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel, \emph{Groupes
alg\'ebriques}~I, Masson and North-Holland, 1970, III, \S\,3, 7. We give them in our own
terms; the numbers in brackets are those of the re-edition. Let \(k\) be a field.

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(a) Morphisms from a group of finite type to an affine
group} [11.18.1]. \emph{Let \(u:G\to G'\) be a morphism of \(k\)-groups, with \(G\) of
finite type and \(G'\) affine, and let \(N=\operatorname{Ker}u\). The morphism
\(\tau:G/N\to G'\) induced by \(u\) is a closed immersion; in particular \(G/N\) is affine.
If \(u^\natural:\mathcal O(G')\to\mathcal O(G)\) is injective, \(\tau\) is an isomorphism,
so that \(G'\) is of finite type and \(u\) is faithfully flat} (compare S.~Anantharaman,
\emph{Sch\'emas en groupes, espaces homog\`enes et espaces alg\'ebriques sur une base de
dimension~1}, M\'em.\ Soc.\ Math.\ France \textbf{33} (1973), 5--79, 2.3.3.2). Indeed,
write \(G'=\varprojlim G'_i\) as in \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-11.13}{11.13}, with
projections \(q_i:G'\to G'_i\). The kernels \(N_i\) of the \(q_i\circ u\) form a
decreasing filtered family of closed subgroups of \(G\) whose intersection is \(N\), a
point of \(G'\) being the unit as soon as all its images in the \(G'_i\) are; as \(G\) is
noetherian, \(N=N_i\) for some \(i\). Then \(q_i\circ\tau:G/N\to G'_i\) is a monomorphism
of algebraic \(k\)-groups, hence a closed immersion
\textup{(\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2}{1.4.2})}; so \(G/N\) is affine, and
\(\mathcal O(G'_i)\to\mathcal O(G/N)\) is surjective. This map factors through
\(\tau^\natural\), which is therefore surjective: \(\tau\) is a closed immersion of affine
schemes. If \(u^\natural=p^\natural\circ\tau^\natural\) is injective, \(p:G\to G/N\) being
the projection, so is \(\tau^\natural\), which is then bijective.

\par\smallskip\noindent\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-VIB-11.18.2}%
\textit{(b) Morphisms of affine groups} [11.18.2]. \emph{Let \(u:G\to G'\) be a morphism
of affine \(k\)-groups, with algebras \(A\) and \(A'\); put \(\varphi=u^\natural:A'\to A\),
\(B=\varphi(A')\) and \(N=\operatorname{Ker}u\). \textup{(i)} If \(\varphi\) is injective,
\(u\) is faithfully flat and \(G'\) represents the \textup{(fpqc)} quotient sheaf \(G/N\).
\textup{(ii)} In general, \(B\) is a sub-Hopf algebra of \(A\), \(\operatorname{Spec}B\)
represents \(G/N\), \(u\) is the composite of the faithfully flat morphism
\(G\to\operatorname{Spec}B\) and of the closed immersion \(\operatorname{Spec}B\to G'\),
and \(N\) is the closed subscheme of \(G\) defined by the ideal \(AB_+\), where \(B_+\) is
the augmentation ideal of \(B\). \textup{(iii)} If \(u\) is a monomorphism, it is a closed
immersion.} For (i), identify \(A'\) with a sub-Hopf algebra of \(A\), and let \(A\) be the
filtered union of finitely generated sub-Hopf algebras \(A_i\), as in
\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-11.13}{11.13}. Then \(A'_i=A'\cap A_i\) is a sub-Hopf algebra
of \(A_i\) (for two subspaces \(U\), \(V\) of a vector space,
\((U\otimes U)\cap(V\otimes V)=(U\cap V)\otimes(U\cap V)\)), and \(A'\) is the filtered
union of the \(A'_i\). By (a), applied to \(\operatorname{Spec}A_i\to\operatorname{Spec}A'_i\),
\(A_i\) is faithfully flat over \(A'_i\); hence \(A\) is faithfully flat over \(A'\), by the
note to the second case of the proof of 11.17. So \(u\), which is affine, is a covering for
the \textup{(fpqc)} topology; as \((g,n)\mapsto(g,gn)\) is an isomorphism
\(G\times_kN\to G\times_{G'}G\), \(G'\) represents \(G/N\)
\textup{(IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:4:4:3}{4.4.3})}. For (ii), the image of a
morphism of Hopf algebras is a sub-Hopf algebra; apply (i) to \(G\to\operatorname{Spec}B\),
whose kernel is \(N\) because \(\operatorname{Spec}B\to G'\), a closed immersion, is a
monomorphism; and \(N\), the fibre of \(G\to\operatorname{Spec}B\) at the unit, is
\(\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_BB/B_+)=\operatorname{Spec}(A/AB_+)\). Then (iii) follows,
\(N\) being the unit group.

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(c) Sub-Hopf algebras and invariant subgroups} [11.18.3,
11.18.4]. Let \(G\) be an affine \(k\)-group, with algebra \(A\), comultiplication
\(\Delta\), and let \(N\) be a closed invariant subgroup, with ideal \(J\). By 11.17, \(G/N\)
is affine; as \(G\to G/N\) is faithfully flat and quasi-compact, descent identifies
\(\mathcal O(G/N)\) with the functions \(x\in A\) such that \(x(gn)=x(g)\) for all points
\(g\) of \(G\) and \(n\) of \(N\), with values in the same \(k\)-algebra, that is,
\[
  \mathcal O(G/N)=\{\,x\in A\mid\Delta(x)-x\otimes1\in A\otimes_kJ\,\}.
\]
Combined with (b), this gives: \emph{a commutative Hopf algebra \(A\) over \(k\) is
faithfully flat over each of its sub-Hopf algebras \(B\), and
\(N\mapsto\mathcal O(G/N)\) is a bijection from the set of closed invariant subgroups of
\(G=\operatorname{Spec}A\) onto the set of sub-Hopf algebras of \(A\), with inverse
\(B\mapsto\operatorname{Spec}(A/AB_+)\).} Indeed, the first assertion is (b)~(i) for
\(G\to\operatorname{Spec}B\); for \(N\) invariant, \(\mathcal O(G/N)\) is a sub-Hopf algebra
of \(A\), and (b)~(ii), applied to \(G\to G/N\), gives
\(N=\operatorname{Spec}(A/A\,\mathcal O(G/N)_+)\); for \(B\) a sub-Hopf algebra,
\(\operatorname{Spec}(A/AB_+)\) is the kernel \(N\) of \(G\to\operatorname{Spec}B\), and
\(\operatorname{Spec}B\) represents \(G/N\) by (b)~(i), so that \(\mathcal O(G/N)=B\).

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(d) Remarks} [11.18.5]. It follows that the category of
commutative affine \(k\)-groups is abelian (Demazure--Gabriel, loc.\ cit., III, \S\,3,
7.4; see 7.4--7.8 there for further results): the kernel of \(u:G\to G'\) is \(N\), its
image \(G/N\) is a closed subgroup of \(G'\) by (b)~(ii), and \(G'/(G/N)\), which is affine
by 11.17, is its cokernel. M.~Takeuchi gave another proof of 11.17 and of (b) and (c)
(\emph{A correspondence between Hopf ideals and sub-Hopf algebras}, Manuscripta Math.\
\textbf{7} (1972), 251--270, \S\,5), and showed that in (c) \(A\) is even a projective
\(B\)-module (\emph{Relative Hopf modules---equivalences and freeness criteria}, J.\
Algebra \textbf{60} (1979), 452--471, Theorem~5; see also A.~Masuoka and D.~Wigner,
\emph{Faithful flatness of Hopf algebras}, J.\ Algebra \textbf{170} (1994), 156--184,
Theorem~3.6).
