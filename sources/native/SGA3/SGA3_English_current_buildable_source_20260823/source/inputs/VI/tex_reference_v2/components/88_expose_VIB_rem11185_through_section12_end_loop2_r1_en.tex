

\par\bigskip\noindent\phantomsection\label{sec:sga3-VIB-12}%
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B},
\S\,12, ``Compl\'ements sur \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) et les groupes anti-affines''; our
formulation). The re-edition adds a section on the affine envelope \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) of
\SGARefLink{def:sga3-VIB-11.3}{11.3} and on the kernel of \(G\to G_{\mathrm{af}}\), in which it
also proves two statements of M.~Raynaud: Remark~\SGARefLink{rem:sga3-VIB-11.11.1}{11.11.1}, and
Proposition~2.1 of Appendix~III of Expos\'e~XVII. We give its content in our own terms; the
numbers in brackets are those of the re-edition. In (a)--(c), \(k\) is a field.

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(a) Monomorphisms into an affine group} [12.1, communicated to
the re-editors by M.~Raynaud]. \emph{A quasi-compact monomorphism \(u:G\to H\) of
\(k\)-groups, with \(H\) affine, is a closed immersion.} Indeed, \(u\) is quasi-compact and
separated, so that its closed image \(H_1\) is a closed subgroup of \(H\), and \(u\) factors
through a schematically dominant quasi-compact morphism \(u_1:G\to H_1\)
(\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-6.4}{(c)} of the Addition at the end of Expos\'e~VI\(_A\)); by
\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIA-6.6}{(e)} there, \(u_1\) is faithfully flat, and a faithfully flat
quasi-compact monomorphism is an isomorphism (IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:remark:1:14}{1.14}). For
\(G\) of finite type this is contained in \SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2}{1.4.2}; in general it
is a case of the theorem of D.~Perrin quoted in the note to 1.4.2.

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(b) The kernel of \(G\to G_{\mathrm{af}}\)} [12.2, 12.3]. Let
\(G\) be an algebraic \(k\)-group, \(\rho:G\to G_{\mathrm{af}}\) the canonical morphism and
\(N=\operatorname{Ker}\rho\). \emph{\textup{(i)} \(\rho\) is faithfully flat and induces an
isomorphism \(G/N\simeq G_{\mathrm{af}}\); so \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) is algebraic and
\((G/N)_{\mathrm{af}}=G_{\mathrm{af}}\). \textup{(ii)} \(N\) is a characteristic subgroup of
\(G\). \textup{(iii)} \(\mathcal O(N)=k\). \textup{(iv)} \(N\) is smooth, connected and
commutative.} Assertion (i) is (a) of the preceding Addition, applied to \(\rho\), whose
comorphism is the identity of \(\mathcal O(G)\). For (ii), let \(S\) be an affine
\(k\)-scheme: by \SGARefLink{lem:sga3-VIB-11.1}{11.1},
\(\mathcal O(G_S)=\mathcal O(G)\otimes_k\mathcal O(S)\), so that
\((G_S)_{\mathrm{af}}=(G_{\mathrm{af}})_S\) and \(N_S\) is the kernel of
\(G_S\to(G_S)_{\mathrm{af}}\); every morphism from \(G_S\) to an affine \(S\)-scheme factoring
uniquely through this morphism, every automorphism of the \(S\)-group \(G_S\) extends to
\((G_S)_{\mathrm{af}}\) compatibly with it, and therefore preserves \(N_S\). For (iii), let
\(N'\) be the kernel of \(N\to N_{\mathrm{af}}\); by (i) and (ii) for \(N\),
\(N/N'\simeq N_{\mathrm{af}}\) is affine, and \(N'\) is characteristic in \(N\), hence
invariant in \(G\). The projection \(G/N'\to G/N\) is the quotient by the affine group
\(N/N'\) (VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{par:sga3-VIA-5.3.2}{5.3.2}), hence an affine morphism
(\SGARefLink{concl:sga3-VIB-9.2-vii}{9.2~(vii)}); as \(G/N=G_{\mathrm{af}}\) is affine, so is
\(G/N'\), and the projection \(G\to G/N'\) factors through \(G_{\mathrm{af}}=G/N\). Thus
\(N\subset N'\), \(N_{\mathrm{af}}\) is trivial and
\(\mathcal O(N)=\mathcal O(N_{\mathrm{af}})=k\). Finally, (iv) holds for every algebraic
\(k\)-group \(N\) with \(\mathcal O(N)=k\) [12.3]. As \(\mathcal O(N_{\bar k})=\bar k\) for an
algebraic closure \(\bar k\) of \(k\) (11.1), we may suppose \(k\) algebraically closed. The
quotient of \(N\) by the closed subgroup \((N_{\mathrm{red}})^0\) is a \(k\)-scheme of finite
type with finitely many points, hence finite, and its ring of functions embeds in
\(\mathcal O(N)=k\), \(N\) being faithfully flat over it; so it is \(\operatorname{Spec}k\), and
\(N=(N_{\mathrm{red}})^0\) is smooth (VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-1.3.1}{1.3.1}) and
connected. The quotient of \(N\) by its centre \(Z\) is affine (Expos\'e~XII,
\SGARefLink{sga3:xii:6.1}{6.1}, recalled in \SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-6.2.6}{the Addition after
6.2}), and its ring of functions embeds in \(k\); so \(N=Z\).

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(c) Chevalley's structure theorem; anti-affine groups}
[12.4--12.6]. The re-edition records without proof the following theorem of C.~Chevalley:
\emph{if \(k\) is perfect and \(G\) is a smooth connected algebraic \(k\)-group, there is a
unique smooth connected affine subgroup \(L\), invariant in \(G\), such that \(G/L\) is an
abelian variety \textup{(}a proper, smooth and connected \(k\)-group\textup{)}; its formation
commutes with extension of the base field.} Chevalley announced it in 1953 and published his
proof in \emph{Une d\'emonstration d'un th\'eor\`eme sur les groupes alg\'ebriques},
J.\ Math.\ Pures Appl.\ (9) \textbf{39} (1960), 307--317; other proofs were found independently
by I.~Barsotti (\emph{Un teorema di struttura per le variet\`a gruppali}, Atti Accad.\ Naz.\
Lincei Rend.\ Cl.\ Sci.\ Fis.\ Mat.\ Nat.\ (8) \textbf{18} (1955), 43--50) and M.~Rosenlicht
(\emph{Some basic theorems on algebraic groups}, Amer.\ J.\ Math.\ \textbf{78} (1956),
401--443); on the history see C.~S.~Seshadri, \emph{Chevalley: some reminiscences}, Transform.\
Groups \textbf{4} (1999), 119--125. B.~Conrad gave a scheme-theoretic form of Chevalley's proof
(\emph{A modern proof of Chevalley's theorem on algebraic groups}, J.\ Ramanujan Math.\ Soc.\
\textbf{17} (2002), 1--18), and Ng\^o B.-C.\ one of Rosenlicht's (course at Orsay, 2005--2006).
Over an arbitrary field \(k\), a smooth connected algebraic \(G\) still has a smallest
invariant connected affine subgroup \(L\), not necessarily smooth, with \(G/L\) an abelian
variety (S.~Bosch, W.~L\"utkebohmert and M.~Raynaud, \emph{N\'eron Models}, Springer, 1990,
9.2, Theorem~1). Smoothness of \(G\) can then be dropped: in characteristic \(0\), \(G\) is smooth (Cartier); if
\(k\) has characteristic \(p>0\), \(G'=G/\operatorname{Ker}(F^n_G)\) is smooth for \(n\) large (Expos\'e~VII\(_A\),
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIIA:8.3}{8.3}), and the inverse image \(L\) in \(G\) of the subgroup \(L'\)
of \(G'\) just described is connected and affine, being an extension of \(L'\) by the
infinitesimal group \(\operatorname{Ker}F^n_G\), with \(G/L\simeq G'/L'\). So every connected
algebraic \(k\)-group is an extension of an abelian variety by an affine group; by D.~Perrin
(\emph{Approximation des sch\'emas en groupes, quasi-compacts sur un corps}, Bull.\ Soc.\
Math.\ France \textbf{104} (1976), 323--335, 4.2.9), this holds for every connected
\(k\)-group. Connectedness is needed: if \(k\) is algebraically closed and
\(G=E\rtimes\{\pm1\}_k\), \(E\) an elliptic curve on which \(-1\) acts by \(x\mapsto-x\), a
closed invariant subgroup \(L\) with \(G/L\) connected maps onto \(G/E\), so it contains a
point \((x,-1)\), hence the commutators \((2y,1)\) of \((y,1)\) and \((x,-1)\), \(y\in E(k)\),
hence \(E\); so \(L=G\), which is not affine. Following M.~Brion, a \(k\)-group \(N\) is called
\emph{anti-affine} if \(\mathcal O(N)=k\); if \(k\) is perfect, an anti-affine algebraic
\(k\)-group is, by (b) and the theorem above, an extension of an abelian variety by a smooth
connected commutative affine group. For their structure see M.~Brion, \emph{Anti-affine
algebraic groups}, J.\ Algebra \textbf{321} (2009), 934--952, and C.~Sancho de Salas and
F.~Sancho de Salas, \emph{Principal bundles, quasi-abelian varieties and structure of algebraic
groups}, J.\ Algebra \textbf{322} (2009), 2751--2772.

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(d) A flatness criterion} [12.7, a statement of M.~Raynaud in a
form due to O.~Gabber]. \emph{Let \(R\) be a discrete valuation ring, with uniformizer \(\pi\)
and fraction field \(K\), let \(A\) be a flat \(R\)-algebra and \(M\) an \(A\)-module flat over
\(R\). If \(M/\pi M\) is flat over \(A/\pi A\) and \(M\otimes_RK\) is flat over \(A\otimes_RK\),
then \(M\) is flat over \(A\).} Our proof: as \(\pi\) is a non-zero-divisor on \(A\) and on
\(M\), \(\operatorname{Tor}^A_i(M,A/\pi A)=0\) for \(i\geq1\); so if \(P_\bullet\) is a
projective resolution of \(M\), \(P_\bullet/\pi P_\bullet\) is one of \(M/\pi M\) over
\(A/\pi A\), and \(\operatorname{Tor}^A_i(M,Q)=\operatorname{Tor}^{A/\pi A}_i(M/\pi M,Q)=0\)
for \(i\geq1\) and every \(A/\pi A\)-module \(Q\). By d\'evissage and passage to inductive
limits, \(\operatorname{Tor}^A_i(M,Q)=0\) for \(i\geq1\) and every \(A\)-module \(Q\) all of
whose elements are killed by powers of \(\pi\). If \(Q\) has no \(\pi\)-torsion, the exact
sequence \(0\to Q\to Q_K\to Q_K/Q\to0\), with
\(\operatorname{Tor}^A_1(M,Q_K)=\operatorname{Tor}^{A_K}_1(M_K,Q_K)=0\) and
\(\operatorname{Tor}^A_2(M,Q_K/Q)=0\), gives \(\operatorname{Tor}^A_1(M,Q)=0\); and for an
arbitrary \(Q\), so does the exact sequence \(0\to Q_{\mathrm{tors}}\to Q\to
Q/Q_{\mathrm{tors}}\to0\). (The re-edition gives two other proofs, one after M.~Raynaud and
L.~Gruson, \emph{Crit\`eres de platitude et de projectivit\'e}, Invent.\ Math.\ \textbf{13}
(1971), 1--89, II, 1.4.2.1, the other due to Gabber.)

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(e) Flatness of \(\mathcal A(X)\)} [12.8]. \emph{Let \(S\) be a
regular locally noetherian scheme of dimension \(\leq1\) and \(X\) a flat, quasi-compact and
quasi-separated \(S\)-scheme. Then \(\mathcal A(X)\) is a flat \(\mathcal O_S\)-module, and if
\(S=\operatorname{Spec}R\), \(R\) a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer \(\pi\) and closed
point \(s\), the map \(\mathcal A(X)/\pi\mathcal A(X)\to\mathcal A(X_s)\) is injective.} Indeed,
locally \(S\) is the spectrum of a field or of such an \(R\); multiplication by \(\pi\) is
injective on \(\mathcal O_X\), \(X\) being flat, with cokernel \(\mathcal O_{X_s}\); taking
sections, \(\pi\) is injective on \(\mathcal A(X)\), which is therefore torsion-free, hence flat
over \(R\), and \(\mathcal A(X)/\pi\mathcal A(X)\) embeds in \(\mathcal A(X_s)\). (The
re-edition notes that \(\mathcal A(X)\) is still flat when \(S\) is regular of dimension
\(\leq2\): M.~Raynaud, \emph{Faisceaux amples sur les sch\'emas en groupes et les espaces
homog\`enes}, Lecture Notes in Math.\ \textbf{119}, Springer, 1970, VII, 3.2.)

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(f) \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) as a quotient} [12.10~(i), (ii); this is
Proposition~2.1 of Appendix~III of Expos\'e~XVII, where \(G\) is of finite type; the re-edition
assumes only \(G\) quasi-compact and quasi-separated, using Perrin, loc.\ cit., 4.2.5]. Let
\(S\) be a regular locally noetherian scheme of dimension \(\leq1\), \(G\) a flat,
quasi-compact and quasi-separated \(S\)-group, \(\rho:G\to G_{\mathrm{af}}\) the canonical
morphism (defined by (e) and \SGARefLink{def:sga3-VIB-11.3}{11.3}) and
\(N=\operatorname{Ker}\rho\). \emph{Then \(\rho\) is faithfully flat and quasi-compact, and
\(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) represents the \textup{(fpqc)} quotient sheaf \(G/N\). If \(G\) is of finite
type over \(S\), \(\rho\) is of finite presentation, \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) is of finite type over
\(S\) and represents the \textup{(fppf)} quotient sheaf \(G/N\).} The morphism \(\rho\) is
quasi-compact, \(G\) being quasi-compact over \(S\) and \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) affine over \(S\).
The other assertions are local on \(S\), and the formation of \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) commutes with
flat base change (\SGARefLink{lem:sga3-VIB-11.1}{11.1}); so let \(S=\operatorname{Spec}R\), with
\(R\) a field or a discrete valuation ring, with fraction field \(K\) and residue field
\(\kappa\). The fibres \(\rho_K:G_K\to(G_{\mathrm{af}})_K=(G_K)_{\mathrm{af}}\) and
\(\rho_\kappa:G_\kappa\to(G_{\mathrm{af}})_\kappa\) are quasi-compact morphisms of groups over a
field, into affine groups, whose comorphisms are injective (for \(\rho_K\) it is the identity;
for \(\rho_\kappa\) see (e)); so they are faithfully flat (\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIA-6.6}{(e)} of
the Addition at the end of Expos\'e~VI\(_A\)). If \(R\) is a field, this is the assertion.
Otherwise, for every affine open subset \(U\) of \(G\), (d) applies to the
\(\mathcal A(G)\)-module \(\mathcal O(U)\): it is flat over \(R\), its reduction modulo the
uniformizer is flat over \(\mathcal A(G)\otimes_R\kappa\), and its generic fibre over
\(\mathcal A(G)\otimes_RK\). So \(\rho\) is flat, and it is surjective: it is a covering for
the \textup{(fpqc)} topology, and as \(G\times_SN\to G\times_{G_{\mathrm{af}}}G\),
\((g,n)\mapsto(g,gn)\), is an isomorphism, \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) represents \(G/N\) (IV,
\SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:4:4:3}{4.4.3}). If \(G\) is of finite type, \(G\) and \(N\) are
of finite presentation over the locally noetherian \(S\), and \(N\) is flat over \(S\), being
deduced from \(\rho\) by base change along the unit section of \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\); so \(\rho\)
and \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\to S\) are of finite presentation
(\SGARefLink{concl:sga3-VIB-9.2-xiii}{9.2~(xiii)}), and \(\rho\) is a covering for the
\textup{(fppf)} topology.

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(g) Affine groups over a base of dimension \(\leq1\)} [12.9,
the re-edition's proof of Remark~\SGARefLink{rem:sga3-VIB-11.11.1}{11.11.1}]. \emph{Let \(S\) and
\(G\) be as in \textup{(f)}. The following conditions are equivalent: \textup{(i)} \(G\) is
affine over \(S\); \textup{(ii)} \(G\) is quasi-affine over \(S\); \textup{(iii)} \(G\) acts
faithfully on a flat quasi-affine \(S\)-scheme \(X\); \textup{(iv)} \(G\) acts linearly and
faithfully on a flat quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_S\)-module \(\mathcal E\); \textup{(v)}
\(\rho:G\to G_{\mathrm{af}}\) is a monomorphism.} Clearly (i)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii)\(\Rightarrow\)(iii)
(take \(X=G\), acting on itself by translations). Under (iii), \(\mathcal A(X)\) is flat over
\(\mathcal O_S\) by (e), \(X\) being flat (the argument uses this), so that
\(\mathcal A(G\times_SX)=\mathcal A(G)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal A(X)\)
(\SGARefLink{lem:sga3-VIB-11.1}{11.1}), and the action of \(G\) on \(X\) gives a linear action
on \(\mathcal A(X)\) and an action on \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A(X)\), of which \(X\) is an
invariant open subscheme; so a point of \(G\) acting trivially on \(\mathcal A(X)\), after any
base change, acts trivially on \(X\), and (iv) holds with \(\mathcal E=\mathcal A(X)\). Under
(iv), the action of \(G\) on \(\mathcal E\) comes from an action of \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\)
(\SGARefLink{par:sga3-VIB-11.6}{11.6}), so \(N\) acts trivially, and is the unit group: (v).
Under (v), \(\rho\) is a monomorphism which, by (f), is faithfully flat and quasi-compact, hence
an isomorphism (IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:remark:1:14}{1.14}): (i). The last condition of 11.11.1
is treated in the next Addition.

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(h) The kernel \(N\); an affineness criterion} [12.10~(iii),
12.10.1]. \emph{Let \(S\), \(G\), \(N\) be as in \textup{(f)}, and suppose that \(G_\eta\) is
affine for every maximal point \(\eta\) of \(S\). Then \(N\) is \'etale over \(S\); it is the
unit group if \(G\) is separated over \(S\).} We may suppose \(S=\operatorname{Spec}R\) local,
as in (f). Since \(G_K\) is affine, \(\rho_K\) is an isomorphism, and \(N_K\) is the unit
group; this settles the case of a field. Let \(R\) be a discrete valuation ring, with
uniformizer \(\pi\); by (f), \(N\) is flat over \(R\). Let \(x\) be a point of the closed fibre
of \(N\) and \(U=\operatorname{Spec}A\) an affine open neighbourhood of \(x\) in \(N\). By
flatness, \(U_K\) is not empty, so it is the one-point open subscheme \(\operatorname{Spec}K\)
of \(N_K\), and \(A\otimes_RK=K\); as \(A\) is torsion-free, \(R\subset A\subset K\), and
\(\pi\), which vanishes at \(x\), is not invertible in \(A\). Every element of \(K\) not in
\(R\) being of the form \(u\pi^{-n}\), \(u\in R^\times\), \(n\geq1\), it follows that \(A=R\):
\(U\to S\) is an isomorphism, and \(N\) is \'etale over \(S\). If \(G\) is separated over \(S\),
the inverse \(S\to U\) and the unit section are two sections of the separated morphism
\(N\to S\) which agree at the generic point, hence everywhere, \(S\) being integral; so \(U\) is
the image of the unit section, and \(N\) is the unit group. \emph{Consequently, if \(R\) is a
discrete valuation ring with fraction field \(K\), every separated flat \(R\)-group \(G\) of
finite type with \(G_K\) affine is affine}: \(N\) being the unit group, \(\rho\) is a
monomorphism, and by (f) a faithfully flat quasi-compact one, hence an isomorphism. Other proofs of this consequence are given
by S.~Anantharaman (\emph{Sch\'emas en groupes, espaces homog\`enes et espaces alg\'ebriques
sur une base de dimension~1}, M\'em.\ Soc.\ Math.\ France \textbf{33} (1973), 5--79, 2.3.1)
and by G.~Prasad and J.-K.~Yu (\emph{On quasi-reductive group schemes}, J.\ Algebraic Geom.\
\textbf{15} (2006), 507--549, Proposition~3.1).

\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{(i) Examples} [12.10.2]. O.~Gabber has given examples of flat
groups of finite type over a discrete valuation ring whose generic fibre is an abelian variety
and for which the kernel \(N\) of \(\rho\) is not smooth. M.~Raynaud has given an example, over
the affine plane \(S\) over a field, of a smooth quasi-affine \(S\)-group with affine connected
fibres which is not affine over \(S\) (Raynaud, loc.\ cit., VII, \S\,3, p.~116); so (g) does
not extend to bases of dimension \(2\).
