
\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-4.2}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 4.2.---}
\emph{Let \(G\) be an \(S\)-scheme locally of finite presentation,
equipped with an \(S\)-section \(\epsilon\) and satisfying the following
two conditions:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{prop:sga3-VIB-4.2:item:a}(a)}] \emph{For every \(s\in S\) and every
  \(x\in G_s\), one has}
  \[
    \dim G_s=\dim_x G_s
  \]
  \emph{\textup(or, what amounts to the same, for every \(s\in S\), all the
  irreducible components of \(G_s\) have the same dimension\textup).}
\item[\textup{(b)}] \emph{For every \(s\in S\), if \(\underline{G_s^0}\)
  denotes the connected component of \(G_s\) containing
  \(\epsilon(s)\), then \(\underline{G_s^0}\) is geometrically irreducible.}
\end{enumerate}
\emph{\textup(Recall that an \(S\)-group \(G\) satisfies conditions
\textup{(a)} and \textup{(b)} \textup(\hyperref[lem:sga3-VIB-1.5]{1.5} and Expos\'e~VI\(_A\),
\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIA-2.4.1}{2.4.1}\textup).\textup) The following conditions are equivalent:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{prop:sga3-VIB-4.2:item:i}(i)}] \emph{\(G\) is universally open over \(S\) at
  the points of \(\underline{G_s^0}\).}
\item[\textup{(i bis)}] \emph{\(G\) is universally open over \(S\)
  at every point of a neighborhood of \(\epsilon(s)\) in
  \(\underline{G_s^0}\).}\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has ``at the points of
\(\epsilon(s)\)'', and proves that (i~bis) implies (ii) with \(x\) the generic point of \(\underline{G_s^0}\).
Universal openness at \(\epsilon(s)\) alone does not suffice: for the subgroup \(xy=0\) of the additive
group over \(S=\operatorname{Spec}k[x]\) (the example in the note to 2.2), \(G\) is universally open over
\(S\) at the unit point over \(s=(x)\), while \(\dim G_t\) is \(0\) for \(t\neq s\) and \(1\) for \(t=s\).
The reading above, and the proof of (i~bis)\(\Rightarrow\)(i) below, are those of the Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}.}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{prop:sga3-VIB-4.2:item:ii}(ii)}] \emph{The function}
  \[
    t\longmapsto\dim G_t
  \]
  \emph{is constant in a neighborhood of \(s\) in \(S\).}
\end{enumerate}

Evidently \textup{(i)} implies \textup{(i bis)}. By EGA~IV\(_3\), 14.3.3.1(ii), the set
of points of \(\underline{G_s^0}\) at which \(\pi\) is universally open is
closed in \(\underline{G_s^0}\); since \(\underline{G_s^0}\) is irreducible,
\textup{(i bis)} implies \textup{\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-4.2:item:i}{(i)}}. Let us show that \textup{(i)} implies
\textup{\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-4.2:item:ii}{(ii)}}. Let \(T\) be the set of \(t\in S\) such that
\(\dim G_t=\dim G_s\). By \hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-4.1]{4.1}, \(T\) is locally constructible, so
that (EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.10.1), to show that \(T\) is a neighborhood of \(s\), it
suffices to show that every generalization \(s'\) of \(s\) belongs to
\(T\). Now (EGA~IV\(_3\), 14.3.13~b\('\))), if \(x\) denotes the generic point of
\(\underline{G_s^0}\), one has, for every generalization \(s'\) of \(s\),
\[
  \dim G_{s'}\geq\dim_xG_s=\dim G_s;
\]
on the other hand, by \hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-4.1]{4.1}, the function \(s\mapsto\dim G_s\) being upper
semicontinuous, one has \(\dim G_{s'}\leq\dim G_s\); hence \(s'\in T\).

Let us show that \textup{(ii)} implies \textup{(i)}. Since \(\pi\) is locally of finite presentation, there exist an open
subset \(U\) of \(G\) containing \(\epsilon(s)\) and an open subset \(V\) of
\(S\) containing \(s\) such that \(\pi(U)\subset V\) and the morphism
\(\pi':U\to V\) induced by \(\pi\) is of finite presentation. Put
\[
  T=\epsilon^{-1}(U),
  \qquad
  W={\pi'}^{-1}(T)=U\cap\pi^{-1}(T).
\]
Then the morphism \(\pi'':W\to T\) induced by \(\pi'\) is of finite
presentation and admits as a section the morphism \(\epsilon'':T\to W\)
induced by \(\epsilon\). Moreover, for every \(t\in T\), \(\underline{G_t^0}\)
being irreducible, \(W\cap\underline{G_t^0}\) is dense in
\(\underline{G_t^0}\), hence irreducible, hence connected: it is therefore
the connected component of \({\pi''}^{-1}(t)\) containing \(\epsilon''(t)\).
By EGA~IV\(_3\), 10.7.1 and 10.6.2, \(W\cap\underline{G_t^0}\) being a dense
open subset of \(\underline{G_t^0}\), one has
\[
  \dim(W\cap\underline{G_t^0})=\dim\underline{G_t^0}=\dim G_t,
\]
so that the function \(t\mapsto\dim(W\cap\underline{G_t^0})\) is constant in a
neighborhood of \(s\) in \(T\). Let us finally show that, for every
\(t\in T\), \(W\cap\underline{G_t^0}\) is geometrically irreducible: let \(K\)
be an extension of \(\kappa(t)\); then
\[
  (W\cap\underline{G_t^0})\otimes_{\kappa(t)}K
    =
  \bigl(W\otimes_{\kappa(t)}K\bigr)
    \cap
  \bigl(\underline{G_t^0}\otimes_{\kappa(t)}K\bigr)
\]
is a nonempty open subset of \(\underline{G_t^0}\otimes_{\kappa(t)}K\), which is
irreducible, and is therefore irreducible.

We are then in a position to apply EGA~IV\(_3\), 15.6.6(ii), which asserts
that \(\pi''\) (hence \(\pi\)) is universally open at the points of
\(W\cap\underline{G_s^0}\). But, by EGA~IV\(_3\), 14.3.3.1(ii), the subset of
\(G_s\) consisting of the points at which \(\pi\) is universally open is
closed in \(G_s\); since it contains \(W\cap\underline{G_s^0}\), it contains
its closure \(\underline{G_s^0}\).

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}; our
formulation). Under the hypotheses of 4.2, conditions \textup{(i)} and \textup{(ii)} are also equivalent
to the following condition, which the re-edition attributes to O.~Gabber:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(iii)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-4.2-iii} \emph{for every morphism \(S'\to S\), every point
  \(s'\) of \(S'\) over \(s\), every point \(g\) of \(\underline{G'^0_{s'}}\) and every open
  neighborhood \(V\) of \(g\) in \(G'=G\times_SS'\), the set \(\pi'(V\cap\underline{G'^0})\) is a
  neighborhood of \(s'\) in \(S'\)}, where \(\pi':G'\to S'\) is the projection and
  \(\underline{G'^0}\) the union of the \(\underline{G'^0_{t'}}\) for \(t'\in S'\).
\end{enumerate}
Indeed, \textup{(iii)} implies \textup{(i)}, since \(\pi'(V)\) contains \(\pi'(V\cap\underline{G'^0})\).
Conversely, assume \textup{(ii)}. The hypotheses and \textup{(ii)} pass to \(G'\) at \(s'\): fibre
dimensions do not change under extension of the residue field, and \(\underline{G'^0_{s'}}\) is
\(\underline{G_s^0}\otimes_{\kappa(s)}\kappa(s')\) (see the proof of \hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-3.3]{3.3}). We may therefore
suppose \(S'=S\) and \(s'=s\). An open subset of \(G\) meeting \(\underline{G_s^0}\) contains its generic
point \(\eta\); so we may suppose \(g=\eta\). We may also suppose \(S\) affine and contained in
\(\epsilon^{-1}(U)\), where \(U\) is an affine open neighborhood of \(\epsilon(s)\) in \(G\) of finite
presentation over \(S\). As \(\underline{G_t^0}\cap U\) is connected, and open and closed in \(U_t\), for
every \(t\in S\), EGA~IV\(_3\), 9.7.12, applied to \(U\) and the section \(\epsilon\), shows that
\(\underline{G^0}\cap U\) is locally constructible in \(U\). Replacing \(V\) by an affine open
neighborhood of \(\eta\) in \(V\cap U\), we see that \(\pi(\underline{G^0}\cap V)\) is locally
constructible in \(S\) (EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.8.4); so by EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.10.1, it suffices to show that it
contains every generalization \(t\) of \(s\). Let
\(\xi\) be the generic point of \(\underline{G_t^0}\), and \(X\) the closure of \(\{\xi\}\) in \(G\); then
\(X_t=\underline{G_t^0}\), and \(X\) contains \(\epsilon(t)\), hence its specialization \(\epsilon(s)\). By
Chevalley's semicontinuity theorem (EGA~IV\(_3\), 13.1.3), applied to \(X\to S\), and by \textup{(a)},
\textup{(b)} and \textup{(ii)},
\[
  \dim_{\epsilon(s)}X_s\geq\dim_\xi X_t=\dim G_t=\dim G_s=\dim\underline{G_s^0}
  \geq\dim_{\epsilon(s)}X_s.
\]
So an irreducible component of \(X_s\) through \(\epsilon(s)\) has the dimension of the irreducible space
\(\underline{G_s^0}\), in which it lies; it is \(\underline{G_s^0}\), and \(\eta\in X\). As \(V\) is an
open neighborhood of \(\eta\), it contains the generalization \(\xi\) of \(\eta\); thus
\(\xi\in\underline{G^0}\cap V\) and \(t=\pi(\xi)\).
