\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}; our
formulation). The re-edition adds here a special case of 7.1 (its Corollary~7.1.1), which it uses in
the proof of 7.8. \emph{Let \(G\) be a \(k\)-group locally of finite type, and let \(A\) and \(B\) be
smooth \(k\)-group subschemes of \(G\) of finite type such that \(B\) normalizes \(A\). Then the set
\(A\cdot B\) is closed in \(G\), and the reduced subscheme of \(G\) with underlying space \(A\cdot B\)
is the closed group subscheme \(\Gamma_G(i_A,i_B)\) generated by the inclusions \(i_A\), \(i_B\) of
\(A\) and \(B\) in \(G\).} Indeed, \(B\) acts on \(A\) by the inner automorphisms of \(G\), and
\((a,b)\mapsto ab\) is a morphism of \(k\)-groups \(u\colon A\rtimes B\to G\), where \(A\rtimes B\)
is the semidirect product (Exposé~I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:definition:2-3-5}{2.3.5}): for points
\(a,a'\) of \(A\) and \(b,b'\) of \(B\), \(u\bigl((a,b)(a',b')\bigr)=a\,(ba'b^{-1})\,bb'=ab\,a'b'\). The
source of \(u\) is of finite type over \(k\), so \(u\) is quasi-compact, and its image \(A\cdot B\) is
closed (\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-1.2]{1.2}); by the proof of 1.2, this set is stable under the
multiplication and the inversion of \(G\). Let \(H\) be the reduced closed subscheme of \(G\) with
underlying space \(A\cdot B\). As \(A\rtimes B\) is smooth, \(H\) is geometrically reduced
(EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.10.7), so \(H\times_kH\) is reduced, and the multiplication and the inversion of \(G\)
restrict to morphisms \(H\times_kH\to H\) and \(H\to H\): \(H\) is a closed group subscheme of \(G\).
Since \(A\) and \(B\) are reduced and contained in \(A\cdot B\), \(i_A\) and \(i_B\) factor through
\(H\), so \(H\) contains \(\Gamma_G(i_A,i_B)\); conversely, the set \(A\cdot B\) is contained in the
group subscheme \(\Gamma_G(i_A,i_B)\), which is reduced (7.1 (ii)). Hence \(H=\Gamma_G(i_A,i_B)\).

\phantomsection\label{defrem:sga3-VIB-7.2}%
\noindent\textbf{Definition and remark 7.2.---}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}]\phantomsection\label{def:sga3-VIB-7.2-i}
  Let \(G\) be a \(k\)-group, let \((X_i)_{i\in I}\) be a
  family of geometrically reduced \(k\)-schemes, and, for every
  \(i\in I\), let \(f_i:X_i\to G\) be a \(k\)-morphism. The
  \emph{closed group subscheme of \(G\) generated by the family
  \((f_i)_{i\in I}\)} is the smallest closed group subscheme of
  \(G\) through which every \(f_i\) factors; in this subsection
  it is denoted
  \[
    \Gamma_G\bigl((f_i)_{i\in I}\bigr).
  \]
  When \(i\) is the inclusion of a subscheme \(X\) of \(G\),
  geometrically reduced over \(k\), we write \(\Gamma_G(X)\)
  instead of \(\Gamma_G(i)\).

\item[\textup{(ii)}]\phantomsection\label{rem:sga3-VIB-7.2-ii}
  If \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) are geometrically reduced
  \(k\)-schemes and \(f_1:X_1\to G\) and \(f_2:X_2\to G\) are
  \(k\)-morphisms such that the sets
  \[
    \overline{f_1(X_1)}
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \overline{f_2(X_2)}
  \]
  are equal, then it is clear that
  \[
    \Gamma_G(f_1)=\Gamma_G(f_2).
  \]

\item[\textup{(iii)}]\phantomsection\label{def:sga3-VIB-7.2-iii}
  Let \(E\) be a subset of \(G\) such that the reduced
  subscheme \(\overline E\) of \(G\) is geometrically reduced.
  The \emph{closed group subscheme of \(G\) generated by \(E\)}
  is the group subscheme
  \[
    \Gamma_G(E):=\Gamma_G(i),
  \]
  where \(i:\overline E\hookrightarrow G\) is the inclusion of
  that reduced subscheme.

\item[\textup{(iv)}]\phantomsection\label{rem:sga3-VIB-7.2-iv}
  When \(G\) is locally of finite type over \(k\), every group
  subscheme of \(G\) being closed (\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-1.4]{1.4}),
  we shall speak of the ``generated group subscheme'' rather
  than the ``generated closed group subscheme.''\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
  Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, adopts this convention for every \(k\)-group \(G\): every group subscheme of a
  group scheme over a field is closed (Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, \hyperref[sga3:VIA:0.5.2]{Addition after 0.5,
  (c)}).}

\item[\textup{(v)}]\phantomsection\label{rem:sga3-VIB-7.2-v}
  Let \(X\) be a geometrically reduced \(k\)-scheme and let
  \(f:X\to G\) be a \(k\)-morphism. Suppose that \(f(X)\)
  contains the unit element \(e\) of \(G\). Put
  \[
    X'^{\,1}=X\times_kX,
    \qquad
    f'^{\,1}=\mu\circ\bigl(f\times_k(c\circ f)\bigr),
  \]
  and, for \(n>1\), put
  \phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-7.2-v-recursion}%
  \[
    X'^{\,n}=X'^{\,1}\times_kX'^{\,n-1},
    \qquad
    f'^{\,n}
      =\mu\circ\bigl(f'^{\,1}\times_k f'^{\,n-1}\bigr).
  \]
  Then \(\Gamma_G(f)\) is the reduced subscheme of \(G\) whose
  underlying space is the closure of
  \[
    \bigcup_{n\geq1}f'^{\,n}(X'^{\,n}).
  \]

  Indeed, since \(f(X)\) contains \(e\), one has
  \phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-7.2-v-inclusions}%
  \[
    f(X)\subset f'^{\,1}(X'^{\,1})\subset f^2(X^2)
  \]
  (notation of \hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-7.1-ii]{7.1\textup{(ii)}}).
  Since one also has
  \[
    f^1(X^1)\subset f'^{\,1}(X'^{\,1})\subset f^2(X^2),
  \]
  there exists an integer \(n\) such that
  \(f^n(X^n)=\Gamma_G(f)\) if and only if there exists an
  integer \(m\) such that
  \(f'^{\,m}(X'^{\,m})=\Gamma_G(f)\).\footnote{Translator's note: taking products, these inclusions give
  \(f^n(X^n)\subset f'^{\,n}(X'^{\,n})\subset f^{2n}(X^{2n})\) for every \(n\geq1\), as the Gille--Polo
  re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, writes them.}

\item[\textup{(vi)}]\phantomsection\label{rem:sga3-VIB-7.2-vi}
  It follows from the preceding remark that, \emph{if \(X\) is a
  geometrically reduced and geometrically connected \(k\)-scheme,
  and if \(f:X\to G\) is a \(k\)-morphism such that \(f(X)\)
  contains the unit element of \(G\), then the \(k\)-group
  \(\Gamma_G(f)\) is connected.} Indeed, each \(X'^{\,n}\) is then
  connected, so that the union of the \(f'^{\,n}(X'^{\,n})\), each
  of which contains \(e\), is connected; hence so is its closure
  \(\Gamma_G(f)\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, adds
  that \(\Gamma_G(f)\) is then irreducible, as is used in the proof of 7.4: a connected group scheme locally of
  finite type over a field is irreducible (Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, \hyperref[prop:sga3-VIA-2.4]{2.4}), and
  so is every connected group scheme over a field (Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A},
  \hyperref[thm:sga3-VIA-2.6.5]{second Addition after 2.4.1, (e)}).}
\end{enumerate}
