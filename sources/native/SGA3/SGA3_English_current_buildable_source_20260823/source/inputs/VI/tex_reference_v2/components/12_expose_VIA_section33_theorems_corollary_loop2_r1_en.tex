\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}; our
formulation). (a) \emph{Complements to Theorem~3.2} (see the note to its statement). Keep the
hypotheses of 3.2, and let \(p\colon G\to X=F\backslash G\) be the canonical morphism.

By \hyperref[par:sga3-VIA-5.1]{5.1} below, \(G\) acts on \(X\) on the right, with \(p(e)\cdot g=p(g)\).
The morphism \(X\times_AG\to X\times_AX\), \((x,g)\mapsto(x\cdot g,x)\), is surjective: every point
of \(X\times_AX\) comes from a pair \((a,b)\) of points of \(G\) with values in a field, and
\(p(b)\cdot b^{-1}a=p(a)\). By (d) of the first Addition after 2.4.1 (for the left action
\((g,x)\mapsto x\cdot g^{-1}\)), \emph{the connected components of \(X\) are of finite type,
irreducible and of the same dimension}, namely \(\dim G-\dim H\), where \(H=p^{-1}(p(e))\) is the
stabilizer of \(p(e)\). The underlying space of \(H\) is the equivalence class \(u(F)\) of the origin
(3.2~(iii)), whose dimension is that of \(F\) by (c) of that Addition, the kernel of \(u\) being
finite. \emph{So these components have dimension \(\dim G-\dim F\).}

Suppose now that \(u\) is a monomorphism. By 3.2 (ii) and (iv), \(p\) is faithfully flat, and it is
locally of finite presentation, \(G\) and \(X\) being locally of finite type over \(A\).
Consequently \(X\) is flat over \(A\), and smooth over \(A\) if \(G\) is
(EGA~IV\textsubscript{4}, 17.7.7). Since \(p\) is a covering for the (fppf) topology and
\(G\times_XG\simeq F\times_AG\), \(X\) represents the quotient sheaf of \(G\) by \(F\) for that
topology, and \(G\) is an \(F\)-torsor over \(X\), locally trivial for it
(cf.\ Exposé~IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:3:4:3:1}{3.4.3.1},
\SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:5:1:6}{5.1.6} and \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:corollary:5:2:2}{5.2.2}). The
morphism \(u\) is a closed immersion ((c) of the first Addition after 2.4.1), and \(X\) is
separated: by 3.2~(iv), the base change of the diagonal of \(X\) by the faithfully flat morphism
\(p\times p\), which is locally of finite presentation, is identified with the morphism
\((x,y)\mapsto(u(x)y,y)\) of \(F\times_AG\) into \(G\times_AG\), the composite of \(u\times\mathrm{id}_G\)
and of the automorphism \((a,b)\mapsto(ab,b)\), hence a closed immersion; so the diagonal of \(X\)
is a closed immersion (EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 2.7.1). If \(F\) is normal in \(G\), \(X\) is an
\(A\)-group (5.2).

(b) \emph{The case where \(G\) is not flat.} The re-edition adds a subsection 3.3, showing that when
\(F\) is a flat closed subgroup of \(G\), the flatness of \(G\) may be dropped, as M.~Raynaud observes
(\emph{Passage au quotient par une relation d'équivalence plate}, in \emph{Proceedings of a
Conference on Local Fields} (Driebergen, 1966), Springer, 1967, 78--85) and S.~Anantharaman proves
(\emph{Schémas en groupes, espaces homogènes et espaces algébriques sur une base de dimension~1},
Mém.\ Soc.\ Math.\ France \textbf{33} (1973), 5--79, Th.~4). The proof rests on the following
theorem of Grothendieck (M.~Demazure, P.~Gabriel, \emph{Groupes algébriques}, I, Masson and
North-Holland, 1970, III, \S\,2, 7.1): \emph{let \(R\rightrightarrows X\) be an equivalence relation on
an \(A\)-scheme \(X\) whose two projections are faithfully flat and of finite presentation, and let
\(X_0\) be a closed subscheme of \(X\), saturated for \(R\) and defined by a nilpotent ideal; if the
(fppf) quotient sheaf of \(X_0\) by the induced relation is representable by an \(A\)-scheme, so is
the quotient sheaf of \(X\) by \(R\).}

\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-VIA-3.3.2}%
\emph{Let \(G\) be an \(A\)-group locally of finite type, not necessarily flat, and \(F\) a closed
subgroup of \(G\), flat over \(A\). Then the (fppf) quotient sheaf of \(G\) by \(F\) is represented
by a separated \(A\)-scheme \(F\backslash G\), locally of finite type; the canonical morphism
\(p\colon G\to F\backslash G\) is faithfully flat and locally of finite presentation;
\((x,g)\mapsto(xg,g)\) is an isomorphism of \(F\times_AG\) onto \(G\times_{F\backslash G}G\), so that
\(G\) is an \(F\)-torsor over \(F\backslash G\), locally trivial for the (fppf) topology; and
\(F\times_AG\rightrightarrows G\to F\backslash G\) is exact in the category of ringed spaces.}
\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-VIA-3.3.2:item:iii}%
\emph{If \(G\) is flat \textup{(}resp.\ of finite type, resp.\ smooth\textup{)} over \(A\), so is
\(F\backslash G\). Moreover \(G\) acts on \(F\backslash G\) on the right, the connected components of
\(F\backslash G\) are of finite type, irreducible and of dimension \(\dim G-\dim F\), and if \(F\) is
normal in \(G\), \(F\backslash G\) has a unique structure of \(A\)-group for which \(p\) is a
homomorphism.} (This is Theorem~3.3.2 of the re-edition, which refers to Anantharaman, loc.\ cit.,
for the proof.) When \(F\) is of finite type, one may argue as follows: apply Grothendieck's theorem
to \(X=G\), to the relation defined by \(F\), whose projections \(\operatorname{pr}_2\) and \(\lambda\)
of \(F\times_AG\) onto \(G\) are then faithfully flat and of finite presentation, and to
\(X_0=G\otimes_Ak\), which is defined by the nilpotent ideal \(\mathfrak m\mathcal O_G\) and is saturated;
over the field \(k\), \(F\otimes_Ak\) and \(G\otimes_Ak\) are flat, so (a) and Theorem~3.2 give the
representability of the quotient of \(X_0\). Then \(p\) is a covering for the (fppf) topology with
\(G\times_{F\backslash G}G\simeq F\times_AG\), and the remaining assertions are proved as in (a):
\(F\backslash G\) is locally of finite type, as \((F\backslash G)\otimes_Ak\) is the quotient of
\(G\otimes_Ak\) by \(F\otimes_Ak\) ((a) of the first Addition after 2.4.1); it is separated; it is flat,
of finite type or smooth when \(G\) is; the exactness in ringed spaces follows from (c) below and from
the openness of \(p\); and the action of \(G\), the dimension of the components and the group
structure come from the corresponding structures of the quotient sheaf, from (d) of the first
Addition after 2.4.1 and from the computation in (a). (When \(F\) is not flat, the quotient sheaf need
not be representable; the re-edition refers to Demazure--Gabriel, loc.\ cit., III, \S\,3, 3.3, for an
example.)

\phantomsection\label{cor:sga3-VIA-3.3.3}%
(c) (Corollary~3.3.3 of the re-edition.) \emph{Let \(H\) be a closed subgroup of \(G\), flat over
\(A\), \(p\colon G\to G/H\) the quotient by the right action of \(H\) \textup{(}deduced from (b) by
\(g\mapsto g^{-1}\)\textup{)}, and \(U\) an open subset of \(G/H\). Then \(\mathcal O(U)\) is the set
of \(\varphi\in\mathcal O(p^{-1}(U))\) such that \(\varphi(gh)=\varphi(g)\) for every \(A\)-scheme \(S\)
and all \(g\in G(S)\), \(h\in H(S)\).} Indeed, \(p\) is a covering for the (fppf) topology, for which
the structure sheaf is a sheaf, and \(p^{-1}(U)\times_U p^{-1}(U)\simeq p^{-1}(U)\times_AH\) by (b); a
function on \(U\) is therefore the same as a function on \(p^{-1}(U)\) whose two pull-backs to
\(p^{-1}(U)\times_AH\), by \((g,h)\mapsto gh\) and by \((g,h)\mapsto g\), agree (compare
Exposé~IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:remark:3:3:3:2}{3.3.3}).
