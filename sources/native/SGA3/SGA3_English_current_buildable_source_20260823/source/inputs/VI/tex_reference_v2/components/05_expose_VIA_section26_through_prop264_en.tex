
\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}; our
formulation). The re-edition then adds a paragraph 2.6, taken from D.~Perrin, \emph{Schémas en groupes
quasi-compacts sur un corps}, Publ.\ Math.\ Orsay, 1975, II, \S\S\,1--2, with complements due to
O.~Gabber (in particular (f) below, which is used in Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, \S\,5). It extends
results of this Exposé to group schemes over a field that are not assumed locally of finite type. In
what follows, \(k\) is a field, \(k'\) a perfect closure of \(k\), and \(G\) a \(k\)-group scheme.
For a \(k\)-scheme \(X\) on which \(G\) acts, consider the condition
\[
  (\star)\qquad G\times_kX\longrightarrow X\times_kX,\quad (g,x)\longmapsto(gx,x),
  \ \text{is surjective};
\]
it holds when \(G\) acts on itself by left translations, and it is preserved by every extension of
the base field. In topological questions we may replace \(k\) by \(k'\)
(\(\operatorname{Spec}k'\to\operatorname{Spec}k\) is a universal homeomorphism), and then \(G\) and
\(X\) by \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) and \(X_{\mathrm{red}}\), which are geometrically reduced, without
affecting \((\star)\). If \(\gamma\) is a point of \(G\) with values in a field \(K\), we write
\(\gamma x\) for its action on the points \(x\) of \(X\) with values in \(K\).

(a) (Lemma~2.6.0 of the re-edition, from Perrin, loc.\ cit., II, 2.1.1.) \emph{If \(X\) is an
irreducible \(k\)-scheme and \(K\) an extension of \(k\), every irreducible component \(X'\) of
\(X_K\) maps onto \(X\).} Let \(L\subset K\) be generated by a transcendence basis of \(K\) over
\(k\). The scheme \(X\times_k\operatorname{Spec}k[T]\) (\(T\) a family of indeterminates) is
irreducible (EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 4.5.8), and \(X_L\) is homeomorphic to its subspace lying over
the generic point of \(\operatorname{Spec}k[T]\), which contains its generic point; so \(X_L\) is
irreducible. A flat morphism maps maximal points to maximal points, generizations lifting along it
(EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 2.3.4); hence the generic point of \(X'\) maps to that of \(X_L\), and as
\(X_K\to X_L\) is integral, hence closed, \(X'\) maps onto \(X_L\), which maps onto \(X\).

(b) (Proposition~2.6.1 of the re-edition.) \emph{Let \(X\) be a \(G\)-scheme satisfying \((\star)\).
\textup{(i)} For every extension \(K\) of \(k\), every point of \(X_K\) lies on only one irreducible
component of \(X_K\). \textup{(ii)} The local rings of \((X_{k'})_{\mathrm{red}}\) are normal.
\textup{(iii)} If \(\eta\) is a maximal point of \((X_{k'})_{\mathrm{red}}\), its closure \(C\) is a
scheme over the algebraic closure \(L\) of \(k\) in \(\kappa(\eta)\), and is geometrically
irreducible over \(L\). \textup{(iv)} If \(x\in X(k)\), the irreducible component of \(X\) through
\(x\) is geometrically irreducible over \(k\).} For (i) and (ii) we may suppose \(K=k\), \(k\)
perfect, and \(G\), \(X\) reduced. If \(\eta\) is a maximal point of \(X\), then
\(\mathcal O_{X,\eta}=\kappa(\eta)\), and for every extension \(K\) of \(k\) the local rings of
\(\kappa(\eta)\otimes_kK\) are normal (EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 6.14.2), so that \(X_K\) is normal at
the points over \(\eta\). Let \(z\in X\). By \((\star)\), there are a field \(K\), a point
\(\eta'\in X(K)\) over \(\eta\) and \(\gamma\in G(K)\) such that \(\gamma\eta'\) lies over \(z\) (take
a point of \(G\times_kX\) over \((z,\eta)\)); \(\gamma\) acts on \(X_K\) by an automorphism, so
\(X_K\) is normal at the point defined by \(\gamma\eta'\), and as \(X_K\to X\) is faithfully flat at
that point, \(X\) is normal at \(z\) (EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 2.1.13). This proves (ii), and (i), a
normal local ring being a domain. For (iii): by (ii), the local rings of \(C\) are those of
\((X_{k'})_{\mathrm{red}}\), so \(C\) is integral and normal; an element of \(\kappa(\eta)\) algebraic
over \(k\) is integral over each local ring of \(C\), hence belongs to it; so
\(L\subset\Gamma(C,\mathcal O_C)\), and \(C\), whose function field contains \(L\) as an algebraically
closed subfield, is geometrically irreducible over \(L\) (EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 4.5.9). For (iv),
the point of \((X_{k'})_{\mathrm{red}}\) over \(x\) is rational over \(k'\) and lies on a component
\(C\), which is an \(L\)-scheme; hence \(L=k'\), and \(C\), and with it the component of \(X\)
through \(x\), is geometrically irreducible.

(c) (Corollary~2.6.2 of the re-edition.) \emph{The unit \(e\) lies on only one irreducible component
\(C^0\) of \(G\) \textup{(}given its reduced structure\textup{)}; \(C^0\) is geometrically irreducible
over \(k\), stable under \((g,h)\mapsto gh^{-1}\), and quasi-compact.} The first two assertions are
(b)~(i) and (iv), for \(G\) acting on itself. As \(C^0\times_kC^0\) is irreducible
(EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 4.5.8), its image under \((g,h)\mapsto gh^{-1}\) is an irreducible set
containing \(e\), hence contained in \(C^0\). So \((C^0_{k'})_{\mathrm{red}}\) is an irreducible
subgroup scheme of \((G_{k'})_{\mathrm{red}}\), hence quasi-compact (Addition after 0.5, (b)), and
\(C^0\) is quasi-compact.

(d) (2.6.3 and Proposition~2.6.4 of the re-edition.) Recall (EGA~IV\textsubscript{1}, 1.9.5~(v) and
1.10.1) that if \(U\) is a retrocompact open subset of a scheme \(Y\), the closure \(\overline U\)
consists of the specializations of the points of \(U\): it is the union of the closures of the
maximal points of \(Y\) lying in \(U\). Hence, if \(Y\) is quasi-separated and \(\eta\) is a maximal
point of \(Y\), the intersection of the closed neighbourhoods of \(\eta\) is
\(\overline{\{\eta\}}\): if \(y\notin\overline{\{\eta\}}\), take an affine open \(V\ni y\) not
containing \(\eta\); it is retrocompact and \(\eta\notin\overline V\), so \(Y-V\) is a closed
neighbourhood of \(\eta\) not containing \(y\).
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIA-2.6.4}%
\emph{Let \(X\) be a \(G\)-scheme satisfying \((\star)\) and \(U\) an open subset of \(X\). Then
\(C^0U\), the image of \(C^0\times_kU\) under the action, is open, and it is the union of the
irreducible components of \(X\) whose generic points lie in \(U\). Consequently, if \(X\) is
irreducible, it is quasi-compact; and if \(U\) is retrocompact in \(X\), then \(C^0U=\overline U\),
which is therefore open and closed.} The set \(C^0U\) is the union, over the points \(g\) of \(C^0\),
of the images in \(X\) of the open subsets \(gU_{\kappa(g)}\) of \(X_{\kappa(g)}\), and the
projections \(X_{\kappa(g)}\to X\) are open; so \(C^0U\) is open. If \(Z\) is an irreducible
component of \(X\) with generic point \(\eta\), the image of the irreducible space \(C^0\times_kZ\)
contains \(\eta\), hence lies in \(Z\) by (b)~(i); so \(C^0U\) lies in the union of the components
whose generic points lie in \(U\). Conversely, let \(Z\) be such a component and \(z\in Z\); to show
that \(z\in C^0U\) we may suppose \(k\) perfect and \(G\), \(X\) reduced. Put \(K=\kappa(z)\). The
orbit map \(\mu_z\colon G_K\to X_K\) of the point \(z\in X(K)\) is surjective by \((\star)\), and flat
at the points over the maximal points of the reduced scheme \(X_K\); so it is flat by (b) of the
preceding Addition, and maps maximal points to maximal points. Let \(\omega\) be the generic point of
the irreducible scheme \(C^0_K\). Now \(z\) lies in the closure of a maximal point \(\beta\) of
\(Z_K\); \(\beta\) lies over \(\eta\), and it is maximal in \(X_K\), generizations lifting along the
flat morphism \(X_K\to X\); so \(\overline{\{\beta\}}\) is the only irreducible component of \(X_K\)
through \(z\) ((b)~(i)). The set \(\mu_z(C^0_K)\) is irreducible and contains \(z\), and
\(\mu_z(\omega)\) is a maximal point; hence \(\mu_z(\omega)=\beta\). Thus, with values in
\(L=\kappa(\omega)\), the point \(\omega z\) lies over \(\eta\in U\), and \(z=\omega^{-1}(\omega z)\)
lies in \(C^0U\), \(C^0\) being stable under inversion. If \(X\) is irreducible and \(U\) is a
nonempty affine open subset, \(X=C^0U\) is the image of \(C^0\times_kU\), which is quasi-compact by
(c). If \(U\) is retrocompact, \(\overline U\) is the union of the closures of the maximal points lying
in \(U\), that is, \(C^0U\).

\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-VIA-2.6.5}%
(e) (Theorem~2.6.5 of the re-edition, due to D.~Perrin, loc.\ cit., II, 2.4; see also D.~Perrin,
\emph{Approximation des schémas en groupes, quasi-compacts sur un corps}, Bull.\ Soc.\ Math.\ France
\textbf{104} (1976), 323--335, 4.1.1.) \emph{There is a unique subscheme \(G^0\) of \(G\) with
underlying space \(C^0\) such that \(G^0\to G\) is a flat closed immersion \textup{(}that is,
\(\mathcal O_{G^0,g}=\mathcal O_{G,g}\) for \(g\in C^0\)\textup{)}. It is a subgroup scheme of \(G\),
quasi-compact and geometrically irreducible, and it is characteristic: for every \(k\)-scheme \(S\),
every automorphism of the \(S\)-group \(G_S\) maps \(G^0_S\) onto itself. If \(G\) is connected,
\(G^0=G\).} The group \(G\) is separated (0.3). Let \(\omega\) be the generic point of \(C^0\). For an
affine open \(U\ni\omega\), \(\overline U\) is open and closed by (d) (applied to \(G\) acting on
itself); the intersection of these \(\overline U\) is \(\overline{\{\omega\}}=C^0\), every closed
neighbourhood of \(\omega\) containing one of them (d). Let \(\mathcal J\) be the sum of the ideals
of the open and closed subschemes \(\overline U\): a quasi-coherent ideal of \(\mathcal O_G\) whose
stalk is zero at the points of \(C^0\) and equal to \(\mathcal O_{G,g}\) at the other points \(g\).
The closed subscheme \(G^0\) it defines has the required properties, and it is the only one, its
ideal being determined by its stalks. A morphism \(T\to G\) that maps \(T\) into \(C^0\) factors
through \(G^0\), since \(\mathcal J\mathcal O_T=0\); this applies to the multiplication and the
inversion restricted to \(G^0\times_kG^0\) and to \(G^0\) (by (c)), and to the unit section, so
\(G^0\) is a subgroup scheme; it is quasi-compact and geometrically irreducible by (c). Let \(\phi\)
be an automorphism of the \(S\)-group \(G_S\). For \(s\in S\), \(C^0_{\kappa(s)}\) is the only
irreducible component of \(G_{\kappa(s)}\) through \(e\) (it is irreducible, and its generic point is
maximal, \(G_{\kappa(s)}\to G\) being flat), so \(\phi_s\) maps it onto itself; hence \(\phi\) maps
the underlying space of \(G^0_S\) into itself, and as the ideal of \(G^0_S\) in \(G_S\), generated by
\(\mathcal J\), vanishes at the points of \(G^0_S\), \(\phi\) maps \(G^0_S\) into \(G^0_S\); so does
\(\phi^{-1}\). Finally, if \(G\) is connected, every \(\overline U\) is equal to \(G\), so
\(\mathcal J=0\).

\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIA-2.6.6}%
(f) (Proposition~2.6.6 of the re-edition, due to O.~Gabber.) \emph{Let \(X\) be a quasi-separated
\(G\)-scheme satisfying \((\star)\), and \(C\) a connected component of \(X\). Then \(C\) is
irreducible. If \(C\) has a rational point \(x\), then \(C\) is geometrically irreducible over \(k\),
and \(g\mapsto gx\) maps \(G^0\) onto \(C\).} Let \(Y\) be an irreducible component of \(X\) meeting
\(C\), hence contained in \(C\), and \(\eta\) its generic point. For every affine open \(U\ni\eta\),
which is retrocompact, \(\overline U=C^0U\) is open and closed (d) and meets \(C\), so it contains
\(C\); and the intersection of these \(\overline U\) is \(Y\) (d). So \(C=Y\). Now let
\(x\in C(k)\): \(C\) is geometrically irreducible by (b)~(iv), and we may suppose \(k\) perfect and
\(G\), \(X\) reduced. By the proof of (d), the orbit map of \(x\) maps the generic point of \(G^0\) to
the generic point \(\eta\) of \(C\), and if \(z\in C\) and \(K=\kappa(z)\), the orbit map \(\mu_z\) maps
the generic point \(\omega\) of \(C^0_K\) to that of \(C_K\). With values in \(L=\kappa(\omega)\), the
point \(\omega z\) lies over \(\eta\). The points of \(G^0_L\) over the generic point of \(G^0\) map
onto the points of \(C_L\) over \(\eta\), as \(\kappa(\eta)\) is a subfield of the residue field of
that generic point; so there are an extension \(L'\) of \(L\) and a point \(h\in G^0(L')\) such that
\(hx=\omega z\) in \(C(L')\). Then \(z=(\omega^{-1}h)x\), with \(\omega^{-1}h\in G^0(L')\).

The re-edition also states that if \(L\) is the algebraic closure of \(k\) in the function field of
\(C\), then \(C\) is an \(L\)-scheme, geometrically irreducible over \(L\), and that
\(G^0_L\times_LC\to C\times_LC\) is surjective. This holds when \(k\) is perfect and \(X\) is
reduced: the first assertion is then (b)~(iii); and for a point \(z\) of \(C\), the connected
component of \(X_{\kappa(z)}\) through the rational point defined by \(z\) is \(C\otimes_L\kappa(z)\),
so that the second follows from what precedes, applied over \(\kappa(z)\). For an imperfect field the
first assertion must be read for \((C_{k'})_{\mathrm{red}}\), as in (b)~(iii): the group \(G\) of
example (b) of the Addition after 1.3.1, acting on itself, has the rational point \(e\), while the
function field of \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) contains the \(p\)-th root \(X/Y\) of \(t\); so \(L\neq k\), and
\(C=G\) is not an \(L\)-scheme.

(g) (Remark~2.6.7 of the re-edition.) Without a rational point, \(G^0\times_kC\to C\times_kC\) need
not be surjective: the constant group \(\{\pm1\}_{\mathbb R}\) acting on \(X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C\)
by conjugation satisfies \((\star)\), and \(X\) is connected, but \(G^0\) is trivial while
\(X\times_{\mathbb R}X\) has two points; here \(L=\mathbb C\), and
\(G^0_{\mathbb C}\times_{\mathbb C}X\to X\times_{\mathbb C}X\) is an isomorphism.
