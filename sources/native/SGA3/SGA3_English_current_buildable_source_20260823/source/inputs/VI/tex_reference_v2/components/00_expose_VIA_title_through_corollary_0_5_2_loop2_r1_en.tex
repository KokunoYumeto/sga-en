\markboth{EXPOS\'E VI\textsubscript{A}. GENERALITIES ON ALGEBRAIC GROUPS}%
  {EXPOS\'E VI\textsubscript{A}. GENERALITIES ON ALGEBRAIC GROUPS}

\begin{center}
  \textit{by P. Gabriel}
\end{center}

Throughout this
chapter, \(A\) will denote an Artinian local ring with residue field \(k\).
A group scheme \(G\) over \(\operatorname{Spec} A\) will be called simply
an \(A\)-\emph{group}. This \(A\)-group is said to be \emph{locally of
finite type} if its underlying scheme is locally of finite type over \(A\);
it is said to be \emph{algebraic} if its underlying scheme is of finite
type over \(A\).

\section*{0. Preliminary Remarks}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIA:0}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{0. Preliminary Remarks}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.1.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIA:0.1}
Let us first consider a group scheme \(G\) over an arbitrary scheme \(S\).
We call the structural morphism
\[
  \mu:G\times_S G\longrightarrow G
\]
\emph{multiplication}, and we call the morphism \(c:G\to G\), defined by
\(c(T)(x)=x^{-1}\) for every scheme \(T\) over \(S\) and every
\(x\in G(T)\), \emph{inversion}. If \(U\) and \(V\) are subsets of the
underlying set of \(G\), we denote by \(U\cdot V\) the image under
multiplication of the subset of \(G\times_S G\) consisting of those points
whose first projection belongs to \(U\) and whose second projection belongs
to \(V\). Likewise, the notations \(U^{-1}\) and \(c(U)\) are equivalent.

Let \(\operatorname{pr}_1\) be the projection of \(G\times_S G\) onto the
first factor, and let
\[
  \sigma:G\times_S G\longrightarrow G\times_S G
\]
be the morphism with components \(\operatorname{pr}_1\) and \(\mu\). For
every \(S\)-scheme \(T\), the map \(\sigma(T)\) is
\[
  (x,y)\longmapsto (x,xy);
\]
it follows that \(\sigma\) is an automorphism. The composite of this
automorphism with the projection \(\operatorname{pr}_2\) of
\(G\times_S G\) onto the second factor is the multiplication morphism.
When \(G\) is flat over \(S\), \(\operatorname{pr}_2\), and hence \(\mu\),
are flat morphisms; when \(G\) is smooth over \(S\),
\(\operatorname{pr}_2\), and hence \(\mu\), are smooth morphisms, and so
on.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.2.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIA:0.2}
From now on we assume that \(S\) is the spectrum of an Artinian local ring
\(A\) with residue field \(k\). We denote by
\((\mathbf{Sch}_{/k})_{\mathrm{red}}\) the category of reduced schemes over
\(k\). For every scheme \(X\) over \(A\), the reduced scheme
\(X_{\mathrm{red}}\) is an object of
\((\mathbf{Sch}_{/k})_{\mathrm{red}}\), and the functor
\[
  X\longmapsto X_{\mathrm{red}}
\]
is right adjoint to the inclusion of
\((\mathbf{Sch}_{/k})_{\mathrm{red}}\) into \(\mathbf{Sch}_{/A}\).
It follows that, for every \(A\)-group \(G\), \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is a
group in the category
\((\mathbf{Sch}_{/k})_{\mathrm{red}}\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, points out here that \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) need not be a
\(k\)-group scheme. In \((\mathbf{Sch}_{/k})_{\mathrm{red}}\) the product of \(X\) and \(Y\) is
\((X\times_kY)_{\mathrm{red}}\), so the group law of \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is only a morphism
\((G_{\mathrm{red}}\times_kG_{\mathrm{red}})_{\mathrm{red}}\to G_{\mathrm{red}}\); when \(k\) is not perfect,
\(G_{\mathrm{red}}\times_kG_{\mathrm{red}}\) need not be reduced, and \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) need not be a
subgroup scheme of \(G\). Examples are given in the Addition that follows 1.3.1.}

Conversely, \emph{if \(k\) is a perfect field}, the inclusion of
\((\mathbf{Sch}_{/k})_{\mathrm{red}}\) into \(\mathbf{Sch}_{/k}\)
commutes with products, so that the groups in
\((\mathbf{Sch}_{/k})_{\mathrm{red}}\) are identified with the
group schemes over \(k\) whose underlying schemes are reduced. In
particular, for every \(k\)-group \(G\), \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is a group subscheme
of \(G\); this subgroup is not normal in \(G\) in general: for example, if \(k\) is a field of characteristic \(3\), the constant
group \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)_k\) acts nontrivially on the diagonalizable
group \(D_k(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)\) (\SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:4-1}{Expos\'e I, 4.1} and \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:4-4}{4.4}); if
\(G\) denotes the semidirect product of \(D_k(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)\) by
\((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)_k\) defined by this action,
\(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is identified with \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)_k\) and
is not normal in \(G\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, mentions a connected example. If \(k\) has characteristic \(p>0\), let
\(\mathbb G_{m,k}\) act on \(\alpha_{p,k}\) by \(t\cdot x=tx\), and let \(G=\alpha_{p,k}\rtimes\mathbb G_{m,k}\),
with product \((x,s)(y,t)=(x+sy,st)\). The underlying space of \(G\) is that of \(\mathbb G_{m,k}\), so
\(G\) is connected, and \(G_{\mathrm{red}}=\{0\}\times\mathbb G_{m,k}\). Conjugation gives
\((x,1)(0,t)(x,1)^{-1}=((1-t)x,t)\); with values in \(R=k[\varepsilon,t,t^{-1}]/(\varepsilon^2)\) and
\(x=\varepsilon\), this element does not lie in \(G_{\mathrm{red}}(R)\), so \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) is not
normal in \(G\).}

If \(k\) is an arbitrary field and \(H\) is a group in the category
\((\mathbf{Sch}_{/k})_{\mathrm{red}}\), then
\[
  (H\otimes_k k^{p^{-\infty}})_{\mathrm{red}}
\]
is a group scheme over \(k^{p^{-\infty}}\). Since
\(H\otimes_k k^{p^{-\infty}}\) has the same underlying
topological space as
\((H\otimes_k k^{p^{-\infty}})_{\mathrm{red}}\), we see that the groups in \((\mathbf{Sch}_{/k})_{\mathrm{red}}\) share
with \(k\)-groups certain topological properties that are invariant under
extension of the base field. For example, it will follow from \hyperref[sga3:VIA:0.3]{0.3} and the
preceding remarks that every group in
\((\mathbf{Sch}_{/k})_{\mathrm{red}}\) is separated.

In what follows, we shall encounter groups in
\((\mathbf{Sch}_{/k})_{\mathrm{red}}\) whenever we deal with a nonempty
locally closed subset \(U\) of an \(A\)-group \(G\) such that
\[
  U\cdot U=U
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  U^{-1}=U.
\]
Indeed, the reduced subscheme of \(G\) defined by \(U\) is then a group in
\((\mathbf{Sch}_{/k})_{\mathrm{red}}\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.3.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIA:0.3}
\textit{An \(A\)-group \(G\) is always separated}: indeed, the diagonal in \(G\times_A G\) is identified with the functor from
\((\mathbf{Sch}_{/A})^\circ\) to \(\mathbf{Sets}\) which associates to
every \(A\)-scheme \(S\) the inverse image of the identity element of
\(G(S)\) under the map
\[
  \varphi(S):G(S)\times G(S)\longrightarrow G(S),
  \qquad (x,y)\longmapsto x\cdot y^{-1}.
\]
We therefore have a cartesian square

\begin{center}
  \phantomsection
  \label{sga3:VIA:0.3-cartesian-square}
  \begin{tikzcd}[row sep=large,column sep=large]
    G\times_A G
      \arrow[r,"\varphi"]
    &
    G
    \\
    G
      \arrow[u,"\text{diagonal morphism}"]
      \arrow[r]
    &
    \operatorname{Spec}A
      \arrow[u,"\text{unit section}"']
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
so that the diagonal morphism, which is obtained from a closed immersion
by base change, is itself a closed immersion.\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text takes for granted that the unit
section \(e\) is a closed immersion; the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, adds a
proof. For instance: let \(g\) be the image of the point of \(\operatorname{Spec}A\). If
\(U=\operatorname{Spec}B\) is an affine open subset of \(G\) containing \(g\), then \(e\) factors through
\(U\), and the corresponding ring homomorphism \(B\to A\) is a retraction of the structure map
\(A\to B\), hence surjective; so \(\{g\}\) is closed in every affine open subset of \(G\) (it does not
meet those that do not contain \(g\)), hence closed in \(G\), and \(e\) is a closed immersion. Only the
fact that \(\operatorname{Spec}A\) is a single point is used: every group scheme over a discrete scheme
is separated (Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-5.2}{5.2}), whereas over a scheme \(S\) with a
non-isolated closed point \(s\), two copies of \(S\) glued along \(S-\{s\}\) form a non-separated
\(S\)-group (Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}, \SGARefLink{ctr:sga3-VIB-5.6.4}{5.6.4}).}


\medskip
\noindent\textbf{0.4.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIA:0.4}
Let \(G\) be an \(A\)-group. We shall say that a point \(g\) of \(G\) is
\emph{strictly rational over \(A\)} if there exists an \(A\)-morphism
\[
  s:\operatorname{Spec}A\longrightarrow G
\]
which sends the unique point of \(\operatorname{Spec}A\) to \(g\).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, makes this definition for any \(A\)-scheme \(G\) and adds that such a
point \(g\) is closed, with \(\kappa(g)=k\). Indeed, the argument of the note to 0.3 shows that \(s\) is
a closed immersion with image \(\{g\}\), and the local homomorphism \(\mathcal O_{G,g}\to A\) defined
by \(s\), being a retraction of \(A\to\mathcal O_{G,g}\), induces an isomorphism of \(\kappa(g)\)
onto \(k\).} We know
that such an \(s\) defines an automorphism \(r_s\) of the
scheme \(G\) over \(A\), called \emph{right translation by \(s\)}. For
every morphism \(\pi:S\to\operatorname{Spec}A\), the automorphism
\(r_s(\pi)\) of \(G(S)\) is defined by
\[
  x\longmapsto x\cdot G(\pi)(s),
  \qquad x\in G(S).
\]
Similarly, \(\ell_s\) denotes \emph{left translation by \(s\)}, namely
the automorphism of \(G\) defined by
\[
  \ell_s(\pi)(x)=G(\pi)(s)\cdot x,
  \qquad x\in G(S).
\]

The schemes \(G\otimes_A k\) and \(G\) have the same underlying
topological space \(\underline G\), \(G\otimes_A k\) is a \(k\)-group,
and \(s\otimes_A k\) depends only on \(g\), not on \(s\). Consequently,
the automorphisms of \(\underline G\) induced by \(r_s\) and \(\ell_s\)
(or by \(r_{s\otimes k}\) and \(\ell_{s\otimes k}\)) depend only on \(g\),
not on \(s\). Thus, if \(P\) is a subset of \(\underline G\), we may write
\(P\cdot g\) and \(g\cdot P\) instead of \(r_s(P)\) and \(\ell_s(P)\), in agreement with
\hyperref[sga3:VIA:0.1]{0.1}.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}, adds (its Remark~0.4.1) that this notation is compatible with base
change. If \(A\to A'\) is a homomorphism of Artinian local rings and \(G'=G\otimes_AA'\), the fibre
of \(G'\to G\) at \(g\) is the spectrum of the local Artinian ring \(A'/\mathfrak m_AA'\), so
\(G'\) has exactly one point \(g'\) over \(g\); it is strictly rational over \(A'\) (use
\(s\otimes_AA'\)), and since \(r_{s\otimes_AA'}\) is deduced from \(r_s\) by base change, \(P'\cdot g'\)
is the inverse image of \(P\cdot g\) when \(P'\) is the inverse image of \(P\).}

\medskip
\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:vi:proposition:0-5}\textbf{0.5.}
\phantomsection
\label{sga3:VIA:0.5}
Consider now \emph{two everywhere dense open subsets \(U\) and \(V\) of an
\(A\)-group \(G\) locally of finite type. The product \(U\cdot V\)
\textup{(\hyperref[sga3:VIA:0.1]{0.1})} is then equal to the whole underlying space of \(G\)}:
indeed, since \(G\) and \(G\otimes_A k\) have the same underlying space, we
may suppose, replacing \(A\) by \(k\) and \(G\) by \(G\otimes_A k\), that
\(A\) is a field \(k\). In this case the multiplication morphism is flat
and locally of finite type (0.1), hence open, so that \(U\cdot V\) is an
open subset of \(G\). If \(\overline k\) is an algebraic closure of \(k\),
we evidently have
\[
  (U\cdot V)\otimes_k\overline k
  =(U\otimes_k\overline k)\cdot(V\otimes_k\overline k);
\]
by SGA~1, VIII, \S~4, it therefore suffices to prove that
\[
  G\otimes_k\overline k
  =(U\otimes_k\overline k)\cdot(V\otimes_k\overline k),
\]
and we are reduced to the case in which \(k\) is algebraically closed. In
this last case, it suffices to prove that \(U\cdot V\) contains every
closed point \(x\) of \(G\). If \(k\) is algebraically closed, such an
\(x\) is strictly rational, and the everywhere dense open subsets
\(U^{-1}\cdot x\) and \(V\) have a closed point \(v\) in common; if \(u\)
is the element of \(G(k)\) defined by the equality \(x=u\cdot v\), then
\(u^{-1}\cdot x\) belongs to \(U^{-1}\cdot x\), so that \(u\) belongs to
\(U\), which proves the assertion.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{A}; our
formulation). The re-edition states 0.5 for an arbitrary \(A\)-group and adds two consequences
(its Corollaries 0.5.1 and 0.5.2, the second of which, it notes, is used implicitly in Exposé~VIII,
6.7).

(a) \emph{If \(G\) is any \(A\)-group and \(U\), \(V\) are dense open subsets of \(G\), then
\(U\cdot V\) is the whole underlying space of \(G\).} As above, we may suppose \(A=k\). Let
\(g\in G\), put \(K=\kappa(g)\), and let \(\gamma\in G(K)\) be the point defined by \(g\). The
projection \(q:G_K\to G\) is open, being deduced by base change from
\(\operatorname{Spec}K\to\operatorname{Spec}k\), which is universally open
(EGA~IV\textsubscript{2}, 2.4.10); hence \(q^{-1}(U)\) and \(q^{-1}(V^{-1})\) are dense open subsets
of \(G_K\), and so is the image of \(q^{-1}(V^{-1})\) under left translation by \(\gamma\). Two
dense open subsets meet, so there is a point \(w\in q^{-1}(V^{-1})\) such that
\(\gamma w\in q^{-1}(U)\). Let \(y\) be a point of \(G_K\) with values in a field \(L\supset K\)
whose image is \(w\). Then \(\gamma_Ly\) and \(y^{-1}\) are \(L\)-points of \(G\) lying over points
of \(U\) and of \(V\) respectively, and \(\gamma_L=(\gamma_Ly)\cdot y^{-1}\); hence
\(g\in U\cdot V\).

\phantomsection\label{sga3:VIA:0.5.1}%
(b) \emph{An irreducible \(A\)-group \(G\) is quasi-compact.} Indeed, a nonempty affine open
subset \(W\) of \(G\) is dense, so by (a) \(G\) is the image of the quasi-compact scheme
\(W\times_AW\) under the multiplication.

\phantomsection\label{sga3:VIA:0.5.2}%
(c) \emph{Every subgroup scheme \(H\) of an \(A\)-group \(G\) is closed in \(G\).} The base change
from \(A\) to \(k\), and then to a perfect closure of \(k\), induces homeomorphisms of the underlying
spaces of \(G\) and \(H\) compatible with the inclusion (the second one is a universal
homeomorphism). We may therefore suppose that \(A=k\) is a perfect field and,
replacing \(G\) and \(H\) by \(G_{\mathrm{red}}\) and \(H_{\mathrm{red}}\) (0.2), that \(G\) and \(H\)
are reduced, hence geometrically reduced. Let \(\overline H\) be the closure of \(H\), with its
reduced structure; \(H\), being locally closed in \(G\), is open in \(\overline H\). The projections
of a product of \(k\)-schemes onto its factors are open, so \(H\times_kH\) is dense in
\(\overline H\times_k\overline H\), and the multiplication of \(G\) maps
\(\overline H\times_k\overline H\) into \(\overline H\); as \(\overline H\times_k\overline H\) is
reduced, it induces a morphism \(\overline H\times_k\overline H\to\overline H\). Since \(\overline H\)
is also stable under inversion, it is a closed subgroup scheme of \(G\), in which \(H\) is a dense
open subset. By (a), \(\overline H=H\cdot H=H\).
