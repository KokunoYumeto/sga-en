
\section*{8. Solvable and nilpotent group schemes}
\phantomsection\label{sec:sga3-VIB-8}%
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{8. Solvable and nilpotent group schemes}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-VIB-8.1}%
\noindent\textbf{8.1.---}
Let \(\mathcal C\) be a category, \(T\) a topology on
\(\mathcal C\) (Expos\'e~IV), \(G\) a presheaf of groups on
\(\mathcal C\), and \(A\) and \(B\) two group subpresheaves of
\(G\). We denote by \(\operatorname{Comm}(A,B)\) the group
presheaf of commutators of the presheaves \(A\) and \(B\) in the
presheaf \(G\). If \(G\), \(A\) and \(B\) are sheaves, we denote
by
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-8.1-CommT}%
\[
  \operatorname{Comm}_T(A,B)
\]
the sheaf associated with the presheaf
\(\operatorname{Comm}(A,B)\).

If \(A\) is invariant in \(G\), one shows easily that
\(\operatorname{Comm}_T(G,G)\) \textup{(}respectively,
\(\operatorname{Comm}_T(G,A)\)\textup{)} is the smallest invariant
group subsheaf \(C\) of \(G\) such that the quotient sheaf
\(G/C\) \textup{(}respectively, \(A/C\)\textup{)} is commutative
\textup{(}respectively, central in \(G/C\)\textup{)}.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}; our
formulation). The re-edition states the fact asserted above, and the inclusion of Exposé~IV, 4.4.7 used in
the proof of 8.2, as lemmas (its 8.1.1 and 8.1.2), with proofs. Here \(\operatorname{Comm}(A,B)(S)\) is the
subgroup of \(G(S)\) generated by the commutators \(aba^{-1}b^{-1}\), \(a\in A(S)\), \(b\in B(S)\). For a
presheaf \(P\) on \(\mathcal C\), let \(\overline P\) be the associated sheaf; when \(P\) is a subpresheaf of a
sheaf \(G\), \(\overline P\) is identified with the subsheaf of \(G\) whose sections are the sections of
\(G\) that are locally sections of \(P\).

(a) Let \(G\) be a sheaf of groups and \(C\subset A\) group subsheaves of \(G\), invariant in \(G\). The
presheaf \(S\mapsto G(S)/C(S)\) is separated: a section of \(G\) that is locally a section of \(C\) is a
section of \(C\), since \(C\) is a sheaf contained in the sheaf \(G\). So the image of \(g\in G(S)\) in the
quotient sheaf \(G/C\) is trivial only if \(g\in C(S)\). As every section of \(G/C\) (respectively, of its
subsheaf \(A/C\)) is locally the image of a section of \(G\) (respectively, \(A\)), and two sections of a
sheaf commute if they commute locally, \(A/C\) is central in \(G/C\) if and only if
\(gag^{-1}a^{-1}\in C(S)\) for all \(S\), \(g\in G(S)\) and \(a\in A(S)\), that is, if and only if
\(\operatorname{Comm}(G,A)\subset C\), or again \(\operatorname{Comm}_T(G,A)\subset C\), \(C\) being a
sheaf. Now \(\operatorname{Comm}(G,A)\) is a group subpresheaf of \(A\) invariant in \(G\), since
\(h(gag^{-1}a^{-1})h^{-1}=(hgh^{-1})(hah^{-1})(hgh^{-1})^{-1}(hah^{-1})^{-1}\); hence
\(\operatorname{Comm}_T(G,A)\) is an invariant group subsheaf of \(G\) contained in \(A\), and it satisfies
the condition. Thus \(\operatorname{Comm}_T(G,A)\) is the smallest invariant group subsheaf \(C\) of \(G\)
contained in \(A\) such that \(A/C\) is central in \(G/C\); for \(A=G\), \(\operatorname{Comm}_T(G,G)\) is
the smallest invariant group subsheaf \(C\) such that \(G/C\) is commutative.

(b) Let \(G\) be a sheaf of groups and \(A\), \(B\) group subpresheaves of \(G\). Then
\(\operatorname{Comm}(A,B)\subset\operatorname{Comm}(\overline A,\overline B)\), and every section of
\(\operatorname{Comm}(\overline A,\overline B)\) over \(S\) is locally a section of
\(\operatorname{Comm}(A,B)\): it is a finite product of commutators \(a_ib_ia_i^{-1}b_i^{-1}\), with \(a_i\)
in \(\overline A(S)\) and \(b_i\) in \(\overline B(S)\), and of their inverses, and on a suitable covering of
\(S\) all the \(a_i\) and \(b_i\) restrict to sections of \(A\) and \(B\). Hence
\(\operatorname{Comm}_T(\overline A,\overline B)=\overline{\operatorname{Comm}(A,B)}\) as subsheaves of
\(G\), which contains the inclusion used in the proof of 8.2.
