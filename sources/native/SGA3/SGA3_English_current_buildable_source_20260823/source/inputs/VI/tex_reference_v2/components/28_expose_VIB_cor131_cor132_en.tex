\phantomsection\label{cor:sga3-VIB-1.3.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Corollary 1.3.1.---}
\emph{Let \(k\) be a field and \(G\) a \(k\)-group. Then, for \(G\) to be
locally quasi-finite (respectively, unramified, smooth, \'etale) over
\(k\), it is necessary and sufficient that \(G\) be quasi-finite
(respectively, unramified, smooth, \'etale) over \(k\) at one point.}

It is enough to apply \hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-1.3]{1.3} in the case where \(H\) is the unit
\(k\)-group, taking into account that the set of points at which \(G\)
is of finite type over \(k\) is open, and the following lemma.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-VIB-1.3.1.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 1.3.1.1.---}
\emph{Let \(k\) be a field and \(G\) a \(k\)-group. If there is a nonempty
open subset \(U\) of \(G\) such that \(U\) is locally of finite type over
\(k\), then \(G\) is locally of finite type over \(k\).}

Let \(x\in G\) and \(y\in U\). Let \(k'\) be an extension of \(k\) that is
a composite of the extensions \(\kappa(x)\) and \(\kappa(y)\), and put
\(G'=G\otimes_kk'\), \(U'=U\otimes_kk'\). There are then a rational point
\(x'\in G'\) lying over \(x\) and a rational point \(y'\in U'\) lying over
\(y\). Then \(V'=x'\cdot y'^{-1}\cdot U'\) is an open subset containing
\(x'\) and locally of finite type over \(k'\). The projection \(G'\to G\)
being open (EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.4.6), the projection of \(V'\) on \(G\) is an
open subset \(V\) of \(G\) containing \(x\) and locally of finite type over
\(k\), by EGA~IV\(_4\), 17.7.5.\footnote{Translator's note: EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.4.6, and EGA~IV\(_4\),
17.7.5, are statements about morphisms locally of finite presentation, and the projection \(G'\to G\)
is one only when \(k'\) is finite over \(k\). For an arbitrary extension \(k'\) the projection is
still open (EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.4.10), but 17.7.5 no longer applies; as \(\kappa(x)\) need not be finite
over \(k\), the argument above proves the lemma only at the points \(x\) with \(\kappa(x)\) finite over
\(k\). A proof for all points follows.}

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Translator's addition: a proof of Lemma~\textup{1.3.1.1} at every point.} By faithfully flat
descent (EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.7.1), \(G\) is locally of finite type over \(k\) if \(G\otimes_k\bar k\) is
locally of finite type over an algebraic closure \(\bar k\) of \(k\), and \(U\otimes_k\bar k\) is a
nonempty open subset of \(G\otimes_k\bar k\) locally of finite type over \(\bar k\); so we may assume
\(k\) algebraically closed. Let \(W\) be the open set of points at which \(G\) is locally of finite
type over \(k\). It contains \(U\), and every automorphism of the \(k\)-scheme \(G\) maps it onto
itself, for instance the inverse map and the translations by rational points. Being nonempty and
locally of finite type over \(k\), \(W\) has a rational point \(a\), so \(e=a^{-1}a\in a^{-1}W=W\).
Below, points are points with values in fields \(K\) containing \(k\), and a statement about such
points may be checked after enlarging \(K\).

(1) \emph{\(W\) is a subgroup.} The multiplication \(m\colon W\times_kW\to G\) is the open immersion
\((g,h)\mapsto(gh,h)\) of \(W\times_kW\) into \(G\times_kW\) (a restriction of an automorphism of
\(G\times_kG\)) followed by the projection \(G\times_kW\to G\), which is flat and locally of finite
presentation. Hence \(m(W\times_kW)\) is open (EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.4.6) and, \(W\times_kW\) being locally
of finite type over \(k\), it is locally of finite type over \(k\) (EGA~IV\(_4\), 17.7.5); thus
\(m(W\times_kW)\subset W\).

(2) Let \(z\in G\), \(L=\kappa(z)\), and \(\tilde z\in G(L)\) the canonical point. For a
\(k\)-embedding \(\alpha\colon L\to K\) let \(\tilde z_\alpha\in G(K)\) be the point it defines. Let
\(O_z\) be the image in \(G\) of the open subset \(\tilde z\cdot(W\otimes_kL)\) of \(G\otimes_kL\);
it is open, since the projection \(G\otimes_kL\to G\) is open (EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.4.10), and it contains
\(z\). After enlarging \(K\), every \(K\)-point of \(O_z\) is of the form \(\tilde z_\alpha w\) with
\(w\in W(K\)). If \(z\notin W\), then \(O_z\cap W=\emptyset\): otherwise some
\(\tilde z_\alpha=(\tilde z_\alpha w)w^{-1}\) would lie in \(W(K)\) by (1). So \(G-W\) is the union of
the open sets \(O_z\), \(z\notin W\), and \(W\) is closed.

(3) \emph{For any two \(K\)-points \(b,b'\) of \(O_z\), \(b^{-1}b'\in W(K)\).} The points
\(\tilde z_\alpha^{-1}\tilde z_\beta\), for pairs of embeddings \((\alpha,\beta)\), are the values of a
morphism \(\operatorname{Spec}(L\otimes_kL)\to G\). As \(k\) is algebraically closed, \(L\otimes_kL\)
is an integral domain, so \(\operatorname{Spec}(L\otimes_kL)\) is connected; the inverse image of the
open and closed set \(W\) contains the diagonal point, whose value is \(e\), so it is everything.
Writing \(b=\tilde z_\alpha w\) and \(b'=\tilde z_\beta w'\), we get
\(b^{-1}b'=w^{-1}(\tilde z_\alpha^{-1}\tilde z_\beta)w'\in W(K)\) by (1). Likewise
\(O_z\cdot W\subset O_z\).

(4) By (3), the map \((y,w)\mapsto(y,yw)\), a restriction of an automorphism of \(G\times_kG\), is an
isomorphism of \(O_z\times_kW\) onto \(O_z\times_kO_z\) compatible with the first projections. So the
first projection \(O_z\times_kO_z\to O_z\) is locally of finite type. If \(A\) is a nonempty affine
open subset of \(O_z\), the projection \(A\times_kO_z\to A\) is therefore locally of finite type, and
as \(A\to\operatorname{Spec}(k)\) is faithfully flat and quasi-compact, \(O_z\) is locally of finite
type over \(k\) (EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.7.1). Hence \(z\in O_z\subset W\), and \(W=G\).

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}; our
formulation). \emph{Proposition~\textup{1.3}, Corollary~\textup{1.3.1} and Lemma~\textup{1.3.1.1}
remain true over an Artinian local ring \(A\), with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak n\) and residue field
\(k\), provided that, in \textup{1.3} and \textup{1.3.1}, the \(A\)-group \(G\) is flat over \(A\).}
Two facts on ring homomorphisms \(A\to B\to C\) are used, \(\mathfrak n\) being nilpotent.

(a) \emph{If \(C/\mathfrak nC\) is a \((B/\mathfrak nB)\)-algebra of finite type, then \(C\) is a
\(B\)-algebra of finite type.} Indeed, if \(C'\) is the \(B\)-subalgebra of \(C\) generated by
liftings of generators of \(C/\mathfrak nC\), then \(C=C'+\mathfrak nC\), hence
\(C=C'+\mathfrak n^mC\) for every \(m\), and \(C=C'\).

(b) \emph{If moreover \(C\) is flat over \(A\) and \(C/\mathfrak nC\) is of finite presentation over
\(B/\mathfrak nB\), then \(C\) is of finite presentation over \(B\).} Indeed, let
\(P=B[X_1,\ldots,X_r]\to C\) be surjective with kernel \(I\). As \(C\) is flat over \(A\),
\(I/\mathfrak nI\) is the kernel of \(P/\mathfrak nP\to C/\mathfrak nC\), hence an ideal of finite
type (EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.4.4); if \(J\subset I\) is generated by liftings of its generators, then
\(I=J+\mathfrak nI\), and \(I=J\) as in (a).

Now let \(u:G\to H\) be a morphism of \(A\)-groups, with \(G\) locally of finite type and flat over
\(A\); put \(u_k=u\otimes_Ak\), and let \(x\) be a point of \(G\), which has the same underlying space
as \(G_k=G\otimes_Ak\). \emph{Then \(u\) is quasi-finite (respectively, unramified, flat, smooth,
\'etale) at \(x\) if and only if \(u_k\) is.} The condition is necessary by base change. Conversely,
\(u\) and \(u_k\) have the same fibers, which settles quasi-finiteness. If \(u_k\) is flat at \(x\),
then \(\mathcal O_{G,x}\), which is flat over \(A\), is flat over \(\mathcal O_{H,u(x)}\)
(EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.3.10.2, \(\mathfrak n\) being nilpotent). If \(u_k\) is locally of finite
presentation at \(x\), so is \(u\), by (b) applied to affine open neighborhoods of \(x\) and of
\(u(x)\). The stalk at \(x\) of \(\Omega^1_{G/H}\) vanishes if its quotient by \(\mathfrak n\), the
stalk of \(\Omega^1_{G_k/H_k}\), does; so \(u\) is unramified at \(x\) if \(u_k\) is. Smoothness at
\(x\) means local finite presentation, flatness at \(x\) and smoothness at \(x\) of the fiber
(EGA~IV\(_4\), 17.5.1), which gives the smooth case, and \'etale means smooth and unramified. Hence,
if \(u\) has one of these properties at one point, so has \(u_k\); then \(u_k\) has it everywhere by
1.3, and so has \(u\). Lemma 1.3.1.1 extends without the flatness hypothesis: if a nonempty open
subset of \(G\) is locally of finite type over \(A\), then \(G_k\) is locally of finite type over
\(k\) by 1.3.1.1, and (a), applied to the affine open subsets of \(G\), shows that \(G\) is locally
of finite type over \(A\). Corollary 1.3.1 over \(A\) then follows as over a field. Flatness of
\(G\) cannot be replaced by flatness at one point: if \(A\ne k\), the closed subscheme
\(G=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\sqcup\operatorname{Spec}(k)\) of the constant \(A\)-group
\((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)_A=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\sqcup\operatorname{Spec}(A)\) is a subgroup, and
\(G\) is \'etale over \(A\) at its unit point but not flat over \(A\) at its other point.

\phantomsection\label{cor:sga3-VIB-1.3.2}%
\noindent\textbf{Corollary 1.3.2.---}
\emph{Let \(A\) be an Artinian local ring, let \(G\) and \(H\) be two
\(A\)-groups locally of finite type, and let \(u:G\to H\) be a morphism of
\(A\)-groups. The following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{cor:sga3-VIB-1.3.2:item:i}(i)}] \(u\) is universally open;
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{cor:sga3-VIB-1.3.2:item:ii}(ii)}] \(u\) is open;
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{cor:sga3-VIB-1.3.2:item:iii}(iii)}] \(u\) is open at some point of \(G\);
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{cor:sga3-VIB-1.3.2:item:iv}(iv)}] the map \(u^0:G^0\to H^0\) induced by \(u\) is
  dominant, or, what amounts to the same \textup{(\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-1.2]{1.2} and
  Expos\'e~VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-2.4}{2.4})}, \(u^0\) is surjective;
\item[\textup{\SGAInlineRefTarget{cor:sga3-VIB-1.3.2:item:v}(v)}] there is a connected component \(C\) of \(G\)
  such that, if \(D\) denotes the connected component of \(H\)
  containing \(u(C)\), the map \(u':C\to D\) induced by \(u\) is
  dominant.
\end{enumerate}}

It is clear that \textup{\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.3.2:item:i}{(i)}} implies \textup{\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.3.2:item:ii}{(ii)}}, and that \textup{(ii)}
implies \textup{\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.3.2:item:iii}{(iii)}}. Since \(G^0\) (respectively, \(C\)) is open in
\(G\) (Expos\'e~VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{par:sga3-VIA-2.3}{2.3}) and \(H^0\) (respectively, \(D\)) is irreducible
(Expos\'e~VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIA-2.4.1}{2.4.1}), we see that \textup{(ii)} implies
\textup{\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.3.2:item:iv}{(iv)}} (respectively, that \textup{(iii)} implies
\textup{\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-1.3.2:item:v}{(v)}}). It therefore remains to prove that \textup{(v)} implies
\textup{(i)} (for, \textup{(iv)} being a special case of \textup{(v)}, this will also show that
\textup{(iv)} implies \textup{(i)}).

We endow the open subset \(C\) (respectively, \(D\)) of \(G\)
(respectively, \(H\)) with its induced scheme structure, and let \(u'\)
denote the morphism \(u':C\to D\) induced by \(u\). Let \(k\) be the
residue field of \(A\). Since \(C\) is quasi-compact over \(A\)
(Expos\'e~VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIA-2.4.1}{2.4.1}) and \(D\) is separated over \(A\)
(Expos\'e~VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{sga3:VIA:0.3}{0.3}),\footnote{Translator's note: here and in the
proof of 1.4 the 1970 text cites 0.2 of Expos\'e~VI\(_A\) for the separation of \(A\)-groups, which is
proved in 0.3 there.} \(u'\) is quasi-compact; hence \(u'\otimes_Ak\) is
quasi-compact and dominant, and so is \(u'\otimes_A\bar k\) (where \(\bar k\)
denotes the algebraic closure of \(k\)), by EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.3.7. Since
\(C\otimes_A\bar k\) is a union of connected components of
\(G\otimes_A\bar k\), it is clear that \(G\otimes_A\bar k\),
\(H\otimes_A\bar k\) and \(u\otimes_A\bar k\) satisfy assertion \textup{(v)}.
We are thus reduced to the case in which \(A=k\) is an algebraically
closed field, in view of EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.6.4.\footnote{Translator's note: EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.6.4,
concerns faithfully flat quasi-compact base changes, such as \(\operatorname{Spec}\bar k\to
\operatorname{Spec}k\). The passage from \(A\) to \(k\) rests on the fact that, the maximal ideal of
\(A\) being nilpotent, \(\operatorname{Spec}k\to\operatorname{Spec}A\) is a universal homeomorphism: for
every \(H\)-scheme \(Y\), the morphisms \(u_Y\) and \(u_Y\otimes_Ak\) have the same underlying continuous
map, so \(u\) is universally open if and only if \(u\otimes_Ak\) is. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, makes this step explicit.}

In this case we may further replace \(u\) by \(u_{\mathrm{red}}\),
and are thereby reduced to the case in which \(H\) is reduced. Then \(D\)
is reduced, and the generic flatness theorem (EGA~IV\(_2\), 6.9.1) asserts
that, since \(u'\) is dominant, \(u'\) (and hence also \(u\)) is flat at the
generic point of \(C\), so that \(u\) is flat (\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-1.3]{1.3}), and therefore
universally open (EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.4.6).\footnote{Translator's note: generic flatness is not
needed here. Since \(u'\) is dominant, it maps the generic point \(\xi\) of \(C\) to the generic point
\(\eta\) of \(D\), and \(\mathcal O_{D,\eta}\) is a field, \(D\) being reduced; so \(\mathcal O_{C,\xi}\)
is flat over \(\mathcal O_{D,\eta}\). Moreover \(u\), being locally of finite type with \(H\) locally
noetherian, is locally of finite presentation, as EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.4.6, requires. The Gille--Polo
re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, argues in this way.}
