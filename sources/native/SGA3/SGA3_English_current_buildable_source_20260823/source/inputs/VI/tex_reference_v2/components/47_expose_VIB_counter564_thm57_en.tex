\phantomsection\label{ctr:sga3-VIB-5.6.4}%
\noindent\textbf{Counterexample 5.6.4.---}
Not every \(S\)-group \(G\) is separated. Let \(S\) be a
scheme having a non-isolated closed point \(s\), and let \(G\) be
the scheme obtained by gluing two copies of \(S\) along the open
subset \(S-\{s\}\). It is easily seen that \(G\) is not separated
over \(S\), and that it carries a natural group structure
whose fibers are all trivial, except for the fiber \(G_s\), which
is isomorphic to the two-element group
\(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\).

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{cor:sga3-VIB-5.6.5}%
\noindent\textbf{Corollary 5.6.5.---}
\emph{Let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group locally of finite presentation, with
connected fibers, and universally open over \(S\); then \(G\) is of
finite presentation over \(S\).}

This follows from \SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIB-5.3}{5.3} and
\SGARefLink{cor:sga3-VIB-3.6}{3.6}.

\phantomsection\label{rem:sga3-VIB-5.7}%
\noindent\textbf{Remark 5.7.---}
A proof analogous to that of \SGARefLink{thm:sga3-VIB-5.3}{5.3}, but
using 4.7,\footnote{Translator's note: there is no 4.7 in this Expos\'e;
as the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011) points out, the number is that of the
1965 edition of this seminar (SGAD), in which 5.6.1 and 5.6.2 were 4.5
and 4.6. The re-edition renumbers this remark as 5.7.1, the number 5.7
going to a theorem of O.~Gabber; see the Addition below.} makes
it possible to establish the following result:

Let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group \emph{locally of finite presentation} and
universally open over \(S\) at the points of \(\underline{G^0}\). Let
\(\sigma\) and \(\tau\) be two \(S\)-sections of \(G^0\) (i.e., let
\(\sigma,\tau\in G^0(S)\)). Then the subscheme of \(S\) of coincidences
of \(\sigma\) and \(\tau\) is \emph{closed}.

\par\medskip\noindent
\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-VIB-5.7}%
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
The re-edition deduces the result of 5.7 from the following theorem of O.~Gabber (its 5.7): \emph{let \(G\) be an
\(S\)-group locally of finite presentation such that \(s\mapsto\dim G_s\) is locally constant on \(S\) (by
\SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIB-4.2}{4.2}, this amounts to \(G\) being universally open over \(S\) at the points of
\(\underline{G^0}\)), acting on an \(S\)-scheme \(X\), and let \(U\) be an open subset of \(X\), separated over
\(S\). Then \(V=\underline{G^0}\cdot U\), the set of the points \(gu\), where \(g\) lies in some
\(\underline{G^0_s}\) and \(u\) in \(U\), is an open subset of \(X\), separated over \(S\).} Indeed, the action
\(\mu\colon G\times_SX\to X\) is the composite of the automorphism \(\alpha\colon(g,x)\mapsto(g,gx)\) of
\(G\times_SX\) and of the second projection \(p\). If \(v=gu\) as above, the point \(\alpha(g,u)\) of the open
set \(\alpha(G\times_SU)\) lies, for the \(X\)-group \(G\times_SX\) (structure map \(p\)), in the identity
component of its fibre over \(v\); by \SGARefLink{concl:sga3-VIB-4.2-iii}{4.2~(iii)}, applied after the base change
\(X\to S\), \(p\) maps the points of \(\alpha(G\times_SU)\) lying in the identity components of the fibres onto a
neighbourhood of \(v\), which is contained in \(V\). So \(V\) is open. Let \(x_1,x_2\) be points of a fibre
\(V_s\). By (b) of the Addition after the proof of 5.6.2, applied to \((\gamma,x)\mapsto\gamma^{-1}x\), the points
\(\gamma\) of \(G^0_s\) such that \(\gamma^{-1}\) maps the points above \(x_1\) and \(x_2\) into \(U\) contain a
dense open subset of \(G^0_s\), hence a closed point \(c\). As \(G\) is universally open at the generic point of
\(G^0_s\), Lemma 5.6.1 gives \(g\colon S''\to S'\to S\) and \(b\in G(S'')\) with \(b(s'')\) above \(c\); the open
subscheme \(b\cdot U_{S''}\cap V_{S''}\) of \(V_{S''}\) is separated over \(S''\) and contains the inverse images
of \(x_1\) and \(x_2\). By 5.6.3, in the form recalled in the note there (\(S'\to S\) is \'etale and \(S''\to S'\)
finite and surjective), \(V\) is separated over \(S\). The result of 5.7 follows: if \(s\in S\) and \(U\) is an
affine open neighbourhood of \(\epsilon(s)\) in \(G\), acting on itself by left translations, then
\(\underline{G^0}\cdot U\) is open, separated over \(S\), and contains \(\sigma(s)\) and \(\tau(s)\); on the
neighbourhood of \(s\) where \(\sigma\) and \(\tau\) take their values in it, they are sections of a separated
morphism, so the coincidence subscheme is closed there. The re-edition also records, without proof, the following
assertion of Gabber: if \(S\) is henselian local with closed point \(s\), the intersection of the open sets
\(\underline{G^0}\cdot U\), for \(U\) running through the affine open neighbourhoods of \(\epsilon(s)\), is an open
subgroup scheme of \(G\), separated over \(S\).
