
\section*{11. Affine group schemes}
\phantomsection\label{sec:sga3-VIB-11}%
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{11. Affine group schemes}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-VIB-11.0}%
\noindent\textbf{11.0. Notation.---}
Let \(S\) be a scheme, let \(X\) be an \(S\)-scheme, and let
\(f:X\to S\) be the structural morphism; we put
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-11-A-X}%
\[
  \mathcal A(X)=f_*(\mathcal O_X).
\]

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
The re-edition opens this section with three standard facts, which it uses below in place of some arguments
of the 1970 text. Let \(q\colon X\to S\) be a quasi-compact and quasi-separated morphism and \(\mathcal F\) a
quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_X\)-module; then \(q_*(\mathcal F)\) is quasi-coherent (EGA~I, 9.2.1).
\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.0-a}%
(a) \emph{If \(\mathcal F\) is the union of a filtered family of quasi-coherent submodules
\(\mathcal F_\alpha\), then \(q_*(\mathcal F)\) is the union of the \(q_*(\mathcal F_\alpha)\).}
\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.0-b}%
(b) \emph{If \(\mathcal E\) is a flat quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_S\)-module, the canonical morphism
\(\mathcal E\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}q_*(\mathcal F)\to q_*\bigl(q^*(\mathcal E)\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal F\bigr)\)
is an isomorphism}; for \(\mathcal F=\mathcal O_X\), this is the morphism
\(\mathcal E\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}q_*(\mathcal O_X)\to q_*q^*(\mathcal E)\).
\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.0-c}%
(c) \emph{If \(p\colon S'\to S\) is flat, and \(q'\colon X'=X\times_SS'\to S'\) and \(p'\colon X'\to X\) are
the projections, the canonical morphism \(p^*q_*(\mathcal F)\to q'_*p'^*(\mathcal F)\) is an isomorphism}
(EGA~III, 1.4.15, for \(q\) separated; EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.7.21, in general).
All three come from one exact sequence. Let \(U=\operatorname{Spec}A\) be an affine open subset of \(S\). As
\(q\) is quasi-compact, \(q^{-1}(U)\) is covered by finitely many affine open subsets \(V_i\), and as \(q\) is
quasi-separated, each \(V_i\cap V_j\) is covered by finitely many affine open subsets \(W_{ijl}\). For every
quasi-coherent module \(\mathcal G\) on \(q^{-1}(U)\), the sheaf axiom gives an exact sequence
\[
  0\longrightarrow\Gamma\bigl(q^{-1}(U),\mathcal G\bigr)\longrightarrow\prod_i\Gamma(V_i,\mathcal G)
   \longrightarrow\prod_{i,j,l}\Gamma(W_{ijl},\mathcal G),
\]
with finite products, the second map sending \((s_i)\) to
\((s_i|_{W_{ijl}}-s_j|_{W_{ijl}})\). Over the affine schemes \(V_i\) and \(W_{ijl}\), taking sections of
quasi-coherent modules commutes with filtered unions, and filtered unions preserve kernels; this gives (a).
If \(E=\Gamma(U,\mathcal E)\), a flat \(A\)-module, the sections of \(q^*(\mathcal E)\otimes\mathcal F\) over an
affine open subset \(V\) of \(q^{-1}(U)\) are \(E\otimes_A\Gamma(V,\mathcal F)\), and \(E\otimes_A-\) is exact; hence
\(\Gamma\bigl(q^{-1}(U),q^*(\mathcal E)\otimes\mathcal F\bigr)=E\otimes_A\Gamma\bigl(q^{-1}(U),\mathcal F\bigr)\), which
is (b). For (c), let \(U'=\operatorname{Spec}A'\) be an affine open subset of \(S'\) lying over \(U\): the
\(V_i\times_UU'\) cover \(q'^{-1}(U')\), the \(W_{ijl}\times_UU'\) cover their intersections, the sections of
\(p'^*(\mathcal F)\) over them are those of \(\mathcal F\) over \(V_i\) and \(W_{ijl}\) tensored over \(A\) with the
flat \(A\)-algebra \(A'\), and therefore
\(\Gamma\bigl(q'^{-1}(U'),p'^*(\mathcal F)\bigr)=\Gamma\bigl(q^{-1}(U),\mathcal F\bigr)\otimes_AA'
=\Gamma\bigl(U',p^*q_*(\mathcal F)\bigr)\).

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-VIB-11.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 11.1.---}
\emph{Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be two quasi-compact and
quasi-separated \(S\)-schemes, and let \(f:X\to S\) and
\(g:Y\to S\) be their structural morphisms. Then the
canonical homomorphism}
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-11.1-phi}%
\[
  \varphi:
  \mathcal A(X)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal A(Y)
    \longrightarrow
  \mathcal A(X\times_SY)
\]
\emph{is an isomorphism in each of the following cases:}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, observes that the quasi-compactness of \(X\) cannot be omitted: if
\(S=\operatorname{Spec}k\), \(k\) a field, and \(X=Y\) is the sum of copies of \(S\) indexed by an infinite set
\(I\), then \(g\) is flat and \(\mathcal A(X)=k^I\), \(\mathcal A(X\times_SY)=k^{I\times I}\), and the function
equal to \(1\) on the diagonal and to \(0\) elsewhere is not in the image of \(k^I\otimes_kk^I\), whose
elements, viewed as \(I\times I\) matrices, have finite rank.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(a)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.1-a}
  \emph{\(f\) and \(g\) are affine;}

\item[\textup{(b)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.1-b}
  \emph{\(f\) or \(g\) is flat and affine;}

\item[\textup{(c)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-11.1-c}
  \emph{\(g\) is flat and \(f_*(\mathcal O_X)\) is a flat
  \(\mathcal O_S\)-module.}
\end{enumerate}

In case~\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.1-b]{\textup{(b)}}, we may
suppose that it is \(g\) that is flat and affine. Put
\[
  S'=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A(X),\qquad
  Y'=Y\times_SS',\qquad
  g'=g\times_SS',
\]
and let \(v\) denote the morphism \(S'\to S\). Then \(Y\) is
quasi-compact and quasi-separated over \(S\), and either \(S'\)
is flat over \(S\)
\textup{(case~\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.1-c]{\textup{(c)}}, by
EGA~III, 1.4.15.5)}, or \(g\) is affine
\textup{(cases~\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.1-a]{\textup{(a)}}
and~\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.1-b]{\textup{(b)}})}. Then
\textup{(EGA~III, 1.4.15 and EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.2.7, or EGA~II,
1.5.2)} we have
\[
  g'_*(\mathcal O_{Y'})
    =v^*g_*(\mathcal O_Y)
    =\mathcal A(Y)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal O_{S'}.
\]

By EGA~II, 1.2.7, \(f\) factors through \(v\) by means of a
morphism \(p:X\to S'\), and we have
\[
  X\times_SY=X\times_{S'}Y'
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  p_*(\mathcal O_X)=\mathcal O_{S'}.
\]
Since \(v\) and \(f\) are quasi-compact and quasi-separated, so
is \(p\) \textup{(EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.2.2 and 1.2.4)}. Either \(Y\)
is flat over \(S\)
\textup{(cases~\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.1-b]{\textup{(b)}}
and~\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.1-c]{\textup{(c)}})}, and then
\(Y'\) is flat over \(S'\), or \(X\) is affine over \(S\)
\textup{(case~\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.1-a]{\textup{(a)}})},
and then \(p\) is an isomorphism. In the first case we may
again apply EGA~III, 1.4.15 and EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.2.7, and in
both cases we find
\[
  p'_*(\mathcal O_{X\times_SY})
    =g'^*p_*(\mathcal O_X)
    =g'^*(\mathcal O_{S'})
    =\mathcal O_{Y'},
  \qquad\text{where }p'=p\times_{S'}Y'.
\]

Hence
\[
  \mathcal A(X\times_SY)
    =v_*g'_*p'_*(\mathcal O_{X\times_SY})
    =v_*g'_*(\mathcal O_{Y'})
    =v_*(\mathcal A(Y)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal O_{S'})
    =\mathcal A(Y)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal A(X)
\]
by EGA~II, 1.4.7.

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{cor:sga3-VIB-11.2}%
\noindent\textbf{Corollary 11.2.---}
\emph{One defines a functor}
\[
  X\longmapsto\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A(X)
\]
\emph{from the full subcategory of
\((\mathbf{Sch}/S)\) formed by the \(S\)-schemes \(X\) that
are flat, quasi-compact, and quasi-separated over \(S\) and
such that \(\mathcal A(X)\) is a flat
\(\mathcal O_S\)-module, to that of the \(S\)-schemes flat and
affine over \(S\). This functor commutes with finite products,
and therefore takes \(S\)-groups to \(S\)-groups.}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{def:sga3-VIB-11.3}%
\noindent\textbf{Definition 11.3.---}
\emph{Given a flat \(S\)-group \(G\), quasi-compact and
quasi-separated over \(S\), such that \(\mathcal A(G)\) is
flat over \(\mathcal O_S\),}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, points out that this hypothesis is automatic over a regular locally noetherian
base of dimension at most \(2\): if \(S\) is such a scheme and \(X\) is flat, quasi-compact and quasi-separated
over \(S\), then \(\mathcal A(X)\) is a flat \(\mathcal O_S\)-module (M.~Raynaud, \emph{Faisceaux amples sur les
sch\'emas en groupes et les espaces homog\`enes}, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 119, Springer, 1970, VII, 3.2).
In dimension at most \(1\) this is elementary: the local rings of \(S\) are fields or discrete valuation rings,
over which flat means torsion-free, and \(\mathcal A(X)\) is torsion-free because \(\mathcal O_X\) is.}
\emph{we write \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\), and call the
\(S\)-group}
\[
  G_{\mathrm{af}}=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal A(G)
\]
\emph{the \emph{affine envelope} of \(G\).}

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
The re-edition adds here the universal property of the affine envelope (its Proposition~11.3.1). Let \(G\) be as
in 11.3, and let \(\tau_G\colon G\to G_{\mathrm{af}}\) be the \(S\)-morphism corresponding to the identity of
\(\mathcal A(G)\). \emph{Then \(\tau_G\) is a morphism of \(S\)-groups; every \(S\)-morphism \(\psi\) from \(G\) to an
\(S\)-scheme \(H\) affine over \(S\) factors as \(\psi=\psi'\circ\tau_G\) for a unique \(S\)-morphism
\(\psi'\colon G_{\mathrm{af}}\to H\); and if \(H\) is an \(S\)-group and \(\psi\) a morphism of \(S\)-groups, so is
\(\psi'\).} Indeed, for every \(S\)-scheme \(X\), \(h\mapsto\mathcal A(h)\) is a bijection from
\(\operatorname{Hom}_S(X,H)\) onto the set of morphisms of \(\mathcal O_S\)-algebras \(\mathcal A(H)\to\mathcal A(X)\)
(EGA~II, 1.2.7). As \(\mathcal A(\tau_G)\) is the identity, composition with \(\tau_G\) is therefore a bijection
from \(\operatorname{Hom}_S(G_{\mathrm{af}},H)\) onto \(\operatorname{Hom}_S(G,H)\); and as
\(\mathcal A(\tau_G\times_S\tau_G)\) is the isomorphism \(\mathcal A(G)\otimes_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal A(G)\to
\mathcal A(G\times_SG)\) of 11.1, composition with \(\tau_G\times_S\tau_G\) is a bijection from
\(\operatorname{Hom}_S(G_{\mathrm{af}}\times_SG_{\mathrm{af}},H)\) onto \(\operatorname{Hom}_S(G\times_SG,H)\).
Since \(\tau\) is natural and the multiplication \(m_{\mathrm{af}}\) of \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) is obtained from the
multiplication \(m\) of \(G\) by 11.2, we have \(m_{\mathrm{af}}\circ(\tau_G\times_S\tau_G)=\tau_G\circ m\): thus
\(\tau_G\) is a morphism of groups. If \(\psi\) is a morphism of groups and \(m_H\) is the multiplication of \(H\),
then \(m_H\circ(\psi'\times_S\psi')\) and \(\psi'\circ m_{\mathrm{af}}\) both become \(\psi\circ m\) after composition
with \(\tau_G\times_S\tau_G\), hence are equal. The re-edition studies \(\tau_G\) and its kernel further in its
added Section~12.
