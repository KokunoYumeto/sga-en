\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-VIB-11.12}%
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 11.12.---}
\emph{Let \(k\) be a field and let \(G\) be a quasi-compact
\(k\)-group. Put \(A=\mathcal A(G)\). Given \(x\in A\),
there is a finitely generated \(k\)-subalgebra \(B\) of
\(A\) such that}
\[
  x\in B,\qquad
  \Delta(B)\subset B\otimes_kB,\qquad
  u(B)\subset B,
\]
\emph{where \(u\) denotes the involution of \(A\)
corresponding to the symmetry of \(G\).}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, states the lemma for an affine \(k\)-group. This restriction is not needed: a
quasi-compact \(k\)-group \(G\) is flat and separated over \(k\) (VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{sga3:VIA:0.3}{0.3}), so that,
by \hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-11.1-c]{11.1~(c)} and \hyperref[def:sga3-VIB-11.3]{11.3}, \(G_{\mathrm{af}}\) is an affine
\(k\)-group with the same algebra \(A\), the same comultiplication and the same involution, which is the
reduction made at the beginning of the proof. The re-edition also extends the lemma, in its added Section~13, to
group schemes affine and flat over a regular base of dimension at most~\(2\).}

By \hyperref[def:sga3-VIB-11.3]{11.3}, we may replace \(G\) by
\(G_{\mathrm{af}}\), and hence suppose \(G\) affine.

Write \(\Delta(x)=\sum_{i\in J}\varphi_i\otimes a_i\), with \(J\)
finite and as small as possible; then the \(\varphi_i\) and the
\(a_i\) \((i\in J)\) are linearly independent, and we complete
the \(\varphi_i\) \((i\in J)\) to a basis \((\varphi_i)_{i\in I}\)
of \(A\). Write
\(\Delta(\varphi_i)=\sum_j\varphi_j\otimes b_{ji}\); axiom
\textup{(HA 1)} of I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:4-2}{4.2}
shows that
\(\sum\varphi_i\otimes\Delta(a_i)=\sum\Delta(\varphi_i)\otimes a_i\),
whence \(\Delta(a_i)=\sum_{j\in J}b_{ij}\otimes a_j\) for
\(i\in J\). Let \(B\) be the \(k\)-subalgebra of \(A\) generated
by the \(b_{ij}\) and the \(u(b_{ij})\), for \(i,j\in J\). It is
clear that \(B\) is a \(k\)-algebra of finite type. Axiom
\textup{(HA 1)}, applied to the last formula, also shows that
\(\sum_j\Delta(b_{ij})\otimes a_j
=\sum_{\ell,j}b_{i\ell}\otimes b_{\ell j}\otimes a_j\), and, the
\(a_j\) being linearly independent, one deduces that
\(\Delta(b_{ij})=\sum_{\ell\in J}b_{i\ell}\otimes b_{\ell j}\) and
that
\(\Delta(u(b_{ij}))=\sum_{\ell\in J}u(b_{\ell j})\otimes u(b_{i\ell})\).\footnote{Translator's
note: the 1970 text takes for \((\varphi_i)\) an arbitrary basis of \(A\), and obtains
\(\Delta(b_{ij})=\sum b_{i\ell}\otimes b_{\ell j}\) from axiom (HA~1) applied to the \(\Delta(\varphi_i)\), the sum
running over the whole basis; this does not show that the subalgebra generated by the finitely many \(b_{ij}\),
\(u(b_{ij})\) occurring in the formulas for the \(\Delta(a_i)\) is stable under \(\Delta\). Writing \(\Delta(x)\) with
as few terms as possible, so that the \(a_i\) are linearly independent, and applying (HA~1) to the \(\Delta(a_i)\),
as above, is the correction of the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}.}
Since \(\Delta\) is a homomorphism of algebras, one deduces that
\(\Delta(B)\subset B\otimes_kB\) and \(u(B)\subset B\). Finally,
axiom \textup{(HA 2)} of I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:4-2}{4.2}
shows that \(a_i=\sum_j\eta(a_j)b_{ij}\) and that
\(x=\sum_i\eta(\varphi_i)a_i\), so that \(x\in B\).

\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-11.13}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 11.13.---}
\emph{Let \(k\) be a field and let \(G\) be an affine
\(k\)-group with algebra \(A\). Then \(G\) is the
projective limit of an increasing filtered system of
affine \(k\)-groups of finite type whose transition
morphisms are faithfully flat.}

As the set of finitely generated \(k\)-subalgebras of \(A\)
stable under \(\Delta\) and \(u\) is stable under generation,
\(A\) is the direct limit of an increasing filtered family
\((B_i)_{i\in I}\) of finitely generated \(k\)-subalgebras stable
under \(\Delta\) and \(u\). Then each algebra \(B_i\), endowed
with the homomorphism
\[
  B_i\longrightarrow B_i\otimes_kB_i
\]
deduced from \(\Delta\) and with the restriction of \(u\) to
\(B_i\), is endowed with a Hopf algebra structure, hence
\textup{(}I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:4-2}{4.2}\textup{)} is
the \(k\)-algebra of an affine \(k\)-group \(G_i\) of finite type
over \(k\). Finally, since
\[
  A=\varinjlim_i B_i,
\]
we have
\[
  G=\varprojlim_i G_i
\]
\textup{(EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.2.3)}. The transition morphisms are
faithfully flat by the following lemma:

\phantomsection\label{lem:sga3-VIB-11.14}%
\noindent\textbf{Lemma 11.14.---}
\emph{Let \(k\) be a field, let \(G\) and \(H\) be two affine
\(k\)-groups of finite type, let \(u:G\to H\) be a morphism of
\(k\)-groups, and let}
\[
  u^\natural:B\longrightarrow A
\]
\emph{be the corresponding homomorphism of \(k\)-algebras. For
\(u\) to be faithfully flat, it is necessary and sufficient that
\(u^\natural\) be injective.}

The condition is obviously necessary \textup{(EGA~IV\(_2\),
2.2.3, and EGA~0\(_{\mathrm I}\), 6.6.1)}.\footnote{Translator's
note: the 1970 text has 0\(_{\mathrm{IV}}\)~6.6.1.} Let us show
that it is sufficient. Put \(K=\operatorname{Ker}u\). Then
\(G/K\) is a \(k\)-group of finite type
\textup{(VI\(_A\), \hyperref[par:sga3-VIA-5.2]{5.2})}, and \(u\)
factors as
\[
  G\xrightarrow{\ p\ }G/K\xrightarrow{\ v\ }H,
\]
where \(p\) is faithfully flat
\textup{(VI\(_A\), \hyperref[thm:sga3-VIA-3.2]{3.2})} and where
\(v\) is a monomorphism of \(k\)-groups of finite type, hence a
closed immersion \textup{(\hyperref[cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2]{1.4.2})}.
Since \(H\) is an affine scheme and \(v\) is a closed immersion,
\(G/K\) is an affine scheme \textup{(EGA~I, 4.2.3)}, and, if
\(C\) denotes the algebra of \(G/K\), the homomorphism
\(v^\natural:B\to C\) is surjective \textup{(loc.\ cit.)}. Now,
since \(u^\natural\) is injective and
\(u^\natural=p^\natural\circ v^\natural\), \(v^\natural\) is also
injective: it is an isomorphism, hence \(v\) is an isomorphism,
and, since \(p\) is faithfully flat, so is \(u\).

\phantomsection\label{def:sga3-VIB-11.15}%
\noindent\textbf{Definition 11.15.---}
Given a field \(k\), a \(k\)-vector space \(V\), a \(k\)-group
\(G\) acting on \(V\), and a vector \(v\in V\), we say that \(v\)
is \emph{semi-invariant} under \(G\) if the vector subspace
\(kv\) is stable under \(G\).

Suppose \(G\) quasi-compact;\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text does not state this hypothesis, which the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, adds to the definition; by
\hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-11.6]{11.6} it ensures that the operation of \(G\) on \(V\) is given by a linear map
\(\rho\), a quasi-compact \(k\)-group being flat and separated over \(k\) (VI\(_A\),
\SGARefLink{sga3:VIA:0.3}{0.3}).} let \(A\) be the algebra of \(G\), and let
\(\rho:V\to A\otimes_kV\) be the linear map that defines the
operation of \(G\) on \(V\). To say that \(v\) is semi-invariant
under \(G\) amounts to saying that there is \(\lambda\in A\) such
that \(\rho(w)=\lambda\otimes w\) for every \(w\in kv\). When
\(v\neq0\), the element \(\lambda\in A\) is uniquely determined
by this relation; we shall call it the \emph{weight} of the
semi-invariant element \(v\). To say that \(v\) is semi-invariant
of weight \(\lambda\) amounts to saying that
\(\varphi(g,v)=\lambda(g)v\)
for every \(k\)-algebra \(R\) and every \(g\in G(R)\),\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has ``pour tout
\(g\in G(k)\)'', and ``semi-invariant d'invariant \(\lambda\)'' for ``of weight \(\lambda\)''. Rational points do not
suffice: for \(G=\mu_{p,k}\), \(k\) of characteristic \(p\), \(G(k)\) is trivial, so that every \(\lambda\) with
\(\lambda(1)=1\) would qualify. The Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, uses the points
with values in all \(k\)-algebras \(R\); taking \(R=A\) and \(g=\mathrm{id}_A\in G(A)\) gives back
\(\rho(v)=\lambda\otimes v\).} where \(\lambda:G\to\mathbb G_{m,k}\)
is the morphism defined by \(\lambda\). One therefore sees that
\(\lambda\) is a character of \(G\), called the
\emph{character associated} with the semi-invariant element
\(v\).

\phantomsection\label{thm:sga3-VIB-11.16}%
\noindent\textbf{Theorem 11.16 \textup{(Chevalley)}.---}
\emph{Let \(k\) be a field, let \(G\) be an affine algebraic
\(k\)-group, and let \(H\) be a closed group subscheme of \(G\).
Then there are finitely many linearly independent elements
\(a_i\) of the algebra \(A\) of \(G\) such that \(H\) is the
largest closed group subscheme of \(G\) under which the \(a_i\)
are semi-invariant; one may moreover suppose that all the
\(a_i\) have the same weight under \(H\).}

Recall that, since \(G\) is affine, so is \(H\)
\textup{(EGA~I, 4.2.3)}. Let \(A\) \textup{(}respectively
\(B\)\textup{)} be the algebra of \(G\) \textup{(}respectively
\(H\)\textup{)}; by EGA~I, 4.2.3, there is an ideal \(I\) of
\(A\) such that \(B\) is isomorphic to \(A/I\). Since \(G\)
acts on itself by left translations, it also acts on \(A\),
this operation being expressed by the diagonal map
\(\Delta:A\to A\otimes_kA\) \textup{(I,
\SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:4-2}{4.2} and
\SGARefLink{sga3:i:paragraph:4-7-2}{4.7.2})}. Since \(G\) is of
finite type over \(k\), \(A\) is of finite type over \(k\), so
\(I\) has a finite system of generators \((g_1,\ldots,g_r)\). It
follows from \hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-11.9]{11.9} that there is a
finite-dimensional vector subspace \(V\) of \(A\), stable under
\(G\) and containing each of the \(g_i\). Put \(W=V\cap I\); it
is a finite-dimensional vector space over \(k\), whose dimension
we denote by \(d\). Since \(V\) contains all the \(g_i\), \(W\)
generates the ideal \(I\). Let \(p:A\to A/I=B\) be the canonical
map, and \(q=p\otimes\mathrm{id}_A:A\otimes_kA\to(A/I)\otimes_kA\).
Then the action of \(H\) on \(A\) is determined by
\(q\circ\Delta:A\to B\otimes_kA\). Since \(H\) is a group
subscheme of \(G\), the diagonal map
\[
  A/I\longrightarrow(A/I)\otimes_k(A/I)
  =\bigl((A/I)\otimes_kA\bigr)/\bigl((A/I)\otimes_kI\bigr)
\]
is deduced from \(q\circ\Delta\) by passage to the quotient,
which shows that \((q\circ\Delta)(I)\subset(A/I)\otimes_kI\); in
other words, \(I\) is stable under \(H\). Since \(V\) is stable
under \(G\), hence under \(H\), we see that \(W\) is stable
under \(H\).

Put \(E=\bigwedge^dV\), let \((w_1,\ldots,w_d)\) be a basis of
\(W\) over \(k\), and let \((e_0,\ldots,e_n)\) be a basis of
\(E\) over \(k\) containing \(e_0=w_1\wedge\cdots\wedge w_d\).
The operation of \(G\) on \(V\) determines canonically an
operation of \(G\) on \(\bigwedge^dV\), which defines a linear
map \(\rho:E\to A\otimes_kE\). Put
\[
  \rho(e_i)=\sum_ja^i_j\otimes e_j,
  \qquad a_i=a^0_i.
\]
Since \(W\) is stable under \(H\), if we put
\(\sigma=(p\otimes\mathrm{id}_E)\circ\rho:E\to(A/I)\otimes_kE\),
it is clear that \(\sigma(e_0)\) is proportional to \(e_0\); in
other words, \(a_i\in I\) for \(1\leq i\leq n\). Axiom
\textup{(CM 1)} of I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:paragraph:4-7-2}{4.7.2},
applied to \(\rho(e_0)=\sum a_i\otimes e_i\), shows that
\[
  \sum_i\Delta(a_i)\otimes e_i=\sum_ia_i\otimes\rho(e_i)
  =\sum_{i,j}a_i\otimes a^i_j\otimes e_j,
\]
whence \(\Delta(a_i)=a_0\otimes a_i+\sum_{j\geq1}a_j\otimes a^j_i\);
hence, since \(a_j\in I\) for \(j\geq1\), we see that
\(q\circ\Delta(a_i)=p(a_0)\otimes a_i\), so that, for
\(1\leq i\leq n\), \(a_i\) is semi-invariant under \(H\) of weight
\(p(a_0)\), independent of \(i\). Extract from the system
\((a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) a maximal system of linearly independent
vectors, which we may suppose to be \((a_1,\ldots,a_m)\). Then
the \(a_i\), \(i=1,\ldots,m\), are linearly independent,
semi-invariant under \(H\), and of the same weight.

Conversely, let \(H'\) be a closed group subscheme of \(G\) under
which each of the \(a_i\) \((1\leq i\leq m)\) is semi-invariant.
Let us show that \(H'\subset H\).\footnote{Translator's note: the
1970 text says here ``Montrons que \(\mathrm H^1=\mathrm H\)'',
and the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B},
like Reinhold's English translation, ``\(H'=H\)''. The
maximality of \(H\) requires \(H'\subset H\), which is what is
proved below.} There is likewise an ideal \(I'\) of \(A\) such
that the algebra \(B'\) of \(H'\) is isomorphic to \(A/I'\); let
\(p':A\to A/I'\) be the canonical map, and
\(q'=p'\otimes\mathrm{id}_A\). By hypothesis, for
\(1\leq i\leq m\), there is \(\lambda_i\in B'\) such that
\[
  q'\circ\Delta(a_i)=\sum_jp'(a_j)\otimes a^j_i=\lambda_i\otimes a_i.
\]
Now axiom \textup{(CM 2)} of I,
\SGARefLink{sga3:i:paragraph:4-7-2}{4.7.2} shows that
\(e_i=\sum_j\eta(a^i_j)e_j\), hence that
\(\eta(a^i_j)=\delta_{ij}\) \textup{(}Kronecker
symbol\textup{)}; the equality
\(\sum_jp'(a_j)\eta(a^j_i)=\lambda_i\eta(a_i)\) therefore reads
\(p'(a_i)=0\) for \(1\leq i\leq m\), since
\(\eta(a_i)=\eta(a^0_i)=0\) for \(1\leq i\leq m\); consequently
\(a_i\in I'\) for \(1\leq i\leq m\), so that \(a_i\in I'\) for
\(1\leq i\leq n\). Hence, if we put
\(\sigma'=(p'\otimes\mathrm{id}_E)\circ\rho\),
\(\sigma'(e_0)=\sum p'(a_i)\otimes e_i\) is proportional to
\(e_0\), so that \(q'\circ\Delta(W)\subset(A/I')\otimes_kW\); in
other words, \(W\) is stable under \(H'\). Since \(W\) generates
the ideal \(I\), \(I\) is stable under \(H'\), so
\(q'\circ\Delta(I)\subset(A/I')\otimes_kI\), and \(q'\circ\Delta\)
defines by passage to the quotient a linear map
\[
  A/I\longrightarrow(A/I')\otimes_k(A/I)
  =\bigl((A/I')\otimes_kA\bigr)/\bigl((A/I')\otimes_kI\bigr);
\]
hence the restriction to \(H'\times_kH\) of the multiplication
morphism of \(G\) factors through \(H\), which implies that
\(H'\) is a group subscheme of \(H\), q.e.d.\footnote{Translator's
note: in this proof the 1970 text has ``(I 4.2.3)'' for
EGA~I, 4.2.3 in the first sentence, ``l'op\'eration de \(G\) sur
\(v\)'' for \(V\), the equality
\(\sum p'(a_j)\otimes\eta(a^i_j)=\lambda_j\otimes\eta(a_i)\), and
\(\sigma'=(p'\otimes\mathrm{id}_E)\circ\Delta\).}

\par\medskip\noindent
\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}; our formulation).
(a) The step ``\(\sigma'(e_0)\) is proportional to \(e_0\), so that \(W\) is stable under \(H'\)'' rests on the
following lemma, which the re-edition adds before 11.16 (after M.~Demazure and P.~Gabriel, \emph{Groupes
alg\'ebriques}, Masson and North-Holland, 1970, II, \S2, 3.5): \emph{let \(H\) be an affine \(k\)-group, \(V\) a
finite-dimensional \(H\)-module and \(U\) a vector subspace of \(V\) of dimension \(d\); if the line
\(\bigwedge^dU\) of \(\bigwedge^dV\) is stable under \(H\), then so is \(U\).} Indeed, let \((u_1,\ldots,u_n)\) be a
basis of \(V\) such that \((u_1,\ldots,u_d)\) is a basis of \(U\), and put \(\omega=u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge u_d\). For
every \(k\)-algebra \(R\) and every \(v=\sum c_iu_i\) in \(V_R=V\otimes_kR\), one has
\(v\wedge\omega=\sum_{i>d}c_i\,u_i\wedge\omega\), and the \(u_i\wedge\omega\) \((i>d)\) are part of a basis of
\(\bigwedge^{d+1}V_R\); hence \(U_R=\{v\in V_R\mid v\wedge\omega=0\}\). If \(h\in H(R)\), then \(h(\omega)=c\,\omega\)
with \(c\in R\), invertible because \(h\) is; so, for \(v\in U_R\), \(h(v)\wedge c\,\omega=h(v\wedge\omega)=0\), and
\(h(v)\in U_R\). Taking for \(h\) the universal point of \(H\) gives the stability of \(U\). Above, the lemma is
applied to \(H'\) and to \(U=W\subset V\).
(b) The re-edition states 11.16 in the form now usual: \emph{\(H\) is the stabilizer of a line in a
finite-dimensional representation of \(G\)}, namely of \(D=ke_0\) in \(E\), that is,
\(H(R)=\{g\in G(R)\mid g(D_R)=D_R\}\) for every \(k\)-algebra \(R\). Indeed, as \(g(e_0)=\sum_ia_i(g)e_i\), the
stabilizer of \(D\) is the closed subscheme \(N\) of \(G\) defined by the ideal generated by \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\); it is
a group subscheme (if \(g(e_0)=c\,e_0\), then \(c\) is invertible and \(g^{-1}(e_0)=c^{-1}e_0\)); it contains \(H\),
because \(a_i\in I\) for \(i\geq1\); and the second part of the proof, applied to \(H'=N\), for which \(a_i\in I'\)
\((i\geq1)\) holds by definition, gives \(N\subset H\). The re-edition also recalls that every group subscheme of
\(G\) is closed (\hyperref[cor:sga3-VIB-1.4.2]{1.4.2}), so that the word ``closed'' may be omitted from the
statement.
