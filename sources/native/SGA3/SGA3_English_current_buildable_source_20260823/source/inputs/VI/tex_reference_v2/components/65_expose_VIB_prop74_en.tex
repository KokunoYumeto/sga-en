
\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-7.4}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 7.4.---}
\emph{Let \(G\) be a \(k\)-group locally of finite type, \(X\)
a geometrically reduced \(k\)-scheme of finite type, and
\(f:X\to G\) a \(k\)-morphism. Suppose that \(X\) is
geometrically connected and that \(f(X)\) contains the unit
element \(e\) of \(G\). Then \textup{(}with the notation of
\hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-7.1-ii]{7.1\textup{(ii)}}\textup{)} there
exists an integer \(N\) such that}
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-7.4-stabilization}%
\[
  f^N(X^N)=\Gamma_G(f)
  \qquad\textup{(set-theoretically)}.
\]

\emph{Proof.}
By \hyperref[concl:sga3-VIB-7.1-iii]{7.1\textup{(iii)}} and
EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.6.1, we may assume that \(k\) is algebraically
closed. By Remark
\hyperref[rem:sga3-VIB-7.2-vi]{7.2\textup{(vi)}}, we may assume
that \(G=G^0\); finally, by Remark
\hyperref[rem:sga3-VIB-7.2-v]{7.2\textup{(v)}}, it is enough to
show that there exists an integer \(N\) such that, with the
notation of 7.2\textup{(v)},
\[
  f'^{\,N}(X'^{\,N})=\Gamma_G(f).
\]

\emph{First case.}
Suppose that \(X\) is irreducible. The closed irreducible
subsets
\[
  \overline{f'^{\,n}(X'^{\,n})}
\]
then form an increasing sequence in the space \(G\), which is
Noetherian because \(G=G^0\) is of finite type over \(k\)
(\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIA-2.4]{VI\(_A\), 2.4}). Hence this
sequence is stationary, and there exists an integer \(m\) such
that
\[
  \overline{f'^{\,m}(X'^{\,m})}=\Gamma_G(f).
\]

Moreover, because \(X\) and \(G\) are of finite type over
\(k\), the morphisms \(f'^{\,n}\) are of finite type over
\(k\). Consequently, \(f'^{\,m}(X'^{\,m})\) is constructible
in \(G\) (EGA~IV\(_1\), 1.8.5), and therefore contains an open
subset \(U\) of its closure \(\Gamma_G(f)\)
(EGA~0\(_{\mathrm{IV}}\), 9.2.3). By
\hyperref[sga3:VIA:0.5]{VI\(_A\), 0.5}, one then has
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-7.4-first-case-chain}%
\[
  \Gamma_G(f)
  \subset U\cdot U
  \subset f'^{\,2m}(X'^{\,2m})
  \subset \Gamma_G(f),
\]
and hence
\[
  f'^{\,2m}(X'^{\,2m})=\Gamma_G(f).
\]

\emph{Second case.}
Suppose that \(X\) has two irreducible components \(A_1\) and
\(A_2\). Then there exists
\[
  a\in(A_1\cap A_2)(k),
\]
since \(X\) is connected. Hence the four irreducible subsets
\[
  A_i\times_kA_j
  \qquad (i,j=1,2)
\]
cover \(X\times_kX\), and the image of each of them under
\(f'^{\,1}\) contains \(e\). Let \(f'^{\,1}_{ij}\) denote the restriction of
\(f'^{\,1}\) to the reduced subscheme \(A_i\times_kA_j\), and
put
\[
\begin{aligned}
  Y={}&
  (A_1\times_kA_1)
  \times_k(A_1\times_kA_2)
  \times_k(A_2\times_kA_2)
  \times_k(A_2\times_kA_1),\\
  g={}&
  \mu\circ\Bigl(
    \mu\circ
      (f'^{\,1}_{11}\times_k f'^{\,1}_{12})
    \times_k
    \mu\circ
      (f'^{\,1}_{22}\times_k f'^{\,1}_{21})
  \Bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
The scheme \(Y\) is irreducible, reduced, and of finite type.
By the first case, there is therefore an integer \(m\) such
that
\[
  g'^{\,m}(Y'^{\,m})=\Gamma_G(g).
\]
But in fact
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-7.4-two-component-chain}%
\[
  f'^{\,1}(X'^{\,1})
  \subset g(Y)
  \subset f'^{\,4}(X'^{\,4}),
\]
so that
\[
  \Gamma_G(f)=\Gamma_G(g)
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  f'^{\,4m}(X'^{\,4m})=\Gamma_G(f).
\]

\emph{General case.}
We argue by induction on the number of irreducible components
of \(X\) (this number is finite because \(X\), being of finite
type over \(k\), is Noetherian). Suppose the proposition has
been proved when \(X\) has at most \(r-1\) irreducible
components, and suppose that \(X\) has \(r\) of them, namely
\(A_1,\ldots,A_r\). Then \(e\) belongs to the image of one of the
\(A_i\); suppose, for example, that \(e\in f(A_1)\). Put
\[
  Y=\Gamma_G(f_r),
\]
where \(f_r\) denotes the restriction of \(f\) to the reduced
subscheme \(A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_{r-1}\) (we suppose the \(A_i\)
numbered so that this scheme is connected, which is always
possible). Put
\[
  Z=\overline{f(A_r)}.
\]
Then \(Y\) is an irreducible, reduced, closed subgroup of \(G\)
(\hyperref[rem:sga3-VIB-7.2-vi]{7.2\textup{(vi)}}). Let then \(T=Y\cup Z\), and let \(i\) be the
injection of the reduced closed subscheme \(T\) of \(G\). It is
clear (\hyperref[rem:sga3-VIB-7.2-ii]{7.2\textup{(ii)}}) that
\[
  \Gamma_G(i)=\Gamma_G(f)
\]
and that \(T\) is connected (since \(X\) is connected,
\(A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_{r-1}\) and \(A_r\) have a common point
\(a\), and \(Y\) and \(Z\) have the common point \(f(a)\)).
Moreover, \(e\in T\), and \(T\) has at most two irreducible
components, since \(Y\) and \(Z\) are irreducible. By the
induction hypothesis, there exists an integer \(m\) such that,
if \(X_r\) denotes the reduced subscheme
\(A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_{r-1}\) of \(X\), one has
\[
  f_r'^{\,m}(X_r'^{\,m})
  =\Gamma_G(f_r)=Y.
\]
Hence, since \(X_r\subset X\) and \(Z\subset\overline{f(X)}\),
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-7.4-general-case-inclusion}%
\[
  f(X)
  \subset Y\cup Z
  \subset\overline{f'^{\,m}(X'^{\,m})}.
\]
On the other hand, since \(T\) has at most two irreducible
components, we have already seen that there exists an integer
\(p\) such that
\[
  i'^{\,p}(T'^{\,p})=\Gamma_G(i).
\]
Now \(\Gamma_G(i)=\Gamma_G(f)\) and
\(T\subset\overline{f'^{\,m}(X'^{\,m})}\); hence
\[
  \overline{f'^{\,mp}(X'^{\,mp})}=\Gamma_G(f),
\]
and one shows, as in the first case, that this last equality
implies\footnote{Translator's note: the exponents in this proof are not exact, in the 1970 text as in the
Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Exposé~VI\textsubscript{B}. As each \(f'^{\,n}(X'^{\,n})\) is stable under
inversion, \(g(Y)\subset f'^{\,4}(X'^{\,4})\) gives \(g'^{\,m}(Y'^{\,m})\subset f'^{\,8m}(X'^{\,8m})\), so the
second case yields \(f'^{\,8m}(X'^{\,8m})=\Gamma_G(f)\); likewise, in the general case,
\(i'^{\,p}(T'^{\,p})\) lies in the closure of \(f'^{\,2mp}(X'^{\,2mp})\), whence
\(f'^{\,4mp}(X'^{\,4mp})=\Gamma_G(f)\). Only the existence of \(N\) matters for 7.4.}
\[
  f'^{\,2mp}(X'^{\,2mp})=\Gamma_G(f).
\]
