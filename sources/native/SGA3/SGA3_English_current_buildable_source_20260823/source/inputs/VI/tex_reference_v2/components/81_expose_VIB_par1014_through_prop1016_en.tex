
\medskip
Statements \hyperref[lem:sga3-VIB-10.2]{10.2} and
\hyperref[lem:sga3-VIB-10.3]{10.3}, concerning the category
of \(S\)-groups of finite presentation, extend to the category
of pairs consisting of an \(S\)-group of finite presentation
and an \(S\)-scheme of finite presentation with operator group
\(G\). More precisely:

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{par:sga3-VIB-10.14}%
\noindent\textbf{10.14.---}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-10.14-i}
  In the situation recalled at the beginning of
  \hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-10.0]{10.0}, let \(j\in I\), and let \(G_j\) and \(G'_j\) be two
  \(S_j\)-groups of finite presentation; let \(H_j\)
  \textup{(respectively, \(H'_j\))} be an \(S_j\)-scheme of
  finite presentation with operator group \(G_j\)
  \textup{(respectively, \(G'_j\))}. For \(i\in I\),
  \(i\geq j\), put
  \[
    G_i=G_j\times_{S_j}S_i,
    \qquad
    G=G_j\times_{S_j}S,
  \]
  and define \(G'_i\), \(G'\), \(H_i\), \(H\), \(H'_i\),
  and \(H'\) similarly. Denote by
  \[
    \operatorname{Dihom}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr.}}
      \bigl((G,H),(G',H')\bigr)
  \]
  the set of di-morphisms of \(S\)-groups and \(S\)-schemes
  with operator groups from the pair \((G,H)\) to the pair
  \((G',H')\). Then the canonical map
  \phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-10.14-Dihom-limit}%
  \[
    \varinjlim_{i\geq j}
      \operatorname{Dihom}_{S_i\text{-}\mathrm{gr.}}
        \bigl((G_i,H_i),(G'_i,H'_i)\bigr)
      \longrightarrow
      \operatorname{Dihom}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr.}}
        \bigl((G,H),(G',H')\bigr)
  \]
  is bijective.

\item[\textup{(ii)}]\phantomsection\label{concl:sga3-VIB-10.14-ii}
  In the situation recalled at the beginning of
  \hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-10.0]{10.0}, let \(G\) be an \(S\)-group of finite presentation and
  \(H\) an \(S\)-scheme of finite presentation with operator
  group \(G\). Then there are an index \(j\in I\), an
  \(S_j\)-group \(G_j\) of finite presentation, an
  \(S_j\)-scheme \(H_j\) of finite presentation with operator
  group \(G_j\), and a di-isomorphism of \(S\)-groups and
  \(S\)-schemes with operator groups from
  \[
    \bigl(G_j\times_{S_j}S,\ H_j\times_{S_j}S\bigr)
  \]
  onto \((G,H)\).
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{def:sga3-VIB-10.15}%
\noindent\textbf{Definition 10.15.---}%
\emph{Given an \(S\)-functor in groups \(G\) and an \(S\)-functor \(X\)
with operator group \(G\), one says that \(X\) is an \(S\)-functor in
homogeneous spaces under \(G\) for the topology \(\mathcal T\) if the
canonical morphism}
\phantomsection\label{form:sga3-VIB-10.15-Phi}%
\[
  G\times_S X\longrightarrow X\times_S X
\]
\emph{\textup{(}defined by \((g,x)\mapsto(g\cdot x,x)\) for \(g\in G(T)\),
\(x\in X(T)\)\textup{)} is an epimorphism of sheaves for the topology
\(\mathcal T\).}\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011),
Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, following M.~Raynaud (\emph{Faisceaux amples sur les sch\'emas en groupes et les
espaces homog\`enes}, Lecture Notes in Math.\ \textbf{119}, Springer, 1970, VI, 1.1), takes \(\mathcal T\)
coarser than the canonical topology, calls such an \(X\) \emph{formally homogeneous}, and reserves
\emph{homogeneous} for the case where moreover \(X\to S\) is a covering for \(\mathcal T\); it calls a principal
homogeneous bundle a \emph{\(G\)-torsor} (see the \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:definition:6:7:1}{Addition at the end of
IV, \S\,6}). Statement 10.16 below holds for either notion, with the same proof, since the property of
\(X\to S\) of being a covering for the \textup{(fppf)} topology also passes to the limit
(\hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-10.1]{10.1}).}

If \(G\) is an \(S\)-group and \(X\) an \(S\)-scheme with operator group
\(G\), to say that \(X\) is an \(S\)-scheme in homogeneous spaces under
\(G\) for the topology \(\mathcal T\) therefore amounts to saying that the
canonical morphism \(G\times_SX\to X\times_SX\) defined as above is a
covering for the topology \(\mathcal T\)
\textup{(IV, \hyperref[sga3:iv:proposition:4:4:3]{4.4.3})}.

Likewise, to say that an \(S\)-scheme \(X\) with operator group an
\(S\)-group \(G\) is a principal homogeneous bundle under \(G\) for the
topology \(\mathcal T\) \textup{(IV, \hyperref[sga3:iv:definition:5:1:5]{5.1.5})}
amounts to saying
\textup{(IV, \hyperref[sga3:iv:proposition:5:1:6:item:ii:01]{5.1.6~(ii)})} that
the canonical morphism \(G\times_SX\to X\times_SX\) is an isomorphism and
that the structural morphism \(X\to S\) is a covering for the topology
\(\mathcal T\).

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-10.16}%
\noindent\textbf{10.16.---}
\emph{In the situation recalled at the beginning of
\hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-10.0]{10.0}, let \(j\in I\), let \(G_j\) be an
\(S_j\)-group of finite presentation, and let \(X_j\) be an
\(S_j\)-scheme of finite presentation with operator group \(G_j\). For}
\[
  X=X_j\times_{S_j}S
\]
\emph{to be an \(S\)-scheme in homogeneous spaces under}
\[
  G=G_j\times_{S_j}S
\]
\emph{\textup{(respectively, a principal homogeneous bundle under \(G\))}
for the faithfully flat topology locally of finite presentation, it is
necessary and sufficient that there be an index \(i\in I\), \(i\geq j\),
such that}
\[
  X_i=X_j\times_{S_j}S_i
\]
\emph{be an \(S_i\)-scheme in homogeneous spaces under}
\[
  G_i=G_j\times_{S_j}S_i
\]
\emph{\textup{(respectively, a principal homogeneous bundle under
\(G_i\))}.}

Taking into account \hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-10.14]{10.14} and
EGA~IV\(_3\), 8.8.2, 8.8.3, and 8.10.5, the assertion follows
from the property of covering morphisms for the
\textup{(fppf)} topology recalled in
\hyperref[par:sga3-VIB-10.1]{10.1}.\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text refers to 10.0 here; the property is
recalled in 10.1, to which the Gille--Polo re-edition (2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, refers. For
passage to the limit for torsors expressed in terms of \v{C}ech cohomology, the re-edition also refers to SGA~4,
VII, 5.7 and its corollaries, and to B.~Margaux, \emph{Passage to the limit in non-abelian \v{C}ech cohomology},
J.\ Lie Theory \textbf{17} (2007), 591--596.}
