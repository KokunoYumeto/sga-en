\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-3.11}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 3.11.---}
\emph{Let \(G\) and \(H\) be two \(S\)-groups locally of finite
presentation, and \(u:G\to H\) a morphism of \(S\)-groups; then, if \(u\)
is flat, the map}
\[
  u^0:G^0\longrightarrow H^0
\]
\emph{induced by \(u\) is surjective; the converse holds if \(G\) is flat
over \(S\) and \(H\) has reduced fibers.}

Suppose then that \(u\) is flat; then for every \(s\in S\), \(u_s\) is
flat and locally of finite presentation, hence open (EGA~IV\(_2\), 2.4.6),
so that the morphism
\[
  u_s^0:\underline{G_s^0}\longrightarrow\underline{H_s^0}
\]
is dominant. Since \(\underline{G_s^0}\) is of finite type over \(\kappa(s)\)
(Expos\'e~VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{prop:sga3-VIA-2.4}{2.4}) and \(H_s\) is separated over \(\kappa(s)\)
(Expos\'e~VI\(_A\), \SGARefLink{sga3:VIA:0.3}{0.3}),\footnote{Translator's note: the 1970 text has 0.2 here
as well; cf.\ the note in the proof of 1.3.2.} \(u_s^0\) is quasi-compact (EGA~I, 6.6.4), hence
surjective (\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-1.2]{1.2}). Hence \(u^0\) is surjective.

Conversely, suppose that \(u^0\) is surjective, that \(G\) is flat over
\(S\) and that \(H\) has reduced fibers. Then, for every \(s\in S\),
\(u_s^0\) is surjective and \(H_s\) is reduced; hence
\(\underline{H_s^0}\) is locally Noetherian and integral, and \(u_s^0\) is of
finite type, hence flat at the generic point of \(G_s\)
(EGA~IV\(_2\), 6.9.1), so that \(u_s\) is flat (\hyperref[prop:sga3-VIB-1.3]{1.3}), and therefore
\(u\) is flat (EGA~IV\(_3\), 11.3.11).\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo re-edition
(2011), Expos\'e~VI\textsubscript{B}, shortens both parts. In the first, \(u_s\) is open, so that by
\hyperref[cor:sga3-VIB-1.3.2]{1.3.2} (condition (ii) implies (iv)) \(u_s^0\) is surjective. In the second, generic
flatness is not needed: \(\underline{H_s^0}\) being reduced and irreducible, its local ring at the
generic point \(\eta\) is a field, so that \(u_s\) is flat at every point over \(\eta\), in particular at the
generic point of \(\underline{G_s^0}\), which \(u_s^0\) maps to \(\eta\).}

\section*{4. Dimension of the fibers of groups locally of finite presentation}
\phantomsection\label{sec:sga3-VIB-4}%
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{4. Dimension of the fibers of groups locally of finite presentation}

\medskip
\phantomsection\label{prop:sga3-VIB-4.1}%
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 4.1.---}
\emph{Let \(G\) be an \(S\)-scheme locally of finite type, equipped
with an \(S\)-section \(\epsilon\), and suppose that, for every
\(s\in S\),}
\[
  \dim G_s=\dim_{\epsilon(s)}G_s
\]
\emph{\textup{(as is the case if \(G\) is an \(S\)-group
\textup(\hyperref[lem:sga3-VIB-1.5]{1.5}\textup))}. Then the function}
\[
  s\longmapsto\dim G_s
\]
\emph{is upper semicontinuous on \(S\). If, moreover, \(G\) is locally of
finite presentation over \(S\), this function is locally constructible.}

Let
\[
  \pi:G\longrightarrow S
\]
be the structural morphism. Chevalley's theorem
(EGA~IV\(_3\), 13.1.3) asserts that the function
\[
  x\longmapsto\dim_x\pi^{-1}\bigl(\pi(x)\bigr)
\]
is upper semicontinuous on \(G\). For every \(s\in S\), however,
\[
  \dim G_s
    =\dim\pi^{-1}(s)
    =\dim_{\epsilon(s)}
       \pi^{-1}\bigl(\pi(\epsilon(s))\bigr).
\]
Since the map \(s\mapsto\epsilon(s)\) is continuous on \(S\), the
composite function
\[
  s\longmapsto\dim G_s
\]
is upper semicontinuous on \(S\).

Now suppose that \(G\) is locally of finite presentation over
\(S\). Arguing as above, to show that \(s\mapsto\dim G_s\) is
locally constructible, it is enough to show that the function
\[
  x\longmapsto\dim_x\pi^{-1}\bigl(\pi(x)\bigr)
\]
is locally constructible on \(G\), which follows from
EGA~IV\(_3\), 9.9.1.
