
\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:viii:corollary:1:4}\textbf{Corollary 1.4.}
\textit{A diagonalizable group is reflexive, and therefore so is a locally
diagonalizable group. If \(M\) and \(N\) are ordinary commutative groups,
the natural homomorphism}
\SGARefTarget{sga3:viii:corollary:1:4:display:natural-isomorphism:01}\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(M_S,N_S)
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(D(N_S),D(M_S))
\]
\textit{is bijective.}

From this one deduces an isomorphism of functors over \(S\):
\[
  \operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(M_S,N_S)
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}
    (D(N_S),D(M_S)).
\]
On the other hand, one defines in an evident way a homomorphism
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr}}(M,N)_S
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(M_S,N_S),
\]
which is always a monomorphism and is an isomorphism as soon as \(M\) is of
finite type.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~VIII,
details this. The bijection of 1.4, formed over every \(S\)-prescheme \(T\) in place of \(S\), is
compatible with base change, which gives the isomorphism of functors. The \(T\)-points of a constant
prescheme \(E_S\) are the locally constant maps \(T\to E\) (I, \SGARefLink{sga3:i:subsection:1:8}{1.8}), and
\(\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(M_S,N_S)(T)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr}}(M,N_S(T))\)
(see above); the homomorphism sends such a map \(f:T\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr}}(M,N)\) to the homomorphism
\(m\mapsto(t\mapsto f(t)(m))\) from \(M\) to \(N_S(T)\), which determines \(f\). If
\(m_1,\ldots,m_r\) generate \(M\) and \(\varphi:M\to N_S(T)\) is a homomorphism, the
\(\varphi(m_i)\), hence all the \(\varphi(m)\), are constant near each point \(t\) of \(T\), so that
\(t\mapsto(m\mapsto\varphi(m)(t))\) is a locally constant map \(T\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr}}(M,N)\)
with image \(\varphi\).}

We thus find:

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:viii:corollary:1:5}\textbf{Corollary 1.5.}
\textit{Let \(M\) and \(N\) be ordinary commutative groups, with \(M\) of
finite type. Then
\(\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(D(N_S),D(M_S))\) is
representable, and there is an isomorphism}
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr}}(M,N)_S
  \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
  \operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}
    (D(N_S),D(M_S)).
\]

From this one concludes more generally that if \(G\) and \(H\) are locally
diagonalizable, with \(H\) of finite type, then
\(\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(G,H)\) is
representable.\footnote{Translator's note: the Gille--Polo draft re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~VIII,
states this as part (b) of 1.5 and proves it by descent; see the Addition after 1.6, (v).} One concludes from \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:corollary:1:5}{1.5}:

\medskip
\noindent\SGARefTarget{sga3:viii:corollary:1:6}\textbf{Corollary 1.6.}
\textit{Under the hypotheses of \SGARefLink{sga3:viii:corollary:1:5}{1.5}, if \(S\) is connected, then}
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(D_S(N),D_S(M))
  \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{gr}}(M,N)
\]
\textit{and}
\[
  \operatorname{Isom}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(D_S(N),D_S(M))
  \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
  \operatorname{Isom}_{\mathrm{gr}}(M,N).
\]

\noindent\SGAInlineRefTarget{sga3:viii:paragraph:1-7}\emph{Addition} (after the Gille--Polo draft
re-edition (2009), Expos\'e~VIII; our formulation). The draft re-edition inserts here a paragraph 1.7
(numbered 1.7.1--1.7.4) making explicit that representability, as it is used in this exposé, is a
local question on \(S\) and descends along faithfully flat quasi-compact morphisms. We state and prove
what is needed.

(i) For \(S\)-preschemes \(X\) and \(Y\), the functor
\(\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_S(X,Y)\colon T\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_T(X_T,Y_T)\) on
\((\mathbf{Sch})_{/S}\) is a sheaf for the faithfully flat quasi-compact topology, hence also for the
Zariski topology. Indeed \(\operatorname{Hom}_T(X_T,Y_T)=\operatorname{Hom}_S(X_T,Y)\); if
\((T_i\to T)\) is a covering for this topology, so is \((X_{T_i}\to X_T)\), and the representable
functor \(\operatorname{Hom}_S(-,Y)\) is a sheaf (IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:6:3:1:item:iii:01}{6.3.1~(iii)};
cf.\ IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:corollary:4:5:13}{4.5.13}).
If \(X\) and \(Y\) are \(S\)-groups, \(\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(X,Y)\) is a
subsheaf: whether a morphism \(X_T\to Y_T\) is a homomorphism is an equality between two morphisms
\(X_T\times_TX_T\to Y_T\), which may be checked after a covering.

(ii) Let \(F\) be a functor on \((\mathbf{Sch})_{/S}\) that is a sheaf for the Zariski topology, and
let \((S_i)\) be an open covering of \(S\) such that each \(F\times_SS_i\) is represented by an
\(S_i\)-prescheme \(X_i\) (cf.\ XI, \SGARefLink{sga3:xi:remark:3:4}{3.4}). Over
\(S_{ij}=S_i\cap S_j\) the restrictions of \(X_i\) and \(X_j\) both represent \(F\times_SS_{ij}\),
whence a unique isomorphism between them compatible with these identifications; by uniqueness these
isomorphisms satisfy the cocycle condition over the \(S_i\cap S_j\cap S_k\), so the \(X_i\) glue to an
\(S\)-prescheme \(X\), and the isomorphisms \(F\times_SS_i\simeq h_X\times_SS_i\) agree over the
\(S_{ij}\). For an \(S\)-prescheme \(T\), put \(T_i=T\times_SS_i\); as \(F\) and \(h_X\) are Zariski
sheaves, \(F(T)\) and \(h_X(T)\) are both identified with the families of elements of the \(F(T_i)\)
that agree on the \(T_i\cap T_j\). Hence \(F\) is represented by \(X\).

(iii) Let \(F\) be a sheaf for the faithfully flat quasi-compact topology, \(S'\to S\) a faithfully
flat quasi-compact morphism, and suppose that \(F\times_SS'\) is represented by an \(S'\)-prescheme
\(X'\). The inverse images of \(X'\) by the two projections of \(S''=S'\times_SS'\) both represent
\(F\times_SS''\), whence a unique isomorphism \(c\) between them; on
\(S'''=S'\times_SS'\times_SS'\) the inverse images of \(c\) are isomorphisms between objects
representing \(F\times_SS'''\), so by uniqueness they satisfy the cocycle condition: \(c\) is a
descent datum on \(X'\) relative to \(S'\to S\) (IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:definition:2:1}{2.1}).
Suppose it is effective, which is the case when \(X'\) is affine over \(S'\) (SGA~1, VIII, 2.1; for
another criterion see X, \SGARefLink{sga3:x:lemma:5-4}{5.4}), and let \(X\) be the \(S\)-prescheme
obtained. For an \(S\)-prescheme \(T\), put \(T'=T\times_SS'\); then \(F(T')\simeq h_X(T')\), and
likewise over \(T'\times_TT'\), compatibly with the two projections. Since \(F\) and \(h_X\) are
sheaves for this topology (for \(h_X\), IV, \SGARefLink{sga3:iv:proposition:6:3:1:item:iii:01}{6.3.1~(iii)}), \(F(T)\) and \(h_X(T)\) are both identified with the elements of
\(F(T')\) having equal images in \(F(T'\times_TT')\). Hence \(F\) is represented by \(X\).

(iv) Consequently, let \(F\) be a sheaf for the faithfully flat quasi-compact topology, and suppose
that \(S\) has an open covering \((S_i)\) with, for each \(i\), a faithfully flat quasi-compact
morphism \(S'_i\to S_i\) such that \(F\times_SS'_i\) is represented by an \(S'_i\)-prescheme \(X'_i\)
affine over \(S'_i\). By (iii) each \(F\times_SS_i\) is represented by an \(S_i\)-prescheme \(X_i\),
affine over \(S_i\) since affineness descends (SGA~1, VIII, 5.6), and by (ii) \(F\) is represented by
an \(S\)-prescheme \(X\) affine over \(S\), with \(X\times_SS'_i\simeq X'_i\). If moreover every
\(X'_i\to S'_i\) is a closed immersion (resp.\ finite and étale), so is \(X\to S\), because these
properties descend along faithfully flat quasi-compact morphisms (SGA~1, VIII, 5.5; EGA~IV\(_2\),
2.7.1; EGA~IV\(_4\), 17.7.3).

(v) In particular the assertion following 1.5 holds. If \(G\) and \(H\) are locally diagonalizable
and \(H\) is of finite type, then near every point of \(S\) one has \(G\simeq D(M_S)\) and
\(H\simeq D(N_S)\), with \(N\) of finite type by 2.1~(b), so that
\(\operatorname{\underline{Hom}}_{S\text{-}\mathrm{gr}}(G,H)\) is representable there by 1.5 (applied
with \(M\) and \(N\) interchanged); by (i) and (ii), it is representable.
